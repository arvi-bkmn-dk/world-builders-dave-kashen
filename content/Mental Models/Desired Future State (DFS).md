# Desired Future State (DFS)

## What It Is

The mechanism for generating creative tension instead of fear-based tension. A DFS is a clearly declared future that you hold *alongside* an accepted present — not instead of it, and not as a stick to beat the present with. It is how [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel|clean fuel]] gets made in practice.

## The Model

1. **Be present to current reality** — accurately, and without making it wrong. → [[Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong]]
2. **Declare the desired future state** — who will do what by when. Specific enough to align people and to be inspiring rather than shaming.
3. **Hold both at once** — the gap between them is creative tension, and that is the fuel.
4. **Stay unattached to it** — a DFS is a guidepost and an alignment device, not a condition of your wellbeing. → [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]]

**The canonical example.** JFK: *we will put a man on the moon and return him safely to the earth by the end of the decade.* Dave's point is what JFK did **not** say — he didn't say the Russians are beating us and we're terrible. He didn't lie about where they were (nothing had left the ground), and he didn't make where they were wrong. He also demonstrated the declaration doing real work: language creating a reality rather than reporting one. → [[Language is a tool to create reality, not only to describe it]]

## How Dave Uses It

He names the characteristic failure directly, on himself: *collapsing the DFS into the present* — holding a vision of impacting millions, seeing that you've reached fifty, and using the vision as a weapon. "What's wrong with me?" The DFS becomes dirty fuel the moment your wellbeing is made to depend on it.

Jesse adds the practical defence: stubborn on vision, flexible on the details. A specific attempt failing is not the DFS failing, because the DFS was never the tactic. → [[Failure is a roadblock to reroute around, not a verdict]]
