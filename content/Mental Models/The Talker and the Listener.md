# The Talker and the Listener

## What It Is

Dave's opening move with almost every new client, and his route into self-awareness. It starts with a question nobody has an answer ready for:

> A thing I do in almost all my initial sessions with clients is ask them: **how do you know when you're thinking?**

Clients look at him quizzically. *I'm always thinking.* *I look up and to the right.* The mechanical answer he's after:

> Consider that the way you know you're thinking is you hear a voice in your head talking.

## The Model

**1. The experience implies two roles.** This is the whole argument, and it's structural rather than mystical:

> In order to have the experience of hearing a voice in your head talking, there must be a talker and a listener. Otherwise, how would you have that experience?

**2. Most people are identified as the talker.** Not as a belief they hold — as the default they've never examined. → [[Hearing a voice in your head means there is a talker and a listener]]

**3. The shift is to identify as the listener.**

> One of the most powerful shifts you can make as a human being is to identify as the listener instead, because you get freedom and you get choice.

→ [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]]

**4. Then examine the relationship with the voice.** Dave's image: you're peacefully sipping tea in a coffee shop and a muttering stranger sits down and says *you suck, you're not good enough, you're not as good a coach as that other guy.* Do you listen, believe him and engage — or keep sipping your tea?

The difficulty is precisely that this voice sounds like you and appears to come from inside. A drunk kid shouting *you suck* on the street is obviously ignorable. The same content in your own voice isn't.

## How Dave Uses It

As the prerequisite for everything else. Jesse spent roughly a year being told *first you must notice* — which he heard as Mr Miyagi wax-on-wax-off — before he could isolate the voice as separate from himself even a little. His verdict: without that self-awareness cultivated early, the rest of the work is impossible.

Dave is clear about the scope: "that could be years of growth for someone, just starting to distinguish — oh, that's not me, that's the voice in my head talking."

## The Book Chapter

Dave's opening question, which he asks many clients in a first session — **how do you know when you're thinking?** Most answer *when my mouth isn't moving* or *I'm always thinking.*

> Consider that you know you're thinking because **you literally hear a voice in your head talking.** You can test it right now. **Say hello three times in your mind.**

**The drunk-stranger test**, in its fullest written form:

**The arithmetic.** A thought about every **1.2 seconds** — *"your heart pumps blood and your mind makes thoughts."* And citing a National Science Foundation article: **12,000 to 60,000 thoughts a day, 95% repetitive from yesterday, 80% negative.**
