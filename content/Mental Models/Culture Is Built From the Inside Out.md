# Culture Is Built From the Inside Out

## What It Is

Dave's correction of standard culture work, arrived at by watching it fail.

**The building metaphor:** values work is *"analogous to building only the visible parts of a building — its walls, windows and interior structures. Even though no one can see it, **if you don't have a solid foundation below the ground you won't have a structurally sound building.**"*

**And via Maslow:** living your values sits at **self-actualisation**, the top of the pyramid. *"If you don't first create the conditions within yourself and your organisation for **psychological safety and for a sense of belonging and significance**, your efforts to align your values will be in vain."*

## Why Values Work Still Matters — with a warning

Dave doesn't discard the visible half, he **sequences** it: *"a leader who works on her company's values **before** doing her own inner work may be building a house without a solid foundation."*

## The Five Exercises for Clarifying Personal Values

Because *"it can be hard to simply name the set of values that ring true to you,"* all of these **reverse-engineer** values from lived experience.

1. **No fear** — *"What would you do if you weren't afraid?"* Asked what she'd do about Rupert, Erin answered instantly: **let him go.** Unpacking why produced safety, kindness, openness, flexibility, collaboration, consideration, respect.
2. **Peak experiences** — graph your energy and aliveness from birth to now; what's at the peaks? Erin's was an eighth-grade championship, and what moved her wasn't the win but the coach subbing in a novice player before halftime — who then scored. She landed on **inclusivity.**
3. **Loved ones** — the people you *chose*, not the ones you were born to. What do you most value in them? Erin's boyfriend: **generous** — which she added as an **aspirational** value, not one she already embodied.
4. **The Stanford application** — *"what matters most to you, and why?"* One of only two questions on the Stanford GSB application.
5. **Anti-values** — *"what pisses you off?"* *"The reason these traits anger you is that they violate some of your most deeply held values."* → [[Being irritated by someone shows you a part of yourself you have not loved]]

Plus the **Enneagram** as a shortcut, with each type's characteristic values listed. → [[The Enneagram Box]]

**Then rank, and cap at five:** *"trying to hold more than five values in your mind is difficult, and **it's better to have a few memorable ones than a comprehensive list you forget.**"*

**Then rate yourself** 1–10 on how fully you're honouring each, as a **weekly** practice — *"it's easy to drift away from our values unless we regularly and deliberately turn our attention to them."*
