# The conditions you learned to survive become the conditions your survival depends on

Dave recalls a line from a couple of years of neurolinguistic programming training, and it supplies the mechanism underneath [[The Upper Limit Problem]].

> The conditions we learn to survive become the conditions upon which our continued survival depends.

His unpacking:

> Our creature brain knows that everything we've experienced up until now — all the sadness, anger, fear, dysfunctional relationships — we have *survived*. So it knows with 100% certainty we can survive this level of joy. But no more.

The logic is impeccable and that's what makes it hard to argue with. Survival is the only thing the system has evidence for, and it has evidence only up to the level already experienced. Everything beyond is untested.

> When we start to allow more love, more joy, more aliveness, more freedom, there's a part of our brain that's like: I actually don't know if this is survivable. I don't know with certainty.

This explains two things that otherwise look like different problems. It explains the defence of dirty fuel — *don't I need my pain and my fear and my sense of not-good-enough in order to drive me?* — as the system protecting a configuration with a proven survival record. And it explains Andrew's superstition, which he names himself:

> In order to get here I had all that. I want to get all the things, in order to make sure they don't go away.

The familiar suffering isn't valued for its results. It's valued because you're still here, and it was present the whole time. → [[Letting go of self-criticism does not make creative drive disappear]]
