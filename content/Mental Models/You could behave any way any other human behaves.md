# You could behave any way any other human behaves

The payoff of the whole episode — what you get on the other side of a persona.

> There's this notion that if you could behave any way any other human behaves, you could achieve anything any other human achieves. Like Steve Jobs, or Elon, or whoever your heroes are — there's no fundamental reason why you couldn't do what they do.

The only thing in the way is the sentence Jesse supplies before Dave can: *that's not who I am.* Dave: "That's the comfort thing." → [[That is not authentic is usually just not comfortable]]

The positive name for the alternative:

> The other side of the restrictive personas is total behavioural flexibility. If I could in any given moment show up in full presence, without restrictions on how I'm allowed to be — then I could show up the best way to meet the moment. I could respond in a way that is most likely to create the results that I want.

And he ties it directly to the work:

> That's what we're all about in entrepreneurship — creating something from nothing, creating the results we want. That behavioural flexibility that arises out of transcending the personas is what allows you to create whatever it is you're wanting to create.

So persona work isn't self-understanding for its own sake. A persona is a standing restriction on the range of responses available, which means it caps what can be built. Every situation that needs a response outside the range gets the in-range one instead.

> [!quote] [[If you could behave any way any other human behaves, you could achieve anything any other human achieves|If you could behave any way any other human behaves, you could achieve anything any other human achieves]]
