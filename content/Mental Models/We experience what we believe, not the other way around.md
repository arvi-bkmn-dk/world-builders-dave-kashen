# We experience what we believe, not the other way around

The mechanism, stated as an information-processing problem rather than a psychological one.

Anaïs Nin, quoted: *"we don't see things as they are, we see them as we are."* → [[We don't see the world as it is, we see the world as we are]]

**Why the loop closes**, in Dave's two-children illustration — the clearest version of this in the corpus. One child's needs are reliably met and forms *people are reliable*; another's aren't and forms *people can't be counted on.*

So the belief doesn't merely filter perception, it changes the behaviour that produces the evidence. Both children collect genuine data.

And the sting:

→ Human beings would rather be coherent and right than create what they really want · [[Beliefs determine what you notice and filter out]] · [[Core Beliefs]]

Dave's question for surfacing the belief behind a recurring experience, learned from **Carl Buchheit and Michelle Masters at NLP Marin**: **"what must you believe to be having that experience?"**
