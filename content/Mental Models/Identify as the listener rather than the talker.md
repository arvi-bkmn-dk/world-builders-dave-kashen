# Identify as the listener rather than the talker

> One of the most powerful shifts you can make as a human being is to identify as the listener instead — because you get freedom and you get choice.

Two named payoffs, and both follow mechanically. As the talker, the voice's verdicts are yours, so there's nothing to do with them but obey or fight. As the listener, they're input, and input can be declined.

Dave's image for the relationship:

> I'm in a coffee shop peacefully sipping my tea, and this crazy homeless guy sits down next to me and starts muttering — oh, you suck, you're not good enough, you're not as good a coach as that other coach. Am I going to listen to him and think he's talking to me and believe what he says and engage with it? Or do I just keep sipping my tea?

The metaphor is doing real work: it grants the voice its content and removes its standing. He doesn't dispute the muttering or prove it wrong.

He also names why this is harder than it sounds. A drunk kid shouting *you suck* on the street is trivially ignorable. But when the voice sounds like you and appears to emanate from inside you, "it's not as obvious."

His estimate of the timeline is honest — years of growth for someone just starting to distinguish *that's not me, that's the voice in my head talking.* → [[The Talker and the Listener]]
