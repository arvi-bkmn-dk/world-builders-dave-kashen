# Be-Do-Have

## What It Is

The correction to the default ordering most people run their lives on. Dave names the default as **do-have-be**:

> The water we swim in, in our culture, is: do, have, be. If I do all this stuff, then I'll have these results, and then I'll be happy. And it literally doesn't work.

The inversion — **be-do-have** — puts the state first: be it, and the doing and having follow.

## The Model

**The trap.** Ask why someone needs $25 million and you eventually reach two words: *freedom* and *peace*. Both are states, and both are available now — which is precisely what the mind refuses:

> If you ask people "could you just be free and peaceful?" their brains short-circuit. The brain goes: no, no, no, I need the money first.

**The counter-evidence** is everywhere and is simply ignored: billionaires who are visibly unhappy and unfree, and people with very little money who are free and peaceful.

**The Mexican fisherman parable.** A banker meets a fisherman who fishes a few hours, cooks for people, plays guitar with friends all night. Told to scale up — fish all day, buy five boats, build an international distribution business — he asks what happens at the end. *You retire to a small beach town, fish a little for fun, feed people, play guitar on the beach.* He's already doing it. The whole arc was a detour to arrive where he started.

**The worthiness version.** Charlie Munger says you must deserve what you get; the woo version says raise your vibration. Dave squares them — and then rejects the usual reading of "become worthy":

> Most people hear that as: I have to become worthy by achieving all these things. Another way to become worthy of it is by shifting the belief that you're not worthy of it.

→ [[Everyone is inherently worthy of what they authentically want]] · [[Do-have-be is backwards and it never arrives]]

## How Dave Uses It

To explain why the "it's coming soon" feeling never resolves. The milestone at which you'll finally deserve it keeps moving, because the thing being deferred was never gated on the milestone. Jesse's account of the inversion — from eight days out of ten of *I can't be happy yet* to eight out of ten of *wait, this is what I want* — is the model working.
