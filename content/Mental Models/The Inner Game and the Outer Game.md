# The Inner Game and the Outer Game
> [!info] The frame the whole body of work is named for
> The distinction is Timothy Gallwey's, from *The Inner Game of Tennis*. The application to founders is Dave's, and it is what **The Inner Game of Entrepreneurship** means.

## What It Is

> Timothy Gallwey's *The Inner Game of Tennis* revolutionized performance coaching by revealing that athletes play **two games**: the visible **Outer Game** (technique, skill) on the court and the unseen, critical **Inner Game** within themselves.

> The same is true for entrepreneurship.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outer Game** | The visible strategies and tactics — how to create a fundraising pitch, product development frameworks, marketing tactics, scaling advice |
| **Inner Game** | The invisible foundation — your **state, perception, mindset, thought patterns, beliefs, self-image, and emotional resilience** |

And the causal claim that makes it more than a pair of categories:

> The Inner Game shapes how you see the world and yourself — and therefore how you lead, make decisions, and overcome challenges. **Your outer results are a reflection of your inner world.**

> In other words, **the Inner Game drives the success of the Outer Game.**

→ [[Your outer results are a reflection of your inner world]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**It relocates the lever without dismissing the visible game.** Dave does not say tactics don't matter — he says they are downstream. This is the same structure as [[Content and Context]]: two layers, most attention on the wrong one, and fixing the lower one makes the upper one easier. The newsletter's stated difference from every other founder newsletter follows directly: *"instead of giving you more business tactics, concepts, and strategies, we'll be going straight to the root cause."*

**Mastering the Outer Game alone can make you *appear* successful.** That word is the hinge — it is possible to win the visible game completely and have nothing. → [[Ask any founder what they want then ask why five times and it traces back to a feeling]]

**It pays twice, which is why it isn't a trade-off.** → [[You win twice]]

## Dave's Own Outer Game

He lists it in the first email precisely so the conclusion can't be dismissed as sour grapes:

- Co-founded two VC-backed startups — **Worklife** (Y Combinator W15, acquired by Cisco) and **Wellsphere** (acquired by IAC/Sequoia-backed HealthCentral)
- Started a venture fund, **Fearless Ventures**
- **SPO Partners**, a multi-billion-dollar hedge fund; investment banker at **Goldman Sachs**
- BS in Economics, Wharton; MBA, Stanford GSB

> Despite achieving all of these "outer" successes, **I still felt unfulfilled.** I realized that the fulfillment and contentment I thought I'd find after climbing to the top of the mountain **simply wasn't there.** My whole life shifted when I sold my company and started focusing on the inner game.

> About 15 years ago, I discovered that I'd been driven by a story I told myself — **that I was unworthy.**

→ [[I felt more pressure to not fail than to succeed]] · [[The exhaustion was not preventing failure, it was preventing the clarity I needed to succeed]]
