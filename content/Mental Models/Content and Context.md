# Content and Context

> [!info] Not Dave's model — attributed

## What It Is

Dave calls it *"one of the most important distinctions you can make as an entrepreneur, as a leader, frankly, as a human being."*

> If your life is a movie, **what's on the screen is the content** and **the lens through which you're viewing it is the context.**

## The Model

**Content** is what's happening. *"How much money did I raise? What do I have to do today? What's my next goal, challenge?"* In a meeting, it's the decision on the table.

**Context** is the state everyone is operating from — including you:

→ [[Above and Below the Line]]

→ [[Content does not determine your decision quality, context does]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**It makes an invisible layer visible.** Dave's description of what changes for clients:

The distinction does no work on its own — it is a pointer at something that was always there and never attended to. Which is why the whole course rests on it: [[Above and Below the Line]] is unusable until you accept that there is a context to locate yourself in.

**Content is where the ego prefers to work.** A hard problem is flattering; a bad state is not. Attending to content exclusively is therefore the default, and it feels like diligence.

## How Dave Uses It

The instruction is deliberately small — in your next meeting, attend to the context rather than only the content, and ask throughout the day which state you're in.
