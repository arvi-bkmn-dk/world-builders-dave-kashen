# Spend more of your time in your zone of genius

One of the eight replacement fuels for a founder who has stopped running on fear, and Dave frames it as a scheduling problem rather than a discovery problem.

> Identify the things you love to do and are uniquely good at. Write down tasks or projects you genuinely want to pursue, **even without external pressure.**

The qualifier is the test. Anything you would pursue unprompted is in the zone; anything requiring a push is not.

> There's not one way to do your job — find the way that works for you, and delegate as much of the other things as you possibly can.

That line is doing more work than it appears. Most founders treat the role as fixed and themselves as the variable, which guarantees time spent outside the zone. Dave inverts it.

His method is a weekly audit rather than a one-time inventory:

> After a workweek, note which moments lit you up vs. those that drained you. Gradually align your schedule so more time is spent in the "lit up" column.

And the reason it belongs in a newsletter about motivation without attachment:

> When you connect with what energizes you, you replace forced hustle with **intrinsic drive — fuel that doesn't run out.**

Which makes it a fuel-supply question, not a preference. → [[The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are]]
