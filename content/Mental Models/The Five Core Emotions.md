# The Five Core Emotions

> [!info] Not Dave's model — attributed
> Dave gives the credit himself, on air: *"this was something I learned from the conscious leadership group, to give credit where it's due, which I think they learned from Katie and Gay Hendricks."*
> → [[Conscious Leadership Group]] · [[Katie and Gay Hendricks]]

## What It Is

The top-level map of emotion in Dave's work. Everything else in the [[Emotions and Fear]] tier hangs off it — the individual emotions, their wisdom questions, and the emotions that are **not** on the list, [[Shame is the electric fence|shame]] above all.

Two costs specifically. You lose **presence**, because the feeling is arising now and refusing it sends you into your head — *"a feeling is worth a thousand thoughts. Sometimes if you're stuck in ruminating and thinking, it's really just because you're not letting yourself feel something."* And you lose **information**:

Dave says that second one is what turned him: *"I value wisdom. I value intelligence. I don't wanna miss a source of intelligence. And that kinda got me."*

## The Model

Each emotion is an energy with a question attached. The question is how you extract the wisdom. → [[Each emotion carries its own question, and that is where its wisdom is]]

| Emotion | Energy of | Wisdom question |
|---|---|---|
| **Joy** | celebration | *What wants to be celebrated?* |
| **Excitement** | creation | *What wants to be created?* |
| **Sadness** | letting go | *What wants to be let go of?* |
| **Anger** | boundaries | *What wants to be stopped or destroyed?* |
| **Fear** | the unknown / attention | *What wants to become known?* |

**Joy** reverse-engineers: *"if you want more joy, celebrate more."* Dave's complaint about founders is that they go "right from one victory to the next problem," never spending a minute on the accomplishment their former self would have thrown a party for. → [[Build your joy tolerance]] · [[All emotions run through the same pipe and joy is the matriarch]]

**Excitement** "can feel like sexual energy — conscious leadership refers to it that way," which he notes genuinely confuses people in creative firms, where the energy of something wanting to be made gets misread as being about a person.

**Sadness**: what usually wants to be let go of is "some idea we had about how life would be." His example is the *dream behind the complaint* from a relationship course — a dream the partner never agreed to, complained about for years, "because we're not willing to feel the sadness of letting go of that dream." → [[Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship]]

**Anger** is the one Dave worked on most in himself: *"my parents didn't get angry — angry was, what, Neanderthal, people who couldn't control themselves. I am cool, calm and collected. And I've been welcoming, allowing more and more anger, and I can literally sense my boundaries more."* It runs in flavours, from a simple unmet want up to *this is not OK with me, stop it.* → [[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]] · [[A leader who cannot feel anger cannot hold a boundary with their team]]

**Fear** says pay attention. On a cliff edge that's literal; elsewhere it means "there's something you're not paying attention to that wants to be paid attention to." Dave: *"I think fear is actually a doorway to a more expanded frame of reality."* → [[Fear is excitement plus resistance]] · [[What you actually fear is a feeling]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**Five, not fifty.** A short closed list is what makes the sorting error visible. You can check which of five you're refusing; you can't audit an open-ended vocabulary of moods.

**Every one of them is load-bearing.** The model has no negative half, so there is nothing to legitimately avoid — which is what makes [[The Gradation of Allowing]] applicable to all five rather than only the pleasant ones.

**Wisdom, not catharsis.** The emotion is treated as *intelligence arriving*, not pressure to discharge. That is why each row ends in a question.

## The Book Chapter

**The primary-colours framing:**

**Emotion as energy, defined by sensation rather than by name:**

**The ninety seconds, sourced.** Dr. Jill Bolte Taylor, Harvard-trained neuroscientist: *"all emotions last for less than 90 seconds if you just allow yourself to feel them. If they continue after that, it is because we have added our own story and chosen to hold on to the emotion."* → [[An unresisted emotion moves through the body in 60 to 90 seconds]]

**The cognitive-emotive loop**, defined by Jim Dethmer:

**The wisdom questions**, in their written form:

| Emotion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Joy** | What is there to **celebrate** right now? |
| **Sadness** | What is there to **let go of**? |
| **Fear** | What needs to be **paid attention to**? What wants to come into your conscious awareness? |
| **Anger** | What do you want to **stop**? What is not serving you or your people? |
| **Excitement** | What wants to be **created**? |

**Where guilt and shame sit** — the question Dave says he always gets:

→ [[Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad]] · [[Shame puts the brakes on all the other emotions]]

**Four components for actually feeling a feeling:**

1. **Notice and label** — *"the human brain doesn't deal well with things it can't classify, and naming your feelings can make them more manageable."*
2. **Locate and welcome** — find it as sensation. *"Emotions happen only in the present moment, so tuning into your body will help you move from thinking to feeling."*
3. **The ninja move** — → [[The Sedona Method]]
4. **Match and express** — *"match your inner emotional experience with an outer expression… making a sound or movement that matches your feeling."* → [[Move anger with sound and movement in a safe way]]

**And the warning against doing it instrumentally:**

→ [[You cannot welcome a feeling in order to get rid of it]]

**The argument Dave uses on people who think some feelings have no value:** *"people pay good money to see scary and sad movies. When was the last time you spent a few bucks and a few hours watching a horror movie or an epic tragedy? Clearly, experiencing fear and sadness must have some value to us."*

## Where Shame Sits

**Shame is not one of the five**, and that is the point of the distinction rather than an oversight. Where the five each carry a question and a wisdom, shame carries a verdict about the self — [[Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad]] — and its function is to stop the others:

**[[Shame puts the brakes on all the other emotions]]**

So shame nests under this model as the thing that blocks it. The shame material — [[Shame is the electric fence]], [[Shame is the lock that binds the chains of bad habits]], [[Shame says you are the danger]], [[Welcome the shame but do not believe the thoughts that come with it]] — describes the mechanism that keeps the five core emotions from moving. → [[An unresisted emotion moves through the body in 60 to 90 seconds]]
