# Communicating to Control vs Reveal

## What It Is

Two modes of entering a conversation. **Communicating to control** starts from a fixed outcome and works backwards: what do I say to get this person to think, feel or do the thing I want. **Communicating to reveal** drops the strategy and simply says what is actually happening for you — the fact, the feeling, the story, the want.

Dave names control as the default for most leaders, and the rehearsal it requires as its hidden cost.

> It's like we're playing chess in our minds.

## Why It Is Built This Way

**Control is expensive.** The bandwidth spent modelling the other person, forecasting reactions and composing the optimal sentence is bandwidth not spent on the actual problem. Revealing costs nothing to compose, because you already have the information.

**It is simpler than it sounds.** Coaching a CEO who wanted his team to work harder, Dave's invitation was to say exactly that — and to say the fear underneath it too.

> Basically, just telling the truth even when it's scary.

> literally, all you're doing is revealing the thoughts and feelings and wants that are inside of you

**It produces a better picture of reality at team scale.** People hold different pieces of the puzzle, so a culture where everyone reveals sees more of the board — which is what makes decisions easier.

→ [[The team that sees reality the clearest wins]] · [[Communicating to reveal instead of to control removes the thing there is to argue with]]

**It pairs with the grammar of Speaking Unarguably**, which supplies the form the revealing takes — facts, feelings, stories, wants.
