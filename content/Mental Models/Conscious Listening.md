# Conscious Listening

## What It Is

Dave pairs it with Speaking Unarguably: *"conscious listening and speaking unarguably are like **the one-two punch** of conscious communication."*

### Unconscious listening: the six filters

The crucial framing: this happens **before you open your mouth.** *"This is when the other person is speaking, your mouth is closed. You're still listening through this filter."*

| Filter | Listening for |
|---|---|
| **Fixing** | how to solve their problem |
| **Diagnosing** | *I wonder what's really behind all this* |
| **Defending** | how they're wrong so I can defend my position |
| **Calming** | how to settle them down — *there's something wrong with the way they're being* |
| **Debating** | the angle, the logic, how to win |
| **Correcting** | the error in what they're saying |

Dave names his own, unflatteringly: *"my wife will tell me this whole story about something really important for her, and I'll be like — **wait, I don't think that was on Tuesday, I think that was Wednesday.** My mind is just listening to correct."*

### Conscious listening: head, heart, gut

- **Head** — the content. *What did they actually say?*
- **Heart** — the feelings. *What did they say they feel, or what do you sense they're feeling?*
- **Gut** — the want. *What do they really want, that's having this be an issue for them?*

Then reflect it back: *"Here's what I heard you say. Here's what I think you're feeling. Here's what I imagine you really want. Is that right?"* — and Dave's worked example shows why it pays: the founder frustrated about a late team member turns out to be *"worried that they're disengaged and thinking they might quit."* → [[The presenting issue is almost never the real issue]]

**Signs it's working:** more colour in the face, more flow of feeling, a burst of energy, an exhale or relaxation of breath, a more natural pace.

### The hierarchy

Questions must be open, not leading — *"not *have you thought about this?* but *what have you tried so far? what's important to you about this? what's most frustrating?*"* — because the point is to **stay on their map of the world**, not go out to yours. Covey's version, which Dave cites: *seek first to understand, then to be understood.*

## Why It Is Built This Way

**The real problem is their state, not their problem.** *"Nine times out of ten, conscious listening will help them shift from a state of threat to a state of presence. And when they're in presence, they're **more able to see clearly and solve their own issue.**"* → [[Above and Below the Line]] · [[Content and Context]]

**Fixing transfers the work to you.** Dave cites the HBR article *Who's Got the Monkey?* — solve your team's problems and *"all those monkeys are gonna be on your back."* Listening instead is *"really empowering for your team, because they'll learn to solve their own issues."*

**It is not universal, and Dave is funny about it.** A tourist asking directions does not want *"I hear that you're wanting to get to Ghirardelli Square, and it sounds like you feel lost"* — *"that would be absurd."* Someone who needs the wifi password gets the wifi password; a new team member may need coaching and direction. The technique is for **interpersonal issues where there's emotion.**

Dave's evidence for that claim: *"I've had entire sixty-minute sessions where all I did was — I hear this, I imagine you're feeling this, you want that, is that right, tell me more. At the end: **oh my God Dave, thank you so much for your advice, that was perfect.** Didn't give any."*

## Also in [[GA 02 - A CEO Coach Reflects on His Performance as a Dad, Dad Pod]]

The parenting application. When Dave slips into coach mode his family pushes back (*don't go into coaching mode, just talk to me*). What almost always helps is dropping the urge to fix:

> the thing that I find that when I do almost always things improve is when I really just go into listening mode

He estimates that nine times out of ten, just reflecting *you're really frustrated that happened* lets his son shift and feel heard.

## Also in [[NL 63 - Why Presence Is the Foundation of Everything]]

The newsletter extends the filter list. Beyond the obvious ones (diagnosing and fixing, defending, correcting and debating), Dave adds four most people don't catch:

| Filter | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Self-referencing** | hearing only how their experience relates to yours |
| **Knowing** | deciding what they'll say before they've said it |
| **Avoiding conflict** | nodding along, consoling, keeping things comfortable |
| **Efforting** | trying so hard to listen that the performance becomes the filter |

> You think you're connecting. You're just filtering everything through yourself.

> You look like you're listening. You're not there at all.

> The effort itself becomes the filter.

What every filter shares, and where the fix starts: → [[The disconnection you feel from others starts as a disconnection from your own feelings]]
