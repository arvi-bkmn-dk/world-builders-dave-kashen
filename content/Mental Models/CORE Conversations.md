# CORE Conversations

## What It Is

Dave's own framework, built for the IGE community, for helping someone without advising them.

| | Instead of |
|---|---|
| **C**uriosity | being right |
| **O**penness | withholding |
| **R**esponsibility | victimhood |
| **E**mpathy | judgment |

## The Model

The format is deliberately constrained:

> Someone shares a real challenge. Then everyone takes turns following their curiosity by asking **open-ended (not yes or no) questions.** The person with the challenge? **They don't answer. They just listen.**

The no-answering rule is the design. Answering turns it into an interview and puts the person back into explaining and defending. Listening to questions they can't respond to leaves the questions working on them.

## Why It Works

Dave's case against the default:

> We think we're being helpful when we jump in with solutions, but most of the time we're just **recycling thoughts they've already had.**

> That approach assumes they need more **information.** But most of the time, what we actually need is more **awareness.**

And the cost of advising, which is the part leaders miss:

> Trying to fix reinforces the idea that there's something wrong with them or their life; that **they are a victim and need your help.**

→ [[The hero creates temporary relief but undermines learning and growth]] · [[Heroes require victims]]

> Real transformation happens from the inside-out. You can give someone the perfect advice, but if they don't see and feel it for themselves — if it doesn't click internally — nothing changes.

> Many times, we already know what we want to do deep down. But what we lack is the **clarity or courage** to act on it.

Which is why questions beat answers here: the missing thing was never information.
