# Are you just honoring your values or are you standing for them too

The question that resolved a CEO's impossible choice, and it exposes a gap most founders don't know they have.

Her VP of Sales consistently beat quota and investors loved him. His 360s said abrasive, demeaning, egotistical — "he made smart people feel dumb." People were speaking up less. Her Series A was approaching. Fire the high performer and look unstable, or keep him and watch the culture rot.

She had *Respect, Inclusivity, Team Spirit* on every wall. Dave's question:

> **Are you just honoring your values, or are you standing for them too?**

The distinction:

> Most founders think having values means living them personally. That's **honoring** your values — and it's just the starting point.

> **Standing for** your values means holding others accountable to them, **even when it costs you something.**

Her gap was exact: she was honouring respect by being respectful, and not standing for it by allowing her VP to disrespect people. Both are real, and only one is visible from the inside — which is why the culture problem read to her as a personnel problem.

Dave's test for whether a value is real:

> Values that don't imply real, scary choices aren't really values — **they're just convenient beliefs.**

A value that has never cost you anything hasn't been tested. → [[Values that do not imply real scary choices are just convenient beliefs]]

Which also makes values a boundary problem, and boundaries need anger to hold. → [[A leader who cannot feel anger cannot hold a boundary with their team]]
