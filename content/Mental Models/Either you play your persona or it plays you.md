# Either you play your persona or it plays you

Dave's framing of personas, credited to Diana Chapman at Conscious Leadership, and the reason the episode isn't about eliminating them.

> Either you play your persona or it plays you.

A persona is a survival pattern — "ways of seeing the world, ways of thinking, ways of behaving that sort of get grouped together," learned in the first eight or ten years. Dave is explicit that they're **useful**: "We're not saying it's bad to have a persona."

The variable is consciousness:

> If you're playing a part, if you're playing a role, if you're aware you're playing it, you can do it consciously and say — okay, this situation calls for my firefighter persona, 'cause this is a real crisis.

Unconscious, the same pattern runs the show. The trouble is that most people never see it as a role at all: "for a lot of people, they think of that as who they are. I am the firefighter. I'm the hustler. I'm the hero."

Jesse's newscaster is the clean illustration — a TV reporter turned stay-at-home mother who narrates her toddler in her broadcast voice. Nobody mistakes the newscaster voice for a person. Dave: "it's almost like putting on the uniform to go to work." The personas we can't see are the ones we never think of as a uniform.

Jesse's own version was deliberate and worked: made CEO at 25 with a team of ten, he asked himself "what would the CEO of a company do in this situation?" and used the answer. Dave links it to [[Be-Do-Have]] — choose who you're *being*, then see what occurs to you to do.
