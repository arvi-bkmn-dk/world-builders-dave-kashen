# The Focus-Motivation Model

## What It Is

Dave's formal model for motivating yourself and your team without pressure or exhaustion. Its premise: the reason building feels heavy is not the work, it is the *relationship* to the work. The model names one target state — **Flow** — and the two ways founders predictably fall out of it.

This is the systematised version of what he calls [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel|clean fuel]] in conversation. Flow *is* the clean fuel.

## The Model

**Step 1 — Understand what creates suffering.** Two unconscious patterns, and neither is circumstantial:
1. **Resistance to the present situation** — "this shouldn't be happening," "we should be further ahead."
2. **Attachment to a specific future** — "this must work," "I can't fail," "if this doesn't happen, something is wrong with me."

> Resistance and attachment = suffering.

→ [[Resistance and attachment are what make the founder journey feel heavy, not the circumstances]]

**Step 2 — Operate from Flow.** `Flow = Present Situation + Desired Future`, held simultaneously.
- **Accept the present situation** — not resisting, judging, sugarcoating, dramatising or blaming. "It is what is."
- **Envision the desired future** — expansively, and *felt in the body*. The test isn't whether it sounds inspiring, it's whether you feel the pull. → [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]
- Holding both produces a natural tension that organises attention, clarifies the next step and releases creativity. "It's not pressure. It's not forcing."

**Step 3 — Notice the two drifts.** Flow is unstable; the model's real utility is the diagnostics.
- **Drift into Frustration** — you stop envisioning the desired future *in the future* and start wishing you were already there. → [[Frustration is a desired future collapsed into the present]]
- **Drift into Fear** — your focus slides from the desired future to the **undesired** future. → [[Fear is the undesired future, which is the same sentence framed backwards]]

Each drift has a one-move correction: put the future back in the future; or flip the sentence to its positive form. → [[What you focus on is where you steer]]

## Dave's Fullest Statement of It

From HoE 22, where he says he is distilling this distinction **for the book he is writing**:

> The way I'm referring to it is as the **focus-motivation framework** — because what I've gotten clear on is: **what you focus on determines how you motivate yourself, and then how you motivate yourself determines how you motivate your team.**

> At the simplest level, there are three main types of motivation: **frustration, fear, or flow.**

**Flow** is generated by holding two things at once — present to reality **without resistance**, and a **genuinely desired** future. *"It has to be something you really want."* The pull is forward rather than away: *"most of frustration and fear is a **push** — a drive away from lack, a way to avoid fear. This feels more like a **pull** from the future. You're **called** to it."*

Then the **two drifts**, which is what the model is really for — both are failures of *focus*, not of willpower:

**Drift 1 — into frustration**, by **collapsing the desired future into the present.** *"Instead of 'I'm so excited to double revenue', it's 'darn it, I wish we'd already doubled revenue.'"* Dave: *"if you're honest with yourself, that's probably the most common one I see."* Founders are *"constantly like — I wish I already had an awesome head of engineering."* Jesse adds the accelerant: Twitter, someone's mega-round, someone else at $200M ARR. Dave: **"comparison is the thief of joy."**

**Drift 2 — into fear**, by focusing on the **undesired** future. The mind, *"this safety mechanism scanning the world for what could go wrong,"* drifts to the thing you don't want, and then you spill it onto the team: *"guys, if we don't hit these goals, we won't raise and we'll fail."*

> What's funny about it is **it's the literal converse.** You're saying the same thing in a **disempowering, scary** way instead of an **exciting** way. You could equally say: *if we **do** hit the goals, we're gonna raise, and we're gonna achieve our dreams.*

**The bridge.** Dave is explicit that switching fuels is not a step but a crossing: *"there **is** a bridge between the way you've been motivating yourself your whole life and some new way — and that bridge can feel like a **rickety rope bridge.** It's uncomfortable and new and scary, and I'm not totally sure I'll make it across."* → [[There is a scary neutral period between dirty fuel and authentic desire]]

## How Dave Uses It

As a live diagnostic rather than a philosophy. The question isn't "am I in flow" but "which of the two have I drifted into" — and both drifts have a specific verbal signature ("I wish we'd already…" / "if we can't…, we won't…") that a founder can be trained to catch in their own speech.
