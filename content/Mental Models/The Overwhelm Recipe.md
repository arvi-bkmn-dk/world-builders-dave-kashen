# The Overwhelm Recipe

## What It Is

Dave's method for making an overwhelmed founder the author of their overwhelm without accusing them of anything. It is [[One Hundred Percent Responsibility]] delivered as a game.

> Are you willing to take your 100% responsibility for creating the experience of overwhelm? … If I wanted to experience overwhelm the way you do, will you teach me how to do it? Maybe you're teaching a class at the business school — Overwhelm 101. Give me the lesson.

## The Model

**1. Feel the feelings first.** Dave is explicit that he doesn't lead with perspective: "so we don't try to paper over them." He'll help them name it, and the name is usually *scared* — at which point "their system almost calms down when they realise, oh, I'm just scared." → [[Stress and overwhelm are white-collar words for scared]]

**2. Ask them to teach the recipe.** Played lightly. Clients reliably produce something precise:

> Step 1: think of all the things you possibly could do. Step 2: believe you should do all of those things. Step 3: make a list of 30 hours of tasks. Step 4: believe you should get them done in 10 hours.

The recipe is the proof. Nobody could describe the procedure that well unless they were running it. → [[Ask someone to teach you how they produce their own suffering]]

**3. Point out the arithmetic.** You are never going to do more than ten hours of work in ten hours. All you can actually do is be deliberate about prioritisation — and the layer of overwhelm on top doesn't help you get any of it done.

**4. Run the present-moment test.**

> Can you bring your attention to the present moment? … Without leaving the present moment, can you find any overwhelm? … And when is it not the present moment? Never.

→ [[Overwhelm cannot be found in the present moment]]

**5. Get curious about the layers.** The more you ask *how am I continuously creating this*, the more ingredients appear — including the uncomfortable ones. Dave's chain lands on: I overcommitted because I wanted them to like me, because I wanted to look good.

## How Dave Uses It

The playfulness is load-bearing. "Teach me your recipe" invites authorship where "you're doing this to yourself" would trigger defence — and it produces evidence in the client's own words rather than the coach's.
