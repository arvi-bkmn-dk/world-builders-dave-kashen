# All humans have all the same parts

> I find it helpful to recognize that all humans have all the same parts. We tend to think of ourselves as these unique snowflakes, and in some ways we are. But anything that you recognize in yourself, you could find in me — and anything that you recognize in me, you could find in yourself.

Dave offers this as Andrew is resisting the word *weak*, and it does two jobs at once.

It **removes the stakes from any individual trait**. If weakness is universal equipment rather than a distinguishing defect, then admitting it isn't a concession about your standing. What's left to look at is only your relationship to that part, which is a far smaller and more tractable question. → [[Every story your mind tells is partially true]]

It **makes projection legible**. Since every part is in everyone, a strong reaction to someone else can't be explained by their possessing something you don't. → [[Being irritated by someone shows you a part of yourself you have not loved]]

The premise is doing quiet work throughout the episode. Andrew's discomfort with *scared* — "every time you say I'm scared, I want to punch you to show you I'm strong" — assumes fear is a category some people are in. If all humans have all the parts, the question stops being whether you are afraid and becomes what you do with it.
