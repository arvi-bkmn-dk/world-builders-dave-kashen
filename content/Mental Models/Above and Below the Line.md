# Above and Below the Line

> [!info] Not Dave's model — attributed
> The core distinction of the [[Conscious Leadership Group]] framework, which Dave names as the base of his coaching: *"a lot of my work as a coach is based on the conscious leadership framework, which distinguishes between being in a state of threat — seeing yourself as a victim and believing life happens 'to me' — and a state of presence."*

## What It Is

*"This is one of my favorite distinctions to teach."* It is also the vocabulary Dave uses most often and defines least in the newsletters.

> Most people are operating most of the time from a state of threat. And they're like **fish in water** — what the heck is water? They don't realize that there are different states that they could be operating from.

> Being below the line in a state of threat as, um, essentially victim consciousness, right? Like it's happening to me.

### Why below is the default

Not a character flaw — a hardware mismatch. *"Humans are wired for threat. Thank goodness our ancestors were wired to perceive threat; they're the ones that survived."*

> We perceive that threat to our startup success **exactly the same as a physical survival level threat.**

Dave's deflation of the threat is worth keeping: *"which if you really think about it, what would happen? You'd start a new one. You'd get an awesome job somewhere. Of course you'd lick your wounds and feel the disappointment."*

### What each state actually looks like

**Below the line** — *"we're operating from our ego. Our attention is focused on being right. Most of us have gotten so much ego validation from getting the right answer. We tend to get defensive. We wanna look good and avoid looking bad. We perceive things as wrong and bad, and then we go to blame."* And the definition of victim consciousness: *"we perceive our internal experience, our well-being, as a function of what's happening out there."*

**Above the line** — *"you can be here now in a **non-triggered, non-reactive** state. We're open, we're curious, we tend to be relatively peaceful, and it's a lot easier to just creatively respond to whatever is actually occurring."*

His driving metaphor makes the third option visible: you drive unconsciously, thinking about your to-do list, *"until and unless someone swerves into your lane, and now you're fully present — but you're present in this triggered reactive fight or flight state."* Presence without the trigger is the middle path.

> So much of the time when we're below the line, **we're not actually reacting to what's occurring in the moment. We're reacting to some fear we have of something that might happen in the future.**

And the behavioural tell:

> **Above the line, we respond.** Below the line, we're **reactive** instead of responsive.

## The Model

| | Below the Line | Above the Line |
|---|---|---|
| State | threat | presence |
| Stance toward life | it happens **to me** | I am the **creator** of it |
| Behaviour | reactive, defensive | responsive |
| Action logic | "take the actions your mind says you *should* take to fix the problems in your world" | "accept the world as it is, envision the future you want, take actions you feel a **whole body yes** to" |
| Resolution | drama | [[Win for All]] |

> From Above the Line, we accept our world as it is, envision the future we want, and take actions that, when we consider taking, we feel a whole body yes.

> From Below the Line, we take the actions that our mind says we should take to fix the problems in our world and our lives.

The primary move between them is responsibility:

> **Taking responsibility is the primary shift move from below the line to above the line.** Am I willing to take responsibility for my VP of engineering missing deadlines?

→ [[One Hundred Percent Responsibility]] · [[Responsibility is not self-blame]]

## The Book Chapter — Victim and Creator

Chapter 1 of the manuscript is Dave's fullest written treatment, and it names the two states **Victim** and **Creator**. He prefaces it with *"all models are false. Good ones are useful"* → [[All models are false, good ones are useful]], and with the claim that this is *"one of the world's most useful **maps** to creating the reality and results you truly want."*

His definition of the underlying thing:

> Your **inner state** (or "state of consciousness" or "way of being") describes the **quality of your awareness and attention** in any given moment… **it is who you are being while you are doing whatever you're doing.**

### The two characteristic lists

**Below the line — Victim**
- *"Life happens **to** me."* My well-being is a function of other people, conditions and circumstances
- Perceiving a threat to your sense of **security, control and/or approval** → [[Security, Control and Approval]]
- A sense of anxiety — thinking **there's something wrong here**
- Defensive and reactive · a **commitment to being right**
- Focusing on what you **wish had happened** and what you **don't want** to happen
- Blaming and criticising · **scarcity** of time, money

**Above the line — Creator**
- *"Life happens **by** me."* I create my experience and well-being
- A general sense of **peace and presence** · open and curious
- A commitment to **learning and growth**
- Focusing on **what's happening now**, and what you **want** to happen
- Taking responsibility · **sufficiency** of time, money

### The four contrasts

**Present vs avoiding the present.** Threat pulls you out of the moment to feel safe, and *"your mind extrapolates the current perceived threat into some future scenario you don't want,"* so *"you're solving a future problem that hasn't actually happened."* His counter is the screenwriter. → You are the screenwriter as well as the main character

**Resistance vs acceptance.** *"What you resist, persists. The energy we expend resisting something actually **holds it stuck in place.**"* And the half people miss: → Clinging is another form of resistance

**Being right vs being curious.** *"Your ego equates your self-worth with being right,"* which is why feedback gets dismissed *"either by finding fault with the content or with the giver"* — and why decision meetings stall, with everyone arguing their position instead of listening. A client's phrase for the fix: **debating to win versus debating to learn.** Above the line, *"genuine curiosity is like the wonder of a child."*

**Blame vs responsibility.** → I make my own lunch · [[To be responsible is to be response-able]]

### Location, location, location

> **Diana Chapman** is fond of saying that what matters most in being a conscious leader is **"location, location, location."** The goal is **not to always be above the line** (and make yourself wrong when you're not), but rather to **become aware of where you are** in any given moment.

The cost is only in not knowing: *"you might explode in anger at the slightest annoyance or say something you later regret. Because you're not fully present to see the whole picture… you're likely to make **sub-optimal decisions.**"*

## How to Locate Yourself

**The question.** *"The key is first learning to just ask the question: where am I? In this moment, where am I?"* Dave has clients ask it three to five times a day, prompted by intentional-notification apps (MindJogger on iPhone, Randomly Remind Me on Android) — *"just to build the muscle of awareness."*

**The Self-Location handout.** A set of statements, behaviours and beliefs typical of each state. His instruction is to personalise it: *"when I go below the line, what do I tend to say — either out loud to other people or just to myself? What do I tend to do? What do I tend to believe?"* Clients print it and keep it next to the laptop.

**The River of Life.**

> Imagine you're floating down the river of life and you trust the river. You can kinda kick back, relax. You're open. You might be curious — where is it taking me?

**The body.** *"When I'm below the line, I can feel it in my body. I feel that constriction."* → [[The body signals that you are at an edge before the mind does]]

**Resistance and attachment.** *"When I'm below the line, I'm either **resisting** something about reality — I wish it wasn't this way, I wish they hadn't said that — or I'm **attached** to some outcome, like I think I need to be successful in order to be worthy."*

And the one-line test:

→ [[Apathy is not caring, non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome]] · [[Flow is being present to current reality and imagining your desired future]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**The trap is in the naming, and Dave says so out loud.** *"Part of the issue is, if you think about the model — above the line and below the line — well, above sure sounds like the good one."* Which produces the exact failure the model is meant to prevent:

His correction is a question, not a standard: *"Can I accept myself for being below the line? I'm human, I'm wired for threat."* Self-judgment for being below the line **is** below the line. → [[NL 25 - The Paradox of Self Improvement]]

**Noticing beats shifting**, and this is the part that makes the model practical rather than aspirational:

> Even if you don't shift, just knowing you're below the line, you're likely to create less problems, less drama — because when we're below the line, we're reactive instead of responsive.

**It is practised in motion.** Dave's framing is that meditation is the gym and the startup is the sport — *"can you, in that moment, notice if I'm above or below the line, shift, while you're in real time doing the things you're doing? It's like a living meditation."*

## How Dave Uses It

The distinction sits underneath a lot of the graph without being named in it:

- **Accountability** — *"most companies do accountability from below the line, based on fear and threat and blame. But there is an alternative way to do accountability from above the line."* → [[Separate the accounting from the response]]
- **Goals** — teams "set goals from above the line with inspiration and creativity, but then relate to missing them from below the line, with blame and shame." → [[A goal is a rubber band and the tension is the point]]
- **The Drama Triangle** — Victim, Villain and Hero are the below-the-line roles. → [[Fight is villain, flight is hero, freeze is victim]] · [[NL 21 - Victim, Villain, Hero, Navigating The Drama Triangle]]
- **The body knows first** — *"when I'm below the line, I can feel it in my body."* → [[The body signals that you are at an edge before the mind does]]
