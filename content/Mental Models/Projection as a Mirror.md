# Projection as a Mirror

## What It Is

Dave's most precise account of projection, and its value over the usual telling is that it names **two distinct mechanisms** rather than one.

> When we have an aversion to something someone else is doing, it's a reflection of a part of ourselves that we haven't fully accepted or owned or loved.

## The Model

**Mechanism 1 — you do it and judge yourself for it.**

> We know we have a tendency to be self-centred, and then someone else is being self-centred and we're like: you're so selfish. Just because we don't love the selfish part of ourselves.

**Mechanism 2 — you forbid yourself from doing it.**

> We don't *let* ourselves be selfish, because we have a story that good people are generous and not selfish. And then someone else lets themselves be selfish and we're like: what? Why do they get to and we don't?

The second is the one people miss, and it feels completely different from the inside — closer to indignation than recognition. The charge isn't guilt; it's the resentment of a rule you're keeping and they aren't.

> It's usually one of those two things that's really going on.

**The diagnostic:** → [[Your judgments of others mark the places you have not grown]]

**The scope:**

> The whole world is a mirror for you. You just get to see all of your stuff, all your patterns, all the parts of yourself you haven't fully loved, just by looking out at the world.

## How Dave Uses It

Family at the holidays is the ideal test rig, because you revert. Jesse: "you become a little kid again — oh wait, I actually haven't grown past this issue with my brother. It still shows up only around him, but I've been hiding it." The pattern was never resolved, only unexercised.

Dave adds Michael Singer's *samskaras* (*The Untethered Soul*): the material was already there festering, and contact brings it up **to be released**. Taken that way, a difficult family visit is an opportunity to heal old wounds rather than an ordeal to survive.

Jesse notes the inverse reading is equally available: things that would have bothered him five years ago don't register at all now. The mirror measures growth in both directions.
