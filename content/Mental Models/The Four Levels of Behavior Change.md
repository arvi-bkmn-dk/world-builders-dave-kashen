# The Four Levels of Behavior Change

## What It Is

Dave's diagnostic for why change doesn't stick — built on a failure of his own. At **Meeting Hero**, the product his co-founder Val and he built to improve meetings, early traction looked great. Users loved it, added agendas, tracked action items.

> For about two weeks. **Then they'd stop.**

> **Most people are trying to change behaviors at the wrong layer entirely for their specific problem.**

## The Model

Four layers, surface to foundation. The diagnosis runs **top down**; the fix runs at whichever layer is actually broken.

### Level 1 — Friction

> Sometimes the issue is purely **mechanical.** You want to do the thing, but you're just not doing it.

BJ Fogg's formula, **B = MAT**: behavior happens with sufficient **M**otivation, **A**bility and a **T**rigger.

> Here's the insight most people miss: they try to increase **motivation.** According to Fogg, that's actually the **least effective** thing you can do. What's most effective is **shrinking the ability required.**

> Don't commit to running three days a week. Just commit to **putting out your running clothes before bed.** You're almost tricking your brain because **our brains resist change. The more change, the more resistance.**

> But once you've got your running clothes on, you think *"I might as well step outside."* Then you're outside in running clothes thinking *"I might as well go run."*

### Level 2 — Interpretation

> Sometimes the issue is **how you're interpreting the behavior itself.**

> For years, meditation felt like **eating my broccoli.** Taking my medicine. I had to push myself to do it. At some point, I realized how enjoyable it was. And it shifted from eating my broccoli to **eating my chocolate.** Same activity. **Completely different relationship.**

The second half of this level is what you make it mean when you miss:

> If you make not doing it mean **failure**, there's a part of your brain that says *"don't do that again."* Your system wants to protect you from that feeling of failure.

Joe Hudson's reframe: instead of *"I failed,"* say **"that experiment didn't work."** Try a different time, a partner, a walk instead of the gym. → [[Experiments Instead of Failure]] · [[Run experiments so that the experiment fails instead of you]] · [[Joe Hudson]]

### Level 3 — Identity

> Sometimes the issue is **who you believe you are.**

Self-concept (*"I'm not a gym person"*) and self-judgment (*"I'm not enough"*). → [[When being meets doing, being wins]]

### Level 4 — Authentic Wants

The foundation. → [[Authentic wants arise from wholeness, compensatory behaviors arise from lack]]

## The Book Chapter — behaviour design in full

**The premise:** *"to bring your behavior into alignment with your intentions and values, you need two things: to be **clear about what you intend and value**, and to be **present (non-reactive) enough to consciously choose** your behavior."* And the obstacle — *"we are creatures of habit, and repetitive thoughts and behaviors form **well-worn paths** in our brains and nervous systems."*

**Keep, Start, Stop** is the rubric Dave's clients use to audit behaviour, with catalogued lists of each. The **stop** list — over-working, needing to be right, micro-managing, under-managing, chronic lateness, losing my temper, procrastination, avoiding conflict, poor time allocation — is written in the founder's own voice, as the rationalisation rather than the label. The **start** list maps to the 15 Commitments: authentic listening, giving praise and celebrating wins, giving unarguable feedback, setting clear expectations, creating clear agreements, honoring your word, creating accountability, self-care, putting first things first.

Two scripts worth keeping. **Praise:** *"don't just give general praise like **great job!** — recognise the specific behaviors, how you feel about it, and the impact it had."* And **feedback**, formulaic: **When you… (facts) · I feel/felt… (feelings) · My story is… (stories).** → Speaking Unarguably · [[Fact, feeling, story, want are the four buckets of speaking unarguably]]

**BJ Fogg's model, in its written form** — here as **B = MAP**, Motivation, Ability, Prompt, with Dave crediting Fogg as *"founder and director of the Stanford Behavior Design Lab."*

→ Start with the minimum effective dose

**Prompts and anchors**, the third term of MAP, which the podcast versions skip:

Prompts his clients use: calendar invites to themselves, post-its on the computer or bathroom mirror, a phone home screen that states the intended behaviour, a notebook, or an app like MindJogger. Daria's, from the chapter's opening story, was writing **"Listen, dammit!"** at the top of every page in her notebook.

**Anchors** tie the new behaviour to an existing one — *"every morning, right after you brush your teeth, you'll sit on that chair in your bedroom and close your eyes."*

**Two types of change.** Incremental, and **transformational** — *"sudden and thorough, closer to a metamorphosis… moments of profound insight or paradigm shifts when you suddenly re-contextualise yourself or your life."* Dave's own: seeing he didn't have to believe the unworthiness story, after which *"I quit my job, enrolled in coach training and started coaching **in the span of weeks.**"*

## How to Use It

Dave runs the diagnostic **downward**, which is the opposite of how people behave:

> **Level 4** — *Do I actually want this?* If no, explore your authentic wants first. **What would you choose if you felt completely whole and nothing was missing?**
> **Level 3** — *Do I want it but "I'm not that kind of person"?* Look at your beliefs about yourself.
> **Level 2** — *Do I want it but it feels like broccoli?* Reframe it, or run experiments.
> **Level 1** — *Do I want it, it feels good, but I'm not doing it?* **Shrink the ability required. Make it stupidly easy.**

> **Most people start with Level 1 tactics when the real breakdown is at Level 3.** Or they try to muscle through with willpower when they need Level 2 reframing.

> Once all these levels are aligned, behavior can basically become **effortless action.**

## Why It Is Built This Way

**Each level can silently cancel the one above it.** A perfect trigger cannot survive *"I'm not that kind of person,"* and no identity work survives a want that was never yours. That is why the order of *diagnosis* is inverted from the order of *effort*.

**Meeting Hero is the cautionary case.** *"We optimized Level 1. But people needed to believe 'I'm the kind of person who runs effective meetings.' They needed to want better meetings, not feel obligated to have them."* And the general lesson: *"most successful companies take something people are **already doing** and make it easier. We were trying to help people do things they **wanted to do but weren't doing.** There's a huge difference."*

**The 30-year smoker is the proof of the other direction** — no tactics, just Level 3 and 4 aligning at once. → [[When being meets doing, being wins]]
