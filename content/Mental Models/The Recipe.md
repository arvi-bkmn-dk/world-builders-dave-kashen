# The Recipe

## What It Is

An exercise from the **Conscious Leadership Group**, also called *teach the class*, that Dave says "changed my life and the lives of many of my clients."

It starts from one question:

> **If you had to teach a class on how to create the exact problem you're experiencing — what would the lessons be?**

The premise underneath it:

> Most of us are **experts in the thing we claim we don't want.** We say we want clarity, but we unknowingly teach a masterclass in confusion. We say we want intimacy, but we've perfected the art of avoidance. We say we want fairness, but we habitually defer hard conversations.

## Why It Works

> The beauty of the recipe is that it **cuts through self-deception.** It bypasses the ego and reveals the unconscious choreography behind our most frustrating patterns.

Asking *how did I create this?* invites defence. Asking *how would you teach it?* invites expertise — and people are fluent in their own patterns the moment the question stops sounding like an accusation.

## The Worked Example

A founder trying to buy out a cofounder who'd stopped contributing years earlier but wanted a hefty payout. *"I did all the work. He wants more than is fair. It's infuriating."* Asked to teach the class:

> * Start a business with someone without clarifying expectations
> * Avoid tough conversations about equity when the dynamic changes
> * Resent him silently while doing all the work myself
> * Make up stories about how he *should* understand and what's "fair"
> * Let the pressure build until it becomes unsustainable
> * Then try to negotiate from a place of superiority and blame

> Once he saw it, something cracked open. He wasn't a victim of his cofounder. **He was the author of his own experience.**

## How to Run It

1. Pick a repeating issue — conflict with a team member, constant overwhelm, avoidance of sales
2. **Title your course.** *"How to be consistently overwhelmed while resenting everyone who tries to help 101."*
3. Write the syllabus: What actions do I take or avoid? What emotions do I suppress? What do I have to be right about? What agreements do I avoid making or enforcing? Who do I blame? How do I numb myself from discomfort?
4. **Read it out loud.** "It's sobering. And liberating."
