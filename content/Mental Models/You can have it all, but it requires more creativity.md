# You can have it all, but it requires more creativity

Andrew opens by needling Dave for skiing four or five days a week while supposedly building something significant. Dave names the assumption inside the joke:

> You're giving voice to a trade-off mentality that most people believe — that I can't have it all, I have to sacrifice the things I love in order to achieve my vision.

His counter is not that trade-offs don't exist, but that most are accepted before being tested:

> I really believe you can have it all, and I think it requires more creativity. That's something I stand for in the world, and I aspire to model.

The modelling matters more than the claim. A coach who taught this while visibly sacrificing everything he loved would be making an argument; one who skis five days a week — an hour and a half at a time, not all day, which is the creative part — is supplying an existence proof.

The sacrifice belief also connects downward to the rest of the episode. If the good things must be deferred until the vision is achieved, that is [[Do-have-be is backwards and it never arrives|do-have-be]] operating at the level of a daily schedule, and it produces the guilt Andrew carries on his run.

Dave's more radical extension later: what if your whole life felt like play, and the things that happen to create value for others were as enjoyable as the things that don't? For him, a rich coaching conversation and a day of powder skiing are roughly equivalent experiences.

> [!quote] [[Most trade-offs are false|Most trade-offs are false]]
