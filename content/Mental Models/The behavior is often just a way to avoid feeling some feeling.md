# The behavior is often just a way to avoid feeling some feeling

The mechanism under every habit you can't shift, and the reason willpower is the wrong tool.

> Often the behavior is really just a way to avoid feeling some feeling. And if you allow yourself to feel that feeling, you no longer need to do that behavior.

Dave's demonstration is a bowl of ice cream, the night before recording. Instead of eating it he asked what he was avoiding, put it back in the freezer, and sat with what came up:

> I kind of had a temper tantrum on purpose. I was like — yeah, there's this part of me that's just like, but I fucking want what I want. And I don't wanna not get what I want. I let myself have a temper tantrum.

What surfaced was older than the ice cream:

> I realised there's something in me, I think from childhood relationship with my dad, where I wasn't getting what I want, and I didn't fully allow myself to feel that temper tantrum of how much I wanted it and how sad and frustrated I was not getting it.

Then the outcome, which is the point:

> If I could feel my frustration and sadness around that, then the compulsion — the need to eat the ice cream — kind of dissipated. And then it's more of just a free choice of: do I want it or not?

He then had half a scoop and stopped, "whereas in the past I might not have stopped there."

Note the order Dave gives it in: **feel the shame first**, which unblocks what's beneath it, then feel that. → [[Shame puts the brakes on all the other emotions]]
