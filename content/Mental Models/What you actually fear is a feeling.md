# What you actually fear is a feeling

Dave runs the same chain in both directions, want and fear.

Wanting:

> Why do you want to be wildly successful? Oh, because I'll have all this money and people will respect me. Well, what will having that do for you? Well, then I'll feel worthy. I'll feel powerful. I'll feel whole.

> It's always a **feeling** that is ultimately the thing you want.

Fearing:

> What do you fear if your startup fails? Well, then I'll let all these people down and lose their money. And what's the fear behind that? **I'll feel shame. I'll feel embarrassed. I'll feel sad. I'll feel scared. I'll feel trapped.**

> What we actually fear is a feeling.

And then the step that relocates the whole thing:

> Since feelings are just part of you, it's really just **a part of yourself** that you're scared of.

Which is what makes the outcome much less load-bearing than it looks. The event is not the threat; the feeling you expect the event to produce is. → [[Your results are limited by which feelings you are unwilling to feel]] · [[Fear lives at the edge between the familiar and unfamiliar parts of yourself]]
