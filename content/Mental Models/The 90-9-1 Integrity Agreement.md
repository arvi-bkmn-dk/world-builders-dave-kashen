# The 90-9-1 Integrity Agreement

> [!info] Attribution

## What It Is

A team-wide agreement about what integrity means **before** any specific commitment is made — which is what lets accountability stop feeling like blame.

So founders drop it entirely — *"I just wanna trust people and assume good intent"* — and that is the mistake:

> Most companies do accountability **from below the line**, based on fear and threat and blame. But there is an alternative way to do accountability **from above the line.**

→ [[Above and Below the Line]] · [[Separate the accounting from the response]]

## The Model

**90%** — you do what you say you will do.

**9%** — as soon as you know you're not going to, you renegotiate:

**1%** — you clean up the broken agreement:

The companion piece is agreement clarity — **Who? Will do what? By when?** → [[A clear agreement is who will do what by when]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**The 9% is the load-bearing number.** It gives renegotiation a legitimate, pre-agreed slot, so telling someone you'll miss a date becomes *meeting* the standard rather than confessing to failing it. Without it, the only options are silence or apology, and people choose silence.

**Nobody is asked for 100%.** A standard that cannot be met gets quietly abandoned; one with slack built in stays real. And the numbers are steep enough that the slack isn't cover — 90% is still the floor.

**It is agreed in advance, by everyone.** Which is why the later accounting can be neutral: the person is being held to a standard they signed, not to your disappointment. → [[Integrity is not just keeping your word, it is honoring your word]]

## As Dave Restated It Later

In NL 49 the same three lines arrive with the reason attached:

> Integrity isn't just keeping your word — it's **honoring** your word. Life happens. Priorities shift. You can't always keep every commitment. But you can honor your word by renegotiating when needed or cleaning up when you break agreements.

> When your team has this agreement, accountability becomes an **act of service.** You're supporting each other to stay in integrity, which is energizing.
