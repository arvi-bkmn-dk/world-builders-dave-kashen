# Resistance to the emotion is what blocks flow, not the emotion

Jesse describes a founder's natural flow state getting blocked when a bad number lands and anger arrives. Dave interrupts him mid-sentence to correct the wording, and the correction is the teaching:

> Just to be precise, because I don't want people to think that anger is blocking their flow — then they'll resist the anger. **Resistance to anger is blocking your flow. Anger is the flow.**

If the emotion is the blockage, the sensible response is to remove it, which means resisting it — which installs the actual blockage. The framing generates the problem it describes.

Jesse's plumbing image: anger comes up, something wants to be stopped, and then resistance to it arrives — "now there's something stuck in your pipe." And what typically follows is trying to figure a way around rather than going through. → [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]

Dave's larger frame for what emotions are *for*:

> I think of emotions as love through a prism. It's showing you how you're out of presence, so you can come back to presence. If I'm holding on to something from the past, sadness arises to say: hey, let go of that thing so you can be with reality as it is.

Each emotion as a specific instruction back toward the present — which is why blocking one costs you the instruction as well as the feeling. → [[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]]

He also names the loop: you can't be present while unwilling to feel what's arising, and going out of presence is often *how* you avoid it. "There's anger, I don't want to feel that, I'll go into my head and think about things."
