# Love in leadership can be fierce

The objection that keeps love out of business is that it sounds soft — that choosing love means choosing to be nurturing, accommodating and slow, which no founder can afford. Dave's correction is that this confuses love with one of its registers.

> A lot of people hear the word love and they think, oh, I have to be soft and nurturing. Jesse saying "I don't appreciate being spoken to that way" — that's him coming from love. He's standing for his truth.

His other example is blunter: a child about to step into the road in front of a car. You do not say *oh honey, maybe you want to*. You say **stop**. Nobody would describe that as unloving, and nobody would describe it as soft.

This is the counter to Andrew's framing of the whole conversation — that love in business is a luxury for people who can already afford a calmer, less successful life. Fierceness is not the exception to operating from love; it's part of its range. What changes when the fuel changes isn't the intensity, it's what the intensity is in service of.

Dave also notes, in real time, his own twinge of reticence at saying the word at all in a startup context — which is itself the evidence that the softness association is doing the work.
