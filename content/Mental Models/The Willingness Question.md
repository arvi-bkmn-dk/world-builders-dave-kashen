# The Willingness Question

## What It Is

Dave's signature coaching move, noticeable enough that Andrew calls it out mid-episode: *"Why do you ask 'are you willing' questions? I never have a conversation with anyone other than Jesse or Dave who says 'are you willing' before."*

**"Are you willing to…"** — take responsibility, learn from this, let go of being right, find a win for all.

## The Model

**It replaces "should."** Jesse's gloss, from the inside of having learned it: "*You should learn from this, Andrew*" is very different from "*are you willing to learn from this*". The first pushes; the second leaves the choice with the person. Related: he tried the softer *I would invite you to consider…* on his wife, who called it "worst invitation ever" — the form matters less than whether the choice is genuinely being handed over. → Speaking Unarguably

**It separates capacity from willingness.** "You *could* learn from this" gets an easy yes and changes nothing. Willingness is the variable that actually gates the work.

**It is present tense.** This is Dave's own emphasis, and the reason he hyphenates the word:

> If you think of the word will-ing — are you willing to use your human will to have this experience, right now?

Not "I'd be willing to lose ten pounds someday." Right now, in this moment. → [[Willing means literally right now, not someday in the future]]

**It presumes the creator stance.** Asking *are you willing to take responsibility* is, in Dave's words, a double whammy: are you willing to see how you are the creator of this particular experience? → The Responsibility Roadmap

## How Dave Uses It

As a genuine question with a real "no" available — which is what makes the episode instructive. Andrew declines: *"No, I'm not willing to let that go. Bigger has got to be better."* Dave doesn't push. He asks what Andrew is scared might happen if he let go of the belief, which moves the work to the fear underneath rather than arguing the belief itself.
