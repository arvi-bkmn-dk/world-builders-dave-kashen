# The Paradox of Attachment

## What It Is

Their fear is specific: *"if I let go of attachment, will I still want it? Will I become apathetic? Will I still be motivated?"*

→ [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]] · [[Apathy is not caring, non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome]]

### The harder second question

This is the sharper move, because it targets the **judgment** rather than the preference. People are *"pretty righteous that startup successful is the good path,"* and they've attached a whole bundle to it — financial security, approval, looking good, future opportunities, identity, worthiness. → [[There is a direct inverse correlation between love and judgment]]

### The dating metaphor

And the cost that matters even if the outcome is unaffected: *"the feeling of that desperate clinginess creates a lot more stress, anxiety and fear."*

## Why It Is Built This Way

**Dave's evidence is two personal failures, not theory.**

**The near-failure of his second startup.** Down to the wire, facing having to tell investors — including friends — that he'd lost their money:

They eventually sold. His reading: *"the universe was generous — I got the lesson and I didn't actually have to fail."*

**Losing 70% of his net worth** in crypto and tech stocks inside six months:

→ [[What do you love about yourself that has nothing to do with your money or success]]

**Hence the model's real claim:**

He extends it past the founder: what might failure free your **team** to go do, and what might your **investors** learn that they otherwise wouldn't?

→ [[Failing might be the thing that liberates you from your ego]] · [[Either the startup fails and you learn you did not die, or it succeeds and you learn it was not enough]]
