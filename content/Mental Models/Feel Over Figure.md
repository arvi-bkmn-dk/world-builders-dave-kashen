# Feel Over Figure

## What It Is

A decision-making model Dave uses and credits to coach Joe Hudson. Its premise is that the hardness of a decision is not a property of the decision.

> We make tons of choices throughout the day that we don't even think about. The times when these choices occur to us as *hard decisions* is when we're scared.

→ [[A choice only feels like a hard decision when you are scared of it]]

## The Model

**1. Notice what's happening when a choice feels heavy.** Not that the options are finely balanced — that something about it is frightening. The deliberation is a symptom.

**2. Recognise figuring as avoidance.** The mind escapes into analysis because analysis is more comfortable than the feeling:

> Our minds try to figure out the right answer. It's like they somehow believe they can accurately predict the future — and if I could just figure out the right answer, then I can control the future. All of it is just a strategy to avoid feeling the fear.

→ [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]

**3. Feel instead.** Welcome the fear in the body. Literally feel it. → [[An unresisted emotion moves through the body in 60 to 90 seconds]]

**4. Then see what occurs to you.** Not *decide* — see what arises once the fear isn't driving. "Actually, I want to run" or "actually, I want to focus on the business."

**5. Follow the want rather than the mind's right answer.** Which Dave describes as a genuinely different way of operating.

## How Dave Uses It

Andrew supplies the live case: he went for a run, but carried guilt the whole way, and asks whether he should be running at all while the business needs him. Dave declines to answer the question as posed — the question itself is the avoidance — and redirects to the fear underneath not working for an hour.

Jesse's report on what changes is honest about the scale of it:

> 80% of the time you may do the same thing you were going to do anyway. But you just don't carry the baggage along with you as you do it. And that makes all the difference.

The gain is not better decisions; it's the same decisions without the drag. → [[Following your wants usually leads to the same actions without the baggage]]
