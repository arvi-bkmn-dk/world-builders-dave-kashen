# Win for All

## What It Is

Dave's resolution move when two people are stuck in a dynamic, offered as a willingness question: *would you both be willing to find a win for all solution?* → [[The Willingness Question]]

Its definition is stricter than compromise:

> You each make the other one winning as important as you winning.

Not meeting in the middle, and not trading concessions — adopting the other person's outcome as a genuine objective alongside your own.

## The Model

The sequence Andrew and Jesse run live at the end of episode 2:

1. **Name the story, unarguably.** Andrew: "The story that I'm making up in my head is that my approach to business is driving you crazy." → Speaking Unarguably
2. **Reflect it back and check.** Jesse summarises what he heard, then asks *is that right? Is there more?* → [[Summarising what someone said and guessing their feelings and wants makes them feel heard]]
3. **Guess the feelings and wants underneath.** Dave prompts Jesse to go further: "I'm guessing you're feeling a little scared and a little frustrated, and what you really want is to know and feel like you're having success."
4. **Test the story against reality.** Dave asks Jesse directly: *is it true? Is he driving you crazy?* — "No, you're not driving me crazy at all." The story was false, and inaccessible without asking.
5. **Ask for the win-for-all.** Then: what would winning look like for each of you?
6. **Land on a concrete agreement.** Theirs: keep 20–30 minutes of sacred space for this kind of conversation, protected from operational work.

## How Dave Uses It

The demonstration value is that the presenting problem dissolved on contact. Andrew had been carrying a story about Jesse's frustration; Jesse's actual experience was about disliking remote collaboration on Zoom. Neither would have surfaced without the structure.

Andrew's assessment is honest about what it did and didn't do: *"It hasn't resolved anything, but it's given me more clarity — and that's on the path."*
