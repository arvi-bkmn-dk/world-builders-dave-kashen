# Being irritated by someone shows you a part of yourself you have not loved

Dave's use of projection, and the striking part is the emotional register he reports:

> If I get pissed off by something that someone does nowadays, I get *excited* — because I'm like, oh, this is showing me a part of myself that I haven't loved. My primary commitment is to unconditional love for myself and everyone else.

Irritation reframed as information, and specifically as a locator. The charge marks the part, and the part is yours.

The reasoning rests on [[All humans have all the same parts]] — if everything is in everyone, then a strong reaction can't be explained by the other person having a quality you lack. What's distinctive is your relationship to that quality in yourself.

He applies it to Andrew directly and without accusation: the fact that Andrew doesn't want to be perceived as weak "says something about your relationship with this weak part that we all have." Not that Andrew is weak — that the weak part is unloved rather than absent.

The stated commitment underneath it matters. Projection is useful *because* the goal is unconditional love toward all of it, which is the same destination as [[Acceptance is the more precise word for bringing in light]]. Without that, spotting your projections is just a more sophisticated way to be irritated.
