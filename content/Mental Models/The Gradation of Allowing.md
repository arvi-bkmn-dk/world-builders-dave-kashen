# The Gradation of Allowing

## What It Is

A ladder of increasingly intimate relationships with a feeling. Dave lays it out as a sequence of questions, each a step further than the last — which makes it usable, because you can locate yourself on it rather than failing at the top rung.

## The Model

1. **Acknowledge** — could you admit that you feel it at all?
2. **Allow** — could you let it be there?
3. **Accept** — could you stop needing it to be otherwise?
4. **Welcome** — could you actively invite it in?
5. **Love** — could you love it?
6. **Look forward to it** — could you look forward to feeling shame, or grief, or sadness?

Dave knows how the top of the ladder sounds: "it may sound crazy to some people, and I would challenge and invite you to try it and see what happens."

**Where he actually lives on it**, and this is the evidence it isn't theoretical:

> I've grown to love heartbreak. Let your heart break open — it expands your capacity to love. So I almost look forward to heartbreak. I look forward to anger, because I'm like: oh, there's something I haven't been paying attention to about my boundaries. And then I'll go have this conversation and assert some boundaries or some want.

Anger looked forward to *as information about a boundary* — which is [[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]] taken to its conclusion.

**The mechanics of climbing it** are practical: get curious about location. Where in my body is that showing up? Oh, a tightening sensation in my belly. Okay — if I welcome that, is it a problem?

## How Dave Uses It

With an explicit refusal of authority, which is unusual for him and worth noting:

> You don't need to believe anything we're saying. I would strongly encourage you to run experiments for yourself and see. If some feeling's coming up, what happens if I stop resisting it and welcome it? From my experience, it literally changes the experience of it. It doesn't feel bad any more.
