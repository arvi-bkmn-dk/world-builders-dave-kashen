# Security, Control and Approval

## What It Is

The three needs Conscious Leadership treats as fundamental, and Commitment 11 of the *15 Commitments*: **be the source of your own security, control and approval.**

| Need | The felt version |
|---|---|
| **Security** | I am safe |
| **Control** | I have all the control I need |
| **Approval** | I am worthy |

Dave's crucial qualifier: each is **a sense, not a thing.** "It's not like I have safety, here's my little safety and I put it on my desk."

## The Model

**The default is external sourcing.** [[Most founders source their sense of security, control and approval externally]] — from money, conditions, status, what people think. Jesse's description: so universal "it feels like drinking water or breathing air."

**It cannot work, structurally.** [[The whole game is rigged because when never arrives]] — you reach the number, don't feel it, conclude you had the wrong number, and set a new one. The goalposts move because the premise is wrong, not the target.

**Each resolves inward.**

- **Security** → [[In the present moment can you find any lack of safety]]. A lack of safety requires an imagined future. Also [[What they really mean by security is lifestyle maintenance]] — naming what is actually at risk shrinks it.
- **Control** → [[Sourcing control externally is thinking you need to control what you cannot]]. If you only need to control what is actually yours, there is no unmet need. The tool is [[The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder|sorting the files]].
- **Approval** → the worthiness question, which Dave answers as a premise: we are inherently worthy. → [[The burden of proof is an illusion]] · [[You are already whole, perfect and complete]]

**The practice.** [[Welcome the one who wants security, then relax as the one beyond wanting it]] — the Sedona Method sequence, run through all three.

## The Nuance That Matters

Sourcing internally is **not** wanting nothing. [[If you want a Range Rover I want you to have a Range Rover]] draws the line: wanting is healthy, *needing it in order to be okay* is attachment.

The test between them: [[An impulse feels lighter and a compulsion has constriction attached to it]], and Joe Hudson's version — [[Can you enjoy the wanting]].

The diagnostic for when you have slipped: [[Anytime you are experiencing something as serious you are making it mean something it does not mean]].

## How Dave Uses It

He applies it to himself on air — imagining losing his clients and his house, and finding not fear but **shame**: "I'd have to tell everybody I can't afford my house. Head held low." What's actually at risk is identity, "a made-up construct that's distinct from who you actually are."
