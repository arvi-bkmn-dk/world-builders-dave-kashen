# Creative Tension

## What It Is

Dave's model for motivation without fear, and by his own account one of the most used things he teaches:

The starting observation is that **tension is what motivates**, because tension seeks resolution — *"imagine you were to coil up a towel. There's just a natural wanting for that tension to unfurl."* The question is only which tension you generate.

Most founders generate it from **lack** ("it's not good enough — I'm not good enough, where we're at is not good enough") or from **fear** ("if we don't grow revenue, we'll go out of business"). Both work. Both are [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel|dirty fuel]]:

Creative tension is the clean-fuel alternative.

## The Model

Hold **two** things at the same time:

1. **Present to current reality** — accepting it exactly as it is, not wishing it different.
2. **Your desired future state** — imagined clearly, as future. → [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]

> When you're present to current reality and at the same time present to your desired future state, your mind creates this **cognitive dissonance** between the two, and that's what generates creativity. It's like your subconscious mind trying to close the gap.

> it's called creative tension because it, it results in creativity and inspired action.

→ [[Flow is being present to current reality and imagining your desired future]]

### The two traps

Each trap is one of the two halves collapsing.

**Trap 1 — collapsing the desired future into the present.** You stop imagining getting there and start wishing you were already there, then burn energy on the gap. *"I wish we were further along. I wish we'd already raised more money. All of that is just draining your energy."* → [[Frustration is a desired future collapsed into the present]]

**Trap 2 — focusing on the undesired future.** *"We use this amazing tool of the imagination to imagine the future we don't want to have happen and scare ourselves."* In NL 52: **"like driving a motorcycle looking at the wall instead of the road."**

There is a third, which Dave calls a derivation of the second: **extrapolating a setback.** A churned customer or a VC no gets projected forward into the future instead of being registered as a setback and released.

### The rubber band

The metaphor Dave uses for calibrating the distance between the two poles. It is the sizing rule for the model, not a separate idea:

Both failure modes produce the same nothing. → [[A goal is a rubber band and the tension is the point]] · [[Goals are a means, not the ends]]

## The Book Chapter — and the model's origin

Chapter 9 of the manuscript is the fullest written treatment, and it supplies the **attribution** the spoken versions omit:

Senge, quoted:

**The all-hands worked example**, which calibrates the rubber band with real numbers. Company at **$10M**, up from $8M, against a stated target of **$12M**:

- Announce **$18M** with **no mention** of the miss → *"will people believe you? They likely won't, because they won't see you as grounded in current reality. **The band is unanchored.**"*
- Acknowledge the shortfall, take responsibility, declare **$11M** → *"too close to your current reality to create enough tension."*
- Stretch to **$50M** → *"the band will snap under the impossible strain. Again, no creative tension."*

**Used one-on-one**, with the script for a rep who's missed quota twice: *"I know you missed quota the last two quarters, and I imagine that must be frustrating. You're still learning our product and sales process **(current reality).** I can see you becoming one of our best salespeople a year or two from now… perhaps stepping up into a management role **(desired future).**"* With the condition attached: *"**if** the team member in fact desires that future."*

### Preference versus attachment

The distinction that keeps the model from producing dirty fuel by another route:

The soccer version: *"the game is more fun if you play full-out and do your best to win, but if you start to think that winning is **critical**, you'll create unnecessary anxiety… ironically, you may **worsen your performance.**"*

→ [[The Paradox of Attachment]] · [[You can play to win without being attached to winning]] · Clinging is another form of resistance

**Why fear-motivation backfires**, from the chapter's opening: Martin puts the company on notice at an all-hands — miss again and people go. Effort surges, then *"the frenzy of activity soon led to a bottleneck at the manager level. Everyone was deep in **CYA mode**… **no one wanted to risk being wrong.** People blamed each other and weren't sharing information, and the departments were becoming increasingly **siloed.**"*

## Why It Is Built This Way

**Acceptance is a component of motivation, not the opposite of it.** The model's whole trick is that accepting reality exactly as it is *increases* drive, because the dissonance needs a true reading of where you actually are. "If you resist reality, reality always wins."

**It is a fractal.** Dave's word. The same structure runs on yourself, on one team member ("here's where you are, here's where I see you in a year — don't make them wrong"), and on the company. And the order matters: *"if you're motivating yourself from fear and lack, you can bet you're motivating your team from fear and lack."*

**It replaces rather than suppresses.** Dirty fuel isn't argued out of you; a working alternative is installed, which is why the model comes with a morning visualisation practice rather than a prohibition.

**It answers the objection conscious leadership provokes.** Dave names it directly: *"if I accept reality as it is, what's gonna motivate me to do anything? Because all we know is lack and fear based tension."* Creative tension is the answer — and he concedes the shape of it: *"I acknowledge it is a bit of a paradox. How can you at the same time accept reality as it is and want some different future? But as we evolve as humans, as leaders, we can hold that paradox."* For yourself that paradox has a name, the **Being-Becoming Paradox**: *"you can both accept yourself exactly as you are, that's being, and at the same time imagine an even better version of who you want to become."*

**Leaders fail it in three distinguishable ways.** Ignoring current reality — *"guys, next year's our year, we're gonna take the hill"* — which *"seems delusional. They lose people."* Staying only in current reality, which is *"just like a bummer fest."* Or over-stretching until the band snaps.

## Also in [[MB 05 - Founders, It Doesn't Have to Be This Hard]]

The full written treatment Jesse recommends in HoE 08 adds four things.

**The precise formula.** Each half comes with its condition:

> Current Reality (without Resistance) + Desired Future (without Attachment).

**A leadership fractal.** The same structure works for a team all-hands, a one-on-one with an underperforming salesperson, and a conversation with yourself.

> I think of this Creative Tension model as a core leadership fractal, in that it can be used in a wide variety of situations and at different scales.

**The mechanism.** Cognitive dissonance between what you see and what you envision, plus the reticular activating system tuning attention to the desired future.

**The calibration.** Choose the desired future that makes you feel most alive, not daunted. → [[Scenario planning is chosen once, rumination replays the undesired future all day]]
