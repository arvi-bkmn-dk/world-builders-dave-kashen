# The Storyteller Mind

## What It Is

The model for the narrating voice in a founder's head — what it is, why it lies in a particular direction, and how to stop being run by it. Dave's core move is to deny it the status of *self*: it is an ancient survival mechanism interpreting neutral facts through fear and conditioning, not the voice of truth.

> When you think you're the storyteller, you get trapped in the stories.

## The Model

**Step 1 — Understand what a story actually is.** A story is *meaning your mind adds to facts*, and the two get fused so fast you never see the join.

| Fact | Story |
|---|---|
| A team member missed a deadline. | "They don't care." |
| An investor hasn't replied. | "They're not interested." |
| You wake up feeling tired. | "I'm losing my edge." |

You don't notice the interpretation — you feel its emotional consequence (pressure, resentment, worry, shame) and lead from that state. → [[A story is the meaning your mind adds to neutral facts]]

Dave adds the mechanism that explains the *bias*: good stories need tension, conflict and drama, so a mind competing for your attention will supply problems. → [[Your mind tells stories with problems in them because problems hold attention]]

**Step 2 — Recognise the storyteller.** Map the story onto one of seven recurring characters. → [[The Seven Storyteller Archetypes]]

**Step 3 — Use the Story Release Technique.** → [[The Story Release Technique]]

## How Dave Uses It

He frames this explicitly as a *leadership* skill rather than personal development, because the founder's stories propagate: assume the worst and the team becomes fearful; catastrophise and the team becomes reactive; judge yourself harshly and the team feels never enough. → [[Your stories set the emotional climate of your team]]
