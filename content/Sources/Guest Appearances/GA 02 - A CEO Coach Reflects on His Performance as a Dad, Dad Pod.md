# A CEO Coach Reflects on His Performance as a Dad — Dad Pod

## Summary

Dave on [[Dad Pod]], a fatherhood podcast hosted by Mike and Luke from Madison. They start with how Dave was fathered: parents divorced at five, a loving but emotionally distant father, and a story of unworthiness that drove 25 years of proving himself. He took up coaching, went through the Landmark Forum, and left his startup to coach right as his first son was born. He believes part of the courage came from wanting to model an authentic life. He parents "mostly by contrast", and names the price: he's slow to set limits because he fears disconnection.

The practical core is conscious leadership applied to fathering. He asks whether he is responding to his child or to his fear of who the child might become. He teaches the line to his sons, who now catch him below it. He treats presence as the greatest gift, and tells how his wife's "nagging" turned out to be a call to presence. Stories include the "Daddy are you under the line" spill, the Easter egg meltdown, and taking the boys' phones away.

He brings in [[Clayton Christensen]]'s marginal-decision argument and "life is a series of extenuating circumstances". From the parenting coach he and his wife hired: co-parent polarity, connect before you direct, and the septic tank. He applies creative tension and visualization to his family, and closes with three pieces of advice: focus on your own presence, get your own issues with your parents handled (Jung), and self-compassion, admitting he has spanked one of his kids.

## Key Teachings

- [[It feels safer for a child to believe something is wrong with them than with their parent]]
- [[Parenting by contrast to your own childhood creates its own blind spot]]
- [[What you want to model for your kids motivates more than who you want to be for yourself]]
- [[Ask whether you are responding to your child or to your fear of who they might become]]
- [[Your kids are your greatest teachers because they give you the most chances to practise presence]]
- [[The people asking for your presence are calling you home]]
- [[Most parenting drama traces back to a lack of presence]]
- [[The marginal hour of work always looks like the better choice, which is how you lose what matters more]]
- [[End a visualization with or something better for the highest good of all concerned]]
- [[Co-parents polarize, so the one who empathizes and the one who sets limits push each other further apart]]
- [[Kids make absurd requests to find a limit so they can empty their septic tank]]
- [[Trust your knowing about what is best for your child over the noise]]
- [[You will not do this perfectly, so be compassionate with yourself]]
