# HoE 16 — Security, Control, Approval and the Sedona Method

## Summary

The episode opens on the podcast itself as a worked example: both of them keep making time for it without attachment to outcome, and Jesse names that as "the essence of the heart of entrepreneurship." Dave catches the risk in saying so out loud — "I don't wanna slip into the trap of pretending not to be attached so that we can achieve an outcome" — which is the episode's nuance arriving before the topic does.

The subject is **Commitment 11** of the 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership: be the source of your own security, control and approval. Dave's qualifier matters — each is *a sense*, not a thing. "It's not like I have safety, here's my little safety and I put it on my desk."

The default is to source all three externally, and Jesse's reaction to first hearing it is the useful part: *what are you talking about, of course.* It is so universal "it feels like drinking water or breathing air." Dave refuses the moral framing — "there's nothing wrong with it, other than the fact that it doesn't work" — and argues it structurally: **when never arrives.** You reach the number, don't feel it, and revise the number rather than the premise. The goalposts move because the model is wrong.

The strongest material is the deflation of each need in turn. Security, examined, turns out to mean **lifestyle maintenance** — and underneath that, image and shame. Dave demonstrates on himself: imagining losing his clients and his house, what he finds is not fear but the picture of telling everyone, head held low. Control resolves via Diana Chapman's framing — the need is misaimed rather than excessive, and if you only need to control what is actually yours, there is no unmet need. Approval runs into the burden of proof from the previous episode.

The guard against misreading is careful and repeated. Sourcing internally is not wanting nothing: *if you want a Range Rover, I want you to have a Range Rover* — the problem is needing it to be okay. The tests are somatic (impulse feels lighter, compulsion has constriction) and Joe Hudson's: **can you enjoy the wanting?** Dave applies both to his own want to reach more people and watches it slide between the two. He also notes that activists and non-profit people can be the most below the line, because the premise *the world is not the way it should be* is by definition not presence.

It closes with Dave running the **Sedona Method** live — find the one who wants security, welcome that part, then just for now relax as the one beyond wanting it. Then control. Then approval.
