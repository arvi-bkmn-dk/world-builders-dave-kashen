# HoE 20 — Ego

## Summary

Jesse picks ego, noting the word is a misnomer in everyday use — *"oh, he's arrogant, cocky"* — and that people say *"I don't have an ego at all."*

**The definition.** Ego is the **sense of separate self** — *"the thought about who you are is itself just a thought."* Whatever follows *I am* is the ego. Dave connects it to the colloquial meaning: identifying as a limited self carries an inherent sense that **something is incomplete**, so **insecurity and arrogance turn out to be two sides of the same coin.** He cites the Diamond Approach's **theory of holes** — disconnection from the essential self is experienced as a void, which we misread as something missing out in the world.

**Why it causes trouble.** The mind is a **storyteller**, and good stories need conflict — so with the lions and dragons gone, *"the storyteller is bored"* and keeps weaving problems. Hence a massive negativity bias and relentless self-reference. **If you think you're the storyteller, you get trapped in the stories.** Dave is explicit this isn't a defect: *"the heart pumps blood and the mind pumps thoughts."*

**The formula for suffering:** life on one hand, your mind's idea of how life should be on the other. Peak moments — a birth, a sunset, an exit — are simply the rare minute when the two **match**.

**Two further traps.** Identity built from past evidence means *"your future becomes limited by your past."* And identity as the **source of value** turns any threat into an existential one.

**The report from the other side.** Several clients a year clear $10M+, and within a week the problems reconstitute. One, after a nine-figure sale: *"I'm more stressed about money today than I was before I started the company."* Hence: **no matter what external conditions you create, there you are** — and the question of whether winning your game will give you what you think.

**The spiritual version of the same trap.** The ego wants awakening, but *"the limited perspective can't have the expanded perspective"* — blinders on a horse. Ram Dass: *enlightenment is the ego's ultimate disappointment.* Rupert Spira's resolution: not an achievement, a **recognition**, and **joy is the experience of recognizing your true self.**

Closes on access points: the **I am** that persists from age five, body awareness, [[The Inner Inner Circle]], and meditation reframed as **practice at not reacting to thoughts.**
