# HoE 19 — Anger

## Summary

Jesse picks the topic cold and Dave says up front it is *"super personal for me."*

**The business framing first.** Via Radical Candor's quadrants, Dave redirects from the asshole-CEO archetype to the one nobody guards against: **ruinous empathy** — caring about people and failing to challenge them, which he thinks is now more common than obnoxious aggression, and which he traces directly to discomfort with anger.

**The personal history.** Neither parent expressed anger; a stepdad did, and badly, so young Dave filed anger as *"uncivilized, Neanderthal, lower class."* His parents then divorced with no visible tension beforehand — *"a lot of parents think I need to protect my kids from anger, but it was shocking,"* and he suspects the absence of conflict was itself the problem. Then the story of how he knew his wife was the one: she was furious about a broken promise in the morning, and by evening — *"I was mad this morning. I told you. I'm no longer mad."*

**What anger is.** The energy of **boundaries**, firing when something you want is blocked or something you value is violated — distinguished from sadness, which is the energy of letting go.

**The critical distinction:** feeling anger versus **anger *at***, which comes from blame and sits below the line. The healthy form is revealed — *"I notice I feel angry when you…"* — not aimed. *"You're not making me angry."*

**Where it goes when refused.** Outward as reactivity and eventually violence, or **inward as depression** — *"a lot of depression is actually anger repressed, and therefore at self."*

**The practice.** Welcome it, locate it, then move it with sound and movement: a wiffle ball bat, Diana Chapman's anger stations, a qigong chop, Jesse's car. Fake it if it's buried — at Hoffman, Dave went from faking to accessing in two minutes.

**Boundaries.** They **require nothing of the other person** — a request needs compliance, a boundary is something you do — and they exist so you can **keep your heart open** rather than stay in the room with it closed.

**Why it matters commercially.** No anger means no compass: *"if you're not clear, go get angry."* Founders who can't feel it have no read on what they want, and the company pays a **clarity tax** with decisions and culture in limbo.

Four steps to close: identify, welcome, express, then set a boundary or communicate a want.
