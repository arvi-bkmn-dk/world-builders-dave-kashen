# HoE 11 — Personas and the Fear Triangle

## Summary

Jesse opens by naming the assumption the whole podcast exists to challenge — that building a startup means eating glass and getting punched in the face — then introduces personas, a tool he has been using heavily. Dave credits it to Conscious Leadership and, behind them, Katie and Gay Hendricks: personas are groupings of patterns learned in the first eight or ten years as ways of getting needs met. They are useful. The variable is whether you know you're wearing one, which Diana Chapman compresses into the line the episode keeps returning to — **either you play your persona or it plays you**.

The mechanism that makes them sticky is perceptual. We don't see the world as it is, we see it as we are, so a firefighter filters for fires, attracts people who see fires, and then genuinely complains about all the fires. The persona validates itself and the complaint is part of the loop.

Most of the episode is the **fear triangle** — victim, villain/bully, hero/savior. Dave's strongest material is on the hero, because it's the one founders defend: rescuing produces temporary relief, denies everyone the learning, and quietly assigns the rest of the team the victim role, which they then feel. Heroes require victims, victims feel diminished rather than grateful, heroes resent not being appreciated, and the cycle runs again. Jesse plays it forward through a missed sales quota in a way both parties would recognise as fair.

Then two turns inward. First, the triangle is not three people — it's the voice in your head rotating through all three, which is also the engine of most self-improvement (villain criticises, victim complains, hero buys the 30-day plan). Second, Internal Family Systems: parts, exiles, protectors, and the NLP claim that every part has an intended positive outcome, so conflicting parts are a negotiation rather than a defect. It closes on behavioural flexibility as the prize — if you could behave any way any other human behaves, you could achieve anything any other human achieves, and the only thing in the way is *that's not who I am*.
