# HoE 17 — Feeling Stuck

## Summary

The episode on the call founders make in private and never in public: *"Hey Dave, I'm stuck. I don't know where to take the company."*

Dave's opening reframe is that the call is **good news** — *"those conversations usually excite me, because this client has reached their growing edge."* If they were inside their comfort zone they wouldn't feel stuck. And treating stuckness as a problem is self-fulfilling: *"now you're stuck around being stuck."*

He then separates several distinct things that all present as stuck.

**The should.** The commonest pattern is *"I don't know how to do something, and I think I should know how to do it"* — absurd for founders doing things for the first time, but installed by a lifetime of accolades for right answers. The move is to be with *I don't know*, which "invites a state of curiosity and wonder, from which ideas emerge."

**Avoidance dressed as time management.** Founders present a calendar problem; what's happening is that they do what's comfortable and avoid what they fear. Jesse diagnoses himself live with it.

**Wanting clarity by force.** The sand-and-water bottle: agitating it is what keeps it murky. Plus the mechanism — *"insight is caused by two weak signals connecting,"* so noise in the mind prevents the connection.

**The wrong question.** The right answer is unknowable — *"life is not a math puzzle"* — so Dave substitutes *what do I really want*, on the grounds that **authentic wants are the right answer for you.** Rick Elias to Jesse: *"you can't build a business you don't want to."*

**Certainty itself.** Given a choice between better prediction and greater capacity to be with uncertainty, Dave takes the second — LTCM were the best predictors alive until they weren't.

The back half covers the survivorship error in persistence advice (*"the ones that persisted were the ones that actually loved the thing"*), the atrophied muscle of wanting and a coach's instruction to throw a five-minute temper tantrum, anger as the sense organ for wants, the presumption of trade-off and how to dissolve it by separating wants from strategies, and finally the magical thinking that feeling a fear will cause it — closing on an experiment.

Jesse opens with Brent Beshore's line that we each have **10 problems and 10,000 blessings**, and Dave adds his friend's grandmother: *"I've had so many problems in my life and none of them ever happened."*
