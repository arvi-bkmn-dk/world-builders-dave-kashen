# HoE 09 — Emotions and the Wisdom in Them

## Summary

Andrew is away again, and Jesse withholds the topic until recording so the conversation stays unrehearsed. He opens on the received wisdom he used to hold himself — that business demands you be unemotional, stoic, rational — and notes how it doubles as a vehicle for sexism. Dave admits the same inheritance from a different angle: happiness was the good one, sadness and anger and fear the bad ones, and anger in particular was what uncivilised people expressed. Jesse's inversion is neat — anger was the *only* emotion he could access until seven or eight years ago.

The episode's spine is that emotions are energy in motion, felt physically, and that each carries a specific wisdom: anger is boundary energy, sadness the energy of letting go, fear the energy of the unknown, excitement the same unknown without resistance, joy the energy of celebration. Dave issues an on-air correction to his own teaching — having called fear dirty fuel for years, he had apologised to his community the day before, because the problem was never fear but *resistance* to fear.

The business translation is the strongest material here. Leaders who cannot feel anger cannot hold boundaries, and present instead with a dull frustration about why people won't do what they want. Leaders who cannot feel sadness won't fire anyone or kill a project. Jesse offers a costly worked example: unfelt sadness about a valuation below the number in his head led him to decline a sale and construct an elaborate alternative deal around that number — and the enterprise value didn't move for five years.

It closes on clarity. Founders are rarely short of capability, only of clarity, and thinking harder for it is like shaking a bottle of sandy water. Dave invites listeners to drop attention below the neck, Jesse recalls visualising an elevator from head to heart, and they tee up resistance as the next episode — with full credit to Joe Hudson for the distinction that a feeling *with* resistance is a fundamentally different experience from the same feeling without it.

- [[A leader who cannot feel anger cannot hold a boundary with their team]]
- [[Fear is excitement plus resistance]]
- [[Each emotion carries its own question, and that is where its wisdom is]]
- [[Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad]]
- [[Saying I am angry turns a passing feeling into an identity]]
- [[Personality is a defence structure built from the feelings you would not feel]]
- [[More thinking makes clarity worse, like shaking sand in a bottle]]
- [[Holding an emotion in costs more energy than feeling it]]
- [[The presenting issue is almost never the real issue]]
