# HoE 01 — Clean Fuel vs Dirty Fuel

## Summary

The first episode of The Heart of Entrepreneurship sets out the premise the whole show rests on: that a founder can be ambitious without running on fear, and that the state a leader operates from determines the results they get. Dave frames the startup as "one of the best personal and spiritual growth dojos on the planet" — and Jesse supplies the counter-image that the episode keeps returning to, the **whipping room**, the way most founders actually use it. Andrew plays the skeptic throughout, pushing the question that makes the episode work: is love in business just a comfortable story for people who are already rich?

The answer that emerges is the **clean fuel / dirty fuel** distinction. Most founders motivate themselves and their teams by manufacturing tension out of lack and fear, because they believe the drive would vanish without it. Jesse tells the story of testing that belief directly — Dave gave him permission to do nothing, and by day five or six the creative energy came back on its own. The alternative isn't less tension, it's *creative* tension: being present to current reality and to a desired future state at the same time, without making the present wrong.

From there the conversation moves through **unarguable language** (revealing facts, stories, feelings and wants rather than stating your thoughts as truth), the reframing of **anger as boundary energy** and a form of love rather than its opposite, and the paradox of attachment — the less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create, and you can't fake it because the body knows. It closes on Dave's correction of Jesse's "bring in more light": the word he'd use is *acceptance*, because light-versus-dark quietly recreates the idea that some feelings are good and others bad.

## Notable Quotes

> My view is that the startup is one of the best personal and spiritual growth dojos on the planet. And that's not the experience or the way most entrepreneurs are experiencing it.

> Most leaders, most entrepreneurs motivate themselves from fear and lack. You know, what I've been calling dirty fuel.

> Most people communicate to control. They're trying to control other people, the outcome, what feelings people feel or don't feel. What Jesse is describing is communicating to reveal.

> Anger is just boundary energy. And I think it's one of the most misunderstood of the emotions.

> The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create. And the problem is you can't fake it. Because believe me, I've tried.

> The state you're operating from is ultimately what determines your results.

> I might translate the word light to acceptance. Like radical self-acceptance. The risk of light is some people start to think, oh, so joy is good and sadness is bad — those are not dark, and it's actually the acceptance of all the parts and all the feelings.
