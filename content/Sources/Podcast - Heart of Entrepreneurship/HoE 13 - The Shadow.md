# HoE 13 — The Shadow

## Summary

**Andrew Warner is back** after three episodes away, and the episode is better for it — he plays the sceptic properly and the argument has to earn him. He opens asking for a personal example, spends twenty minutes calling it "wussy stuff that is going to make me weaker," and ends up asking for the diagnostic question himself.

The shadow is what doesn't see the light: the parts denied, repressed, negated. Dave's plainest formulation is that **all the things you think you're not are shadow** — *I am kind, I'm not mean; I'm not a liar; I'm not a cheater* — and the test is judgment, in yourself or in others. Jesse's shadow is the victim, which he has been unpacking for three years; Dave's is the jerk, and separately, the loser.

Jesse's reset at minute five is what makes the rest land. *What do you think you get when you get all the money and success?* Pushed twice, everyone arrives at peace, freedom, wholeness — which means money was a strategy for the goal rather than the goal, and an unreliable one. That opens the case for shadow work as the more direct route.

Dave's material here is unusually self-exposing. He does the loser shadow in public — *I lose all the time, I lose money, I make bad bets, I don't go for clients I want because I don't want the rejection* — and names its cost: less risk, holding back, programs not attempted. The resolution is not disproving it but refusing the polarity: both *loser* and *winner* are partially true, and needing only one to be true is what generates the twisting.

Andrew's best challenge comes late: isn't entrepreneurship exactly *not* accepting reality? Dave separates bending the future from denying the present, Jesse renames it a **possibility distortion field**, and the loop closes back on the shadow — what stops founders declaring big futures is the fear of being seen as a fraud.
