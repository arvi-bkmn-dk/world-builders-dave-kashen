# HoE 10 — Resistance

## Summary

The direct sequel to [[HoE 09 - Emotions and the Wisdom in Them]], and the payoff of it. Where episode 9 established that emotions carry wisdom, this one takes up the thing that blocks it, and Dave's central borrowed claim reframes everything before it: an emotion *plus resistance* is a fundamentally different experience from the same emotion alone. Not milder — different in kind. Which means most people have never met their own sadness or fear, only the resisted composite, and have been drawing conclusions from it. The loop is self-sealing: because we resist them they feel painful, because they feel painful we resist them.

Jesse opens on his 18-month-old daughter, who cries for 25 seconds on being handed over and is then entirely fine — the uncontested case of energy arriving, moving and completing. Between her age and eight, both of them agree, we learn which feelings earn positive attention and which earn negative, and the narrowing begins. Neither blames parents; Dave's own household simply had no anger in it, so he never learned it existed as an option.

Because resistance becomes invisible once practised, much of the episode is diagnostics. What annoys you when others feel it. What you judge, in others or yourself. Where it sits in the body — neck and shoulders for anger, stomach for fear, chest and behind the eyes for sadness, the jaw as the last line of defence. Dave runs the experiment live with Jesse, having him first exaggerate the resistance and then welcome the sensation, so the variable becomes visible within a minute.

The rest is what to do. Healthy expression as the third option between reacting and holding it in — the whiffle ball bat, screaming in the car, shaking like a zebra after the lion. The caveat that you cannot welcome a feeling in order to get rid of it, which Dave admits to attempting for years with his own sadness. And the workplace case: the drama leaders fear is produced by the resistance, not the emotions, so resolving conflict without allowing feelings is "kind of an insane act."
