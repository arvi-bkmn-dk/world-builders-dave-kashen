# HoE 23 — Acceptance

## Summary

Jesse picks acceptance and tells the story against himself. Early in their work, at $10M revenue aiming at $100M, Dave asked whether he could accept being at $10M. *"I was like — no way. It's **unacceptable**… I'm looking for a new coach."*

**Why the resistance is rational.** Most high achievers spent twenty years being told, and telling themselves, that things aren't good enough **yet** — with real mile markers rewarding it. Dave reaches for Alan Watts's *Life is Not a Journey*: kindergarten, then first grade, then college, then the job, *"and then eventually you're just old, like — I guess this is it,"* having missed it. **You don't dance to arrive at a spot in the room.** So a coach asking you to accept your company is threatening the whole motivational system.

**The cost of not accepting.** Resistance and attachment as the two sources of suffering; dirty fuel with its toxic residue; stress-driven health conditions. And the seven-year arithmetic: years of reactive survival mode, difficulty being present with family, then an exit that produces mostly **relief** — *"a week, if you're lucky"* — before the same mental patterns reattach to something new. Jesse: *"there are parts of the Ampush journey that are complete blurs to me. I genuinely don't remember them."*

**The core claim:** *"the biggest myth is that your external conditions determine your internal conditions."* Dave concedes 20–30% and then makes the ROI argument. He also owns that part of his own mind still believes the myth.

**The definition.** Acceptance is letting go of wishing anything about your current experience was different. **Not resignation** — and the paradox: **accepting things as they are maximizes the probability of them changing.** Held with a desired future, that's the Being-Becoming paradox and creative tension. Two ordinary models: the missed flight and the roadblock.

**Love.** Since love is unconditional by nature, removing a condition **is** experiencing love — hence a **direct inverse correlation between love and judgment**, and the reframe that *to love your life is simply to stop judging it.*

**The practice.** Quiet the mind, accept what's here, then notice whether what arises is a **compulsion** (relieve this feeling) or an **impulse** (I'd love this). Compulsion is addiction's structure — filling a need with something that can't fill it.

Closes on the reassurance that this rarely changes what you do: Jesse's wife helps people either way. **Same activity, different source.**
