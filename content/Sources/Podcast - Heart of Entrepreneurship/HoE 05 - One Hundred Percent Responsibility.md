# HoE 05 — One Hundred Percent Responsibility

## Summary

A year-end episode that opens on family at the holidays and ends on a reflection practice, with the first commitment of conscious leadership in between.

The family section produces the episode's sharpest small idea: your judgments of others mark the places you haven't grown, which makes relatives uniquely useful — you revert to the person you used to be and discover which patterns are merely hidden rather than resolved. Dave adds Michael Singer's *samskaras*: the material was already festering, and contact brings it up to be released. He then lays out projection's two mechanisms, which is more precise than the usual telling.

The middle is **100% responsibility**, read verbatim from the 15 Commitments along with its below-the-line counterpart — *I commit to be a victim, villain or hero, and to take more or less than 100% responsibility.* Dave heads off the magical-thinking objection (you can't control the weather; you can control your experience of it) and insists on the distinction that makes it workable: responsibility is not blame, and *my fault, buck stops here* is usually a way of ending the conversation before the actual inquiry begins.

The live coaching is on Andrew's overwhelm, and it's the best sequence in the batch. Dave asks him to teach a class in how to experience overwhelm — clients reliably produce a perfect recipe — then runs the present-moment test: *without leaving the present moment, can you find any overwhelm?* No. Overwhelm requires an imagined future. It closes on Jesse designing a Wednesday work week by following a want rather than figuring out the right answer, Andrew admitting he sandbags his own wants, and six year-end reflection questions.
