# HoE 02 — Facts, Stories and the Willingness Question

## Summary

Where episode 1 laid out the fuel distinction, episode 2 gets operational. It opens with the first exercise Dave ever gave Jesse — a worksheet with two columns, **facts** on one side and **stories** on the other — applied to Jesse's entire company history. The revelation wasn't that his interpretations were wrong; it was how many of them he had been carrying as facts. "It was a mistake not to sell the business in 2015" had felt like a recording of what happened.

The middle of the episode turns on Andrew, and it is the most useful coaching demonstration in his work so far because Andrew refuses most of it. Dave asks whether he's *willing* — a word Jesse stops to unpack as present-tense human will rather than future intent — and Andrew says no, plainly: bigger has got to be better. What follows is Dave working the belief rather than the behaviour, surfacing that "bigger is better" is a treadmill that by construction never arrives, and separating **preference** from **righteousness** in wanting a large business.

Jesse supplies the other spine of the episode: Dave's answer to what he'd do with thirty minutes to change someone's life. Find their one to three **core beliefs** — a belief being nothing more than a deeply held story — and loosen the grip by showing the opposite is equally true. His own was *money equals love*, and the negotiation down to "incrementally more money would not make me more loved or more safe" was, in his words, life-changing. The episode ends with Andrew and Jesse running an unscripted conscious-communication repair on their own working relationship, live.

## Notable Quotes

> A fact is something that a video camera could record. It's what actually literally occurred. And the story is just the narrative, the interpretation that our mind does around the facts.

> Humans are like storytelling machines — we just do it so automatically that we don't realize it.

> You can't get there until you're here.

> There's life, and then there's our mind's idea of how life should be. And at any given moment, we're sort of choosing between the two.

> Would you rather be right, or have your life work?

> Every story that our mind tells us is partially true.

> Are you willing to use your human will to have this experience, right now?

> Every person has 1 to 3 core beliefs. And a belief is really just a fancy way of saying deeply held story.

> If I get pissed off by something that someone does nowadays, I get excited — because I'm like, oh, this is showing me a part of myself that I haven't loved.

> Bigger is better is a treadmill that's always speeding up. When is it big enough? Never.

> There's nothing wrong with wanting to build a big business. It's: are you doing it from this place of there's something wrong with the way it is now — in which case you're not present.

> What feels more inspiring in your soul? "It should be bigger" — or "how amazing would it be if it were bigger?"
