# HoE 21 — Loneliness and the Drama Triangle

## Summary

Built around Brian Chesky's viral post — $100B company, $10B net worth, and the morning after the IPO: *"my life was exactly the same. I'm alone. I put on my sweatpants, I go onto my iMac, and I have 10 to 12 hours of Zoom meetings."* The line Dave asks Jesse to read twice: *"so much of my life was about being successful, probably if I were to dig deep, because I thought it would make people love me."*

Dave's first response is sadness and empathy rather than analysis. Then the mechanism: **disconnection from yourself becomes disconnection from other people** — and the loop is closed, because achieving in order to be loved makes it impossible to know whether you are loved for yourself. He gives his own version: **"I was so busy trying to become worthy of my family that I almost lost them"** — never as one decision, always as the marginal hour that had a tangible outcome attached.

Jesse adds how selfish entrepreneurship felt, and Dave the irony that *"it doesn't actually give you the experience you want, so it's a bad version of selfishness where you end up unhappy."*

On the loneliness of scale: nobody to complain to, motives you can't trust, and the mask — *"I can't tell my investors that I'm scared."* Which leads to Joe Hudson's reframe of imposter syndrome: **being an imposter isn't what creates it; thinking you shouldn't be one is.** Dave: *"I've yet to meet a human being who doesn't experience self-doubt,"* and he catches his own version mid-session, wondering what Joe or Diana would say.

Then two corrections. **Heart open doesn't mean soft** — you can be fierce, hold the line, and create accountability with your heart open; and the fear-versus-love shorthand wrongly implies you shouldn't feel fear, *"which ironically is a way of closing your heart."* The reliable instrument is the body: **constriction or expansion**, because identical words land differently depending on the state behind them.

And the sentence Dave repeats twice: **the way you block yourself from feeling how you want to feel now is by believing you need the conditions to feel it.**

Closes on the **Drama Triangle** (Karpman): not casting other people in roles, but a storyteller rotating through victim, villain and hero — with the observation that **your least favourite role is your blind spot.** Dave's was heroing his own clients for years.
