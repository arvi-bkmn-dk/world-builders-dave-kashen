# HoE 12 — The Seven Core Distinctions

> [!warning] This episode teaches someone else's framework
> The seven distinctions are **[[Joe Hudson]]**'s, from the [[Art of Accomplishment]] Masterclass, which Dave finished the day of recording. He credits them throughout. See [[Quote Attributions]] and [[The Seven Core Distinctions]].

## Summary

The most heavily attributed episode in his work, and the one that makes Dave's relationship to his own material clearest. Jesse frames it up front: when Dave starts working with a new coach, Jesse pays attention, because "if Dave thinks that coach is worth getting coached by, then he must be the man." Dave calls himself "a perpetual student", mentions a lifetime goal of spending a million dollars on his own development — itself copied from Diana Chapman — and quotes Steve Hardison: *the difference between the master and the student is that the master knows he'll always be a student.*

The body of the episode is Joe Hudson's seven core distinctions, each an *X over Y*: connection over perfection, enjoy over manage, feel over figure, want over should, authenticity over improvement, empower over power, love over defense. Dave is careful about confidentiality — he shares the structure and one of Tara's questions, and holds back the exercises so participants get the whole arc.

Two run deepest. **Enjoy over manage** produces the episode's most provocative claim: enjoyment is not a reward for effectiveness but a cause of it, which makes hustle "a lose-lose" — both less pleasant and less effective than enjoying the journey. Dave's evidence is domestic and unglamorous: years of resisting his taxes *while doing them*, fixed by dropping the obligation frame (via Diana Chapman's "you don't have to do your taxes, there are just consequences") and asking his wife to sit beside him.

**Authenticity over improvement** carries the episode's most personal moment. Tara's question — *if you could not improve anything about yourself, what would you have to let go of to feel peaceful?* — surfaces Dave's own deferred life, which turns out to be enlightenment. He describes letting his heart break over never arriving, and finding it left him "more peaceful, more awake."

It closes on the arc: empowerment and unconditional love turn out to be the same state reached from opposite sides, which is why the seventh distinction is love over defense — and why what you are defending is worth identifying, because it is the ego rather than the self.
