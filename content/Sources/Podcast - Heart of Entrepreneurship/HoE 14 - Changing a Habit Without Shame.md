# HoE 14 — Changing a Habit Without Shame

## Summary

Jesse opens with fifteen years of yo-yoing: gain thirty pounds, do P90X, lose twenty-five, repeat. What broke the cycle was not more discipline — it was caring less. He works out consistently now and reports that if he misses a week on holiday, "it's no big deal either way," where the old version would have concluded *my streak is ruined, forget it.* Dave names what makes that counterintuitive: most people believe more pressure equals more results, and the evidence here is that **less pressure, not none, produces better results** — especially over the long term.

The mechanism is **counter-will**. Humans resist being forced, and the first thing a child learns is *no.* Turn that on yourself and you get an inner war: a drill sergeant who wins for a while, and a suppressed part that eventually rebels into the binge. Dave is careful not to dismiss the David Goggins version outright — he respects the indomitable spirit — but notes it runs on a chip-on-the-shoulder and is not sustainable.

The strongest material is on **shame**, drawn from Joe Hudson. Three images: shame as the **electric fence** used to train a dog, shame as the thing that **puts the brakes on all the other emotions**, and shame as **the lock that binds the chains of bad habits**. Dave walks his own ice-cream cycle through it — eat, self-reproach, want the reproach to stop, eat more — and shows how the shame is fuel rather than consequence.

His alternative is exact: welcome the shame as a physical sensation, but do not believe the thoughts that arrive with it. The night before recording he put the ice cream back, had a deliberate temper tantrum, found an old frustration from his relationship with his dad, felt it, and the compulsion dissipated — then had half a scoop and stopped.

The practical tool is the **cost-benefit question**: before changing a behaviour, name what it has been getting you. Dave frames it as an invitation to self-acceptance, on the grounds that you cannot sustainably shift what you do not accept. The episode closes on shoulds in management — the micromanagement meme, founder mode as the same rule flipped, and Dave's verdict that *anytime there's a should involved, it's situational awareness distortion.*
