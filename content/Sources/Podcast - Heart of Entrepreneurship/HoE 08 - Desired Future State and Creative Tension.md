# HoE 08 — Desired Future State and Creative Tension

## Summary

The first episode recorded without Andrew, and the first built around a single named framework rather than a conversation. Jesse hosts it deliberately: DFS is the Dave concept he uses most — "you'd hear that term being said constantly" at his companies — and he sets out to get the idea on the table and then be coached on it live.

The framework comes from Peter Senge's **creative tension**, and Dave's entry point is that tension is a real motivator because tension seeks resolution. The question is only which kind you generate. Most people unconsciously manufacture it from **lack** ("we should be at 30, we're at 10") or **fear** ("if we don't hit this, we can't raise") — both of which work, and both of which Dave calls dirty fuel for the residue they leave. Creative tension comes instead from holding two things simultaneously: full acceptance of current reality, and a vivid desired future. The distinction is one of *time* — frustration is the desired future collapsed into the present, and the suffering is in the collapse, not the ambition.

Most of the episode is spent on the failure modes, which is where the specifics are. Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and produces the yo-yo. A DFS stated as a sentence does not generate tension; it has to be a picture you can inhabit, which Dave demonstrates by walking Jesse into end-of-2025 with his eyes closed. The tension band can be too slack (no gap, no deadline) or snapped (magical thinking), and a snapped band leaves a team feeling like it loses daily while doubling the business. Acceptance gets carefully fenced off from both spiritual bypass and resignation — it means facing the number, and anger about the number is welcome. The episode closes on the objection Dave hears most, that a leader cannot declare a future they do not know how to reach, and his inversion of it: the declaration is what generates the creativity that finds the path.
