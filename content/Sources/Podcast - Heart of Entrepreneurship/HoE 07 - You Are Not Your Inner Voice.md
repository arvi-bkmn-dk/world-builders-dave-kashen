# HoE 07 — You Are Not Your Inner Voice

## Summary

Prompted by a newsletter Jesse wrote about spirituality — which drew a large response, much of it *it's funny you're only talking about this now, I could already feel it* — the episode tackles the loaded word directly and lands on meditation as the practical entry point.

Jesse's argument that you aren't your thoughts is the best version of it in his work, because it uses evidence everyone has and nobody admits: *have you ever had the thought that you wanted to kill someone? Be honest. Why didn't you do it? Because you're not your thoughts.*

Dave supplies the definitions. His teacher defined the divine as **the unknown**, making spirituality the exploration of what isn't yet known — which he contrasts with our constant effort to feel certain and sound smart. He also separates **mythicism from mysticism**: believing stories someone else wrote down, versus investigating your own direct experience. Identity, he notes, is itself a thought.

The practical core is a correction: meditation is not quieting the voice. It's noticing. With a thought we normally act on it, feel something, or judge it — meditation is the practice of having the thought and doing none of those. Dave's actual instruction is five or ten minutes on the breath, and the point is the *returning*, like training a puppy. "It's not that you're supposed to stay focused on your breath for 10 straight minutes."

The benefits he names: living in the reality you're actually in rather than the dystopian version a negatively-biased narrator reports, and resilience. It closes on **via negativa** — there is nothing to attain, only barriers to identify — and Dave's insistence that none of this is a *should*.

## Notable Quotes

> Have you ever had the thought that you wanted to kill somebody? … Why didn't you do it? If you're your thoughts, why didn't you do it?

> I had a teacher once who defined the divine as the unknown.

> A lot of people think the goal is to quiet the voice in the head. It often doesn't quiet — and that's not a problem, if you can see through it.

> Notice that your inner voice doesn't tend to say things like: you're so amazing, you look so perfect today, I'm proud of you.

> You live in a reality that's level five, and the voice would have you think reality is minus five.

> Meditation is literally just the practice of having the thought and not reacting to it.

> It's not that you're supposed to stay focused on your breath for 10 straight minutes. The practice is just the noticing.

> If there was a muscle to build, it's the muscle of exerting conscious choice around where you put your attention.

> Aliveness, freedom, peace, fulfilment, sense of wholeness — they're always already here.

> Your task is not to seek for love, but to identify all the barriers you've built against it. — Rumi

> Identity is itself a thought.

> If love is unconditional by its nature — and I think we are love at our essence — then the journey is from living conditionally to living unconditionally.

> This is not a should.
