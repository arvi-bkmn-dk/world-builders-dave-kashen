# HoE 15 — Gagan Biyani on Control, Proof and Comparison

## Summary

**The first guest episode**, and the first without Jesse or Andrew. Dave interviews [[Gagan Biyani]] — Udemy, Sprig, Maven — whom he met at a founders' retreat in Sun Valley. The format change matters: instead of two hosts developing an idea, this is Dave coaching in public on a founder's actual history, which makes it the clearest demonstration in his work of what he does in a session.

Gagan's arc supplies the material. A speech-and-debate camp at 13. Udemy at 21, fired by his co-founder at 23. Sprig — an early ghost kitchen — to 1,300 employees and then eighteen months of layoffs to zero. Three years off. Then Maven, which also rose and collapsed before restarting.

Three threads run through it.

**Churn and letting people go.** Gagan's claim that every company has an erased co-founder, and that of your first ten employees, 20% survival at five years is normal. Dave's position that keeping someone who isn't a fit is a lose-lose — they get the message anyway — and the line with no counterexample: *I always wish I had done it sooner.*

**Control.** Dave introduces Conscious Leadership's **sorting the files** exercise, and the observation that the trouble is never wanting control but misfiling what you have. Gagan's macro version: below a certain scale you control everything and can't blame the environment; above it, timing dominates.

**Proof and comparison**, which is where it goes deepest. Gagan's burden of proof *rose* with his success — a million dollars at 21 would have been plenty; by 25 it was two billion-dollar companies by 30. What broke it was not achieving more but three years of nothing, discovering he was happy anyway. Dave's leading question — *what's the burden of proof now?* — gets *"is there a burden of proof?"*, which was the answer he was fishing for.

The coaching itself is the most visible it gets. Dave demonstrates **welcome the feeling, question the story** on his own twinge of embarrassment after misremembering a baseball statistic, then applies it to Gagan's guilt about firing people.

## Key Teachings

- [[The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder]]
- [[Welcome the feeling, question the story]]
- [[I feel bad usually means sad and guilty and embarrassed]]
- [[Keeping someone who is not a fit is a lose lose]]
- [[Every company has a co-founder who left and got erased from history]]
- [[You make it mean so much more about you than it actually means]]
- [[Equating your value to the value of your company is a major trap]]
- [[The burden of proof is an illusion]]
- [[Talent is not the defining factor of your success in a high variance business]]
- [[If you are envious of someone you have to trade their entire life for yours]]
- [[What if what they did was be unapologetically themselves]]

Restated here from earlier sources:

- [[If the person wants to do the thing you need them to do, they do not require a lot of management]] — Dave cites Joe Hudson's masterclass directly
- [[Heroes require victims]] — the hero move behind not firing people
- [[Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship]]
- [[Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad]]
- [[Everyone is inherently worthy of what they authentically want]]
- [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]]
- [[You cannot decouple strategy from what you actually want]]
