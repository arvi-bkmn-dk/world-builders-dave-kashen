# How to Choose a Co-Founder You Won't Regret

## Summary

*"You'll likely spend more time with your co-founder than with your spouse. More emotionally intense experiences. Higher financial stakes."* And yet most founders reduce the choice to a checklist — talented technologist, relevant experience, fills a gap, available.

Dave has both outcomes in his own history. His first startup: *"my co-founder was much more of a go-go, don't trust people, work all the time kind of guy. My style is much more optimized for energy and balance."* And the venture fund he built with his best friend, where *"every single conflict has actually strengthened our relationship."*

**Choosing.** Complementary skills, low overlap, and *"the co-CEO thing very rarely works."* But the overlooked criteria are felt: do you find them one of the most inspiring people you've known — *"you want to feel like you 'married up'"* — and does your energy rise or fall around them. The test: **would you be psyched to be stuck in the Cleveland Airport with this person for hours?**

**Aligning.** Money, world-change or growth? Venture scale or lifestyle? *"Neither set of answers is wrong. But if they don't match, you'll be in tension about something neither of you can name."*

**Foundation.** Diana Chapman's line — **shared commitments and clear agreements** — split into directional commitments (win-for-all, candor, responsibility) and concrete agreements (roles, decision rules, effort, equity). Tune in to whether each *"feels good in your system"*; a hero-move equity split *"can fester for years."*

**Governance.** *"Founder is not a role; it's a vestige of time."* Vest over four years.

Closes: **trust is an efficiency multiple.**
