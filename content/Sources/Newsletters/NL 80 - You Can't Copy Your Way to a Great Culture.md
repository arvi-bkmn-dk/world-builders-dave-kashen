# You Can't Copy Your Way to a Great Culture

## Summary

**Nine times out of ten, no one in a company knows its values.** Yet every company has a culture; the question is whether you built it on purpose. A founder Dave has coached for years is starting a new company and asked how to get culture right from day one.

**The step most founders skip.** They borrow admired companies' values; his first team laughed that *he wishes we were like this, but we're not.* Borrowed values breed cynicism. Defining values is self-discovery: what's actually true for me? And culture gets set by who you let in: hire, fire, promote, compensate, recognise, attend to.

**Reverse engineer your values.** The people who light you up, the peaks of your energy graph, the bathroom mirror. Then work backwards into five to seven interview questions and a culture-based review with a behaviour per value. The sharp-elbowed ML engineer is how culture erodes, one reasonable exception at a time.

**Your leadership team is the loudest statement.** Hire leaders who'd do the job as well as you; run Reed Hastings' keeper test.

**The moment that undoes it.** A value is risk-taking; someone's big risk fails; you're scared and rip into them. You meant every word, but threat overrides values, which is why Dave now titles his talks *Building Culture from the Inside Out.* **Where are you letting fear stop you from being authentic?**
