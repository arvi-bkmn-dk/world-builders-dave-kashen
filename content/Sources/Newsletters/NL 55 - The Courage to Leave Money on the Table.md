# The Courage to Leave Money on the Table

## Summary

Dave's coach put him on hold: *"I'm going to hold. You're going to call the CEO of Health Central right now or else we're done."*

The stakes were real — first son born a month earlier, sole provider, six months into a one-year retention bonus. He clicked over and told Chris Schroeder: *"I found out what I'm here on earth to do and I got to go do it."*

**"Everyone thought I was crazy."** The argument for waiting was airtight, and that is precisely what made it dangerous: *"I'd been making that same choice of status and money over purpose my entire life."* Christensen's line again — **it never feels like the right time** — *"what happens after 'just six more months'? You make that same choice again. And again."*

Two things pushed him. **His soul was dying**, which he distinguishes from discomfort: *"is your soul dying or just uncomfortable? There's a difference between 'this is hard and scary' versus 'I can't do one more day of this work that isn't aligned with who I am.'"* And he needed to be the example — *"I need to live the way I'm going to coach"* — including for his son.

Then the lean year: a laundry room turned office, making his wife's friends cry, charging very little, eating into savings. *"God bless my wife. She just believed in me."* Then Nir Eyal, then word through the Stanford network. The mechanism: love the work → keep getting better → **mastery → abundance.**

And the coda: Chris Schroeder hired him as a coach. One of his first clients.
