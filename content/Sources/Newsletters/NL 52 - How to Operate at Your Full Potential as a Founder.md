# How to Operate at Your Full Potential as a Founder

## Summary

Founders rate themselves 5, 6, sometimes 4 on operating at full potential — and Dave's interest is in what the number measures. "The mind invents an abstract ideal of full potential and then obsesses over the gap. But that obsession drains the very thing you need most: creative energy."

His definition of **flow** is two things at once: **present to current reality** without resisting it, and **imagining your desired future** clearly. The cognitive dissonance between them is what generates ideas — "both highly productive and experiencing high joy and creativity."

The two exits are the two halves failing. **Frustration** collapses the desired future into the present. **Fear** focuses on the undesired future — *"like driving a motorcycle looking at the wall instead of the road."*

External events trigger the drift, but so does nothing at all: the mind "is scanning for problems and will even invent them to try to solve." Dave's own example is the Monday after Thanksgiving, his mind labelling ordinary decisions as threats.

So the replacement measure is a set of percentages — time in flow versus frustration or fear; motivation from excitement versus fear; and the same for how he motivates the team. Comparison against "full potential" fails because you see your own robot held together with **duct tape and bubble gum** and everyone else's polished exterior.

Objective outcomes still matter, but they're "downstream. The cause is: are you operating from a state of flow more and more of the time?"
