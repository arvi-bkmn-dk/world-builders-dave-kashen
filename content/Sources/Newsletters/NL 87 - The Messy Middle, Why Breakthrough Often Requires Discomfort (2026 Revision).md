# The Messy Middle: Why Breakthrough Often Requires Discomfort

## Summary

A 2026 revised repost of [[NL 17 - The Messy Middle]]. The teaching is the same; the substance lives in the original's notes, linked below. What changed:

- The "what if" questions become direct statements: the discomfort *is* often the doorway, the messy middle *is* where competitive advantage develops.
- The welcome-the-discomfort prompt changes from "What if this discomfort is exactly what needs to be here?" to "Is this discomfort exactly what needs to be here right now?"
- The standalone line "Transformation rarely looks like transformation while it's happening" no longer appears as its own sentence.

## Key Teachings

From the original, unchanged in this revision:

- [[Transformation rarely looks like transformation while it is happening]]
- [[Resolving conflict without allowing the feelings is an insane act]]
- [[The more cheesy and uncomfortable it feels, the more likely it is to be effective]]
- [[Every transformation has a chasm between the familiar and the unknown]]
- [[Treat the startup as an emotional intensity gym]]
- [[The presenting issue is almost never the real issue]]
