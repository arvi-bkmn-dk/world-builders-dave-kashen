# Victim, Villain, Hero, Navigating The Drama Triangle

## Summary

The written version of the drama triangle material from [[HoE 11 - Personas and the Fear Triangle]], credited here to **Stephen Karpman**, who first described it.

Opens with the startup-specific forms: a fundraising setback triggers victim ("investors just don't get our vision"), product delays spark villain ("engineering keeps dropping the ball"), churn launches hero ("I'll personally handle these accounts").

What the newsletter adds over the episode is the **conviction** problem. Each corner arrives with its own justification, so nothing in the cycle feels irrational from inside it. Dave's client, after a missed deadline, moved through all three in a single session and every statement felt true to him.

And then Dave runs it on himself — sign-ups to his San Francisco founder workshop came in lower than projected, and he watched his own mind complete the triangle in minutes. A coach who teaches the framework, caught by it, with the tell being that the thoughts felt *right*.

## Key Teachings

- [[Each position feels completely justified and true in the moment]]

Restated here from earlier sources:

- [[The voice in your head is mostly playing those three roles]]
- [[Fight is villain, flight is hero, freeze is victim]]
- [[Heroes require victims]]

- [[So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle]]
- [[Interpreting a situation as a problem is what drops you into blame]]
