# Money Isn't the Problem, Your Relationship With It Is

## Summary

Opens with a question Dave put to a founder in the IGE community: *imagine you're on a date with money — would it be a toxic relationship, a distant affair, a desperate clinging, or a harmonious partnership?* The answer: "Part of me clings desperately to money, part of me resents it, and another part dreams of freedom but fears becoming someone I hate."

Dave's frame: money is a neutral human invention. What creates suffering is the relationship. And relationships are shaped by **parts** — the chapter of his book-in-progress he quotes here opens with wanting and not wanting the chocolate cake at the same time.

Around money the parts conflicts are especially loud, and all of them sincere: one part chasing wealth, one judging it morally, one clinging out of scarcity, one afraid that succeeding would turn them into someone they'd despise. His line for it: **anytime there's something we want but aren't getting, there's a part of us that doesn't want it.**

The most personal section is his own. Having lost a significant portion of his net worth, his achievement-oriented part was devastated — until his wife asked *what do you love about yourself that has nothing to do with your money or success?* The answer ("I'm just a kind, caring, fun-loving guy") revealed that both warring parts wanted the same thing: peace and worthiness. Financial success was one part's **strategy** for getting there.
