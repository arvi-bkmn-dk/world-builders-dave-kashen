# The Real Reason Startups Fail

## Summary

The strongest single claim in the newsletters: **startups don't fail because they run out of money, they fail because their founders run out of energy.**

Dave argues it causally. The company had no money before it existed — the founder's creativity and vision attracted the capital, so a founder with energy intact can attract it again. He then traces the real sequence, and the point is how early it begins: energy depletes unnoticed, clarity diminishes, morale declines, opportunities get missed, decision quality drops, financial problems emerge, fundraising gets harder, and only then does anyone say "we ran out of money." By step eight the founder has been running on empty too long to see their own contribution.

His counter-case is a founder with two weeks of runway who took a basketball reframe — *fourth quarter, two minutes, down eight, how do you want to show up?* — answered "give me the ball, coach," and closed over \$1m within the week. Empty bank account, full energy reserves.

The body is four **energy traps** with escapes: the victim state, the resistance loop, the identity delusion (*I am running this company, I am not this company*), and dirty fuel.

He explicitly declines to turn it into an obligation to persevere: choosing to stop "can be just as courageous and virtuous" as continuing.
