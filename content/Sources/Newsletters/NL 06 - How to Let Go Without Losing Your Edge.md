# How to Let Go Without Losing Your Edge

## Summary

The third piece in a row on surrender, and the one that answers the objection head-on: *if I drop my attachment to external achievements, won't I lose my fire?*

Dave opens with the question he puts to clients — **are you willing to see how your startup failing could be as good as your startup succeeding?** — and reports that most resist, because they believe attachment is the engine. He takes the worry seriously and names the failure on both sides: confuse letting go with not caring and things descend into chaos; leave the drive to finally feel enough unresolved and you stay on the hamster wheel no matter what you achieve.

The body is eight replacement fuels, each with a practice and a note on why it sustains motivation: desired future state and morning visualisation; zone of genius; commitments you'd keep in total anonymity; small visible progress; swapping *have to* for *get to*; letting service guide you; curiosity over perfection; and accountability framed as growth rather than blame.

The sharpest line is filed under burnout prevention: **you can't be curious and afraid at the same time.**

> Are you willing to see how your startup failing could be as good as your startup succeeding?

> You can't be curious and afraid at the same time.

> Fear makes you myopic and stressed; curiosity is enlivening and expansive.

> When you respond to each unsuccessful attempt with 'the experiment failed' instead of 'I failed', you'll maintain your motivation to keep making additional attempts.

> A sense of contribution outlasts any personal trophy.
