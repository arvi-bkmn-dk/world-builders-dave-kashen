# How to Set Boundaries That Actually Work

## Summary

Opens on the conversation you've been avoiding for weeks — with a co-founder, a client, an employee — and the stomach tightening every time you think about it.

Dave's correction to why: **you're not avoiding it because you don't want to hurt them. You're avoiding it because you don't want to feel what you'll feel when they react.** Which moves it from a kindness problem, where no move exists, to a capacity problem, which can be trained.

The cost: unexpressed boundaries don't disappear, they turn into resentment — "and resentment always finds a way out, usually in ways that damage relationships far more than the original honest conversation would have."

The piece then sharpens the request/boundary distinction with business examples, and adds the best new material: Dave asked Diana Chapman what separates a threat from a consequence, and her one-word answer was **follow-through.** A threat manipulates and is usually a bluff; a consequence states what you will do. He names the spiral threats produce — threaten, don't follow through, escalate the threat.

Closes on using anger as navigation for where a boundary wants setting.
