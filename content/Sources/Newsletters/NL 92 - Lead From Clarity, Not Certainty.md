# Lead From Clarity, Not Certainty

## Summary

A client trying to write a three-year vision says *"it's just not clear to me."* Underneath: *I need to get it right. I should know.* Whatever the decision (a hire, a strategy, the product), the experience is the same: cycling in your head, waiting to feel sure.

**The illusion of certainty.** We're wired to crave certainty; Dave cites an unnamed shock study where a 50% chance of a bad outcome stressed people more than a 90% chance. The mind fills the unknown with worst cases, fear follows, and we escape into our heads looking for certainty that doesn't exist. Startups are uncertainty machines.

**Clarity is not certainty.** Certainty is about the future; clarity is about you, what you want and where you stand now. Conviction without certainty is coherent. The need for certainty is what obscures clarity.

**Ending the cycle.** The loop is held together by unfelt fear. Find it in the body, welcome it, ask what's actually answerable (a hard call is usually 52/48), and treat the decision as an experiment. *The role of a leader is to decide in the face of uncertainty, and that means deciding in the face of fear.*
