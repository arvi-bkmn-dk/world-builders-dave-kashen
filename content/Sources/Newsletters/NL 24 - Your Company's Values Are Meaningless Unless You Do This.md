# Your Company's Values Are Meaningless Unless You Do This

## Summary

A CEO whose VP of Sales beat quota every quarter and whose 360s said abrasive, demeaning, egotistical — "he made smart people feel dumb." People were speaking up less. Series A approaching. Fire the high performer and look unstable, or keep him and watch the culture rot.

*Respect, Inclusivity, Team Spirit* were on every wall. Dave's question: **are you just honoring your values, or are you standing for them too?**

Honoring is living them yourself — the starting point. **Standing for** them means holding others accountable, even when it costs you. Her gap was exact: respectful herself, and permitting disrespect. Which is why the culture problem read to her as a personnel problem.

The test: values that don't imply real, scary choices aren't values, they're convenient beliefs.

The rest is procedure — four exercises for finding your authentic values (the people you choose, peak experiences, the anti-values mirror, the no-fear test), narrowing to 3–5, rating yourself 1–10 on each, and naming what you'd risk to uphold them. Plus a bottom-up step most leaders skip: ask the team *what values, if violated, would cause you to quit?*
