# Projection, How Your Mind is a Mirror

## Summary

Opens with a founder who couldn't stand his co-founder's indecisiveness — *he never makes a call, he overthinks everything* — and the turn: it wasn't the indecision that bothered him, it was that he felt he had to always know the answer. Beneath the quick decisions was a fear of getting it wrong, and he couldn't tolerate hesitation in others because he wouldn't allow it in himself.

The piece states the mechanism plainly — **we don't see people as they are, we see them as we are** — and works through founder-specific examples: the arrogant investor, the disengaged team member, the VC who "doesn't get it."

Its distinctive contribution is **the flip side**. Aversion shows you what you've rejected; admiration shows you what you haven't yet claimed. The charisma you admire, the boldness you respect, the presence that feels magnetic — each points at something of your own you haven't stepped into. Both are invitations to expand.

It closes with two procedures, one for triggers (notice the pattern, turn inward, integrate rather than reject) and one for admiration (recognise the signal, own it, take action).
