# It's Only Lonely at the Top If You Can't Be Yourself There

## Summary

Brian Chesky built Airbnb to a hundred billion dollars and said no one told him how lonely it would get; the day after the IPO he did ten hours of Zoom in sweatpants. Half of CEOs report loneliness (HBR). Picture **the minute in the car in the driveway**, the only part of the day that belongs to the person under both roles.

**Nowhere to set it down.** Carrying the full burden, taking more than 100% responsibility, everyone else's glory days, old friends crowded out, a resentful family, then shame when the company struggles. Deep connection means being fully yourself and loved for it, so **loneliness is the distance between what you experience and what you let anyone see.**

**The belief underneath.** *I can become worthy and lovable by achieving enough.* Dave was so busy trying to become worthy of his family that he almost lost them. That belief is itself a kind of loneliness.

**The way back.** Find two or three people you can fully share with (*for you or for them?*). Let work feel like play, since play is how humans connect (the rat park experiment). Have the conversation you've been avoiding: it reconnects you twice. Get in person with people on the same path.

**We're all human.** Closing your heart doesn't protect you; it guarantees you carry the pain alone. Chip Conley: *the most neglected fact in business is that we're all human.*
