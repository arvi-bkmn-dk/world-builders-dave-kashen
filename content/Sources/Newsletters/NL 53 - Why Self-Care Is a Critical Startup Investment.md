# Why Self-Care Is a Critical Startup Investment

## Summary

A client drowning in detail, terrified of a non-negotiable two-week honeymoon, spent months preparing to be gone — hiring, delegating, moving from the weeds to *"leading and orchestrating."* **"The irony? Preparing to step away made him more valuable to his company than any hour he'd spent buried in the work."**

The myth Dave is attacking is zero-sum: *"either you thrive as a person OR your company thrives."* Founders drop exercise, hobbies and friends *"on the altar of startup success,"* then operate drained, reactive and uncreative. He invokes **"one cannot drink from an empty cup"** and the *Manage Your Energy, Not Your Time* article — *"if you think about your energy being the most important contributor to startup success, it changes how you think about using your time."* And what people actually want from a founder is **"your best self, not the maximum quantity of hours."**

The reason it must be forced: Clayton Christensen's **"life is a series of extenuating circumstances"** — it never feels like the right time. So the vacation becomes a diagnostic. *"If there's no one you can delegate to, that tells you something about your hiring."*

Which surfaces the real scope of the job: **there are very few things only you can do** — thinking about the future, raising money, hiring senior executives. Dave challenges founders to *"hire themselves out of a job. I want them to be bored for a little bit."*

Ends on the shift: from *"I have to sacrifice myself for my startup"* to **"investing in myself IS investing in my startup."**

## Notable Quotes

> One cannot drink from an empty cup.

> They convince themselves that last marginal hour of grinding is what's going to make the difference.

> What people really expect and want from you as the founder or CEO is to bring your best self, not to maximize the quantity of hours you give.

> And we don't bring our best self by sacrificing ourselves.

> Life is a series of extenuating circumstances.

> What that means is it never feels like the right time.

> As the founder or CEO, there are very few things only you can do.

> I like challenging my founders to hire themselves out of a job. I want them to be bored for a little bit.

> The gap between your performance and theirs is the short-term cost for the future gains.

> You may need to show your nervous system that things don't fall apart.

> That's the shift: from I have to sacrifice myself for my startup to investing in myself IS investing in my startup.
