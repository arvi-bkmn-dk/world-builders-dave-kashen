# Lessons From the First Annual Sun Valley Retreat

## Summary

A report from the first Sun Valley Founder's Retreat, and the structure of the piece is the finding: after days of inner-voice work, unblocking feelings, conscious listening, speaking unarguably and skiing past their edges, the most powerful session was the last one — **five hours of appreciation.**

Dave is precise about how it started: awkward, uncomfortable, people squirming. Not because giving appreciation is hard but because receiving it is. "We are generally scared to receive and therefore close ourselves off." Then it turned — tension melting, self-protection softening, founders feeling seen "maybe for the first time in years," and crucially trusting what they heard over their own inner critic.

The leadership case follows, with numbers attached: effective teams run a 5:1 ratio of positive to negative interactions, appreciation outweighs tens of thousands in compensation, and clients still vividly recall single moments of it from a decade ago. Against that, most leaders think it and don't say it — because the negativity bias makes what's wrong easier to notice than what's right.

It closes on three smaller takeaways: the freedom founders found in vulnerability, the value of connection and camaraderie, and the impact of letting love flow.
