# The Counterintuitive Path to Getting Everything You Want

## Summary

A founder with over \$100m raised, thousands of customers and 200 employees: *"I feel less and less enthusiastic about going into work each day."* Early on he was coding and talking to customers; now it's "meetings about meetings, managing politics, feeling pressure to grow just to satisfy investors."

Dave's diagnosis is a single unconscious substitution — from *how can we help more people?* to *how can we look more successful?* — and the claim that follows: **when you focus on your own achievement instead of serving others, you end up with less of both.**

The parable of the three bricklayers carries it. *I'm laying bricks. I'm building a wall. I'm creating a place for people to connect with the divine.* Same job, completely different energy — because "the quality of your experience isn't determined by what you're building but by who you're building it for."

The turn is that service is self-interested in the most literal way: **the greatest beneficiary isn't your customers, it's you.** Attention on your own problems is a prison where every setback is personal; attention on *how can I help* doesn't remove the problems, it stops them consuming you.
