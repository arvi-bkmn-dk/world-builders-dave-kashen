# Why Investor Rejections Hit So Hard (And What to Do in That Moment)

## Summary

A self-aware founder ran eight back-to-back investor meetings in New York, thought he was fine, and came home **discouraged**, a word he'd never had applied to him. Dave's claim: rejection hits deeper than founders expect because **it lands at three layers at once.**

**Grief:** letting go of the imagined yes. Sadness is the energy of letting go. **Identity:** one no becomes *maybe I'm not as good as I thought*; Martin Seligman's optimists read setbacks as temporary and specific, pessimists as permanent and pervasive. **Childhood:** the nervous system registers it as *I don't want to be your friend.*

**What to do in that moment.** Learning from it is right; the timing usually isn't. Skip the feeling and it leaks into the next meeting. The reset: pause between meetings, drop into the body, welcome what's there, then examine the story, and when rushed, breathe four by four.

**The presence dojo.** The week in New York wasn't evidence the inner work failed; it showed exactly where the edge still is. Resilient founders don't feel rejection less, they know what to do in the moment after.
