# Seeking Approval Is Sabotaging Your Startup

## Summary

Opens three hours before a board meeting, presentation revised fifteen times, the loop running: *will they approve of this strategy? I need them to think I'm doing a good job.*

Dave's explanation is neurological rather than moral — brains are built to seek approval from authority figures, because pleasing parents kept us alive. When a board holds perceived power over the company's future, the primitive brain files them as parents. His correction is a fact about the relationship: **your parents controlled your survival; your board chose to back you.** They're extensions of your team, not judges.

The cost list is specific: filtered information, avoided candour, decisions made to please rather than progress, and the prefrontal cortex going offline under threat — "a business running on partial information, delayed course corrections, and compromised decision-making."

The centrepiece is the **Stakeholder Meeting Paradox**, from sitting in on hundreds of these: the founders who get the most respect are the ones who need it least. Approval-seekers spend 80% of the meeting on past performance; partnership-seekers spend 80% on future strategy and unknowns. The more you try to impress, the less insight you extract — and the less impressive the results become.
