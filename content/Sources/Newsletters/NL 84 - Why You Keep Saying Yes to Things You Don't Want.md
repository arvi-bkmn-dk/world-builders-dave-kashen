# Why You Keep Saying Yes to Things You Don't Want

## Summary

Somewhere on your calendar is **a yes you didn't want**: a call, the on-the-fence hire, the investor you settled for, venture capital because that's the way it's done. Each felt reasonable, and forcing yourself drains energy into frustration and burnout. *What has you say yes to things you don't really want to do?*

**Why we default to yes.** People-pleasing; the internalised *should* voice; fear of letting go; and guilt about handing off what you dislike, when one man's heaven is another's hell. That guilt usually hides the belief that success requires suffering. All of it is the shaking of the sand jar; set it down and what you want settles into view.

**Go on a no diet** (from the Conscious Leadership Group). Default to no inside your mind and sit with it: anger means you wanted it, relief means you didn't, fear or sadness is often what the yes was avoiding. Don't confuse fear with a no; Dave is scared about publishing his book and still has a whole body yes. Tell people what you're doing, and treat any no from head, heart or gut as a veto.

**When you stop settling.** The fear stories turn out backwards; people end up more successful. The no diet and Michael Singer's surrender experiment both take the fear-based mind out of the driver's seat. *What are you gonna do with your one wild and precious life?*
