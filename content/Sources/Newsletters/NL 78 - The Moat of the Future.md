# The Moat of the Future

## Summary

**Tim Urban's thousand-page book of history:** farming on page 950, most of the modern world on page 1,000, and page 1,001 likely as different again. Our ability to predict what comes next is the lowest it's ever been, and every article declaring which moats survive is a prediction made at exactly that moment.

**Grasping at certainty.** Fear exaggerates risk and lives at the edge of the unfamiliar, and this is the most unfamiliar time any of us will face. People pretend to certainty. The truth: we don't know.

**What's at risk.** Brand, customer relationships and regulatory advantage have persisted. Software may not, as AI collapses the cost of building.

**The moat nobody can copy: authentic alignment.** The founder who can't stop thinking about the customer's problem out-spends everyone in time and energy, and you can't fake it. Customers can feel whether you're extracting or creating; Costco's $1.50 hot dog (from Eric Ries's *Incorruptible*) is the ultimate win-win.

**Attuning to your truth.** What lights you up when nobody's watching? Authentic desire or ego desire? Where are you extracting? When uncertainty spikes, a confident prediction or *I don't know*? It comes down to sourcing safety, freedom and worth within. Jon Kabat-Zinn: you can't stop the waves, but you can learn to surf.
