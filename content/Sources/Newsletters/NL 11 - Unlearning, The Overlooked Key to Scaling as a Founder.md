# Unlearning, The Overlooked Key to Scaling as a Founder

## Summary

The argument that scaling is less about what you learn than what you let go of — because the instincts that built the company are the ones now capping it.

Dave's framing: every founder starts in survival mode where hustle, speed and doing everything yourself are genuine superpowers. At scale the same instincts invert — bias for speed becomes bottlenecking, need for control stops the team stepping up, being the smartest problem-solver prevents anyone else solving anything.

Three things nearly every founder has to unlearn: **more work equals more success** (delegate before you feel ready, because you never will), **the need to have all the answers** (a team that depends on you is a fragile system), and **pressure equals performance** (high performance comes from clarity, presence and focus — calm leadership is a competitive advantage, not a weakness).

The piece then goes further than a management newsletter usually does. At the deepest level, unlearning leads past habits to the assumptions underneath — the recognition that "the self you've been protecting, proving and pushing isn't actually who or what you are," and that **self-ing** is a continuous mental effort rather than a fact.
