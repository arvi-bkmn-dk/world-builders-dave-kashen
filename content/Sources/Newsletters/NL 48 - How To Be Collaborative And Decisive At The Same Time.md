# How To Be Collaborative And Decisive At The Same Time

## Summary

*"If everyone on your team agrees with you, what do you need them for?"*

The stuck position Dave names: not decisive enough to move fast, not collaborative enough to use the team's intelligence — two-hour meetings that end with nobody sure what was decided. And his diagnosis: **the problem isn't choosing between inviting dissent and being decisive, it's not being clear about how the decision actually gets made.**

The fix is a **decision rule** stated up front. In most leadership contexts the intended rule is *leader decides with input* — but if the team assumes consensus, they keep lobbying. One sentence settles it: *"I really want to hear your thoughts. We'll talk for 20 minutes, and then based on all your input, I'm going to decide."* Now they need to be heard, not to win.

Three keys to actually getting dissent: ask for it explicitly, **make it safe when it arrives** ("what you appreciate appreciates"), and practise conscious listening from head, heart and gut with a repeat-back. *"When people feel genuinely heard, they can disagree with your decision and still commit to executing it."*

The second half is the inner half. **Indecision is a feeling problem, not a thinking problem** — waiting on certainty to avoid feeling the fear of being wrong. Trust intuition over intellect (*"what feels right to you?"*), treat it as an experiment, and note that **hard decisions are hard because either choice could be great.**
