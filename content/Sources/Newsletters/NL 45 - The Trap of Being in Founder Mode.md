# The Trap of Being in Founder Mode

## Summary

Dave takes a public position against a popular idea: *"I've coached founders who've created over $100 billion in combined value. And I don't believe in Founder Mode."*

The objection isn't to Brian Chesky's talk — the permission to care about details again was valuable — it's to what founders did with it. **We just traded one dogma for another.** The old gospel was delegate and stay strategic; now "Founder Mode" is used to justify micromanagement and avoid "the genuinely scary work of letting go."

The correction is the gearbox: a car stuck in fifth is great on the highway and a disaster in traffic. **You don't want to get stuck in any mode** — you want the discernment to read the moment, on three questions: what lights you up, what serves the business, and *what am I avoiding?*

The third is where the piece turns. Founders who are maxed out say "I keep stepping back in whenever something goes wrong." The issue isn't hours, it's **protecting themselves from the fear of letting go** — and Founder Mode "gives that protection a name. Makes it seem strategic instead of scared."

Closes on flow rather than hours: 80 hours in flow beats 40 hours grinding. *Play to win, but make sure you're actually playing.*
