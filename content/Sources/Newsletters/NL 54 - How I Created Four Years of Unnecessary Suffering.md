# How I Created Four Years of Unnecessary Suffering

## Summary

The most personally exposed piece in his work. Christmas 2008, weeks after Lehman, Dave breaks down crying at his then-girlfriend's family's house in Florida after **four years of 16-hour days, seven days a week** at Wellsphere. *"I don't know what to do. I feel totally trapped."*

**"And I was trapped, but not by what I thought."** Not the startup, not the hours, not the crisis. Starting the company in 2005 out of Stanford with heavy loans and having told everyone, he tied the outcome to worth: *"if this fails, it would reveal and confirm the belief of my unworthiness. Not 'the startup failed.' **I** would be exposed as a failure."*

Which inverts the motivation: **"I felt more pressure to not fail than I did to succeed."** He couldn't quit even while miserable — *"trapped by my own making"* — and the hours were an alibi: *"at least if I work as much as humanly possible… if it fails, I can't blame myself for not trying hard enough. I was trying to outrun a feeling through sheer effort."*

Then the outcome argument. Wellsphere pivoted, hit six million monthly visitors, entered acquisition talks in August 2008 — and Lehman collapsed, cutting the price. *"The macro environment, completely outside my control, decided what the outcome would be anyway."*

The retrospective verdict, after coaching and the Landmark Forum: **"I wasn't operating effectively. I was operating performatively… The exhaustion wasn't preventing failure. It was preventing the clarity I needed to succeed."**

Closes on the irony that the feared judgment doesn't exist — **"the startup world is probably the most gracious industry about failure"** — and two year-end questions.
