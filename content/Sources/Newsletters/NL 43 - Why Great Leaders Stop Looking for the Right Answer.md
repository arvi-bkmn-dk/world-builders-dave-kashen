# Why Great Leaders Stop Looking for the Right Answer

## Summary

Opens with Alice and the Cheshire Cat — *which way should I go? That depends on where you're trying to get to. I don't know. Then any road will do.*

The founder version is *"how do I know if I'm focused on the right priorities?"* Dave's reframe: **the real danger isn't choosing the wrong road, it's waiting at the crossroads hoping certainty will show up.** You're not confused about priorities, you're trapped in a search for certainty that doesn't exist.

The line that lands it: **the more you search for certainty, the more doubt you create.** Each round of searching generates more considerations, which generate more uncertainty.

And the observation about the people founders compare themselves to: **the world's best founders don't have more certainty than you — they've just stopped making uncertainty a problem.**

The alternative instrument is energy rather than intellect, illustrated with the sailboat captain versus the motorboat captain, and with Valve letting employees "vote with their chairs" — roll to whatever project excites them, whenever.
