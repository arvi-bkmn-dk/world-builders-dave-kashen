# Why Trying to Speed Up Keeps You Behind

## Summary

When a competitor ships your feature or a peer raises twice your round, most founders feel behind and **speed up**. Dave's claim: trying to speed up is what puts them further behind.

**How far back does it go?** Most people can't remember ever not feeling there was something left to accomplish. The urge came in with you; the competitor just gives it a number. Projected onto the company, it makes the future something you should already have, and that friction feels like falling behind even on a good day.

**What speeding up does.** It runs on adrenaline, a cheap substitute for aliveness. It trades the important for the urgent, so you sprint to put out fires you started by sprinting. And it convinces you to carry everything yourself.

**Slow down to get ahead.** What you want is effectiveness: a coal miner and Sam Altman have the same 24 hours. Slow is smooth, smooth is fast. See clearly, ask for help, prioritise and let the rest go. *If you let go of the belief that you're behind, what would you do next?*
