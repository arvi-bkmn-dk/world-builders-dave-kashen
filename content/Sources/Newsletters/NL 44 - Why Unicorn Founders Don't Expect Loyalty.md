# Why Unicorn Founders Don't Expect Loyalty

## Summary

A top performer quits and the founder feels betrayed — *"after all the time and energy I put into developing them"* — then catastrophises a mass exodus that never comes. Dave's questions: *what if the story your mind is making up isn't true? How might the opposite be just as true?*

The substance is blunter than most of his work: **you're not entitled to loyalty.** And the argument is symmetry — if revenue falls you'll probably lay people off, so expecting more loyalty than you offer is not reasonable. He doesn't moralise about layoffs; the point is that "loyalty is a two-way street, and most of us aren't driving both directions."

The replacement question is where the value is: **how do I create conditions where people actually want to stay?** — which redirects effort from securing commitment to earning it.

First condition: generosity **before** you need it. Leaders get stingy with appreciation and development, then scramble to match a competing offer once it's too late. **People don't stay for the compensation, but they might leave for it.**
