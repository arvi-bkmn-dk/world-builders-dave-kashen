# Why Coaching Unicorn Founders Often Leads to Spirituality

## Summary

Opens on Brian Chesky the day Airbnb went public, net worth $10 billion: *"my life was exactly the same. I'm alone. I wake up. I put on my sweatpants. I go onto my iMac and I have 10 to 12 hours of Zoom meetings."* He'd chased success because he thought it would make people love him, reached the pinnacle, and found it empty.

Dave places himself in the same arc — *"I wasn't particularly 'spiritual' myself until 2016"* — and states his actual job: **use your startup as a dojo for personal and spiritual growth**, which turns out to be *"the most efficient and direct path to what they're looking for."*

The developmental map is Michael Beckwith's **to me, by me, through me, as me**: victim consciousness, creator consciousness (*"where most typical coaching lives"*), the shift from *what do I want to create* to **what's wanting to happen through me**, and finally the recognition of awareness itself.

The efficiency argument: ask any founder what they want, then **ask why five times**, and it always lands on a feeling. *"But $10 billion doesn't create peace… So if the goal is peace and freedom, why take the long way around?"*

Then the measurement — **the gap between how much you can feel a feeling now and how much you think you'll feel it after** is exactly how attached you are. And the paradox: **the less attached, the easier to create.** *"You can't fake this. Believe me, I've tried."*

Ends on **winning twice.**
