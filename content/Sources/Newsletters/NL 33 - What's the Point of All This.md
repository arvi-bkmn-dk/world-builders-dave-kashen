# What's the Point of All This

## Summary

Three coaching clients sent Dave the same video in one week: Scottie Scheffler at a press conference before the Open Championship, having just won his fourth major, asked about celebrating.

> "It feels like you work your whole life to celebrate winning a tournament for a few minutes. It only lasts a few minutes, the euphoric feeling. You win, celebrate, hug my family, such an amazing moment — and then it's like, what are we going to eat for dinner?"

And then the part Dave says stood out more: *"What is the point? Why do I want to win this tournament so bad? If I win it's going to be awesome for about two minutes, and then the next week — you won two majors, how important is it to win a third? We are back here again."*

The world's best golfer asking the question. Dave's read is that this isn't depression, it's clarity — and that most people arrive at it one of two ways: by achieving everything they imagined and finding it isn't what they thought, or through the recognition of mortality.

The second half turns to death as teacher: how loss reveals not just that someone is gone but that you will be, and how the ego projects permanence, treating life as something here for good while we spend it "preparing for the rest of our lives."
