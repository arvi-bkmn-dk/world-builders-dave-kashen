# How to See Reality Clearly

## Summary

Opens with a hedge fund manager running a billion-dollar fund off his worst quarter ever, spiralling through scenarios of client withdrawals. Dave's question: *"Q1 is done. It is what it is. Are you going to waste your precious creative energy ruminating over what's already happened, or channel it toward creating your desired future?"* The tension in his shoulders visibly released.

The argument: founders believe their stress comes from circumstances — chaotic markets, demanding investors — when the real source is the inability to see reality through the fog of their own interpretations. Two founders with identical challenges get different outcomes on that factor alone.

Dave introduces **presence surface area** — the set of conditions under which you can stay present — and reframes the work as expanding it rather than achieving presence once. The mechanism: our brains perceive what is, then delete, distort and generalise according to our stories, producing an inner experience separate from reality.

The target is a newborn's perception, free of conditioned interpretation, which makes the process one of **undoing** rather than acquiring.

He names three blocks: collapsing stories into facts, creating problems that don't exist ("unnecessary suffering — the gap between reality and your expectation of reality"), and living in imagined negative futures — where the line lands: **it's not preparation when you're preemptively suffering.**

## Notable Quotes

> The real source of your struggles isn't the chaos itself, but your inability to see reality clearly through the fog of your own worries and interpretations.

> Our feelings are created by our interpretations of reality. Not from reality itself.

> Expanding your presence 'surface area' — the set of conditions under which you can remain in a state of presence.

> The process of seeing clearly is largely a process of 'undoing' or 'unlearning.'

> When you resist what is, you create unnecessary suffering — the gap between reality and your expectation of reality.

> It's not preparation when you're preemptively suffering.
