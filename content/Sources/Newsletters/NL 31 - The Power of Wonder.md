# The Power of Wonder

## Summary

A client spiralling about a Series B twelve hours into a live data room, with two investors having opened it, already at *maybe I should tell the team what happens if we don't raise, maybe I should pivot.*

Dave's intervention was one sentence — **you're trying to solve a problem that doesn't exist yet** — and the client's own read afterwards is the finding: *"I've been treating this uncertainty like the worst is guaranteed to happen when I actually have no idea."*

The argument: there are two ways humans meet uncertainty, **fear and wonder**, and it is the same uncertainty. The fear doesn't come from not knowing; it comes from the imagination of what could go wrong.

The most interesting claim is about knowledge itself: **when you know, you are relating to the current situation from the past** — pattern-matching a memory onto what's actually in front of you. Which makes *"I don't know"* the bridge back into the present.

Dave names what founders have trained out of themselves: always have a plan, appear confident, solve quickly, know your numbers — each a competence that became compulsory, and collectively an atrophied capacity for wonder.
