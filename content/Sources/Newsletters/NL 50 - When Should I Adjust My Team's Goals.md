# When Should I Adjust My Team's Goals

## Summary

A founder asks whether to adjust a goal he's unlikely to hit. Dave doesn't answer — he points out there's no universal answer, because **the real question isn't whether you're going to hit the goal, it's whether the goal is still serving its purpose.**

He opens on calibration: Google's OKRs treat two-thirds as a win; other companies set goals at 95% probability for the consistency. Neither is right, but the probability has to be **shared**, or two people will have very different reactions to the same miss.

Then the reframe: **goals are a means, not the ends.** The ends are enjoying the pursuit, maximising creativity, generating focus and alignment. Same goal, different teams, opposite effects — "it's not about the number, it's about the relationship you have with that number."

The image is a **rubber band** between where you are and where you want to be. Too slack and you coast; too tight and it snaps into resignation. Both extremes produce no tension. Basketball down 20 in the fourth: you don't adjust to losing by 15.

Three questions before adjusting: has the band actually snapped (can't imagine how is not the same as unlikely), is there any *how might we* excitement left, and what happens if you miss — because teams "set goals from above the line but relate to missing them from below the line."

His answer to the founder was a question. The founder chose to keep them.
