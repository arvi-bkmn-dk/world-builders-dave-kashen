# Your Fear Might Actually Be Excitement in Disguise

## Summary

When I think about acquiring this business, there's this heavy, pressing down sensation on my chest."* Strong track record, solid diligence, deal made financial sense — and he was stuck.

Dave asked what if fear and excitement move through the same physiological pathways. After a silence: *"I actually do think there's excitement there too."*

The claim: same energy, different story. The racing heart and butterflies are identical whether you're jumping out of a plane or pitching your dream investor; the story about what the sensations mean is the whole difference. **Excitement is simply fear unresisted.** And the loop — resist fear and you create more fear *and* block the excitement.

The most valuable section is Dave's confession. He spent **five years** welcoming his feelings so they would go away — "it doesn't work, and it's not actually welcoming" — until someone asked him the real test: *if you had to experience that feeling for the rest of your life, would that be ok?* His immediate reaction was "no way."

The paradox he lands on: the more you genuinely welcome a feeling, the more likely it is to move — but welcoming it *in order* to make it move is still control.
