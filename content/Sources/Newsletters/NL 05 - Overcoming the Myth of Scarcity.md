# Overcoming the Myth of Scarcity

## Summary

Opens at 2am with a client at his laptop, to-do list endless, telling himself *if only there were more hours in the day.*

Dave's argument is that scarcity is a perspective rather than a fact, and he takes time and money separately. **Time**: the shortage is manufactured by imagining a future where you need to do more than is possible — you will never do more than you can in a given period, and presence dissolves the arithmetic. **Money**: your company had zero before you started it and your resourcefulness produced the capital, so the constraint is usually creativity, conviction, or willingness to ask.

The sharpest claim is the loop: the perception of scarcity puts the system into threat, which drains energy and blocks creative flow — so believing you lack resources makes you less able to generate them. Founders who drop it report having more time and getting more done.

He closes on delegation, and locates the block emotionally rather than procedurally: self-reliance learned early becomes maladaptive at scale, and founders back away the moment delegating feels scary. Welcoming that fear is the gate.
