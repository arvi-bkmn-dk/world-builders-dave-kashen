# Stop Managing Adults, Start Developing Humans

## Summary

A founder who'd built a successful company, raised millions and hired well, exhausted: *"It feels like I'm babysitting adults."*

Dave's recognition is that he was that founder. "I didn't realize how much of my leadership was still shaped by my own childhood patterns — avoiding conflict like I had as a kid, seeking approval from my board like they were my parents, trying to prove my worth through performance." And the conclusion: **if I didn't grow up, neither could they.**

The stake he sets: *your manager has a greater impact on your health than your doctor.* Which makes leadership a matter of shaping people's mental and emotional health for years, not just influencing KPIs.

The reframe is from driving to gardening — you can't force human development, only create conditions for it, and Lao Tzu supplies the line: *nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished.*

The practical content is the second appearance of the **C.O.R.E.** framework — curiosity over certainty, openness over withholding, responsibility over victimhood, empathy over judgment — with the leadership version of each, including replacing *"why did you do that?"* with *"I'm curious, what was going on for you when you made that choice?"*
