# The Messy Middle

## Summary

A story about a session Dave thought he had failed.

A CEO asked him to facilitate between two executives whose dysfunction was affecting the whole organisation. Scheduled for an hour, it ran two and a half. Accusations, raised voices, no resolution. "Twenty minutes in, I was questioning my approach. An hour in, I was wondering if I was making things worse." He left thinking he hadn't helped.

A week later the CEO called: they were working together better than they had in months. "It seems like they finally felt heard. Even though they were both taken aback at first hearing just how upset the other was, that honesty created space for actual understanding."

Four lessons follow — transformation rarely looks like transformation while it's happening; increased discomfort often precedes breakthrough; we rush past discomfort but it's where growth happens; and the messiest conversations yield the deepest connections, because superficial harmony maintains the status quo.

The sharpest implication is about judgment in the moment: Dave's own real-time read was wrong at twenty minutes, at an hour, and at two — and he was the expert in the room.
