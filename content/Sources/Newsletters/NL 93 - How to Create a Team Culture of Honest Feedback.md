# How to Create a Team Culture of Honest Feedback

## Summary

Someone on your team is probably wondering where they stand with you. They're not asking, you're not telling, and because we're wired for threat they assume they're below the bar. When leaders tell people where they stand, feedback stops being a loaded event and becomes something the team does constantly, in every direction.

**What feedback is for.** Performance (Andy Grove: feedback is one of a manager's highest-leverage activities, an investment that pays out in all future behavior) and connection: sharing what you've withheld brings you closer. Candor, per the Conscious Leadership Group, is honesty, openness and self-awareness.

**Four practices.** Point it forward (Marshall Goldsmith's feedforward). Use sentence stems as training wheels, with a running document per direct report. Tell people where they stand with a quarterly number, including the hard six-out-of-ten conversation, which is kind. Take it to the team with live hot-seat rounds.

**Three ways to start.** Ask permission first. Match the medium to the intensity. Flip the number upward: ask your reports to rate you as a manager.

## Key Teachings

- [[Feedback is an investment that pays out in all future behavior]]
- [[Sharing what you have been withholding brings you closer, not further apart]]
- [[Without a number, people assume they are below the bar]]
- [[Asking permission lets feedback be invited instead of imposed]]
