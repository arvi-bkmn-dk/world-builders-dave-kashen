# Do You Doubt Yourself for Experiencing Doubt

## Summary

Dave reports that **every** hyper-successful founder he's worked with has eventually said the same thing behind closed doors: *"I just don't know if I have what it takes"* — and more often than they'd admit.

The damage isn't the doubt, it's the meaning attached to it. Founders assume Musk, Altman and Chesky felt certain, so doubt becomes proof of difference: *I must not be like them.* Which produces the second-order trap — **doubting yourself for having doubt.**

The useful distinction is between **judgment of identity** (*maybe I'm not good enough*, binary, attacks worth) and **functional discernment** (*how could I be better? where are my opportunities?*, treats challenges as workable information).

His test for which one you're in is badminton: nobody takes *you're not great at badminton* personally, because it isn't load-bearing for identity. The sharper a critique lands, the more of your worth is resting on that domain.

Three-step shift, beginning with welcoming the doubt without judgment: *"of course I'm experiencing doubt."*
