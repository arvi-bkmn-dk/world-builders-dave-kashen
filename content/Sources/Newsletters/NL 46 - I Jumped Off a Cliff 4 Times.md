# I Jumped Off a Cliff 4 Times

## Summary

Dave at the edge of a 300-foot canyon in Moab, at an Art of Accomplishment retreat called **Welcoming Fear**, with an intention set beforehand: *how much can I take pleasure in the physical sensations of fear?*

> As I intentionally welcomed the sensations and breathed deeply into them, I couldn't really distinguish between feeling fear and aliveness.

The conceptual core: **fear lives at the edge between the known and the unknown** — and since every want and every fear bottoms out in a feeling, the unknown is a **part of yourself**. Joy is familiar; shame is so unfamiliar it's scary. Fear therefore points at exactly the material shadow work is for.

The Joe Hudson quote he's sitting with: *"empowerment is being fully authentic regardless of the consequences"* — and his own corollary, **your edge is where you let fear stop you from being authentic.**

An explicit correction of a client misunderstanding: fear is not below-the-line, not a problem. *"You can be fully present and scared at the same time. By definition, you can't be courageous if you're not scared."*

Then the mechanics from the jumps themselves. The first attempts were forced — superman off the cliff, arms out — and his hand grabbed the rope instinctively, a **Window of Tolerance** contraction. The fourth time he simply enjoyed it, held the rope, and **let go without deciding to.** *"When I allowed my nervous system to have its safety, it felt okay with letting go."*
