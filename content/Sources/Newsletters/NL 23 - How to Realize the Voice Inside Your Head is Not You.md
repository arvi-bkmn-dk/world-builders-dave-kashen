# How to Realize the Voice Inside Your Head is Not You

## Summary

The written companion to [[HoE 07 - You Are Not Your Inner Voice]], and it supplies his work's best image for non-identification.

You're in a roadside diner. A dishevelled stranger sits in the booth behind you and mutters: *you're worthless, you don't deserve to be where you are, you'll never be enough, nobody actually likes you.* You think *poor guy* and keep eating.

> Now imagine instead, you decide to listen intently, tell yourself he's talking to you, and believe he's right. Wouldn't that be insane? Yet every day, many of us do exactly this with our inner voice — and we call it "thinking."

Identical sentences; only the assumed source differs, and that assumption does all the work.

From there it runs the talker/listener distinction, locates the voice's origin in parental figures ("outdated software running in the background"), and lands on a redefinition of confidence: not something to gain, but something already present once you stop granting the voice authority.

## Key Teachings

- [[Your inner voice has no more authority than a stranger muttering in a diner]]
- [[Confidence is not gaining something you do not have, it is letting go of what is in the way]]
