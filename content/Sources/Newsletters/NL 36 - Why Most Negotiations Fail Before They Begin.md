# Why Most Negotiations Fail Before They Begin

## Summary

Dave's move when a client brings him a conflict is to decline to help solve it. He listens, then says two words: **"Prove it."**

They scramble — the numbers, the work they did, the value the other person didn't create — and what surfaces is the finding: *their justifications are the exact thing preventing them from finding a solution.* When you're focused on justifying why you're right, you can't uncover the real underlying issues.

The core claim: **fairness is always a story.** Two people arguing about whose story is fairer are two people trying to be right, not two people building a resolution.

He credits Harvard's interest-based negotiation — positions versus interests — and then names its gap: before you can get curious about their interests, you have to get curious about **your own stories**, because a nervous system in threat mode cannot access curiosity at all. It scans for confirming evidence instead.

The framework is five steps for conscious conflict resolution, opening with a commitment to **win-for-all** and a senior intention to improve the relationship rather than win the exchange. Credited largely to the Conscious Leadership Group.
