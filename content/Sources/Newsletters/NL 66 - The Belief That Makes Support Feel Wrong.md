# The Belief That Makes Support Feel Wrong

## Summary

On a family holiday at one of the most beautiful beaches he'd seen, Dave **couldn't access joy.** Fifteen years of inner work, and a ceiling.

**The theory he was testing.** Depression for most of his life, much lighter after inner work, but still going gray every few weeks. He resisted medication for fear of what it said about him as a coach. His wife: *if your liver wasn't working, you'd take medicine.* A light dose worked. After Joe Hudson's Groundbreakers he theorised that welcoming sadness more fully would let him come off. Six months later, the beach, and the theory failed.

**Two paths, not one.** A coach he respects: once it's a biochemical loop, it takes a biochemical intervention. Ram Dass never lost a neurosis; they became Shmoos he invites to tea. Inner work can shift patterns, but **most reliably changes your relationship to them.** Dave had been asking one path to do the work of two.

**Why not use all the tools.** A one-day version of Stanford's SAINT protocol for TMS, via Don Vaughn of Ampa Health. By mid-January the floor was higher and joy consistent. Learning that Adyashanti takes SSRIs broke the rule that needing support means the inner work isn't working.

**The bigger question.** Strip away what you make support mean, and it becomes available. **Where in your life would that give you permission to let support in?**

## Key Teachings

- [[Needing support does not mean the inner work is not working]]
- [[Inner work most reliably changes your relationship to a pattern, not the pattern itself]]
- [[Strip away what you make it mean and support becomes available]]
