# Why We Resist Joy

## Summary

The claim Dave calls one of the most surprising things he's found coaching founders: **people are more afraid of joy than they are of fear, sadness, or pain.**

The mechanism is familiarity. "The conditions we learn to survive become the conditions upon which our continued survival depends" — so if you've spent years in stress, striving and self-criticism, that is what registers as safe, and joy, which requires openness and surrender, registers as threat.

He catalogues the deflections, and they all sound like judgment rather than avoidance: downplaying wins, avoiding celebration, deflecting appreciation, scanning for problems when things go well, feeling restless on vacation.

Underneath is **the conditional mind**, which won't grant permission for joy until an arbitrary standard is met — and then moves the standard. *I'll feel proud at $10M ARR. I'll be at peace once every issue is fixed.* Which is why high achievers can feel empty: they've trained themselves to postpone joy indefinitely. The fix is the do-have-be inversion, and Dave's argument for inherent worth is the newborn — worthy having accomplished nothing but pooping and crying.

It closes with a three-part practice: notice the resistance in real time, build joy tolerance in 30-second increments, and shift from striving to allowing.
