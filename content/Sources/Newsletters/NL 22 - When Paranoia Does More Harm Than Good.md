# When Paranoia Does More Harm Than Good

## Summary

Takes on Andy Grove's *"only the paranoid survive"* — carefully, by separating what Grove meant (organisational vigilance toward market shifts) from what it became (perpetual anxiety as a prerequisite for success).

The distinction Dave draws: **paranoia isn't the same as clear-eyed awareness.** Both present as vigilance; one perceives, the other fabricates. And the cost list includes the line that matters most — fear-based decisions "create exactly what they're afraid of."

He traces it to overidentification with the intellectual self: "most of us think of ourselves as our minds, treating our bodies like vehicles to transport our heads around."

The remedy is the **Quadrinity Check-In**, from the Hoffman Institute — four selves (intellectual, physical, emotional, spiritual), worked in an order that deliberately puts the loudest one last. Its most unusual prompt: *how old does your emotional self feel in this moment?*

The case is a client hit by a negative piece in a major tech blog who, within hours, had imagined investors pulling out, hires rescinding, customers fleeing.
