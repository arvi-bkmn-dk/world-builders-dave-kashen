# How to Care Deeply Without Attachment

## Summary

A founder asks whether to wait until fear has carried him to his exit before doing the inner work — *because fear worked.*

Dave names the real fear underneath: founders "are scared of not being scared and having nothing to motivate them." That they'll slide into apathy, and apathy will kill the company.

The distinction: **apathy is not caring; non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome.** Caring was learned through attachment — praise for the A, worth from the revenue spike — so identity got built around outcomes. Dirty fuel works, "but it's toxic. I've had clients in the ER for heart issues and panic attacks."

Then only two ways to be non-attached: **don't care at all** (no heartbreak, no aliveness), or **care deeply and be willing to have your heart broken.** Most choose the first, "but that protection costs you everything."

The pragmatic argument is energy. Attachment burns it on the gap, on imagined failure, and on resisting reality — illustrated by the desperate fundraise nobody signed versus the meeting where you were fine walking away.

And the part usually left out: a **scary neutral period** between the two fuels, with no push and no pull, where most founders sprint back to the anxiety they know. Stay, and authentic creative impulses arise — which "tend to be pro-social."

The alternative to waiting: cross now, and "you'll probably build something bigger because you're not wasting half your energy on resistance."
