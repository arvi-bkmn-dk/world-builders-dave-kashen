# A Counterintuitive Way to Solve Problems

## Summary

Instead they asked open-ended questions, and **he wasn't allowed to answer.** He just listened.

Dave's case against the default response is the useful part: jumping in with advice assumes the person needs more **information**, when what they usually need is more **awareness**. And most advice "is just recycling thoughts they've already had." He adds the cost nobody accounts for — trying to fix someone "reinforces the idea that there's something wrong with them; that they are a victim and need your help."

The framework is his own: **CORE** — Curiosity instead of being right, Openness instead of withholding, Responsibility instead of victimhood, Empathy instead of judgment.

## Key Teachings

## Notable Quotes

> We think we're being helpful when we jump in with solutions, but most of the time we're just recycling thoughts they've already had.

> That approach assumes they need more information. But most of the time, what we actually need is more awareness.

> Trying to fix reinforces the idea that there's something wrong with them or their life; that they are a victim and need your help.

> Many times, we already know what we want to do deep down. But what we lack is the clarity or courage to act on it.
