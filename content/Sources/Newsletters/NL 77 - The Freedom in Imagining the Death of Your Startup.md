# The Freedom in Imagining the Death of Your Startup

## Summary

A founder wound down his company, dreading his investors. They were supportive and asked to back what he did next. His lesson: **"No one has judged me nearly as much as I've judged myself."** Dave's point is that you don't have to fail to learn it.

**Why the boogeyman needs the dark.** Carried every day, the fear of failure runs as rumination. Facing it is a choice you make once, with full attention. Left vague, facts and stories fuse into one enormous dread. Looked at closely, the facts are manageable and nearly all the fear lives in the stories, and fear makes bad predictions: 99.999% of those stories turn out untrue.

**How to turn on the lights.** Imagine the startup completely failing, notice the stories, pull each fear into specifics, and ask how the opposite of the biggest stories might be true, welcoming the sensations throughout.

**What's on the other side.** The founder felt more relaxed and creative than in years. Welcomed fully, fear matures into how much you care, often gratitude: *I get to do this.* Keep playing to win, with the lights on.
