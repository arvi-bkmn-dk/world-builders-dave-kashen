# - The Impact of Consciousness on the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict

## Summary

Dave's most exposed piece: applying conscious leadership to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict after October 7th. It opens with his own history (a Reform Jewish upbringing, divorced parents, "Jew-ish") and the fear of publishing at all. "Don't be such a coward," says the other part of his mind.

The frame is **Above and Below the Line** (credited to the Conscious Leadership Group) and Stephen Karpman's **Drama Triangle**. Taking responsibility is not self-blame, not blaming victims, and not denying power imbalances. Dave then runs **four steps** on himself in real time:
1. **Awareness:** he is below the line.
2. **Acceptance:** of his fear, mapped to safety, approval and control.
3. **Willingness:** including the hardest question, letting go of being right that innocent people shouldn't be killed.
4. **Shift.**

Along the way he names **other-ing**, practises **"How am I just like them?"** on a Hamas terrorist and on Netanyahu, and contrasts the Treaty of Versailles with the Marshall Plan. He closes with *what's mine to do?* and three concrete actions chosen by a **whole body yes**.

## Key Teachings

- [[Believing something should not be happening makes you feel righteous without changing it]]
- [[Seeing people as other is what makes violence feel inevitable]]
- [[Blame and vengeance recreate the conflict, investing in a shared future ends it]]
- [[What's mine to do is the question that replaces obligation with choice]]
