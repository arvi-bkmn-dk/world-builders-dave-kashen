# Fight is villain, flight is hero, freeze is victim

Dave maps the drama triangle onto the threat response, live in the conversation:

> I was just mapping it in my mind to fight, flight, freeze. Fight is villain, bully. Flight is hero — we want to run away from feeling the thing, so we save the day. And then freeze as victim, feel stuck.

Which reframes all three as **survival patterning** rather than character flaws. Nobody chooses the corner they land in; it's the nervous system picking a strategy under threat.

The counterintuitive assignment is hero as *flight*. Rescuing looks like the most engaged of the three — the person running toward the problem. Dave's reading is that it's running *away from a feeling*, and the activity is the escape route. It fits what he says the savior's real motive is: not wanting anyone to feel bad, because then he'd have to feel something too.

This is also why Dave insists the three are adaptive rather than defective:

> All these roles are adaptive. It is not a problem. It's just — are we conscious about them or not?

The same claim as [[Either you play your persona or it plays you]], arrived at from biology instead of from Conscious Leadership.
