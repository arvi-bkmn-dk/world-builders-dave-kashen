# Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship

Dave's most practically consequential claim about a single emotion, and it explains an enormous amount of stuck behaviour.

> A lot of people are in relationships that are not really right for them, in jobs that are not right for them, running businesses that are not right for them, doing projects that no longer make sense. A lot of people are way too busy — all because they don't want to feel the sadness and grief of letting go of something.

The busyness is the tell. It looks like commitment and functions as avoidance, because staying is what prevents the grief that leaving would require.

The mechanism is clarity, not courage:

> If you're not willing to feel something, you won't be as clear about what you really want.

You can't decide well about something whose exit is emotionally forbidden, because the option was removed before the deliberation started. The sequence Dave wants is: feel it first, then ask. *I let myself feel it. Okay — now what do I really want?*

Jesse's Red Ventures deal is the worked example. Disappointed by the valuations, he was sad and didn't feel it — "I didn't go, let me cry about the fact that I thought I was going to get 100 million and now I'm getting 60." Instead: *I'm going to hero this*, call Rick back, structure a different deal. The contract is transparently shaped by the unfelt feeling — an investment plus an option to buy the business for exactly the number he'd fixed on.

> It's so transparent what my sadness was trying to accomplish.

Dave's frame is deliberately not about outcomes: "my frame is, what's authentic to you?" It worked out. It was still a decision made by an unfelt emotion. → [[Grieving the future you expected is what frees you to create one]]
