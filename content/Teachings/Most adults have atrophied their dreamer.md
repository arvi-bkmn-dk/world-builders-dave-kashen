# Most adults have atrophied their dreamer

Before you can have a desired future state, you have to be able to desire — and Dave's observation is that the capacity is not missing but wasted from disuse.

> Most people have like atrophied their dreamer. When you're a kid, you just dream all the time. I had a dream, I was like, I'm gonna be a toy entrepreneur and make toys, 'cause toys are so fun and happy. By the time we get to even like twenties, certainly thirties, most of us have stopped dreaming.

Atrophy is the right word and a deliberate one. The muscle was not removed; it stopped being used because using it was discouraged — "life or adults or people, enough times have said, well, you can't have that."

The cost lands before any attempt is made:

> I think we sort of resigned ourselves before we've even tried and limited ourselves of what's possible.

This is why "what do you want?" so often returns nothing. It is not that the person lacks an answer, it is that the faculty that produces answers has not been exercised in a decade — and in the vacuum, someone else's game gets substituted for one's own.

Dave's move with clients is to lift the constraint explicitly before asking: "if you let go of all limiting beliefs, what do you really want to do?" What he reports is telling — "often they know, but they didn't even let themselves consider that as a possibility." The answer was present the whole time and pre-filtered as impossible.
