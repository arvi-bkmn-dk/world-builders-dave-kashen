# 90% of the time the issues my clients bring are literally not real

Dave's count, offered plainly, on what founders actually bring to coaching.

> 90% of the time, the issues my clients bring are literally not real. They're not occurring. They're just feeling fear and they're imagining some issue in the future. What if we run out of money? What if this person leaves? What if we don't close these sales? Well — is that actually happening now? No.

The reclassification follows:

> They don't want to feel the fear. So instead of feeling the fear, they're going to their mind and trying to figure out how to solve this theoretical future problem, and control life so they don't have to feel it.

Which makes the presented problem a **symptom of avoidance**, not a problem. Working it — however rigorously — cannot resolve it, because the thing generating it is a present sensation rather than a future event. That's why a solved hypothetical is immediately replaced by a new one.

This is the same claim as [[Entrepreneurs spend an inordinate amount of time imagining future issues that never happen]], here with a number attached and arriving through *feel over figure*. Dave's alternative is short: "it is a superpower if you learn to just feel your feelings and expand your capacity to feel."
