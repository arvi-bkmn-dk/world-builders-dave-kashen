# Love is unconditional by nature, so the journey is from conditional to unconditional living

Dave's fullest statement of the idea he gestured at in episode 1.

> Love is unconditional by its nature. There really isn't conditional love — that's manipulation, that's something else. And I think we are love at our essence.

He's careful with the epistemic status — "that's not capital-T true, but those words have meaning for me" — and then makes the practical version available even to someone who doesn't buy the metaphysics:

> Even if you don't feel that or believe that: if we want to be loving — which most people do — the journey is from living conditionally to living unconditionally.

**What makes us conditional is the mind:**

> Our mind is very conditioned. Our mind says that's good, that's bad. So if we listen to our mind all the time, we're living conditionally. *I can be happy when this happens, but not when this happens* — those are all the conditions.

Which ties the spiritual claim directly back to [[The Talker and the Listener]]. Listening to the talker *is* living conditionally, because sorting into good and bad is the talker's entire function.

> Meditation allows us to release all those conditions the mind puts on life for what has to be true to feel loving and peaceful and joyful. In some way, that's enlightenment — releasing all the conditions, so I can accept and allow everything to be exactly as it is.

Note that this is the same destination as [[Acceptance is the more precise word for bringing in light]] and [[The Gradation of Allowing]], reached from the other end. Unconditional toward circumstances, toward other people, and toward your own feelings turn out to be one capacity.
