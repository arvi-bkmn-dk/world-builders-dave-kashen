# Entrepreneurs spend an inordinate amount of time imagining future issues that never happen

Dave's account of where founder time actually goes, and what it is really doing.

> Avoidance of fear tends to distort our perception of reality. One way we avoid fear is we go into our minds and imagine all these future things that might happen. That's the clearest example of distortion of reality.

> Most entrepreneurs I know spend an inordinate amount of time imagining future issues that might happen and trying to solve them — which is a crazy way to use your time, because you're not addressing the issues that are actually happening. And most of those issues never happen anyway.

Two separate costs. The imagined problems consume the hours, and the real ones go unattended in the meantime.

The reclassification is the teaching:

> Just to be clear, that's all actually a symptom of avoiding feeling the fear.

Pre-solving future problems presents as diligence — the conscientious founder thinking ahead. Dave's read is that it's an escape into the head from a present sensation, the same move as [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]. The tell is that it doesn't resolve: a fear discharged by planning regenerates a new scenario, because planning was never addressing the fear.

It connects to his standing claim that "the team that sees reality the clearest wins." A mind occupied with hypothetical futures is not looking at the present, which is the only place the real issues are.
