# Shame puts the brakes on all the other emotions

The third of Joe Hudson's observations on shame, and the one that connects it to everything else in his work.

> Shame is essentially a way to put the brakes on all the other emotions. When there's something that occurs that is just too intense to feel — especially as a kid — shame will come up so that we don't have to feel it, and we suppress those other feelings.

So shame is not simply one unpleasant emotion among others. It is a **stopping mechanism**, and it arrives precisely when something else is too much.

Which gives it an original protective function. A child overwhelmed by grief or rage has no way to process it; shame shuts the flow down and makes the situation survivable. The cost lands later, when the mechanism keeps firing.

> When we feel shame, we just block the flow of emotion, and it creates this buildup of energy.

Dave links it straight to emotional fluidity — "how important and powerful emotional fluidity is to our ability to thrive and be fully present and energised and clear." Shame is the specific thing that stops the pipe. → [[All emotions run through the same pipe and joy is the matriarch]]

This is also why welcoming shame releases more than shame. In Dave's ice-cream example, underneath it was "this angry little kid — but I just want what I want", and feeling *that* was what dissolved the compulsion. The shame had been holding the frustration down. → [[The behavior is often just a way to avoid feeling some feeling]]
