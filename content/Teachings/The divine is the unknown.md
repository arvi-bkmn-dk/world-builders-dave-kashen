# The divine is the unknown

Dave's way through a word that stalls most conversations before they start.

> I had a teacher once who defined the divine as the unknown. And I just love the simplicity of that.

What it does is remove the need for agreement about metaphysics. Nobody has to sign up to anything:

> Can we just acknowledge that we don't know everything about this experience of life? So in some way, spirituality for me is the exploration of what's not yet known.

The definition also names the obstacle precisely, and it's a familiar one for high-performing people:

> So much of us, we want to feel certain. So we're constantly trying to know everything, and explore what we already know, and sound smart. Can we explore and be curious about what is unknown?

Certainty-seeking as the thing that forecloses the territory — which is the same mechanism as [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]], operating at the scale of a worldview rather than a decision.

It connects to Dave's preference for **mysticism over mythicism**: rather than believing stories someone wrote down, investigate your direct experience. "If I let go of believing all the things I've been believing, what do I directly experience?"

And to the observation that stops the inquiry from having a floor: *identity is itself a thought.* → [[If you are not your thoughts, the question of who you are opens]]
