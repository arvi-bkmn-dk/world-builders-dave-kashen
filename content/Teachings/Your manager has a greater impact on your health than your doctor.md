# Your manager has a greater impact on your health than your doctor

The line Dave uses to establish what a leadership role actually is.

> Your manager has a greater impact on your health than your doctor. Think about what that means.

> As a leader, you aren't just influencing KPIs and quarterly results. You're shaping the **mental and emotional health of your people — possibly for years to come.**

It reframes leadership from a performance function to something closer to a duty of care, and it raises the stakes on everything else in his work: a leader running on fear doesn't just underperform, they transmit it. → [[Your state is contagious to your team]]

The founder he opens with had built a successful company, raised millions, hired well — and was exhausted. *"It feels like I'm babysitting adults."*

Dave's recognition is the useful part, because he was that founder:

> I didn't realize how much of my leadership was still shaped by my own childhood patterns — avoiding conflict like I had as a kid, seeking approval from my board like they were my parents, trying to prove my worth through performance.

> My unresolved inner world wasn't just limiting me. **It was shaping the culture of my entire company. If I didn't grow up… neither could they.**

Which reframes the job: "effective leadership means helping your team grow up, not just pushing them toward a goal." Not control, not fixing — *creating the conditions* for people to understand themselves, on the grounds that "where there is understanding and compassion, judgment, conflict, and dysfunction can't survive."
