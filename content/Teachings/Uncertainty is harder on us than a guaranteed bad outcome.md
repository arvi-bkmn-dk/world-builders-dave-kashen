# Uncertainty is harder on us than a guaranteed bad outcome

Dave's explanation of why founders grasp at certainty: we are wired to.

> We're wired to crave certainty in an uncertain world.

> [!warning] Unsourced research claim
> Dave cites "a study I came across" without naming it. Participants got a mild electric shock for wrong answers, some 50% of the time, some 90%. Treat the finding as Dave's claim until the study is traced.

> The 50% group showed far more fear and stress than the 90% group. Even a guaranteed bad outcome stresses us less than not knowing.

What the mind does with the gap is where the trouble starts:

> Faced with that uncertainty, your mind fills it in with the things you don't want to have happen.

> Notice your mind rarely fills in "I probably got this right, and it's gonna be awesome."

Then comes fear, and the escape from fear into analysis. *If I can just be certain, the fear goes away.* But certainty about the future doesn't exist, so the best you can find is the illusion of it:

> And if you believe you're certain and you're wrong, you're now relating to a distorted version of reality.

> Startups are uncertainty machines.

If you don't see the pattern, Dave warns, you will spend the whole founder journey inside it, *"mistaking it for diligence."* → [[Fear makes bad predictions]]
