# Failing might be the thing that liberates you from your ego

Dave's conclusion from two of his own losses, and the reason he can ask clients the question without it being abstract.

The two experiences: his second startup nearly failing, which forced him to face *"the way I was associating my worthiness as a human being with my company's success"* — and losing **70% of his net worth** in six months, which finally dislodged an attachment to money he'd been working on for a decade.

That gap — between having understood something for years and having felt it once — is the whole argument. The belief that worth depends on the outcome cannot be disproved by reasoning, because reasoning never puts it at risk. Only the outcome failing does.

He is careful about what he is not claiming — this is offered as *"food for thought around your willingness,"* not as a recommendation to fail.

Dave's own case: losing nearly 70% of his net worth in 2022, the shame that followed, and then the visceral realisation that he was okay.

> The experience of losing money freed me. And that was more valuable than the millions I lost.
