# Your inner voice reports a dystopian version of a reality you are not in

Dave's first named benefit of meditation, and he opens it with a question that lands before the argument does.

> Notice that your inner voice doesn't tend to say things like: you're so amazing, Andrew. You look so perfect today. You just nailed that thing. I'm proud of you.

Andrew's reaction — *you know, you're right* — is the point. The bias is total and invisible, because a narrator that only ever reports problems reads as a narrator reporting accurately.

The calibration:

> You live in a reality that's, let's call it, level five. And the voice would have you think reality is minus five. So if you listen to your voice all the time, you're in a really distorted, dystopian version of reality that's not actually the reality you're in.

> **First benefit is to live in the actual reality you're in.**

Which is a much more concrete case than "feel calmer." It's an accuracy argument: you are receiving a systematically wrong report and making decisions on it.

Dave's explanation of the distortion is evolutionary:

> We're neurobiologically wired for a world that existed a thousand years ago, where lions, tigers and bears were constantly trying to kill us. The voice is a mechanism of survival in a world that was ten times more dangerous than the one we're in.

Not broken — miscalibrated for the present. → The narrating voice in your head is a survival mechanism, not your self
