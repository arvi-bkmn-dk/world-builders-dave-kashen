# If you want a Range Rover I want you to have a Range Rover

The line Jesse has carried for years, from a forum where a founder described a Range Rover as the moment he'd know he'd made it.

> If you want a Range Rover, I want you to have a Range Rover. But if you **need** a Range Rover to feel like you're— then you've gotten attached to that.

The distinction is want versus need, and Dave's general form:

> If you think your wellbeing somehow depends on this condition, then you're going to be attached to the condition.

This is the guard against the most common misreading of the whole framework. Sourcing security internally does not mean wanting nothing, and Dave is explicit that he does not hold the Buddhist line that way:

> People sometimes over-interpret that as: I should have no wants or preferences or vision, I should just zen out all the time and have no intention or purpose. I don't hold it that way. I really love the both-and of — can I be at peace inside, not wishing anything was different, and still honour and follow my authentic preferences?

He also admits how fine the line is, using his own want to reach more people: *wouldn't it be nice if more people knew what was available to them* slips easily into *I'm not fulfilling my potential if I don't.*

> It's so easy to slip into — oh, it's not enough, or it means something.

Which is why the tests matter: [[An impulse feels lighter and a compulsion has constriction attached to it]] and [[Can you enjoy the wanting]].
