# Give up the fight

The most personal thing Dave has published, and the whole newsletter builds to four words from his own coach.

He had come off a seven-day retreat clear and in flow, watched the afterglow fade, and found himself irritable — "snapping at her or the kids, getting really frustrated when I made a small mistake. I felt wound up, coiled, like a snake ready to strike." He is candid about the history: mild periodic depression for most of his life, and more sadness and anxiety since than his circumstances would predict.

What he brought to the session was exasperation at his own effort:

> I've done so much work. Fifteen years of retreats, courses, journeys, meditation, and inner work. I've worked harder on myself than anyone I know. Why am I still struggling with these feelings?

> I'm so tired of the struggle.

Her answer:

> **Give up the fight.**

> They hit me like a tidal wave, and I immediately broke into tears. A dam inside me burst, and with it, the weight I'd been carrying for so long.

The turn is that the *work itself* had become the fight. Fifteen years of effort aimed at overcoming anxiety was still a war against his own experience — and being at war was the thing producing the exhaustion, not the anxiety.

Which makes it the personal proof of the principle he teaches: what you resist persists, and the resistance here was dressed as self-improvement. → [[So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle]] · [[Nature grows and we are part of nature]]
