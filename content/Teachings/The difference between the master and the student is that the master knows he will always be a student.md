# The difference between the master and the student is that the master knows he will always be a student

From Steve Hardison, a coach Dave worked with, and the line that frames why an episode of this podcast is Dave reporting from someone else's classroom.

> He said to me once, the difference between the master and the student is that the master knows he'll always be a student.

Dave describes himself as "a perpetual student" and backs it with spend — a stated goal of putting a million dollars into his own development over his lifetime, itself copied from Diana Chapman. His own account of what earns him the right to work: "what gives me the right to coach another human being is to be constantly doing my own inner work."

Jesse's addition is the observation that removes the stigma: the people he sees growing most are "actually people who are doing this stuff all the time."

Dave also offers a small correction worth keeping. Asked whether he has problems, he objects to the word — not out of denial but definition:

> I don't really think that anyone has problems, in the sense that we typically think of problems as: there's something about reality that isn't the way it should be. I certainly have issues and challenges and patterns — and I intend to not see them as problems, but just something to learn from and grow from.
