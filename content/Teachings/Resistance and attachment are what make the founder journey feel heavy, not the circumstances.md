# Resistance and attachment are what make the founder journey feel heavy, not the circumstances

Dave reduces nearly all founder suffering to two unconscious patterns, and neither of them is the situation itself.

**Resistance to the present situation** — "this shouldn't be happening," "we should be further ahead," "if only X hadn't happened," "this is a problem." **Attachment to a specific future** — "this must work," "I can't fail," "this needs to happen on my timeline," "if this doesn't happen, something is wrong with me."

> Resistance and attachment = suffering. This dynamic, not the circumstance, is what makes the founder journey feel heavy.

The two failure modes are symmetrical and they bracket the present moment on both sides: fighting what is, and gripping what isn't yet. Dave's words for the costs are precise — resistance means you "lose your power to respond creatively," attachment "creates unhealthy tension in the present moment."

What makes this more than a stoic platitude is the immediate objection he anticipates: founders fear that dropping either one drops their motivation with it. The rest of [[The Focus-Motivation Model]] exists to answer that — there is another way to generate tension that produces equal or better results "without the toxic residue."
