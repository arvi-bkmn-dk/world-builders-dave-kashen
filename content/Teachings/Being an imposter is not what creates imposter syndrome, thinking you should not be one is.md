# Being an imposter is not what creates imposter syndrome, thinking you should not be one is

Dave credits a Joe Hudson podcast on imposter syndrome, and calls the move brilliant:

> **If you're a high achiever, you're an imposter.** By definition — you're doing things you don't know how to do. **If you always do things you already know how to do, you're not a high achiever.**

> So **being an imposter is not what creates imposter syndrome. Thinking you shouldn't be an imposter is what creates imposter syndrome.**

The relocation is exact: the painful part was never the gap in competence, it was the belief that the gap is illegitimate. Close the gap and a high achiever simply opens a new one — the condition is permanent, so any relief that depends on eliminating it is unavailable.

Dave sets it beside self-doubt, and the sample size matters:

> I've coached the most incredible, impressive, successful founders and CEOs in the world, and **I've yet to meet a human being who doesn't experience self-doubt.** This idea that *I'm the only one who has self-doubt* is so **tragic.**

He catches the same pattern in himself, in the chair: *"my mind will be like — oh, what would **they** say? If only I knew what **Joe** or **Diana** would say right now, they would create a breakthrough for this client."* Which, he notes, *"obviously gets in the way of me being present."*

> This little pattern of **I should know something that I don't know** is such a pervasive problem for founders and really any high achievers.

And what it costs operationally: *"that sense of **I need to know the answer** actually gets in the way of — no, you just need to **harness your team's collective wisdom.**"*

## Restated on the content call

Dave credits the formulation to Joe Hudson's podcast and puts the logic as a definition rather than a consolation:

> if you're a high achiever, you are an imposter. By definition, if you have any kind of ambition, that means you're doing things today that you haven't done before, which in some ways is like being an imposter. Like, I don't know what I'm doing, but I'm doing it.
