# You cannot get there until you are here

> The cliché thing I sometimes say is: you can't get there until you're here.

Holding onto the past and holding onto stories keeps you somewhere other than where you actually are, and every move forward has to start from your actual position.

Jesse's version is from tennis and is the more visceral one: you can't play the next point if you're still beating yourself up over the shot you just missed. The missed shot isn't costing you anything any more — the rumination is costing you the point in front of you.

What he reports after running [[The Facts and Stories Exercise]] is the mechanism in action. Having spent six or seven years making himself wrong for what had happened, he reached neutrality — "what happened, happened, it was what it was" — and the effect was not relief but *energy*:

> As soon as I got to that place, I felt so much more excited and empowered to go build the future — not that I *had* to go build that future because of the shitty stuff.

Note the distinction in the last clause. He was already building toward the future beforehand; the change was in the fuel. → [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]]
