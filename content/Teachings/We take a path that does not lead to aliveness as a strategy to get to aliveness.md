# We take a path that does not lead to aliveness as a strategy to get to aliveness

The central irony of the episode, and Dave names it as the biggest anti-pattern.

The chain starts with a feeling you won't feel:

> Instead of following the path that is authentic for us, we do something that's more designed to **survive.** A version of that is: *oh, to feel safe I need to have more status.* Or *to feel worthy, I need to succeed.*

> So we focus on success and accomplishment **as a way to feel safe and whole and worthy**, instead of following aliveness directly.

And then the loop closes on itself:

> The irony is — if you go on the other side of fear, and ask what is it you really want, and ask that enough times, usually it's some version of **aliveness.** *I want to feel peace and joy and freedom.* I want to feel fully alive.

> **So we end up taking a path that doesn't actually lead to aliveness, as a strategy to get to aliveness.**

The substitution is invisible from inside because each step is locally reasonable. Status really does produce safety-feelings, briefly. Achievement really does quiet unworthiness, briefly. The strategy works just well enough to keep being chosen, and never well enough to arrive.

Dave locates the origin early: *"all human beings have survival patterns that we learned mostly in childhood, before eight years old,"* which then *"show up when we're leading a team and having sales meetings and fundraising meetings."* Their function is fixed — *"the patterns are designed to avoid feeling feelings that were too intense and overwhelming when we were kids."*
