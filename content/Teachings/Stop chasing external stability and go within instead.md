# Stop chasing external stability and go within instead

Dave's answer to founders describing a period of compounding chaos — startup volatility, policy shifts, markets, AI — and it starts by refusing the premise that it will pass.

> I'm not willing to bet that this chaos will slow down and stabilize in our lifetime. Because, just as we navigate business challenges, we're simultaneously dealing with aging parents, relationship issues, health crises — and the growing realization that **there's no finish line where challenges simply stop happening.**

Which makes the usual strategy a losing one:

> When everything feels overwhelming, our instinct is often to do more — work harder, hustle more, try to catch up. Your mind says, "if you run fast enough, you'll eventually get ahead of the chaos." **But this approach hasn't worked yet, has it?**

> The stability you're seeking isn't out there — it's inside you.

His own worked example is small and concrete, which is what makes it usable. Overwhelmed and nervous about an upcoming group coaching call, with a free hour:

> My mind urged me to fill my free hour with more planning and preparing. Instead, I chose a 40-minute meditation. That counterintuitive choice is precisely what allowed me to show up with presence.

The practices he gives: **find your center point** — a still point in the lower belly, the centre of the chest, or the space between thoughts, which "remains constant regardless of external circumstances." And **lead from stillness**:

> True leadership isn't about having all the answers or projecting false confidence. Your team, investors, and customers don't need you to pretend everything is fine — they need you to remain centered when it isn't, so you can creatively respond.
