# Meditation is practice at not reacting to your thoughts

Dave's recommendation is modest — *"the practice of detaching from your thoughts and observing your thoughts is a useful practice"* — but Jesse supplies the reframe that made it work for him, and it's the more useful half.

The failure mode first:

> It's not like everyone thinks — that you're supposed to **not have thoughts.** If you talk to most people: *I'm not a good meditator.* What does that mean? *Well, I have thoughts, and then I'm pissed off and frustrated, and I'm trying to not have thoughts and it's really hard.*

Dave's summary of that experience: **"sitting there trying not to think while your thoughts are happening."**

> What it, the reframe for me that was valuable, was: it's **practice not reacting to your thoughts.** So it's **building a muscle.**

That changes what counts as success. Under the first reading, every thought is a failure and a busy mind makes the session worthless. Under the second, a thought is the **repetition** — you cannot practise not-reacting without something to not react to. A noisy sit is a productive one.

It also makes the practice continuous with everything else in the episode. If [[If you think you are the storyteller you get trapped in the stories|believing the thoughts]] is the problem, then the skill being trained is not silence but **the gap between a thought arriving and you treating it as true.**
