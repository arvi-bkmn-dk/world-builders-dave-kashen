# It is not the doubt that is destroying you, it is your relationship to the doubt

Dave reports that **every** hyper-successful founder he's worked with has eventually confessed the same thing behind closed doors:

> "I just don't know if I have what it takes." Not just occasionally. But more often than they'd ever admit.

The damage comes from what the doubt is taken to mean:

> Founders think this doubt proves they're different from the most extraordinary entrepreneurs. Those titans must have felt confident all the time, right? So when doubt creeps in, your mind creates a devastating story: **"I must not be like them. I can't do what they did."**

> But it's not the doubt that's destroying you. It's **your relationship to the doubt.**

And then the second-order trap:

> When you make experiencing doubt mean something about your worthiness, you create the most vicious cycle imaginable: **you start doubting yourself for having doubt in the first place.**

The comparison is doing the damage, and it is built on a false premise — the confident-titan picture is an artefact of only seeing people in public. Dave's whole client roster contradicts it privately.

His three-step shift begins with the move his work applies everywhere: **welcome the doubt without judgment.** *"Of course I'm experiencing doubt."*

→ [[Welcome the self-doubt and you actually feel more confident]] · [[Avoiding helplessness keeps you helpless]]

The sorting tool is [[Judgment of identity versus functional discernment]] — not all doubt is the same, and the useful kind is worth keeping.
