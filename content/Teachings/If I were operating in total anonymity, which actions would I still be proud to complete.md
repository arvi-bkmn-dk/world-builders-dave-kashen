# If I were operating in total anonymity, which actions would I still be proud to complete

A filter for separating what you want from what you want to be seen wanting.

> Consider: "If I were operating in total anonymity, which actions or projects would I still be proud to complete?" **These are the moves you'd never regret making.**

Removing the audience removes approval as a motive, and what survives is the part that was yours. Dave calls the results **integrity actions**.

Why it belongs in a newsletter on staying motivated without attachment:

> You're no longer chasing approval. You're acting from a place of personal alignment, which feels far more satisfying and sustainable.

It is the same operation as the burden-of-proof question — *proving what, to whom?* — run forward instead of backward. That one dissolves an existing motive; this one selects a durable one. → [[The burden of proof is an illusion]]

Dave pairs it with support circles, and is careful about the distinction: share the commitments with trusted people so momentum survives, but without "the anxiety of performing." Accountability that doesn't reintroduce the audience you just removed.
