# Meeting your own fear with frustration keeps the pattern running

Jesse's parting observation to Andrew names a second-order move most people don't notice themselves making: you don't just have the fear, you get angry at yourself for having it. "Why am I like this? Why am I this way?"

The problem is that the frustration is made of the same material as the thing it's aimed at. Jesse reaches for the MLK line — darkness doesn't drive out darkness, only light can do that — and the practical translation is to stop working on the negative parts directly and instead bring more in. His honest report is that the patterns don't vanish; he still looks at other people's success and feels it. They get fainter, crowded out rather than removed.

His concrete redirect: not *what work do I need to do on this tomorrow*, but **what am I excited about instead**.

Dave then amends the metaphor, and the amendment is the important part — see [[Acceptance is the more precise word for bringing in light]]. Light-versus-dark smuggles back in the idea that some feelings are good and others bad, which is the original problem in new clothes.
