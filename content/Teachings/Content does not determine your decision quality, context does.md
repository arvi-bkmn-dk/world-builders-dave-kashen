# Content does not determine your decision quality, context does

Dave's reframe for high-stakes decisions, and it moves the whole problem.

> Most founders think high-stakes decision-making is about analyzing **content** — the data, valuations, market timing, and pros and cons.

> **Content doesn't determine your decision quality. Context does.**

> **Content** = the facts, circumstances, and data about your situation
> **Context** = the lens through which you view and interpret those facts

The setup is a founder at 2am with a $100m term sheet, 48 hours to decide, and a gut saying $150m. Seven years, 80-hour weeks, missed family dinners. Spreadsheet says take it; heart says hold. The mind ping-pongs between *what if this is the only offer* and *what if I'm leaving $50m on the table.*

More analysis will not settle that, because the analysis was never the problem:

> Whether you're facing an acquisition offer, considering firing a co-founder, deciding to pivot, or choosing between funding rounds — the decision isn't really about the external circumstances. It's about the **unconscious beliefs and emotional state** driving how you process the choice.

Which explains the paralysis Dave says he gets called about constantly: "unable to sleep, unable to think clearly when they need it most." Sharpening the content while the context is fear produces more precise fear.

Same claim as [[More thinking makes clarity worse, like shaking sand in a bottle]] and [[A choice only feels like a hard decision when you are scared of it]], with a vocabulary that makes it usable in a business conversation.
