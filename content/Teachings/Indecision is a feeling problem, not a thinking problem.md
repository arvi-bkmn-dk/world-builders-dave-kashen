# Indecision is a feeling problem, not a thinking problem

> Most leaders think indecision is a thinking problem. It's not. **It's a feeling problem.** You're waiting for certainty that will never come because you don't want to feel the fear of being wrong.

Which reframes what decisiveness actually is:

> Decisiveness isn't the absence of doubt. It's **feeling the doubt and deciding anyway.**

> The role of a leader is to decide in the face of uncertainty, which means deciding **in the face of fear.**

Dave grants that this holds even in trivial moments — choosing which exercise to run next at a retreat: "part of me wants certainty that it's the 'right' choice. But that certainty doesn't exist."

What helps, in his list:

- **Trust your intuition, not just your intellect.** "Instead of asking clients 'what do you think is the right answer?' I ask: 'what feels right to you?' Those are usually different answers. I've found the feeling is more reliable than the thinking." → [[Feel Over Figure]]
- **Remember hard decisions matter less than you think.** → [[Hard decisions are hard because either choice could be great]]
- **Recognize this is just an experiment.** "You're making a choice to see what happens. Most decisions aren't irreversible." → [[Run experiments so that the experiment fails instead of you]]
- **When you're divided, just choose.** "Flip a coin if you have to, then notice how you feel about the result. That's useful data."
