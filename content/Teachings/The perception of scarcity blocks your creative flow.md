# The perception of scarcity blocks your creative flow

The self-defeating loop in scarcity thinking: believing you lack resources makes you less able to generate them.

> The perception of scarcity actually blocks your creative flow. It puts your system into a state of threat, which drains your energy.

> This is why entrepreneurs who learn to let go of the scarcity tend to feel like they have so much more time and get so much more done. They have more creative energy available to apply to their goals.

Note what is being claimed: not that the belief is merely unpleasant, but that it **reduces output**. Threat narrows cognition and burns energy on vigilance, so the person most convinced they lack time is least equipped to use it.

Dave's reframe of the real constraint:

> We all have the same 24 hours in a day. The difference between those who feel overwhelmed and those who feel on top of things isn't the amount of time they have, but **how they channel their energy.**

> Many entrepreneurs scatter their attention, reacting to emails, putting out fires, and managing distractions. This divided focus perpetuates the feeling of scarcity.

His prescription is uncomfortable and specific: focus creative energy on high-leverage work and let the rest go — "sometimes this means making the short-term sacrifice of letting a few balls drop to prevent them from being dropped repeatedly in the future."
