# In the present moment can you find any lack of safety

The practice that resolves security inward, offered as a question rather than an argument.

> For you, for you listeners — can you find any lack of safety? If you couldn't leave the present moment, in this present moment, can you actually find any lack of safety?

Jesse: "No."

> And for most people realize, no, I can't.

Dave takes it to the extreme case to show it holds:

> Even if a lion were ten feet away from me, in this now moment I'm actually totally fine. It's just a minute from now, or a second from now, it might come and kill me.

Which isolates the mechanism:

> We have to imagine something happening in the future that's not happening now to feel that sense of *I'm not safe.*

So a lack of safety is not a perception of the present; it is a **construction about the future**, and it requires leaving the present to build. The feeling is real; the threat is generally somewhere you are not.

Dave notes this holds for all three needs — security, control and approval — which is why presence is the common route to all of them. → [[Security, Control and Approval]]

Same structure as [[Overwhelm cannot be found in the present moment]]: the state requires a mental operation to sustain, and stopping the operation stops the state.

## Also in [[GA 01 - The Inner Game of Life, Danny Miranda Podcast 394]]

Dave's most extreme test of it, on a psilocybin journey after eating raw ginger. His throat burned, he struggled to breathe, and he was as scared as he has ever been that he would die. What brought him back was a present-moment check:

> In this now moment, you're not dead.

> I can't actually find fear in the present moment unless I put my attention on the future and imagine something that I resist and don't want to have happened.

## Restated on the content call

> security is really a function of not being present. The mind lives in the past and the future, and so the mind creates a sense of I'm not safe

> a relatively simple antidote to that — simple, not easy — is get present and then see if you can actually find any lack of safety. Like in the present moment, you're safe.

He presses the point past the obvious objection:

> when is it not the present moment? Never, right? So in every present moment, you're safe.
