# You are not avoiding the conversation, you are avoiding how you will feel when they react

Dave's correction to the story founders tell themselves about the conversation they've been putting off for months.

> You're **not** avoiding the conversation because you don't want to hurt them. You're avoiding it because **you don't want to feel what you'll feel when they react.** The anger, the disappointment, the guilt.

> Your mind is protecting you from emotional discomfort by keeping you silent.

The reframe matters because the two explanations point at different solutions. *I don't want to hurt them* is a kindness problem, and there's no move available — every version of the conversation risks hurt. *I don't want to feel what I'll feel* is a capacity problem, and capacity can be built.

He generalises it past the other person's reaction:

> Maybe even afraid of just how you'll feel **during** the conversation — it's unfamiliar for most people and therefore uncomfortable.

So some of it isn't about their response at all. It's the unfamiliarity of being direct.

And the cost of the protection:

> When we let fear drive our choices, we end up **enabling the very behavior that's hurting us**, which creates far more suffering in the long run.

Which is the same structure as [[Feeling the fear that comes with delegating is the key to unlocking growth]] — the block is never at the decision, it's at the moment of discomfort, and an unwilling-to-feel founder quietly retreats. → [[Unexpressed boundaries turn into resentment]]
