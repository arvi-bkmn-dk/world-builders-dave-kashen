# You are at this level of success because you are experiencing this level of joy

Dave catches the misreading a listener would make of Jesse's story — he's happy now because he got out of the old business and made more money — and reverses it.

> That's the trap. People interpret it as: you're experiencing that joy because you're at this level of success. As opposed to: you're at this level of success because you're experiencing this level of joy.

This is the causal claim that makes the inner game an *investment* rather than a consolation. If state follows results, working on state is self-indulgence to be done after you've won. If results follow state, it is the highest-leverage work available. → [[The state you operate from is what determines your results]]

The counter-evidence to the ordinary reading is in Jesse's own history. He had achieved nearly everything on a PowerPoint he'd made with two college friends — millions of dollars, fifty people, his own terms — and was experiencing tension, fear and no contentment. Rick Elias read the list back to him bluntly: thirty-two years old, millions in the bank, nobody tells you what to do, two healthy children, married, parents alive.

> And I wasn't experiencing the joy of any of that stuff.

The results were fully delivered. The joy did not arrive with them, which is the disproof.
