# Founder Mode gives the fear of letting go a strategic name

The third of Dave's three discernment questions is the one that costs something to answer: *what am I avoiding?*

> Are you getting into the details because it genuinely serves the business? Or because **delegation feels scary**?

What he sees in practice, in founders who are "maxed out, stressed, and approaching burnout":

> "I keep stepping back in whenever something goes wrong. I try to handle it all. It's too much."

> The issue isn't that they need to delegate more hours to become better CEOs. It's that they're **protecting themselves from the fear of letting go.**

And the mechanism that makes it invisible:

> Founder Mode gives that protection a name. Makes it seem **strategic** instead of **scared**.

This is the general pattern applied to a specific vocabulary — a behaviour whose real function is avoiding a feeling, dressed as a strategy. → [[The behavior is often just a way to avoid feeling some feeling]] · [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]

The test isn't whether the involvement is defensible on paper. Anything can be defended on paper. It's whether the fear would still be there if you stepped back.
