# The body signals that you are at an edge before the mind does

Step 2 of [[The Edge Expansion Process]] is detection, and the detection is physical. Before you have a thought about avoiding something, the body has already reported.

**Physical**: constriction (often throat, chest, stomach), heart racing, sweaty palms, holding your breath.
**Behavioural**: distraction patterns — doomscrolling, checking email, drinking; prioritising the easy things you already know how to do; irritation; urgency; defensiveness; mental spinning.

The behavioural list is the more useful half, because those behaviours all present as something other than fear. Reorganising your task list toward work you're already good at feels like productivity. Urgency feels like drive. Defensiveness feels like being right. None announces itself as an edge.

Dave's instruction at the moment of detection is deliberately minimal:

> This is your cue. Not to judge yourself. But to realize: "I'm at an edge."

No analysis, no fixing — because judging the reaction is itself an avoidance move, and because recognition alone interrupts the default pattern. "Just recognizing the edge is the first step to interrupting the default fear pattern."
