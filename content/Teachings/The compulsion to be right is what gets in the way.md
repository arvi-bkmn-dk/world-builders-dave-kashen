# The compulsion to be right is what gets in the way

Having named suffering as choosing your mind's version of life over the actual one, Dave points at what keeps the choice locked in.

> Let's be honest, most of us get pretty righteous. We want to be right about our stories.

He borrows the marriage-counselling cliché and extends it: *would you rather be right, or have your marriage work? Would you rather be right, or have your life work?*

Andrew's response is the valuable part, because it's an admission most people don't make:

> I hear people say "do you want to be right or be happy" and I honestly didn't understand what it meant for many years. I was like, what do you mean? There's always a right and a wrong. I did not even understand the concept that you didn't have to be right about something — or that their rightness was also sort of a point of view.

That is the condition the teaching addresses. Inside it, "being right" isn't a preference you could set down; it's just accuracy, and setting it down would mean accepting falsehood.

Wanting to be right is also Dave's marker for being below the line — it is what a threatened state produces. → [[Presence or threat is the simple version of above and below the line]] · [[Where your heart is matters more than whether the language is technically unarguable]]
