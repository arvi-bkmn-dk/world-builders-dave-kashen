# That is not authentic is usually just not comfortable

Dave's response to the most respectable-sounding refusal in leadership coaching — *that's not me, it wouldn't be authentic.*

> A lot of clients will be like, oh no, that's not me, that's not authentic. I'm like — your team needs you to be the rah-rah cheerleader right now. And they're like, no no, that's not me, that's not authentic. I'm like: bullshit. It is just not comfortable.

The distinction he's drawing:

> There's this subtle distinction between true authenticity and just comfort.

Authenticity language gives a persona's boundary a principled name. The behaviour genuinely feels wrong — but the wrongness is unfamiliarity, not falseness.

Dave's own example is the one he had to break to do his job. His persona was the nice guy — "I thought that was just me, I'm just a nice guy" — and he had to disidentify from it to become the coach he wanted to be:

> Sometimes it's great to be loving and compassionate and the nurturer, and sometimes clients need a loving kick in the ass.

The test he applies is whether the impulse is actually present:

> To really serve people, sometimes I have to express anger that's arising, or some fierce challenger — which is not inauthentic. It actually is arising in me. It's just with my persona, it almost doesn't feel comfortable.

So the anger was real and the persona was the thing filtering it out. Suppressing a genuine impulse because it's off-brand is the *less* authentic move. → [[People hide behind authenticity when they mean comfort]]
