# Self-reliance learned early becomes maladaptive when you scale

> One of the most common ways founders limit their resourcefulness is by **trying to do everything themselves.**

> Many entrepreneurs learned, **typically at an early age, to be self-reliant and not rely on others.** This **adaptive strategy in childhood becomes maladaptive** when you're trying to scale a business.

The shape of this matters: it is not a flaw, it is an old solution still running. It worked — that is why it stayed. Which is why exhorting a founder to delegate rarely does anything; you are arguing with something that once kept them safe. → [[Personality is a defence structure built from the feelings you would not feel]] · [[Feeling the fear that comes with delegating is the key to unlocking growth]]

Dave then shows how it compounds into scarcity from three directions at once:

> When you limit the support you allow yourself to have while still wanting to get done more than what can be reasonably done, you **trap yourself in scarcity from multiple angles.** You spend your creative energy **doing work you could hand off**, you **reinforce the "not enough time" story**, and you **cut yourself off from the very resourcefulness** that would create a way forward.

Each of the three makes the other two worse, which is what turns a habit into a trap.
