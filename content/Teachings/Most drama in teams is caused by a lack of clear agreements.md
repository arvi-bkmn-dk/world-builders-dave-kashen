# Most drama in teams is caused by a lack of clear agreements

A structural diagnosis, and Dave states it as the single largest source:

> I've seen so many clients frustrate themselves by having **expectations without clear agreements.** In fact, most drama in teams and relationships is caused by a lack of clear agreements.

The failure is invisible because it sounds like alignment:

> Ending meetings with phrases like "let's get this done" or "sounds like we all agree" leaves huge gaps for confusion and no one with clear responsibility.

Everyone leaves satisfied and nobody has agreed to anything. The resentment that follows is then experienced as a people problem.

**An agreement has a who, a what and a when** — and, critically, someone actually saying yes:

> "Will you finalize the hiring plan by Friday at noon?" "Yes, I will."

That exchange is the whole difference. An expectation lives in one head; an agreement exists between two.

Dave pairs it with a **culture of integrity**, stated as percentages so that broken agreements are planned for rather than moralised:

> * 90% of the time, we do what we say we will
> * 9% of the time, we will renegotiate our agreements as soon as we know we won't deliver
> * 1% of the time, we will acknowledge our broken agreements, and take responsibility for the impact on others

The 9% is the useful part. Renegotiating *as soon as you know* is a named, expected move rather than a failure — which is what stops people hiding a slip until the deadline passes.
