# Fear and wonder meet the same uncertainty

Dave's framing of the only real choice available when you don't know what happens next.

> If you really look, there are **two primary ways** that we humans meet uncertainty: **fear and wonder.** And it's the same uncertainty.

The input is fixed; the response is not. Which is the same structure as [[Fear is excitement plus resistance]], here aimed at a business situation rather than a feeling.

The case is a client spiralling about a Series B. The data room had been live under twelve hours, two investors had opened it, and his mind was already at *maybe I should talk to the team about what happens if we don't raise, maybe I should pivot.* A knot in his stomach, "like everything's closing in."

Dave's intervention was one observation:

> You're trying to solve a problem that doesn't exist yet. The fundraise isn't failing — **you just don't know how it's going to unfold.**

The client's own read afterwards:

> "I've been treating this uncertainty like the worst is guaranteed to happen when I actually have no idea."

And Dave's summary of the mechanism:

> **The fear doesn't come from the uncertainty itself. It comes from your imagination of what could go wrong.** You could meet that same uncertainty with wonder instead.

Which relocates the problem from the situation to the imagination — and makes it addressable, because the future hasn't supplied any content yet. → [[It is not preparation when you are preemptively suffering]] · [[You cannot be curious and afraid at the same time]]
