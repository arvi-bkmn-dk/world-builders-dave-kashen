# Hearing a voice in your head means there is a talker and a listener

Dave's argument for the separation, and it rests on the structure of the experience rather than on any claim about consciousness.

> Consider that in order to have the experience of hearing a voice in your head talking, there must be a talker and a listener. Otherwise, how would you have that experience?

The force of it is that it's hard to refuse. It doesn't ask you to believe anything about a higher self; it asks you to account for an experience you're already having. Hearing requires a hearer.

What's revealed is the default nobody chose:

> Most people spend their entire lives identified as the talker.

Not as a position they hold — as an assumption so complete it was never visible. This is why Dave's opening question to clients (*how do you know when you're thinking?*) gets quizzical looks. They've never separated the two enough for the question to parse.

It is the same claim as The narrating voice in your head is a survival mechanism, not your self, arrived at by a different route: that one says what the voice *is*, this one shows that you cannot be identical with it.
