# We make decisions emotionally and rationalise them afterwards

Andrew asks the fair question — he understands attending to the voice in his head, but why does the *body* matter? Dave names the assumption behind the question first: "isn't my body just the vehicle to move my head around so I can think and reason?"

> We pretend that we're rational creatures and that we make decisions rationally, but it's not true. We actually make decisions emotionally, and then we rationalise them.

His evidence is the brain-injury research on patients who lost emotional processing. IQ tests: unimpaired, as good as ever. Then ask which colour pen they'd like to use, and they deliberate for hours.

The result is devastating to the rationalist picture precisely because the deficit is invisible in reasoning and total in choosing. Without feeling, there is no basis on which one option is preferable — so deliberation never terminates.

The practical consequence for a founder who doesn't notice their body is that the decisions are being made emotionally anyway, just outside of awareness. → [[Your life is limited by which sensations you are willing to feel]]

Jesse's shortcut for anyone unconvinced: think about a time you were really nervous, about to give a speech. Was that in your head? "No — when you're really nervous, you *feel* it." Start at the extreme, admit the sensation was real and was a signal, then ask what makes you think a balance sheet isn't doing a smaller version of the same thing.
