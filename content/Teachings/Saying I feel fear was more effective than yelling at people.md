# Saying I feel fear was more effective than yelling at people

Jesse's practice, arrived at about six months into the [[Thoughts, Feelings, Sensations]] check-ins, and it is the clearest before/after in his work.

**Before:** someone presents numbers that are down 30%. He doesn't shout, but he asks *how come we didn't think to do this? What about this? Somebody else is doing it this way, why aren't we?* People leave the conversation feeling stupid. Andrew's diagnosis: you don't have to yell to make someone feel terrible — "you're basically just preying on their worst insecurities of themselves."

**After:** the same numbers, the same tightening chest. He says it out loud.

> I feel fear. I notice my chest is getting tight and I feel fear right now.

> It was so much more effective than me starting to yell at people for numbers being down.

Two things make this work. It **reveals rather than controls** — an unarguable statement of his own experience, not a verdict on theirs. → [[Communicating to reveal instead of to control removes the thing there is to argue with]]

And it **discharges the energy honestly**, so it doesn't leak sideways into interrogation. Jesse is precise that the point wasn't to push the fear away: "it allowed me to notice it, not push it away, but just be aware of it — and sometimes make other people aware of it."

Dave's read of what was happening before: "you probably went into blame mode, because you're scared and in a state of threat." → The Responsibility Roadmap
