# Either the startup fails and you learn you did not die, or it succeeds and you learn it was not enough

Dave relays an argument made *against* the standard coaching advice, and takes it seriously.

> Someone wrote a post that was basically: **hey, all you coaches telling founders not to tie their identity to their startup — here's an argument why you should.**

The argument: go all in, tie your identity to it completely, and **one of two things happens.**

> One is your startup will **fail**, and you will learn **that you didn't die.** And actually that people still love you and you still feel okay. So that ego-driven idea that my worth is a function of being successful **might burn up in the fire of your startup's failure** — which, from a spiritual journey, **great. That's just as good.**

> Or you'll be **successful** — the Brian Chesky example — and you'll discover that achieving that outward success **doesn't actually create sustained long-term happiness, peace, freedom.**

Either way the belief gets tested to destruction, so:

> **All roads lead to Rome.** That's why we don't necessarily need to be attached to one path.

Dave adds the third outcome, which is the one that doesn't resolve:

> The other thing that can happen is **you don't have that wake-up call.** You have the exit, you don't feel the fulfilment you thought, and at that point **your mind says: I guess this wasn't enough.** And **it doubles down.** *Okay, ten million — I thought I'd feel free. Maybe that's not fuck-you money. A hundred million is.*

> That's the **hedonic treadmill.** And it's not just money — status, running a public company, Series B, Series C. **How much do you need to feel okay? More.**
