# Most founders source their sense of security, control and approval externally

Commitment 11 of the 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership, and Dave's framing of what it is up against.

> If you look at our lives, most people — most founders — are sourcing their sense of security, control and approval **externally.** Based on what people think of them, their conditions, money, success.

Jesse's reaction the first time he heard it is the useful part:

> I was like: what are you talking about? Of course. My whole life I've thought about how I need to make a certain amount. Once I hit the number, then I'll feel free.

> The idea that we source them externally is so common that it feels like drinking water or breathing air.

Dave: "it's like hiding in plain sight — this unexamined part of our experience."

He declines the moral framing entirely. Asked what's wrong with it:

> There's nothing wrong with it, other than the fact that it doesn't work.

And his test is empirical rather than doctrinal: look at people who made a ton of money. Do they have a deep sense of security, freedom and worthiness? And if so, is it *because* of the money?

Jesse notices mid-conversation that he maps money specifically onto **security** — "I looked at myself in the mirror for a second" — and that someone else might map the same money onto control, or approval. Same substance, different need underneath.
