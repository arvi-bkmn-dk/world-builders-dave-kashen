# Time is finite but not enough is always a story

Dave draws a line that keeps the scarcity teaching from collapsing into denial — a distinction people reliably trip over.

> We made up time, but within the construct of time, it is finite. 24 hours in a day, a week before your launch. It is finite. We're not magically thinking, oh, you have all the time.

> But scarcity has within it the notion of **not enough**. And that is always a story, that's always a perspective.

The quantity is a fact. The verdict on the quantity is an interpretation — and it's the verdict, not the quantity, that produces the fear.

> There's X amount of time. That is what it is. If I tell myself it's not enough, then I'm going to experience fear, stress and overwhelm.

Which is exactly the [[A fact is something a video camera could record|fact/story boundary]] applied to resources. A camera records that there are five days until launch. It cannot record that this isn't enough.

Dave then generalises, and the list is the point:

> Same thing with money, love, recognition, fulfilling my potential. It's all versions of: I'm not enough, it's not enough, there's not enough.

The three phrasings are one structure. *Not enough time* and *not enough love* and *I'm not enough* are the same sentence pointed at different objects — which is why scarcity in one domain rarely stays there.
