# Disconnection from yourself becomes disconnection from other people

The episode opens on Brian Chesky's viral post — a $100B company, $10B net worth, and: *"I woke up the next day and my life was exactly the same. I'm alone. I put on my sweatpants, I go onto my iMac, and I have 10 to 12 hours of Zoom meetings."*

The line Dave asks him to read twice: *"so much of my life was about being successful, probably if I were to dig deep, **because I thought it would make people love me.**"*

Dave's first response is not analysis:

> I notice **sadness** as I hear that. First just empathy… I really appreciate his vulnerability in sharing that.

Then the mechanism:

> **We're disconnected from ourselves, and then by being disconnected from ourselves, we end up feeling disconnected from other people.**

> That sense of *I need to achieve in order to feel loved* — there is a kind of loneliness in that, in the sense of **I can't be loved just for who I am.**

And the closed loop it creates:

> If I go try to be loved by **achieving** all of these things — well then **how do I know if I'm loved for me, or if I'm loved for having achieved all these things?** So it's sort of a **fool's errand.**

That is why the loneliness doesn't resolve with more success. Every additional achievement adds another reason someone might be there for the achievement, so the evidence you actually want becomes harder to obtain as you succeed.

Dave adds the quote he'd posted that week: **"the only way to become confident in yourself is to be yourself."** And, from his son's wall: *"be yourself — your uniqueness is far more important and valuable than your ability to conform."*

The compounding factors he sees in wealthy founders: *"I can't complain about anything because I got $10 billion — who's gonna listen?"*, the inability to trust motives, and the mask: *"I can't tell my team what I'm really thinking. I can't tell my investors that I'm scared."*
