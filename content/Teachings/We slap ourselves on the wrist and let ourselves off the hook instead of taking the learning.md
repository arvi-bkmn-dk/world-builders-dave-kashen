# We slap ourselves on the wrist and let ourselves off the hook instead of taking the learning

Andrew mentions he has regrets. Dave gets curious, because "regret sort of inherently comes with some story that something should have been different than it was" — and then names the mechanism that makes regret feel like accountability while functioning as its opposite.

> It's almost like we slap ourselves on the wrist and then let ourselves off the hook, instead of actually taking the learning so we can do it differently next time.

The self-punishment *is* the discharge. Having felt bad, the account seems settled, and no learning is extracted — which is why the same pattern recurs. Guilt does the same work.

Dave's redirect is a single question: *what do you get to learn from that experience?* — aimed forward, at the downstream consequences that are still live.

Andrew's follow-up is the best evidence for the teaching. He thought he'd beaten burnout — he'd married, started running, built a life with other aspects — and burned out anyway.

> So I don't think I actually learned as much as I thought I did.

Feeling bad about burning out had produced coping additions rather than an understanding of how he creates it. → How am I creating this is the question that converts acceptance into power
