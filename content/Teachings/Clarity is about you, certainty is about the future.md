# Clarity is about you, certainty is about the future

A founder trying to write a three-year vision tells Dave *"it's just not clear to me."* Dave hears the demand underneath: *I need to get it right. I need to know. I should know.* His answer is a distinction he calls maybe the biggest source of the problem: thinking that clarity means certainty.

> Certainty is about the future, and the best you can ever do there is an educated guess. Clarity is about you. What you authentically want. Where you actually stand right now.

Certainty can't be had, but clarity can, because it's about the present and about you. Conviction fits on the clarity side too:

> You can even have conviction, a strong feeling or intuition about the way to go, without pretending it's certainty.

> "I feel strongly about this, and I don't know for sure" is a completely coherent place to lead from.

The twist is that chasing certainty is what hides clarity:

> Here's the irony: the need for certainty actually obscures clarity. When you demand something that doesn't exist, its absence feels like confusion, so you keep searching, and the searching is the noise.

Letting go of the need doesn't make you less rigorous:

> When you're at peace with not knowing, your discernment gets sharper, because you're seeing reality as it is: genuinely uncertain, rather than falsely certain.

The newsletter closes on the feeling that remains once the demand is gone: you can be clear while being completely uncertain, *"except instead of fear, there's excitement for what's possible."* → [[Fear is excitement plus resistance]]
