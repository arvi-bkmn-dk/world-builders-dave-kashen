# Turn the whole world into a mirror

Dave's answer to *what do I actually do with this*, and it converts every irritating person into information.

> One thing you could do with it is turn the whole world into a mirror. What if every other human being is just a mirror for you?

Set inside the larger frame:

> If the journey is a journey to unconditional love — for self and others — well then the world will just show you the parts of yourself that you haven't yet loved.

And it runs both ways, which is what makes it practical from whichever end is available:

> If you can love yourself unconditionally, you can love others unconditionally. If you can love others unconditionally, you can love yourself unconditionally. So you can play with it either way, and both are useful.

For the founder unmoved by unconditional love as a goal, Dave offers a substitution: **acceptance**. "If there's something about reality that I'm not accepting, I block my flow." Judging a colleague is the same move as wishing the river ran a different direction — "Jesse should be kinder to me, Andrew should be more open-minded, I wish this client wasn't churning."

Jesse's version makes it operational: "love and flow are somewhat synonymous. Anytime the love has stopped because you're judging that guy, you're not flowing anymore."
