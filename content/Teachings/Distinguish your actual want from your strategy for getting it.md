# Distinguish your actual want from your strategy for getting it

The move that dissolves most apparent trade-offs.

Dave first names the trap: *"there's a **presumption of trade-off** that is very, very common."* A founder says *I want ease and relaxation* and *I want my startup to be wildly successful*, then treats those as opposing and concludes they don't know what they want.

> They **actually know what they want.** They just don't believe it's possible. So they're wrestling internally between this **false trade-off** of two things that they want.

> Even just recognizing that you **do** know what you want — you just don't believe it's possible — is useful. If you're in *do I want this or this*, **switch the "or" to "and."**

But he doesn't hand-wave the conflict, and this is the useful part:

> It **is** important to distinguish your **actual want** from your **strategies** for getting what you want. Because when you go to the level of **strategy**, there are sometimes real trade-offs.

His worked example: *"I wanna work two hours a week and I want to build a successful startup"* — as strategies, genuinely unlikely. So go underneath:

> What's behind wanting to work two hours a week? *Oh, I want to feel a sense of **lightness and freedom.*** And what's behind wanting to build a successful startup? *I wanna feel **challenged**, I wanna see what I'm made of.*

> If you get behind the strategies and get to the underlying wants — from my experience, **you can always find a way to have it all.**

Two hours a week and a successful startup collide. Lightness *and* challenge do not. The conflict lived entirely in the implementations. → [[Most trade-offs are false]] · [[Surf and Turf]]

Jesse adds the complementary question — *"what would have to be true?"* — which takes a want that sounds impossible and starts building the strategy backwards from it.

And Dave's closing extension:

> Our **deepest** wants actually end up being for experiences that are **already available to us without achieving anything.** Like peace and joy and freedom.
