# You cannot fake non-attachment because the body senses it

The immediate temptation on hearing that non-attachment makes creation easier is to perform it. Dave closes that door from experience rather than principle.

> And the problem is you can't fake it. Because believe me, I've tried. It'd be like, "no no, I'm not attached to that happening, that's just my preference." But my body can sense it, and I'm attached, and then I get scared around it, and I'm not showing up as creatively or confidently.

The reason the performance fails is that attachment was never a verbal position — it's a belief that your wellbeing depends on the outcome, and the body is already organised around that belief regardless of what you say about it. Declaring detachment adds a second layer of pretence on top of the fear, which is worse than the fear alone.

This is the same structural point as [[Where your heart is matters more than whether the language is technically unarguable]]: correct words over an unchanged state don't produce the benefits of the state. The vocabulary of this work is unusually easy to adopt without doing any of it, and both failures — performed detachment, weaponised unarguable language — come from the same shortcut.

The implication for practice is that the only route through is noticing the attachment honestly, which at least makes it available to work with.
