# Aliveness can be quiet, it does not have to be big

A correction Dave makes against his own earlier reading:

> People could misunderstand it as **like it has to be huge.** And I think I did for a long time, so I empathise with that. Like *aliveness is this big energy and I'm so excited* — but you could have this **deep sense of aliveness in still, quiet moments.**

> Some people talk about the **still small voice.** Instead of listening to the voice of fear, your inner critic — can you listen to that still small voice?

He notes people often recognise it retrospectively, by how out of character the action was:

> People reflect back: *that was so not like me. That's one character I don't — I never do that.* But in that moment, I quit. Or I went up to that woman at the bar and ended up marrying her.

The matching example is Jesse's, from a post about an airport arrivals hall — a man met by his daughters, then his wife, and the writer sitting for twenty minutes watching *"human after human, colour, gender, age, it didn't matter,"* all arriving into the same embrace. *"In the small moments you find that aliveness also."*

Which leads Dave to the other ingredient:

> There's something around **connection** and aliveness. Whether it's connection to ourselves, connection to somebody else, connection to the life-force energy itself, connection to something bigger than us. **When we're in connection, we feel more alive.**

The practical upshot: if you only accept the loud version as evidence, you will discard most of the signal you're supposed to be navigating by. → [[Aliveness is the north star for authenticity]] · [[Connection is felt, not checked off]]
