# Would you let me take away all your setbacks and failures

The question Dave uses to dismantle protective heroing, and it works because the answer is instant and unanimous.

> I often say to clients — **what if I offered to go back in your life and just take away all of your setbacks, all your failures? Would you let me?**

The asymmetry is the whole point. Nobody would surrender their own formative difficulties, and almost everybody works to spare other people theirs — which means the protection is not a judgment about what helps, it is a way of not feeling something. → [[Heroes require victims]] · [[Fight is villain, flight is hero, freeze is victim]]

Dave names the pattern directly: *"as human beings, especially when we're below the line in a state of threat, one of the roles we can play is **hero** — we wanna protect people. So we think about firing that person and we feel bad."*

He adds the check on whether the status quo is actually the kind option:

> If someone was **thriving** in the role and performing, you wouldn't be thinking about letting them go. So it **can't actually feel great for them**, if they're self-aware, to be in a role where they're not thriving.
