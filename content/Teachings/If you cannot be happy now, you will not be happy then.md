# If you cannot be happy now, you will not be happy then

Dave calls it a cliché he says to clients a lot, and then adds the clause that keeps it from being fatalistic:

> If you can't be happy now, you won't be happy then — **but you can be happy now.**

The target is the deferred life:

> So many people play that deferred life game. Once I have this, then I'll be happy, then I'll be safe, then I'll feel fulfilled. It's just bullshit. It doesn't work.

Jesse's supporting evidence is empirical and blunt: "I've met enough rich people to know that money and that stuff doesn't give you any semblance of that. Some of the richest people are some of the least happy people." And he notes the strange gap — "people feel it in their body, but they don't necessarily believe it in their head."

Jesse also runs the ladder that exposes the substitution, on Andrew, live. *When you get the money and the success — what do you think you're gonna get then?* Most people, pushed twice, arrive at peace, freedom, wholeness. Which means the money was never the goal; it was a strategy for the goal, and a poor one.

Dave's version of the fix is not to want less but to reverse the order: recognise the wholeness is already available, and "you can actually be way more effective" building the thing anyway. → [[Fulfilled just means feeling full]]
