# Shame is the electric fence

Joe Hudson's image for what shame is *for*, which explains why it is so widely deployed and so ineffective.

> Shame is like the electric fence. If you're trying to train your dog, you have this electric fence, and when they touch it they feel the shock, and ultimately they learn not to go near it.

It is a training device, and it rests on a premise Dave rejects:

> Back to this idea, this false presumption that we're fundamentally unruly, antisocial, somehow bad — that we need to be corrected. Shame is the core mechanism from that. It's how we civilise the uncivilised.

He traces the premise through original sin, through parenting — "parents use shame to get us to do the things they want us to do" — and through the psychoanalytic picture of a superego restraining an appetite-driven id. Jesse adds governments running on fear.

The image also carries its own verdict. Picture the dog at the fence:

> They take on the posture of shame — their head will slump down, they don't look very empowered, they look beaten down. It's a little bit like beating ourselves into submission.

Dave notes the same failure in parenting that he describes in habit change: "the things you try to use shame to get your kid to do — it's like mutually assured destruction. You end up having to layer on more shame because they resisted more." → [[Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and that is what creates the yo-yo]]
