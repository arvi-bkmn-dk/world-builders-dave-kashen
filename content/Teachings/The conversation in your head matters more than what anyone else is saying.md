# The conversation in your head matters more than what anyone else is saying

Andrew relays a line from Rick Elias, founder of Red Ventures:

> What you say in your head is more important than what other people are saying around you. Pay more attention to it.

The reasoning is volume: the person you talk to most every day is yourself, by an enormous margin — and it is the one conversation you're usually not attending to.

Andrew's own example is the demonstration, and its absurdity is the point. Two weeks ago he fought to get a distribution provision into the operating agreement. Today Jesse proposes taking distributions next month and Andrew thinks *the guy's trying to take money out of the business, we need it in there or it'll die.* Same person, opposite positions, a fortnight apart.

> If you really pay attention to the voice in your head, you'll notice it's schizophrenic. That's just what it is to be human — to have a voice in our head constantly saying things, often literally the opposite.

Which is the argument for the whole episode. A narrator that contradicts itself within two weeks is not a source of truth, and Dave's qualifier on Rick's line matters:

> Not that you should *listen* to the voice in your head — but that the relationship you have with that voice is really important.
