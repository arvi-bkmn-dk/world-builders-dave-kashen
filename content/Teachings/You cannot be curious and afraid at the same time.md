# You cannot be curious and afraid at the same time

Dave's argument for curiosity as the practical antidote to fear — not because it is nicer, but because the two states are mutually exclusive.

> **You can't be curious and afraid at the same time.** Whereas fear makes you myopic and stressed, curiosity is enlivening and expansive.

That makes curiosity actionable in a way "don't be afraid" never is. You cannot instruct yourself out of fear, but you can ask a genuine question — and the asking displaces the state.

He gives it two forms. **Experiment over perfection**: treat every attempt, successful or not, as data. "Curiosity turns obstacles into puzzles and fosters excitement over *what might happen next.*" → [[Run experiments so that the experiment fails instead of you]]

And **wonder questions**, which he frames as recovering something children do by default:

> "What would we do if we started all over?" · "What would be the most fun way we can imagine moving forward?" · "What's one thing we've never tried that just might work?"

> Treat the questions as doorways into new possibilities rather than quizzes where you need to get the answer right.

The last clause is the whole distinction. A question asked to find the right answer is fear wearing a question mark; a question asked to see what happens is the other state.

Dave's claim for it: "curiosity breathes life into daily tasks" — and it is the item in this newsletter he files under *why it prevents burnout.*
