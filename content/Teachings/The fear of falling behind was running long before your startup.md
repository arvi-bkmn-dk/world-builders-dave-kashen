# The fear of falling behind was running long before your startup

A competitor ships your planned feature; a peer raises twice your round. Most founders feel instantly behind and speed up. Dave's claim:

> From my experience coaching some of the world's best founders, the fear of falling behind has very little to do with the competitor.

His question:

> Sit with this for a second: how far back would you have to go to find a version of yourself who didn't feel there was something you still needed to accomplish?

> The urge to keep making progress isn't something your startup handed you. You walked in with it.

The competitor just gives the pattern a number to point at. Projected onto the company, it turns the round you haven't raised into something you should already have, and the friction between where you are and where you think you should be is what you feel as falling behind, *"even at the end of a genuinely good day."*

> The race against the clock you feel is all in your head.
