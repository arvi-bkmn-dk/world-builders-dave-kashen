# Go as far into the darkness as you can without extinguishing your own light

A line from Dave's own coach, and his work's clearest statement that capacity work has a limit.

> As my coach once told me: "Go as far into the darkness as you can **without extinguishing your own light.**"

> Know your limits. Turn down the volume when needed. The chaos will still be there when you're ready to face it again — but you'll face it from a place of strength.

This is the counterweight to everything else Dave teaches about going toward difficulty — Treat the startup as an emotional intensity gym, welcoming fear, approaching what you avoid. Those all push in one direction. This says the direction is right and the distance is bounded.

The gym comparison holds: training load builds capacity, and load past capacity causes injury. "As far as you can" is genuinely far. "Without extinguishing your own light" is a real stopping condition.

It sits in a newsletter about navigating sustained chaos, where Dave declines the usual consolation:

> I'm not willing to bet that this chaos will slow down and stabilize in our lifetime. Waiting for external conditions to stabilize before you can find peace is a bet I wouldn't take.

Which is why the limit matters. If there is no calmer period coming, pacing is not a temporary concession — it is the operating condition.
