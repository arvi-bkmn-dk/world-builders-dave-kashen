# Suppressing anger does not prevent reactive anger, it causes it

In Dave's family, anger was what uncivilised people felt. So he became cool, calm and collected, carried it into his leadership, and was proud of it, without seeing what it cost.

Most people's evidence about anger is reactive anger: yelling, throwing staplers, shutting people down. Dave separates the two:

> But the reactivity isn't caused by anger. It's caused by the unwillingness to be with anger.

It happens two ways. In the moment, unwelcomed sensations get reacted to instead of felt. Over time, chronically suppressed anger accumulates until a small trigger produces an explosion.

> Suppression doesn't prevent reactive anger. Suppression is what causes it.

The composure he'd been proud of had a practical price too:

> One thing I've noticed from not feeling my anger for most of my life: I struggled to make decisions.

> On the other side of the feeling is clarity.
