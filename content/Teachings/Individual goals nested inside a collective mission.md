# Individual goals nested inside a collective mission

Dave found the model of the team culture he now helps founders build in an amateur endurance program, not at Goldman Sachs or the hedge fund. He joined **Team in Training** — the Leukemia and Lymphoma Society's endurance program — while depressed after a move to the Bay Area and a breakup. *"I had no idea it would parallel the type of team culture I'd later help the world's best founders build."*

Three things made it different.

**Intent behind every interaction was unambiguous.**

> When coaches gave feedback — *"keep your fingers closer together when you swim"* — it never felt like criticism. It felt like constructive feedback. **Always.** The difference? **You knew it was coming from someone who wanted you to succeed.**

**Individual goals nested inside a collective mission.**

> Everyone had their personal development path. That person's trying to get faster on the run. This one's working on becoming a better swimmer. **You knew what people were working on, and you could help them on their paths.**

> But there was also this group goal: raising money to fight cancer, getting a certain number of people across the finish line. **We were literally in it together.**

Both levels at once — not company OKRs *plus* individual performance reviews as separate systems, but each person's growth visible to everyone and legibly connected to the shared finish line.

**Cultural symbols made the frame tangible.** Purple jerseys; strangers in Team in Training gear at any race saying *"Go team!"* to each other. *"You'd see it modeled, and then you'd perpetuate it. An upward spiral: people cheer you on, which makes you want to cheer them on."*

Dave's four practical moves: say the intent out loud (*"I'm sharing this because I care about you and want you to win"*), nest development inside vision, build a ritual, and raise the appreciation-to-correction ratio. → [[The default team norm is high corrective activity and low appreciative activity]]

His closing question:

> When your team gets feedback, do they hear **"you're being judged"** or **"we want you to win"**? That difference is everything.
