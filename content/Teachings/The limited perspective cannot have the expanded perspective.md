# The limited perspective cannot have the expanded perspective

The trap for anyone who takes inner work seriously, and the structural reason it can't be solved by trying harder.

> There are many people for whom **spiritual enlightenment is the exact parallel to a founder wanting to sell their company for a billion dollars.** It's this thing — basically **the ego wants spiritual awakening.**

> But the ego is the thing that — **the limited perspective can't have the expanded perspective.**

> If the ego is like the **blinders on a horse**, you can never see what's beyond the blinders **through** the blinders.

Jesse supplies Ram Dass's line: **"enlightenment is the ego's ultimate disappointment."**

And Dave's resolution, from Rupert Spira:

> It's **not an achievement. It's not even an experience.** It's just a **recognition of something that's already true** — which is that you are not your ego.

That distinction does real work. An achievement can be pursued, failed at, and used as evidence about your worth; a recognition cannot. Which is why making awakening into a goal guarantees you approach it with the very apparatus you are trying to see past.

Dave's practical form of the same point: *"it's probably useful to consider that **your ego and your mind are not gonna go away.** So what's the so-what of that — rather than trying to make them go away? And, you know, **what you resist persists** — that can make it worse."*
