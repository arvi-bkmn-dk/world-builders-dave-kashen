# Imagining someone's ideal self and criticising them for not being there yet is not support

Andrew makes the case every demanding leader makes: if I hold people to a high bar, they rise to it, and my frustration when they fall short is how I drive them there.

Dave separates two things that feel identical from the inside.

> There's an important distinction between seeing the good in others — relating to people as their best, looking for the good — versus expecting or feeling entitled for them to be a certain way.

The variable is **tense**:

> What generally happens is we're imagining this amazing future of who you're going to become, and we're like: I wish you were already there now. That's a bit of a trap — you could think you're doing them a service by imagining their ideal self and then criticising them for all the ways they're not yet there.

Which is exactly [[Frustration is a desired future collapsed into the present]], aimed at a person instead of at a company. The vision is fine. Demanding they already inhabit it is a demand reality cannot meet, and the person absorbs it as a verdict on who they are.

The version that works keeps the future in the future:

> I believe in you. I think you can be all these things in the future, and I'll support you to become them.

Same high bar, opposite experience for the recipient — belief rather than deficiency.

Dave's closing note is the one that makes it land: "which is, by the way, a thing we totally do to ourselves as well." The standard you hold people to is usually the one you're already running on yourself. → [[Your state is contagious to your team]]
