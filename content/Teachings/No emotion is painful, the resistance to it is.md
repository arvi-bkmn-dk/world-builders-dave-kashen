# No emotion is painful, the resistance to it is

Dave flags this as something most people don't understand, and it is the load-bearing claim underneath all of his emotional work.

> No emotions are painful or bad. It's actually the resistance to the emotion that feels painful. An emotion plus resistance is a fundamentally different experience than the emotion without resistance.

Two different quantities that everyone treats as one. What gets called "shame is painful" is actually shame-plus-resistance, and the second term is supplying most of the pain.

The everyday proof, which is why this is checkable rather than doctrinal:

> If you've ever had a failed business or a failed relationship, or you missed a flight — there's the feeling of what's about to happen, and you resist it and resist it. And finally when you're like, alright, this is not going to work, I missed my flight — **the bad thing never feels bad when it actually happens.** You're kind of like, oh.

The dread of the thing exceeds the thing. Everyone has that data; almost nobody draws the conclusion.

Andrew's live application from his last session: when his wife wants to plan the next thing, he doesn't want to do it. Dave's questions were *what are you feeling there? And what are you resisting?*

> It's the resistance to it that's making you dislike the conversation and suffer through it, or suffer through procrastinating on it.

## Also in [[GA 01 - The Inner Game of Life, Danny Miranda Podcast 394]]

Dave extends the claim, carefully and hedged, to physical pain. He points to childbirth and other reports where full acceptance changes how sensation is experienced, and adds that his wife warned him never to tell her it was just sensation.

> that it might be the resistance to the sensation That we experience as pain, not the sensation itself.
