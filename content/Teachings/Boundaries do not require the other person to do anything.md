# Boundaries do not require the other person to do anything

Dave calls this "a big reframe for me as a parent," and the distinction it draws is between a boundary and a request that has been mislabelled as one.

> Guys, you need to get off your screens by 9:00 PM — that's actually not a boundary, that's a request. And I thought that was a boundary, and I'm like, I thought I'm doing boundaries, and what's going on, and I'm so mad at them because they didn't honor the boundary.

The anger is diagnostic. A request can be declined, so when you have issued one while believing it was a boundary, non-compliance reads as violation and you end up resentful about an outcome you never actually controlled.

The corrected version moves the action to the only person who can perform it:

> Hey guys, I'm gonna come upstairs and take your screen away at 9:00 PM. That's a boundary. Just like an actual boundary — you hit a wall, the wall's there. Nothing you have to do, the wall's there.

The test is simple: if keeping it depends on someone else's cooperation, it is a request. If you can keep it alone, it is a boundary.

Jesse's illustration is the peanut allergy — no negotiating, no asking the child to please comply, no inconsistency. Dave adds what that firmness actually feels like from the inside, and it is not coldness: "you'd feel some of that anger. It's this fierce love of, no, I'm protecting something." → [[Love in leadership can be fierce]]

## Also in [[HoE 19 - Anger]]

*The same distinction, restated in the anger episode with the work and relationship versions.*

> I really like this distinction: **boundaries don't require the other person to do anything.** Boundaries are something **you** do.

Dave's worked example is his own kids and screens:

> I used to say — *okay, you have to be off your screens by nine.* Or even, *we have an agreement, you're gonna be off by nine.* But those are really just **requests**, or **agreements.**

> It's different than: *hey, at nine o'clock **I'm gonna come upstairs and take your screens away.*** That's a boundary. It's like **hitting a wall.**

The same shape at work — *"if you keep exhibiting this behavior, **I'm going to let you go.** I'm gonna stop paying you"* — which Jesse notes is all a firing actually is. And in a relationship, at every scale from *I'm going to sleep in a different room* down to the micro version:

> *I don't like the way you're speaking to me. **I'm gonna leave the room for ten minutes**, and I'll come back, and if you're ready to speak to me differently, then we can continue.*

The test is whether it still works if the other person refuses to cooperate. A request needs their compliance; a boundary needs only your own action. Which is why stated rules that depend on someone else's agreement keep failing and getting re-stated — they were never boundaries.
