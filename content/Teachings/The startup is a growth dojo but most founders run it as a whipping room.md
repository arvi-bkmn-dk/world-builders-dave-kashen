# The startup is a growth dojo but most founders run it as a whipping room

Dave's view of the startup is that it is "one of the best personal and spiritual growth dojos on the planet" — a practice room, somewhere you go deliberately to get better and where the difficulty is the point. What makes the claim useful is the sentence immediately after it: that this is not remotely how most entrepreneurs are experiencing it.

Jesse supplies the word for what they experience instead. A **whipping room** — the same intensity, the same difficulty, but organised around beating yourself up rather than developing. The image lands because it is not a caricature; Jesse describes recognising himself in it and *liking* it. "I wanna go do something that's gonna make me feel worse about myself, because it's gonna make me want to do even better."

> If I whip myself I'm going to finally get to run faster than anyone else can imagine — and do not take that away from me.

Note that the room is the same room. The difference is entirely in what the founder is doing inside it, which is why this is an inner-game problem rather than a circumstances problem. The same eighty-hour week is a dojo or a whipping room depending on the state it is run from.
