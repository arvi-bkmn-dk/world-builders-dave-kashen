# Apathy is not caring, non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome

The fear Dave is answering is specific. Founders running on dirty fuel see it exhausting them — but *"they are scared of not being scared and having nothing to motivate them."* That they'll slide from attachment into **apathy**, and apathy will kill the company.

The distinction that dissolves it:

> **Apathy** = not caring.
> **Non-attachment** = not believing your **well-being depends on** the outcome.

Caring was learned through attachment: "when you got the A, you got praise. When revenue spiked, you felt worthy. So you built your entire identity around outcomes." Dirty fuel works — *"it's fuel, it got you here"* — but "it's toxic. It leaves residue that builds up over time. I've had clients in the ER for heart issues and panic attacks."

→ [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]] · [[Care deeply and be willing to have your heart broken]]

There is also a hard-nosed argument, not only a health one. Attachment spends energy on three things: **the gap** (being frustrated you haven't already doubled revenue instead of doubling it), **imagining outcomes you don't want**, and **resisting reality as it is.**

> All of that is burned energy. Energy that could be building your company.

His illustration is the desperate fundraise: "the investor felt your desperation. The clinging to the outcome was palpable. Nobody signed." Versus the meeting where you were genuinely unattached and "all your energy was available for the actual interaction."
