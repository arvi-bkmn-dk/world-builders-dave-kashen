# The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create

Dave gives attachment a measurement, which is what makes this usable rather than aspirational:

> **The gap between how much you can feel a feeling now versus how much you think you'll feel it once you achieve the thing: that gap determines how attached you are to the outcome.**

> If you believe you can't feel confident until you raise your Series A, you'll be **intensely attached** to that raise. But if you can access confidence now — **genuinely, not as a trick** — the attachment loosens.

Then the paradox:

> **The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create.**

> When you're attached, you're **grasping.** You're not showing up as creatively or confidently or with as much energy. But when you can access peace now, you operate from **presence, and presence is the most resourceful state there is.**

The honesty about faking it is what keeps this from being a technique:

> **You can't fake this. Believe me, I've tried.**

> But when it's genuine, you get both: the inner experience you were chasing all along, **and** the outer results become easier to create.

That doubling is what Dave calls **winning twice** — the founders who go direct *"get the inner experience they were actually chasing all along… and because they're operating from those states, the outer results become easier to create."* → [[You win twice]] · [[Apathy is not caring, non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome]]
