# Your startup is a dojo for your personal and spiritual growth

How Dave frames his own job to clients:

> I usually tell clients **not to think of me as coaching them to support their startup's success**, but instead supporting them to use their startup as a **dojo for their personal and spiritual growth.**

> And in doing so, founders discover that this way of approaching it is actually **the most efficient and direct path** to what they're looking for.

> Building a startup **is already a spiritual experience if you allow it to be.**

The inversion is complete: the company stops being the goal that personal growth serves, and becomes the training environment that personal growth uses. The pressure, the stakes and the exposure are the curriculum rather than the obstacle.

Dave places himself in the arc rather than above it — *"I wasn't particularly 'spiritual' myself until 2016, when I had my first profound experience. I felt the boundaries of myself dissolving, fully immersed in this beautiful white light energy that I can only describe as love."*

And he describes the progression he watches founders move through: they stop being victims, take ownership, build, achieve, hit the milestones — *"and then the deeper questions emerge. Why do I still feel like something's missing? Who am I, really?"* → [[To Me, By Me, Through Me, As Me]]

The trigger varies: *"sometimes through crisis. Sometimes through curiosity. And sometimes, like Chesky, because they've achieved the success they were chasing and hit the ceiling."*
