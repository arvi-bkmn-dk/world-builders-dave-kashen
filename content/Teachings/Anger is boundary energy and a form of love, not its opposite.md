# Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite

Dave treats anger as one of the most misunderstood emotions, and diagnoses why: most people's only experience of it is the reactive, violent version, so they conclude that's what anger *is*. His reframe puts it inside love rather than opposite it — all emotions as "love through a prism," with anger as the facet that does boundaries.

> Anger is just boundary energy.

The practical consequence, which he reports from his own recent breakthrough, is a gain in *speed of detection*: being able to feel anger means sensing more quickly when your wants or needs aren't being met, and standing for them. Anger that can't be felt doesn't disappear — it just stops functioning as information and only shows up once it has become reactive.

Jesse's story is the demonstration, and the surprise in it is that he is on the receiving end. He asked to move a session at short notice; Dave told him he felt angry about it, and asked him to fix the underlying problem. Jesse hired a better EA within the week.

> His anger really served me. It's fierce love.

Worth noticing what Dave's anger looked like in practice: a stated feeling, a named agreement (24 hours' notice), and a request. Unarguable, and still unmistakably angry. → Speaking Unarguably
