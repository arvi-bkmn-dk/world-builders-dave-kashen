# Frustration is a desired future collapsed into the present

The first of the two drifts out of Flow, and the more subtle one because it starts from something healthy. You begin with an inspiring vision, operating with energy and creativity. Then, unnoticed, you stop envisioning the desired future *in the future* and start wishing you were already in it.

> It is not possible to already be somewhere other than where you are. So, instead of inspiration, you create frustration.

The verbal signature is the word *wish*, past-tensed. "I wish we'd raised our next round already." "I wish revenue was growing faster." "I wish our team was operating more effectively." Each one is a desired future dragged into a present where it cannot exist, which makes it a form of resisting reality.

The correction is a single move, and Dave states it almost mechanically: put the desired future back into the future, where it's possible. Imagine raising in a few months, revenue growing next quarter, the team firing on all cylinders next year. Same vision, correct tense — and you're back in Flow.

This is the framework-level version of what he names about himself in conversation as using a vision as a weapon. → [[Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong]]

## Also in [[MB 05 - Founders, It Doesn't Have to Be This Hard]]

The article's worked case is a client nearly out of cash, berating himself over the fundraising he hadn't done. Dave had him push the desired future three weeks out and frame it as the fourth quarter, down by ten. Three weeks later he had raised over $1mm.

> “Give me the ball coach,” he replied with a smile on his face.
