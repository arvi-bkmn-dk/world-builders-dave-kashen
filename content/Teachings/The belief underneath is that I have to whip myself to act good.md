# The belief underneath is that I have to whip myself to act good

Jesse describes exactly what his mind predicted would happen if he stopped driving himself — not as a worry, but as a certainty:

> I'm gonna eat lots of chocolate cake and brownies and cookies. I'm gonna watch a bunch of garbage television. I'm gonna gamble. **I'm gonna be a waste of space.**

> And a lot of people I know — **if they don't push themselves, that's actually what they are.**

Dave names what's underneath:

> You're revealing almost a **deeper human-level pervasive assumption: that I am bad, worthless.** And basically **I need to whip myself, or manage myself, or shame myself, or scare myself, to act good.**

And his reaction to it, which he doesn't hide:

> As I say it, I can feel some **sadness** around **how pervasive that belief is.** I don't believe it's true.

The belief is self-sealing: it cannot be tested without letting go of the driving, and letting go is exactly what it forbids.

Jesse did run the test:

> It took me six days maybe, or eight days, where I was being a waste of space. But then **I had ideas, and I caught up with people, and I was like — oh, this is cool.**

> And you asked me: *did you really think that you didn't have that?* And I was like — **I guess I did. I really did think that I wasn't a creative, that I didn't have that creative energy in me.**

Dave's account of what actually arises: *"what doesn't seem to happen is people become lazy couch potatoes. What happens is **authentic impulses arise. We are creative beings**"* — and they tend to be pro-social, because *"we seem to be designed in such a way that it feels good to serve other people."*
