# Rejecting humanity is just ego in disguise

The trap specific to people who are serious about inner work — which makes it the most self-implicating idea in his work.

> **Rejecting humanity is just ego in disguise.** The self can disappear for a moment, but it always returns. **Spiritual bypassing doesn't dissolve ego. It refines it.**

> This is why **holiness can become pride.** Why **purity can become violence.** Why **transcendence can become disconnection.**

The mechanism is that renunciation gives the self a superior project. Fasting, self-denial, ego suppression — all of it can be performed *by* the ego, which then has something new to be right about. Effort aimed at eliminating the self is still the self, working harder. → [[You do not start with a fixed self and then defend it, the act of defending creates the self]] · [[NL 25 - The Paradox of Self Improvement]]

Dave catches himself doing the emotional version of exactly this:

> For a long time, I was **"welcoming" feelings transactionally.** I'd welcome sadness so it would take its turn and leave, making room for my "real friends" — joy and excitement. **But that wasn't true welcoming. That was still resistance in disguise.**

→ [[You cannot welcome a feeling in order to get rid of it]] · [[You cannot welcome a feeling in order to get rid of it]]

And the biological version, where he concedes a position he'd held for years. A mentor told him: *"from my experience, once it becomes a biochemical thing, it really needs a biochemical treatment."*

> That landed. Because **biology precedes and greatly influences psychology.** I've tried to bifurcate biology and spirituality for so long, but they work together. When mental health becomes biochemical, it needs biochemical support alongside the spiritual work. **It's a "yes and," not an "either/or."**

Dave discloses the context: *"I've struggled with depression for most of my life — relatively mild, but periodic. Every four to six weeks I'd get gray."*
