# Need is dirty fuel and want is clean

Andrew arrives at the distinction himself while working through what "how amazing would it be if" asks of him, and it is the tightest available handle on [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]].

> Fundamentally what we're talking about is want versus need. When you *need* something, the energy you bring and the feeling and the suffering — it's a really negative, it's a dirty fuel. Whereas: man, I really want this, I'm excited about this, but I don't need it.

The same object, the same effort, two different states. Need makes the outcome a condition of your wellbeing, which is precisely Dave's definition of attachment. → [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]]

The reason this distinction is hard to adopt is captured in Jesse's account of going through it: it genuinely felt like Dave was asking him to give up his dreams, and that if he agreed he'd end up on the couch. He did it anyway.

> And then I did it, and there was just a different energy that took over. Not always, by the way — the other energy shows up all the time. It's just learning which one to choose in any given moment.

The honesty of that qualifier is worth keeping. This isn't a conversion; the old fuel remains available, and the practice is selection. → [[You cannot fake non-attachment because the body senses it]]

## Jesse's Language Test (HoE 08)

In [[HoE 08 - Desired Future State and Creative Tension]], [[Jesse Pujji]] adds a practical check Dave taught him years earlier: notice which words you reach for. *We need to be at 30 million* and *how awesome would it be if we got to 30 million* describe the same target, but the first comes with constriction and the second with pull. Dave's gloss: *should*, *need to* and *have to* presuppose something wrong that must be fixed; *want to* presupposes only a direction. The words are diagnostic because of the feeling that comes with them — saying the nicer sentence as lip service doesn't count.
