# Each emotion carries its own question, and that is where its wisdom is

Dave's operating claim is that emotions are intelligent, and that the intelligence is specific rather than general. Treating them as flavours of energy, each one arrives with a question attached:

| Emotion | Energy of | The question |
|---|---|---|
| **Anger** | boundaries | what is not okay here? |
| **Sadness** | letting go | what is finished? |
| **Fear** | the unknown | what wants to become known? |
| **Excitement / creative energy** | the unknown, unresisted | what wants to get created? |
| **Joy** | celebration | what wants to be celebrated? |

The general version is available even when you cannot place the specific one: "when you're feeling something, you can get curious — huh, what is this here to teach me?"

Dave makes a point about naming that is easy to skip past. The labels we inherited are poor pointers:

> If we humans had labeled it boundary energy, that might have been a more useful label than anger. 'Cause that's more like, oh, that's what it is. When we feel anger, we're like, ah, what's going on, what does that mean?

Jesse reports this as the practical unlock for a self-described thinker: feel it first, then ask what it is telling you. "Once there's a question of why are you feeling angry, that has led me to a lot of unlocks — in addition to just being with the anger."

The last clause matters. The question is not a substitute for feeling it. Analysing an emotion instead of feeling it is the head-route Dave warns about in [[More thinking makes clarity worse, like shaking sand in a bottle]].
