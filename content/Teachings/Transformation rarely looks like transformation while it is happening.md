# Transformation rarely looks like transformation while it is happening

The lesson Dave draws from a session he thought he had failed.

A CEO asked him to facilitate between two executives whose dysfunction was affecting the whole company. What was scheduled for an hour ran two and a half. Accusations, raised voices, no resolution.

> Twenty minutes in, I was questioning my approach. An hour in, I was wondering if I was making things worse. Two hours in, we still had no clear resolution.

> I left thinking I hadn't helped much at all.

A week later the CEO called: *"Something shifted. They're working together better than they have in months."*

> "It seems like they finally felt heard. Even though they were both taken aback at first hearing just how upset the other was, that honesty created space for actual understanding."

The four observations Dave takes from it:

> 1. **Transformation rarely looks like transformation while it's happening.** In the moment, it often feels like confusion, failure, or chaos.
> 2. **Increased discomfort often precedes breakthrough.** When things feel most uncomfortable, you're likely closest to the insight or shift you need.
> 3. **We try to rush past discomfort, but it's where the growth happens.**
> 4. **The messiest conversations often yield the deepest connections.** Superficial harmony maintains the status quo; productive discomfort creates change.

The practical consequence is about **judging in the moment**. Dave's real-time read of that session was wrong at twenty minutes, at one hour, and at two — and he is the expert in the room. If the discomfort is the mechanism, then the feeling of *this isn't working* is not evidence.
