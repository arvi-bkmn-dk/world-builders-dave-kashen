# What you focus on is where you steer

Dave supports the fear-drift correction with two physical analogies, and they carry more weight than the abstract version because they describe systems where the effect is immediate and unforgiving.

**Object fixation**, from motorcycle training: if you're looking at the wall instead of the road, you're likely to hit the wall. Attention is not a neutral observer of the path; it selects it.

**Point positive**, from whitewater guide school: if you're yelling and pointing at someone a hundred feet away in roaring water to "not go over there," all they perceive is *go over there*. Under load, a negation doesn't survive transmission — the listener receives the noun, not the "not."

The leadership implication follows directly. Under pressure, teams hear the thing you name, not the instruction wrapped around it. A warning about what must not happen installs that image as the target for everyone receiving it.

This is the practical reason the fear drift is expensive at team scale rather than merely unpleasant for the founder. → [[Fear is the undesired future, which is the same sentence framed backwards]] · [[Motivating a team from vision unlocks ideas hustle cannot reach]]
