# Fear lives at the edge between the familiar and unfamiliar parts of yourself

The idea arrives after four cliff jumps in Moab, in the debrief:

> Fear lives at the edge between the known and the unknown.

Dave then asks what is actually unknown — and since [[What you actually fear is a feeling]], the answer is not a future event:

> Fear lives at the edge between the **parts of yourself that are familiar** and the parts that are **unfamiliar**. When you realize that, you realize that moving toward the fear is key to expanding beyond your current edges.

The calibration example:

> You're probably fine experiencing some level of joy. That's familiar. You know that one. But **shame**? That's so unfamiliar that it's scary.

> If you could love your shame and get to know it, it wouldn't be so scary, right?

This is the link to shadow work — "identifying the parts of ourselves we haven't fully integrated yet. **Fear points us to those parts.**" And the disclaimer about identity claims:

> "Oh, I'm scared to be too loud." Why? "Because I'm not loud. That's not who I am." No. That's just a part of yourself you're not familiar with yet. Because **we all have all the parts.**

So fear stops being a stop sign: *"it shows you your edges so you can expand beyond them."* → The body signals that you are at an edge before the mind does · Your personal edges create your company's ceiling
