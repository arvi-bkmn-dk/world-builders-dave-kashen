# Can you enjoy the wanting

Joe Hudson's test, and Dave calls it his preferred one for telling an impulse from a compulsion.

> Can you enjoy the wanting?

> To me that's a good test. If it's an impulse, I can enjoy the want. Can I enjoy *wanting* to impact more people? If it's a compulsion, I don't enjoy the wanting — the wanting feels like a problem.

The elegance is that it tests the want directly rather than asking you to evaluate your motives, which is exactly the kind of analysis his work says people use to avoid feeling. → [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]

A clean want is pleasurable in itself. The anticipation is part of the good of it. A compulsion is a state of deficit — the wanting is a discomfort you are trying to end, so it can only be enjoyed once it stops.

Which explains why chasing a compulsive want never satisfies even when it succeeds: the thing you wanted to end was the wanting, and a new one arrives. → [[The whole game is rigged because when never arrives]]

It pairs with the somatic test — [[An impulse feels lighter and a compulsion has constriction attached to it]] — and with Dave's own worked example, the want to reach more people, which he watches slide between the two.
