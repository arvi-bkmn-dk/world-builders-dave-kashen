# Insecurity and arrogance are two sides of the same coin

Jesse opens on the everyday misuse of *ego* — *"oh my God, somebody's got such a big ego, he's arrogant, cocky"* — and people who say *"I don't have an ego at all."* Dave explains why the two meanings are connected rather than confused.

The technical definition first: **ego is the sense of separate self — the thought about who you are.**

> One of the traps of identifying as this conceptual self is there's a **temptation to aggrandize** it. What you'd hear an arrogant person say is *I'm amazing, I'm the greatest, I'm the best* — 'cause they want to aggrandize this concept of self.

And underneath that:

> Inherently, the challenge of being human and having an ego — being identified as this limited self — is there's an inherent sense that **something's incomplete about it.**

> It's basically like **insecurity and arrogance are two sides of the same coin.** They feel like there's something fundamentally wrong or missing, and so **to cover over that, they show up as arrogant.**

So the loud ego and the shrinking one are the same structure with opposite surfaces, which is why *"I don't have an ego"* is not a claim to modesty — it's a claim to having no self-concept at all, which nobody has.

Dave reaches for the **theory of holes** from the Diamond Approach: we are disconnected from our essential self in certain ways, *"and that disconnection feels like a hole, like a void. And we don't tend to recognise that that's what's happening"* — so we conclude something is missing out in the world.
