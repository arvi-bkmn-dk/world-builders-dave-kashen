# The best relationships are forged through the fire of conflict

Dave's parents divorced when he was five, with no visible tension beforehand:

> There was no visible tension, disagreement, upset. And I think a lot of parents think **oh, I need to protect my kids from anger.** But it was **shocking.**

> I think that may have been **some of the issue**, frankly. From my experience now — **the best relationships are forged through the fire of conflict**, and being able to actually get **closer** through conflict. Navigate disagreement, express anger, and realise **you can be with all that** — not sweep it all under the rug and pretend everything's okay.

The absence of conflict was not evidence of a healthy marriage. It was the mechanism by which the marriage ended without warning.

The story he tells against it is how he knew his wife was the one. Early in dating, he promised to help her move a TV and didn't, because he was working late:

> She was **really mad** that morning. So I went to work, she went to work, and I'm like — *oh God, are we done?* I'd **never experienced someone express that level of anger and not have it be some real significant thing.** So all day I was just off.

> Then she calls at the end of the day. *Hey, how was your day?* And I'm like — *don't you hate me? Aren't you mad?* She's like: **"No. I was mad this morning. I told you. I'm no longer mad."**

> I was like — **oh, that's a thing. You can just express your anger and be done with it.**

That is the whole argument in one exchange: anger that is expressed completes, and anger that is avoided accumulates into something that arrives later as a verdict.
