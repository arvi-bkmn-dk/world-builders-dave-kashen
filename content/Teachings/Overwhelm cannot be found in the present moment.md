# Overwhelm cannot be found in the present moment

The cleanest piece of live coaching in the batch. Dave asks Andrew three questions in sequence:

> Can you bring your attention to the present moment? … Without leaving the present moment, can you find any overwhelm? … And when is it not the present moment? Never.

> To me, that's the most important insight. It's literally an invention of mind.

The demonstration is self-administered, which is what makes it stick — Andrew searches and finds nothing, rather than being told there's nothing there.

Dave then specifies the required ingredients, and every one of them is imaginary:

> You have to imagine some kind of future. Imagine a certain way it should be, and then imagine it not going that way. Specifically, we generally imagine having to get a certain amount of stuff done, and then failing to do that or not having enough time. The whole thing is future tripping.

And the future resolves to something ordinary in every branch: you do the things, or you do some and discover it was enough, or it took less time than you thought, or you don't and you learn from that. None of the outcomes contains the overwhelm; the overwhelm exists only in the rehearsal.

> So much of it is just about trying to control the future.

This is the same structure as [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]] — the mind simulating a future it can't control, to manage a feeling it doesn't want.
