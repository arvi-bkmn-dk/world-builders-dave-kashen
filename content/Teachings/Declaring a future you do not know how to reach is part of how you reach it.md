# Declaring a future you do not know how to reach is part of how you reach it

The objection Dave hears most from leaders is a credibility worry: how can I tell the team we will get there when I do not know how?

His answer inverts the dependency. The knowing does not precede the declaring — the declaring is what generates the knowing.

> The creative tension is a big part of the how. If you're not willing to create the creative tension because you're scared, because you don't know how to get there — it's actually the creativity that is how you get there.

His example is JFK in 1961: a man on the moon and returned safely by the end of the decade, declared when "the technology didn't exist to invent the technology needed to actually make that happen." The declaration came first and the capability followed.

Dave also reads the grammar of it. JFK said *we will*, not *I want* — and that boldness is load-bearing:

> There is some boldness in just making a declaration, because it creates that possibility in people's minds that then becomes self-fulfilling.

This sits in tension with his own warning elsewhere about *need* and *must* language, and the resolution is where the declaration comes from. A declaration rooted in authentic desire is generative. The same words rooted in lack — "we must hit this or we're worthless" — deliver what Jesse calls the heaviness.

On the fear of declaring and missing, Dave is dismissive in a useful way: "everyone knows what you're doing. You're being a leader. They don't hear it as a promise or guarantee." And you do not need the full path — "you really only have to know the next step. The path is made by walking."
