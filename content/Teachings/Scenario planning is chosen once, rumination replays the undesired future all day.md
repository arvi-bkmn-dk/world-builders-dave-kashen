# Scenario planning is chosen once, rumination replays the undesired future all day

The objection founders raise to focusing on the desired future is that imagining what could go wrong helps them avoid it. Dave concedes the point, then separates the useful version from the one people actually live:

> While it’s true that it can be useful to do some scenario planning and imagine all the things that could go wrong and what you might do about them, this is an intentional exercise you choose to sit down and do, once.

> What most people actually experience is their minds drifting to imagine the Undesired Future state again and again through the day. That’s rumination, not scenario planning.

The difference isn't the content, which is the same bad outcomes. It's that one is a bounded, chosen exercise and the other is attention drifting back to the undesired future on repeat. That repetition is what produces fear-based tension. → [[Fear is the undesired future, which is the same sentence framed backwards]]

Dave's prescribed interrupt is to step into the body of the future self in the desired future, every time you notice the drift. → [[Step into the body of your future self]]

## Also in [[GA 02 - A CEO Coach Reflects on His Performance as a Dad, Dad Pod]]

The dad version. Founders can't be present at home because their minds keep looping on work, justified as *seeing around corners*. Dave's prescription is the same: an hour with a whiteboard to plan for what could go wrong, put it in the drawer, then go be present.
