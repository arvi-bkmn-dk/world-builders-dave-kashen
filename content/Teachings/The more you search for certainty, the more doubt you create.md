# The more you search for certainty, the more doubt you create

Dave opens with Alice at the crossroads asking the Cheshire Cat which way to go — *"that depends on where you're trying to get to"* — *"I don't know"* — *"then any road will do."*

The founder version is *"how do I know if I'm focused on the right priorities?"*, asked with "that mix of doubt and urgency."

> The real danger isn't choosing the wrong road. It's **waiting at the crossroads**, hoping certainty will show up.

> You're not confused about priorities. You're trapped in a **search for certainty that doesn't exist.**

The hard part:

> Most founders believe that good leadership means finding the right answer. So when you don't feel certain, you think something's wrong. You doubt your judgment. You second-guess every decision. You freeze.

> The certainty you're searching for is a **myth.** There is no "right answer" waiting to be discovered. Life isn't a quiz where you get an A if you pick the correct option.

> **The irony? The more you search for certainty, the more doubt you create.**

Each round of searching produces more considerations, which produce more uncertainty, which prompts more searching.

What Dave says the best founders do instead is not acquire certainty:

> **The world's best founders don't have more certainty than you. They've just stopped making uncertainty a problem.**

And they trust a different instrument — "their energy, their intuition, their whole body's wisdom" — which turns the question from *what should I focus on?* into *what lights me up? what feels aligned with where I'm trying to go?* → [[The Sailboat and the Holes]] · [[Fear and wonder meet the same uncertainty]]
