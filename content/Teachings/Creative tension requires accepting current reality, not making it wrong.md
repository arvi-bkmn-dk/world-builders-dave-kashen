# Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong

Most people intuitively sense that tension motivates, and they're right. Dave's point is that they then manufacture the tension the only way they know: out of lack and fear. Creative tension is generated differently — by being present to current reality and to a desired future state **at the same time**.

The load-bearing word is *accepting*. JFK is the example because of what he didn't do: he didn't lie about where they were (nothing had gone up), and he didn't make where they were wrong. No "we're horrible, the Russians are beating us, it'll be terrible if we don't make it." An honest present and an inspiring future, held together.

The failure mode Dave names on himself is what he calls collapsing the future into the present:

> We sometimes collapse the desired future state into the present and wish we were already there. I have this vision of impacting thousands or millions of people, and I've only impacted fifty. And I could then use my DFS as a weapon to say, oh God, what's wrong with me?

That is the exact moment a vision turns into dirty fuel. The vision didn't change; the relationship to the present did. Wanting a $10M business while a $1M business is *wrong* produces shame, not creation.
