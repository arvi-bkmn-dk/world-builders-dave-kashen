# Believing something should not be happening makes you feel righteous without changing it

Dave arrives at this while working the willingness questions on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and calls it a truth bomb. He had been below the line: wishing it weren't happening, blaming a long list of parties, certain it shouldn't be. Then he noticed what the certainty was actually doing:

> My believing it shouldn’t be happening isn’t actually making it happen less often; it just makes me *feel* like a righteous, good person without actually doing anything about it.

The belief feels like moral seriousness, and it works like an anaesthetic. It also blocks the look at responsibility. Once he let go of being right that it shouldn't be happening, he could ask how he contributes to the world being this way, and found real answers in money, attention and silence.

He is careful about what this does *not* mean. Violence is still abhorrent, and he keeps a strong preference for a future with less of it. The difference is preference versus righteousness. → [[Preference and righteousness produce different energy from the same goal]]
