# Your greatest strength when overused becomes your greatest weakness

Dave calls this the key insight of the Enneagram, and it explains why founders keep hitting the same wall despite genuine effort.

> Your greatest strength, when overused, becomes your greatest weakness.

It reframes the problem entirely. The thing limiting you is not a deficit to be fixed — it is the capability that got you here, applied past its useful range. Which is why working harder makes it worse: effort intensifies the strength, and the strength is the problem.

Dave's opening question sets it up:

> Have you ever wondered why, despite your best efforts to grow, you keep running into the same walls? Or why certain challenges seem to follow you from company to company, team to team, relationship to relationship?

> It's not that you're broken or failing.

His examples show the pattern producing its own evidence:

> The founder who micromanages isn't just struggling to delegate; they're unconsciously creating a team that can't function without them. The leader who avoids conflict isn't just preserving harmony; they're building a culture where important issues go unaddressed.

Each founder can point at a real situation justifying the behaviour. The situation was manufactured by the behaviour.

> Left unexamined, these patterns don't just hold you back — they **actively create the very problems you're working so hard to solve.**
