# Your board and investors are partners, not parents

Dave's explanation for why approval-seeking spikes specifically around boards — and it is neurological rather than a character flaw.

> Our brains are designed to seek approval from authority figures. This stems from our earliest survival strategy: pleasing our parents kept us safe and cared for. When board members and investors hold perceived power over your company's future, your primitive brain categorizes them as "parents" who must be pleased to ensure your survival.

The correction is a fact about the relationship rather than an affirmation:

> **Your parents controlled your survival as a child. Your board and investors chose to back YOU** because they believe in YOUR vision and leadership. They're extensions of your team, not your judges.

The direction of the original choice is the whole argument. They opted in, on the basis of you.

What the miscategorisation costs, in Dave's list:

> * **You filter information.** Instead of sharing the complete picture, you cherry-pick facts that make you look good.
> * **You avoid candid conversations** — downplaying problems until they become unavoidable crises
> * **You make decisions to please, not progress**
> * **Your creative thinking shuts down.** When your nervous system is in threat mode, your prefrontal cortex—responsible for strategic thinking and innovation—goes offline.

> The result? A business running on partial information, delayed course corrections, and compromised decision-making.

His pre-meeting reset: *"These are my partners in building this company."*
