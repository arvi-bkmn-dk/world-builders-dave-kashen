# How am I creating this is the question that converts acceptance into power

The creator question, and step 4 of [[The Responsibility Roadmap]]. Once a situation is no longer a problem, space opens to be genuinely curious about your own part in it.

Dave is emphatic about the intent, because the question has an obvious dark twin:

> The goal isn't to create self-blame, but instead self-empowerment.

The difference is detectable in what the question produces. Self-blame produces a verdict; the creator question produces specific, actionable sub-questions:

- Have I set clear expectations and made clear agreements?
- Did I withhold feedback or avoid a hard conversation?
- Did I assume someone understood what I meant when they didn't?
- Am I modelling the behaviour I want from my team?
- How did I create the conditions for this to occur?

Every one of those points at a lever. That is the tell — if asking how you created a situation yields something you can do differently, you're on the creator path; if it yields a characterisation of yourself, you've slid back into blame aimed inward. → [[Interpreting a situation as a problem is what drops you into blame]]

Dave also guards the other flank: taking responsibility doesn't mean you should be controlling everything. Owning your contribution is not claiming authorship of the whole situation.

> when you start to take responsibility in the first breath, it's like, oh, shit. Like, I created this, but in the next breath, you're like, oh, well, if I created it, then I can see how to create something different
