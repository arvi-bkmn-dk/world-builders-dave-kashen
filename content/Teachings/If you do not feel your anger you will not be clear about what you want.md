# If you do not feel your anger you will not be clear about what you want

The episode's central practical claim, and Jesse calls it the one still buzzing for him at the end: **anger is your compass to know your wants.**

> If you're someone who represses anger — *well, I don't even get angry, I'm cool as a cucumber, I'm kind* — I want you to know **what you're missing is attunement to your wants and needs.**

> When we're not in touch with our anger, **we're not clear about what we really want.**

The mechanism is simple once stated: anger fires when something you want is blocked or something you value is violated. Switch off the alarm and you lose the readout — *"you don't feel it when your wants are being violated."*

Joe Hudson's addition, which Dave credits:

> **Anger unresisted becomes determination.** And clarity. So when you're like *ah, I'm not clear, I don't know what I really want* — there's likely some anger that you're not fully feeling.

> So: **if you're not clear, go get angry. Go express anger.**

Why it matters at company scale — the **clarity tax**:

> I have a client who really wants a high-commitment culture where people work weekends. In my view, not good or bad. But there are founders who are like *eh, do I want that? Should I?* — they're looking for **what's the right answer**, what's the way I'm **supposed** to run my company. And because they don't feel their anger, **they don't have their own compass around what they want.**

> There's like this, this like **lack of clarity tax.** It's like you don't feel your anger, you don't create clarity. A lot of things are in limbo, both decisions, culture.

> **This is why it's so important for founders to access their authentic wants. You're kind of the spiritual leader of this company.**

Jesse's recognition: *"the indecisiveness — oh, if you don't feel your anger, you just don't know what you want."*
