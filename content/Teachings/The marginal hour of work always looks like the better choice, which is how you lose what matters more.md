# The marginal hour of work always looks like the better choice, which is how you lose what matters more

Dave credits [[Clayton Christensen]]'s *How Will You Measure Your Life*. Christensen treated the ambitious classmates who ended estranged from their families as a strategy failure: they had a goal and missed it. His diagnosis borrows his own case of incumbents ceding the low end of the market one reasonable decision at a time:

> in every moment the marginal decision to focus on work feels like the better choice

An extra hour of work has visible payoff; an extra hour with your kids doesn't. Choose that way often enough and "you win the battle lose the war", winning the thing that matters less and losing the thing that matters more.

Christensen's fix, as Dave retells it:

> to make rules for yourself and stick to them like gravity
