# If you think you are the storyteller you get trapped in the stories

The hinge of the episode:

> **If you think you're the storyteller, you get trapped in the stories.** You believe the thoughts are true. And that's what creates, I think, **a huge amount of suffering in the world.**

Dave is careful about what he is *not* saying:

> **It's not bad. It's not wrong. It's not a problem.** I often think of it just like **the heart pumps blood and the mind pumps thoughts.** It's just what it does.

He grants why the illusion is so convincing: *"I'm the only one that can hear them. They sound a lot like me. They're about me and my life, often in my own voice."*

Two demonstrations that it isn't you. Dave's is structural — examine the experience of hearing a voice in your head, and *"it becomes clear that in order to have that experience, there must be something listening."* → [[Hearing a voice in your head means there is a talker and a listener]]

Jesse's is blunter:

> Have you ever had the thought that you wanted to punch someone in the face? Everyone's like *yes.* And I'm like — **did you do it?** *No.* Well, **if you're your thoughts, then you would have.** So there must be somebody else making the call.

And the third: try to stop. *"If that's you, stop thinking."* Nobody can.

The cost of believing otherwise, given the storyteller's negativity bias:

> If you think that's you, and you spend your day listening to and engaged in the thoughts — **you'll have a really limited, negative, self-referential life.** You'll think life is really bad and **you're** really bad.

→ [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]] · [[Your mind is a wonderful servant but a terrible master]]
