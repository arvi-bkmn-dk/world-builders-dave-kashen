# What they really mean by security is lifestyle maintenance

A relabelling Dave says has helped clients, and it works by making the actual stake visible.

> Even when they talk about security, what they really mean is **lifestyle maintenance.**

He is careful to fence the claim first. There are people for whom money genuinely determines survival, "and I don't want to pretend that's not true." But for this audience:

> The chances of actually not having enough food, clothing and shelter for your physical survival are close to zero. So then what are we actually talking about? We're really talking about your lifestyle. Like — oh, I'd have to sell my house. Okay, maybe you would. And then you'd live somewhere else, a smaller house in a different neighbourhood.

Once stated plainly, the catastrophe turns out to be an inconvenience. And then Jesse spots what is actually underneath, and Dave agrees immediately:

> It becomes more about **image and shame.**

Dave demonstrates on himself rather than a client — imagining losing his clients and his investments and being unable to pay his mortgage:

> I can feel constriction in my throat, in my chest when I imagine it. I imagine shame. Having to tell everybody I can't afford my house, I have to sell it and move — head held low. *I was a bad coach, so I got fired by a bunch of people.*

> There's so much wrapped up in my identity, and that's really what's at risk. Which isn't true — it's a made-up construct that's distinct from who you actually are.

So the fear labelled *security* is usually a fear about **identity**, and it was never a survival calculation at all.
