# Needing support does not mean the inner work is not working

Dave has struggled with depression for most of his life. Years of inner work and medicine journeys made it much lighter, but every few weeks he'd still go gray, and he resisted medication his whole adult life. Part of it was fear about what it would say about him as a coach:

> Doesn't that mean the inner work isn't working?

His wife cut through it:

> "If your liver wasn't functioning properly, you would clearly take medicine. Why wouldn't you take medicine for your brain?"

A light dose worked. After a retreat on welcoming feelings he tested a theory, that welcoming sadness more fully would let him come off, and went off for six months. On a family holiday at one of the most beautiful beaches he'd seen, he couldn't access joy at all.

> The theory had failed.

What finally dissolved the rule was learning that [[Adyashanti]], one of the most respected spiritual teachers alive, takes SSRIs:

> If someone that genuinely awake made that choice, the equation I'd been running for years, that needing support means the inner work isn't working, simply doesn't hold.

> I felt relieved. Less ashamed. Like I'd been carrying a rule that was never actually true.
