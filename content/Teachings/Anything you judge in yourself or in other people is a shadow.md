# Anything you judge in yourself or in other people is a shadow

Dave's definition, and then the test that makes it usable.

The definition runs off the metaphor: the shadow is "the places where the sun doesn't shine — the parts of ourselves that we don't allow to see the light of day." Denied, repressed, negated, resisted. The everyday form of it is a sentence everyone says about themselves:

> A really simple way to think about it is: I am this way, I'm not that way. Oh, I am kind and friendly, and I'm not mean, I'm not a liar, I'm not a cheater. **All the things you think you're not are shadow.**

The test:

> Anything that you judge in yourself or in other people is a shadow.

Dave splits it into two forms. Sometimes you know you have the trait and judge it — "I know I can be arrogant, and when I see someone else being arrogant I judge them, because I also judge myself." Sometimes you don't allow it at all, and judge it when others do.

His clinical example is the second kind and it turns on a strength rather than a vice. A client was outraged by a colleague who said *if this happens, I'm going to quit* — "that's so manipulative and so coercive." What surfaced was that the client "didn't allow herself to set clear boundaries and be firm like that." The trait she couldn't tolerate was one she needed.

Jesse's opening example is the same shape: his wife, "the most giving" person, gets riled by selfishness in others.
