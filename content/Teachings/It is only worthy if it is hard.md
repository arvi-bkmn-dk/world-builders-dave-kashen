# It is only worthy if it is hard

Dave's answer to why founders who *do* know what they want still won't trust it.

> A lot of humans have this belief, often from childhood: **I don't deserve it.** Or — **it's only worthy if it's hard.** That's actually a really common one.

> This is why a lot of people **don't play in their zone of genius.** 'Cause you're like, *well, that's just easy for me.*

> We have this kind of **puritanical work ethic** culture. And so if it starts to feel easy, it's like — *yeah, but I want that, but I should do this.*

The belief is self-disqualifying in a precise way: it takes ease as evidence of unworthiness, so the exact signal that marks your zone of genius gets read as proof it doesn't count. The better you are at something, the less it feels like it should be allowed to be your work.

→ [[Spend more of your time in your zone of genius]] · [[The Focus-Motivation Model]] · [[Everyone is inherently worthy of what they authentically want]]

It also sits underneath [[Sometimes the most courageous thing to do is to let go]] — if hardness is what makes a thing worthy, then persisting through misery reads as virtue and walking away reads as failure, regardless of which one is true.
