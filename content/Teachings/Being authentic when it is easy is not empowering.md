# Being authentic when it is easy is not empowering

Working from Joe Hudson's definition — [[Empower is being fully authentic regardless of the consequences]] — Dave draws out the part that does the work:

> Almost by definition — when you're authentic **when it's easy**, that's actually **not that empowering.** It's like hanging out with my kids and I'm joking. It's great, it's lovely.

> **When you're authentic when it's scary** — that's the interesting connection.

The *regardless of the consequences* clause is the entire content. Authenticity that costs nothing is just comfort, and it is available to everyone at all times, which is why it produces no expansion.

And Dave's structural reason why following aliveness must feel frightening:

> Everything that we've done to date feels **safe.** If we haven't been living an authentic life up until now — which most people haven't, 'cause they're not following their actual aliveness — then **by definition it is going to be scary.** You're gonna have to access **parts of yourself that you haven't** in the past. Be ways you haven't been, do things you haven't done.

> So **building the capacity to be with fear is pretty critical to actually following aliveness.**

This is [[Fear lives at the edge between the familiar and unfamiliar parts of yourself]] stated as a life strategy rather than an observation: the unfamiliar parts are precisely where the unlived life is, so the fear is a map rather than a warning.

His own test case was literal — cliff jumping at the Welcoming Fear retreat: *"I'm terrified and I'm kind of enjoying being terrified."*
