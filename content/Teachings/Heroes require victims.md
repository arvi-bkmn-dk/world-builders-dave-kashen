# Heroes require victims

The sharpest line in the episode, and Dave's answer to the founder who asks what's wrong with saving the day — *that's my job, I'm the founder.*

> Heroes require victims. If you're in the hero role, you are seeing other people on the team as victims. You're not seeing them as empowered, responsible humans, leaders, contributors.

The role is not neutral toward the people in it. Casting yourself as the rescuer casts everyone else as someone who needs rescuing, and that assignment travels:

> Even just seeing them that way will affect the way they see themselves. You're kind of complicit in the story that they're a victim.

Then the cycle closes on the hero. People treated as victims don't feel grateful — "they feel diminished" — so the appreciation the hero is working for never arrives:

> The heroes keep heroing and then they expect to be celebrated and appreciated for their active heroism. And when they're not, they get really resentful.

Jesse plays the loop forward with a sales team: the founder fixes the comp plan, then resents the sales leader; the sales leader concludes there's no point setting a plan the founder will override, and leans out; the founder sees the disengagement as proof nobody takes ownership. Both readings are sincere. Each is produced by the other.

> It makes everything a little messy. It's just not very clean.
