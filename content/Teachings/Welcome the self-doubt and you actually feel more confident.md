# Welcome the self-doubt and you actually feel more confident

Self-doubt as shadow, and the inversion that resolves it.

> Most founders I know have self-doubt. And they think that other founders don't. Oh God, look at Sam Altman, look at Elon. And so they think, in order to be confident I need to not have self-doubt — which is another version of shadow. It's like, oh, doubt's bad, I gotta push that away.

Then:

> Ironically, when you just welcome it — oh yeah, I have some self-doubt — you actually feel more confident. You don't make it a problem that you have self-doubt. You don't make that mean that there's something wrong, or you're not effective, or you're not capable. You're just human.

The fight against the doubt was supplying the evidence that something was wrong. Remove the fight and the doubt becomes unremarkable.

Dave includes himself after fifteen years and plenty of accolades: "I have thoughts many times of — you're not doing this as well as you could, that session didn't go well, if some other coach were doing it, it would've been better. And I've learned to just welcome that."

Jesse's two examples land it commercially. Rick Elias, a billionaire founder, opening with *"we have no idea what we're doing"* — which Jesse initially read as modesty and then understood as a reminder, and as empowering. And Bootstrap Giants' sponsor pitches: Jesse's version was "I have no idea if this is gonna be effective for you guys, but I think it's worth us trying." They all tried it, it worked for none of them, and "they would all still take our phone calls."

Andrew names the alternative honestly: promise it, use the promise as fuel, and "two out of ten times feel shitty about myself for not getting there."
