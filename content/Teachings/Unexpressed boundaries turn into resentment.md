# Unexpressed boundaries turn into resentment

What happens to a boundary you never state — and Dave's point is that it doesn't simply vanish.

> **Unexpressed boundaries don't disappear — they turn into resentment.** And resentment always finds a way out, usually in ways that damage relationships **far more** than the original honest conversation would have.

The trade is the whole thing. Avoiding the conversation feels like avoiding damage; it is deferring damage at interest. The leak is worse than the conversation because it arrives without context, at a time you didn't choose, aimed at whatever happens to be in front of you.

> That avoided conversation is slowly poisoning your relationship and your leadership.

> Every day you don't, the resentment builds a little more.

Dave locates the real block not in kindness but in capacity — [[You are not avoiding the conversation, you are avoiding how you will feel when they react]] — and his navigation instrument is anger. **Use your anger as navigation**: it marks where a boundary wants setting, which is why leaders who can't feel it can't find theirs. → [[A leader who cannot feel anger cannot hold a boundary with their team]] · [[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]]

The same mechanism as [[Withhold, withdraw, project is how relationships calcify]], one step earlier: withholding produces the distance, and this names what fills it.
