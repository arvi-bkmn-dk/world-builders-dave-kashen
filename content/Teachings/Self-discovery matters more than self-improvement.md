# Self-discovery matters more than self-improvement

Dave's framing of what he is actually doing, and it inverts the category most people file coaching under.

> My real focus is on self-awareness and self-discovery, not self-improvement. I think most people are totally focused on self-improvement — how do I make myself better — and they're so focused on that they're not actually looking in the mirror to see what's really true for me now.

The improvement drive functions as a way of *not looking*. Attention runs to the gap between where you are and where you should be, and never rests on what is actually the case.

Then the claim he offers as something to play with rather than accept:

> At a certain level, your true self can't be improved. Discovering your true self is a way more important and powerful path than trying to improve your persona or personality.

If true, self-improvement is misdirected by construction — work applied to the persona, which is the part that was never the problem.

This is also why Jesse's first year felt like nothing was happening. He asked to be made a great CEO and got *first you must notice*, which he heard as Mr Miyagi wax-on-wax-off for about twelve months. He'd asked for improvement and was handed discovery. → [[The Talker and the Listener]]
