# Trying to speed up creates the fires you then sprint to put out

> What almost no one stops to consider is that trying to speed up is the thing putting them further behind.

Dave names three ways urgency compounds: it runs on adrenaline, it trades the important for the urgent, and it convinces you to carry everything yourself.

> You end up sprinting to put out fires you started by sprinting.

> Running faster on your own treadmill is how you stay exactly where you are.

What you actually want underneath speed is effectiveness:

> Give a coal miner and Sam Altman the same twenty-four hours, and the difference in what each one creates is so vast it's hard to argue, with a straight face, that what matters is how fast you're moving.

> There's a reason the saying goes slow is smooth, and smooth is fast.

His three moves: slow down enough to see clearly and choose the few things that matter, ask for the help you've been refusing, and prioritise well then let the rest go.

> if you let go of the belief that you're behind, what would you choose to do next?
