# Your life is limited by which sensations you are willing to feel

The conclusion Dave draws from decisions being emotional, and it is [[Your results are limited by which feelings you are unwilling to feel]] stated one level more precisely — at the level of physical sensation rather than named emotion.

> Most people make decisions in order to avoid certain feelings they don't want to feel. And if we're even more precise: in order to avoid feeling certain sensations in their body that they don't want to feel. So most people's lives are literally limited by which physical sensations they're willing to experience in their body.

The precision matters. "Avoiding an emotion" sounds psychological and optional. "Avoiding a sensation in the chest" is concrete, locatable, and explains why reasoning with yourself doesn't help — the aversion isn't operating at the level of argument.

It also explains why avoidance is invisible. Nobody experiences themselves declining a sensation; they experience themselves having a preference, a strategy, a reason. The sensation was ruled out upstream of anything that reached awareness.

Dave's stated goal for clients follows directly:

> One of my goals for my clients is to expand their capacity to be with more intense sensations — anger, fear, rage, sadness, grief. Because the more you can be with it, the less you're limited.

→ [[The Law of Expanding Possibilities]]

> [!quote] [[Most people's lives are limited by which physical sensations they're willing to feel|Most people's lives are limited by which physical sensations they're willing to feel]]

## Also in [[WS 01 - The Inner Keys to Becoming a World-Class Leader]]

Dave's own example is being a young man attracted to someone at a bar and not going over, because of a constricted belly and a tight throat he didn't want to feel.

> So I let my behavior be limited by which physical sensations I'm willing to feel.
