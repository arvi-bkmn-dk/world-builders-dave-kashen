# Fulfilled just means feeling full

A word taken literally, and it relocates the whole goal.

> Fulfilled is such an interesting word. People throw it around a lot — oh, I want to feel fulfilled. And they think that means fulfilled like I've achieved some outcome. But it's just like **feeling full**. That's literally the feeling you want.

Heard as achievement, fulfilment is an outcome you might reach later. Heard as *full*, it is a present-tense state with a specific obstacle: the parts you have excluded.

> That's wholeness. You feel full when you allow — and like you said, Jesse, integrate all the parts.

Which makes shadow work the direct route rather than a detour. Jesse's word for it is **integration**: "by integrating all of your parts into your whole, you become more peaceful, and you become more free."

The argument this dismantles is the founder's implicit trade — that fulfilment is downstream of success. Dave calls it "a trap if you think that the success and the money are gonna give you the sense of wholeness," and notes the reverse works better: recognise you are already whole and "you can actually be way more effective and way more in flow as you create whatever you're trying to create."

He is careful that this is not an argument against ambition: "I want my clients to have everything that they want, including their companies being successful and making lots of money." The claim is about ordering, not renunciation. "To me it's the ultimate win-win."
