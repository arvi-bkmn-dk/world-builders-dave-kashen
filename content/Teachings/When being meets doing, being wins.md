# When being meets doing, being wins

Level 3 of [[The Four Levels of Behavior Change]], and the reason habit tactics fail for people who are executing them perfectly.

> Sometimes the issue is **who you believe you are.**

> First, there's your **self-concept.** The labels you carry. *"I'm not a gym person." "I'm lazy." "I'm disciplined."*

> **When being meets doing, being wins.** In other words, your beliefs about who you are will **override any habit you try to form.**

The second face of identity is heavier than a label:

> There's **self-judgment.** This is the voice that says *"I'm not enough"* or *"I'm unworthy."* Self-judgment creates a **sense of lack**, a feeling that something is fundamentally missing.

> And when you're operating from that sense of lack, behavior change often requires **lots of force and friction.** It can happen, but it's exhausting.

That last concession matters — Dave doesn't claim it's impossible from lack, only that it costs everything you have. Which is exactly his own Wellsphere years. → [[I felt more pressure to not fail than to succeed]]

His illustration of the alternative is the strongest case in his work. A client who had smoked for 30 years:

> I asked him, *"are you willing to love yourself unconditionally?"* He realized he wasn't. He looked at the parts of himself he wasn't loving and was able to accept and love those parts. Then he started to feel the love he had for his son and just feel that energy. And he asked himself, *"could I point that at myself?"*

> For the first time in his life, he felt real self-love. And **almost immediately quit a 30-year nicotine addiction.**
