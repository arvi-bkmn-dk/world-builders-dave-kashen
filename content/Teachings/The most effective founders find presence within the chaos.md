# The most effective founders find presence within the chaos

Dave's correction to what meditation practice is actually for.

> While meditation is a powerful practice, the most effective founders **aren't the ones who meditate for 20 minutes every morning.** They're the ones who can find presence within the chaos. The ones who can drop into awareness while walking to their next meeting, during a difficult conversation, or while reading their emails.

The distinction is between practice as a **session** and practice as a **capacity**. Twenty minutes of stillness is training; the return on it is whether anything survives contact with a hard conversation.

> Most meditation practices **pull you away from your life.** But what if we could bring meditation **into** your life?

He then does the unusual thing of demonstrating rather than describing — the newsletter *is* the practice. The reader is walked through softening focus until peripheral vision returns, noticing that the words still read effortlessly with relaxed attention, expanding awareness into temperature and sound and the weight of the body, feeling the contact points of the device, and watching thoughts arrive without following them.

The instruction that carries it: feel the raw sensations rather than labelling them.

> Instead of labeling your emotions with your mind ("I'm anxious" or "I'm calm"), feel the raw sensations. Not your thoughts about your body. Not the story about why you feel a certain way. **The direct, physical sensations themselves.**

This is [[Expand your presence surface area]] as a practice rather than a concept — widening the set of conditions under which presence holds, starting with something as ordinary as an email.
