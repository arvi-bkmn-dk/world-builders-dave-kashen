# You will never do more than you can in a given period

A tautology Dave uses as a lever, because stated plainly it dissolves the arithmetic behind time scarcity.

> * You will never do more than you can in a given period.
> * The only thing that makes sense is to prioritize well and move deliberately.

The feeling of not having enough time is not a measurement. It is a comparison against an imagined volume of work that was never possible:

> The scarcity of time is a product of the mind — the result of imagining a future where we "need" to do more than is reasonably possible.

And because it requires that imagined future, it cannot survive presence:

> If you bring your attention fully to the present moment, you can find no scarcity of time. And it's never not the present moment. In each moment, you are simply doing what you're doing.

> The idea that you're not doing enough or don't have enough time is a story your mind makes up.

Dave's claim for dropping the story is practical rather than consoling: "letting go of the story of *not enough* calms your system and helps you think clearly, enabling better prioritization." The panic about time is itself degrading the judgment you would need to use time well.

Same structure as [[Overwhelm cannot be found in the present moment]] and [[In the present moment can you find any lack of safety]] — a state that requires leaving the present to sustain.
