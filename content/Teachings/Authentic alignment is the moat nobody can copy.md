# Authentic alignment is the moat nobody can copy

If AI collapses the cost of building, software itself may stop being what makes a company hard to copy. Dave's question is what's left, and his answer is human:

> One of the things I find most interesting about human beings is that, for whatever reason, different humans get genuinely energized by different things.

A founder energised by a particular problem spends more time and energy on it than anyone else.

> I think we could argue that authentic alignment, at a human level, will matter more than ever.

> The founder who can't stop thinking about their customer's problem is playing a different game entirely.

> And here's why this holds up in the market: you can't fake it.

His tests for finding it:

- **What genuinely lights you up?** Would you still pursue it if nobody were watching?
- **Authentic desire, or ego desire?** If the problem stopped looking like a good market tomorrow, would you still want to spend your years on it?
- **Where are you extracting, and where are you creating?**
- **When uncertainty spikes, what do you reach for first?** A confident prediction, or *I don't know*?
