# You cannot force a flower to bloom faster

The limit on what a leader can do about someone else's growth.

> You **can't force** human development. You can only **create the conditions** for it to emerge.

> As Lao Tzu wrote: *"Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished."*

> You can't force a flower to bloom faster, but when you provide the right soil, water, and sunlight, it flourishes naturally. The same is true for your team.

The distinction is between **driving** and **gardening**, and it disqualifies most of what founders reach for when someone isn't developing — pushing harder, more accountability, more direct feedback. Those are attempts to force a bloom.

It also resolves the frustration Dave opens the newsletter with. The founder who felt he was "babysitting adults" was trying to produce ownership in people directly. Ownership isn't installable; conditions for it are.

What the conditions are, in Dave's framing: an aim that isn't control or fixing, but "creating the conditions for people to understand themselves," from which compassion emerges — and "where there is understanding and compassion, judgment, conflict, and dysfunction can't survive."

The practical version is [[CORE Conversations]], which is why he restates the framework here: curiosity over certainty, openness over withholding, responsibility over victimhood, empathy over judgment.

Same claim as [[Nature grows and we are part of nature]], pointed outward at a team instead of inward at yourself. Dave's own arrival at it: *"if I didn't grow up… neither could they."*
