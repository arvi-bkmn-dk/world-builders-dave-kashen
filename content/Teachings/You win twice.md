# You win twice

The promise the whole newsletter is built on, stated in the first email:

> Chasing only the Outer Game leads to burnout and dissatisfaction — **even if you win.** Mastering the Inner Game allows you to **separate your self-worth from business outcomes**, which both makes the journey more enjoyable and fulfilling **and makes you more likely to succeed.**

> **You win twice.**

The structure of the claim is what makes it more than a slogan. It is not *inner work is worth it even though it costs you outer results* — it is that the same work produces both, because the state that makes a life enjoyable is also the most resourceful state to build from. → [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]]

Dave returns to it as the answer to why coaching unicorn founders leads to spirituality:

> They get the inner experience they were actually chasing all along: **peace, freedom, aliveness, feeling enough. It was always available.**

> And **because** they're operating from those states, **the outer results become easier to create.** It's easier to lead. Easier to bring out the clean fuel.

The word *twice* is doing specific work. The founders who go direct do not trade one win for the other, and they do not get the inner win as a consolation — they get the thing they were chasing **and** the thing they thought they had to chase it through.

→ [[Peace and success are completely independent of each other]] · [[You can build a big company from love, presence and a beautiful inner state]]

## Also in [[GA 01 - The Inner Game of Life, Danny Miranda Podcast 394]]

The interview ends on two games. Game one is optimising external conditions to match the ego's idea of how life should be. Game two is learning to be present with a wider and wider range of circumstances, like the sky that doesn't mind the weather.

> Game two is can I learn to be with a wider and wider variety of circumstances?

> You get to win twice.

## Also in [[GA 03 - The Inner Game of Entrepreneurship, The Conscious Entrepreneur Podcast]]

Dave's cleanest statement of the two games:

> there's two primary games that people tend to play in life one is um can I get life to match my mind's idea of how life should be the other is can I just be with life as it is

The second is winnable, and you win twice: first the peace of presence, then results that come more easily from that state.
