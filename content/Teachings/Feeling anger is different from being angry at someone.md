# Feeling anger is different from being angry at someone

The distinction that keeps the rest of the episode from being read as permission.

> That's a really important distinction. **Anger — feeling anger, being with anger — versus anger *at*.**

> People sometimes hear *oh, anger can be healthy, it's important to feel your anger* — **I'm not advocating for go get angry at people.**

Why anger *at* is a different thing entirely:

> That tends to come from **blame.** It's below the line. You're likely **not taking responsibility.**

→ [[Above and Below the Line]] · [[Responsibility is not self-blame]]

Dave's explanation for why anger gravitates outward more than any other emotion is mechanical: it is the **fight** response. *"Generally the fight is with someone — or in the case of traffic, a car that we perceive as attacking us."* Sadness by contrast reads more like flight or freeze.

And the pressure behind it:

> A lot of it's 'cause we just have **so much repressed anger.**

He offers himself as the case, unflatteringly: *"I was such a jerk with customer service reps. Anonymous, never see them again… that's where I would let my anger come out."* Jesse's reply: *"these poor customer service reps are America's therapists."*

The healthy form is **revealed**, not aimed:

> I like this specific phraseology: **"I notice I feel angry when you micromanage me"** — in part because I'm telling myself and the person that **I am in the driver's seat.** I'm not out of control in reactive anger. **I am the awareness noticing.** *Anger is here.* **You're not making me angry.**
