# Your authentic wants are the right answer for you

Dave's substitution for the question founders are actually stuck on.

> It's really hard to be clear on the right answer, because **the right answer is almost always unknowable. Life is not a math puzzle.** Three times seven has a right answer. But pretty much everything we do, it's unknowable what the right answer is — **even after we do it**, whether it was the right answer.

> So what is more true is: **they're not clear about what they really want.**

> I spend a lot of time supporting clients to go from *what's the right answer* to **what do I really want** — because I believe that **your authentic wants and impulses are the right answer for you.**

The business case comes from Jesse, via Rick Elias, and it's the moment Jesse says he couldn't unsee:

> I was like, *well, here's the market, here's how we have to attack it.* And he's like, **"but it doesn't sound like you actually wanna do that."** And I'm like, *but who cares what I want? This is what's right for the business.*

> He was like — **you can't build a business you don't want to.** If you're not excited about it, if you don't wanna run it, **it doesn't matter how right it is.**

Dave's reframe of founder-market fit follows: not *do you have experience in this market* but **is this a strategy you are truly excited about.** And the reason it compounds — genuine excitement is *"this infectious energy that draws talent and capital,"* while forcing yourself through an inauthentic strategy *"is gonna drain your energy until you're burnt out."* → [[Startups do not fail because they run out of money, they fail because founders run out of energy]]
