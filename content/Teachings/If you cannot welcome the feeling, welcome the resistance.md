# If you cannot welcome the feeling, welcome the resistance

The move Dave makes — quoting Joe Hudson — when a client honestly can't do the thing being asked.

Andrew has located a sensation in his stomach: tightening, buzzing, swirling, "like if you had 1000 flies in your stomach all trying to get attention." Asked to welcome it, he refuses truthfully: *what I want to tell you is no, I hate them, I want to poop them out of my body.* He tries, and reports he didn't really do it.

> To quote Joe Hudson: if you can't welcome the feeling, welcome the resistance.

Which is elegant, because the resistance is *also* present, and is the thing actually available. It converts a failed instruction into a workable one without pretending the first one succeeded. There is no rung of [[The Gradation of Allowing]] you can't step onto, because whatever is stopping you becomes the new object.

Dave's diagnosis of what was happening:

> There's a bunch of sensations we judge and label as bad, and then we resist them — and what you resist persists. So they stick around and become like a mood, almost chronic. Where the original wave of fear, if you just fully allowed it, you would likely no longer be experiencing it.

The chronic quality is the signature of resistance, not of the feeling. → [[An unresisted emotion moves through the body in 60 to 90 seconds]]

Worth noting how Dave handles the refusal: "it's okay if in this moment you're not willing or ready to fully allow it" — and then he asks again, for *any more willingness*. Incremental, and genuinely optional. → [[The Willingness Question]]
