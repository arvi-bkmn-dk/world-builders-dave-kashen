# Your life reflects your unconscious commitments

Dave reaches for the iceberg image — what's conscious above the water, what's unconscious below — and draws the conclusion that makes it useful:

> Our life is essentially a reflection of our unconscious commitments.

Applied to Andrew, who has just described burning out as something that happens to him with a certain inevitability: "maybe I'm just so unconsciously committed to burning out. And so it is."

The reframe converts a pattern from misfortune into evidence. If your life keeps producing the same outcome, the most economical explanation is that something in you is committed to it — and the investigative question follows:

> What are you getting from playing this game of push, push, push, burn out?

Note it asks what you *get*, not what's wrong with you. An unconscious commitment persists because it pays something — relief, an ending, permission to stop, a confirmation. Finding the payment is what makes the commitment available to change.

This is the creator question aimed at a recurring pattern rather than a single incident. → How am I creating this is the question that converts acceptance into power
