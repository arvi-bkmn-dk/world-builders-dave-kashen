# Meditation builds resilience by stopping the downward spiral

Dave's second named benefit, aimed squarely at founders:

> Especially for founders — what's more important than resilience? It's just constantly facing setbacks and challenges.

The mechanism isn't toughness. It's that a specific chain doesn't get started:

> The more you're meditating, the more you're able to bounce back quickly, because you don't go down the downward spiral that most people do: oh God, this thing happened, extrapolate that into the future, it's going to be terrible, I'm terrible.

Three moves, and only the first is real. An event occurs; it gets extrapolated forward; the extrapolation becomes a verdict on the self. The event was survivable — the spiral is what takes the day.

> You just see it for what it is. Okay, this was a setback. What's my desired future state? You can stay present and move toward the future you want instead of spinning out of control.

Note that the recovery routes straight into [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]. Resilience here isn't endurance; it's the ability to get back to the present quickly enough that the DFS can pull again.

Dave's supporting anecdote: Joe Hudson interviewed Sam Altman about meditation, and Altman said he'd thought it was a waste of time and a bunch of BS — then described it as a superpower for being resilient, not getting reactive, seeing things more for what they are, and being more playful about it.
