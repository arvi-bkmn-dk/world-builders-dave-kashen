# You do not start with a fixed self and then defend it, the act of defending creates the self

The sharpest statement of **selfing** in his work, and it inverts the obvious order.

> You don’t start with a fixed self and then defend it. The act of defending creates the self.

> That’s selfing. It’s not something you are—it’s something your mind does, automatically, continuously throughout the day.

Dave walks the loop it runs on, through a board meeting:

| Stage | In the room |
|---|---|
| **Contact** | a partner challenges your product roadmap |
| **Feeling** | tightness in the chest — anxiety |
| **Craving** | *"they're questioning my competence"* — to be seen as capable |
| **Becoming** | you explain and justify at length, assuming the confident-decisive-CEO identity |
| **Clinging** | you replay every comment, reread the follow-up email three times |
| **Death** | a curt reply lands, and the self-image you were defending is threatened |

The consequence for leadership is immediate:

> They’re not responding to what’s actually happening in the room. They’re reacting to how it might reflect on them. Not listening to understand, but to protect.

And the cost, stated as a trade:

> And here's what I've learned after 15 years of coaching some of the world's most successful founders: the more energy you spend preserving that identity, the less present, available, and adaptive you become…
