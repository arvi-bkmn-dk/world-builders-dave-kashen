# Fear of failure gets its power from staying vague

A founder Dave worked with wound down his company, dreading his investors, some of whom had come in months earlier. They were supportive; several asked what he'd do next so they could back it. His line:

> "No one has judged me nearly as much as I've judged myself."

> He went through the actual "failure" to learn that. You don't have to.

Dave's image for why the fear was so much bigger than the reality:

> I think of it a little like taking the boogeyman out of the closet. In the closet, it's terrifying. Then you turn the lights on and realize it's a mop.

> The dark is what gives fear its power.

Left vague, the things that would actually happen and the stories about them fuse into one dread. Looked at closely, the facts (emailing investors, telling the team) are hard but doable.

> Nearly all of the fear lives in that story layer.

> From my experience with hundreds of clients, including many who failed completely, 99.999% of those fear-based stories turn out to be untrue.
