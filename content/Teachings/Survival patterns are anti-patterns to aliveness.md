# Survival patterns are anti-patterns to aliveness

> In general, I think our **survival patterns are anti-patterns to full aliveness.** When we're trying to protect ourselves from something, we're generally not following aliveness — **we're following safety and self-protection.**

> If you're focusing on **survival**, you're not focusing on **fully living.**

The example Dave gives is a client at a party, asked by another parent whether he enjoys his job, who *"had to really face — eh, not that much."* What came out of it is the distinction worth keeping:

> It led to this really deep reflection on: **is it the job itself, or is it the way I'm doing the job?** Which is often the case.

What he was actually doing was **heroing** his whole team out of fear of things going wrong — taking over whenever a customer was upset, telling everyone what to do, exhausting himself.

> He **loved what he was actually doing.** But the **heroing** was not enjoyable.

So the aliveness had not drained from the work. It had drained from a survival pattern wrapped around the work — which is a repairable problem, and a completely different one from *I'm in the wrong job.*

> **Enjoyment and aliveness are pretty closely tied together.** So that could be another simple way to ask yourself: **how much am I enjoying what I'm doing?**
