# Time management is often the presenting issue for fear avoidance

> A lot of founders show up with **time management** as their presenting issue. *I want help managing my time. I need a better system. I need to understand my priorities.*

> And really what's happening is they **spend time on the things that are comfortable, easy, they know how to do** — they can check the boxes — **and avoid doing the things that they actually have fear around.** That's often the real issue.

Jesse supplies the live example on himself, mid-episode. Gateway X is considering buying businesses rather than building them; on the whiteboard it sounds fun. The actual next step — writing a thesis page, then talking to twenty or thirty companies — keeps not happening:

> My calendar just seems busy, like I haven't been able to make time for it. And I think you and I last week, we were like — **oh wait, it's actually some fear.**

And his observation about why this one is hard to catch: *"sometimes it's not obvious that you're stuck."* A full calendar looks like diligence. The avoided task never appears as avoidance; it appears as something you'll get to.
