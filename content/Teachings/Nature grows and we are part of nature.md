# Nature grows and we are part of nature

The argument that growth does not require the intention to grow.

> The oak tree is not like — I need to get taller and expand my branches. Nature grows. We are part of nature. We will naturally grow.

Which reverses where attention should go:

> So putting your attention on loving yourself as you are, being authentic, will lead to all the growth and improvement that wants to happen.

Joe Hudson's demonstration is an experiment Dave recounts: a client told not to improve for two weeks — nothing, no self-work. "And of course, in the two weeks, the client's like, oh my God, I grew in this way and this way." Growth continued once the effort to produce it stopped.

Jesse arrives at the same place through his children: "they really just grow. It doesn't really matter what you do or don't do. I could die tomorrow and my kids will end up becoming adults." Dave extends it to learning to walk — no toddler makes a plan; "they just want to walk, and so they start walking." The drive is instinctive and curiosity-led, not disciplined.

The claim Dave attaches at the end is the one that matters for founders: the improvement project "actually tends to make the improvement less efficient and effective." Self-improvement from *something is wrong with me* runs on the same fuel as the grind, with the same cost. → [[The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are]]
