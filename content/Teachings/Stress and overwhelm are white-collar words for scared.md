# Stress and overwhelm are white-collar words for scared

> I heard it said once — I think this is PC to say — stress and overwhelm are white collar words for scared. You're just scared.

The translation matters because the two vocabularies point somewhere different. *Stressed* and *overwhelmed* describe a workload and imply a workload solution: more hours, better systems, fewer commitments. *Scared* describes you, and points at something that can actually be felt through.

Dave reports the relief that arrives with the accurate word:

> I'll often try to help them identify what they're actually feeling — because you're scared. Even that distinction is sometimes useful. Their system almost calms down when they realise: oh, I'm just scared.

The calming is worth noticing. It happens on naming alone, before anything is solved, which is evidence that the precise word was doing work the vague one couldn't.

His account of where the fear comes from:

> What creates fear is imagining the future we don't want to have happen. If a snake jumps up at you on a trail, that's genuine, authentic, in-the-moment fear. But most of us are just manufacturing fear all the time by imagining futures we don't want.

Two different things sharing a name. Snake-fear is information about the present. Manufactured fear is a story, and is what [[Overwhelm cannot be found in the present moment|can't survive the present-moment test]].
