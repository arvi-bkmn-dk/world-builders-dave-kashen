# Acceptance is not resignation

Jesse opens with his own reaction the first time Dave asked the question — at $10M revenue, aiming at $100M:

> *Can you accept that you're at 10 million?* And I was like — **no way. What are you talking about? It's unacceptable.** It needs to improve, it needs to grow. It felt like you were asking me to **throw in the towel**… **I'm looking for a new coach.**

Dave's definition:

> **Acceptance is letting go of wishing anything about your current experience was different than it is.** It's more of a **letting go, a release of resistance,** than anything else.

And the distinction that dissolves the objection:

> One of the key distinctions is **acceptance versus resignation.** People conflate that. The mind says: *if I accept my company exactly as it is, that means it's not gonna improve* — that **I'm resigned to it continuing to be that way.**

> And what's ironic, and I think really a paradox, is: **accepting things the way they are maximizes the probability of them evolving and changing and growing.**

The mechanism is the **Being-Becoming Paradox**: *"I accept myself exactly as I am — that's **being** — and I can imagine an even better version of me in the future. Being able to hold that paradox creates a different kind of tension: **creative tension.**"* → [[Creative Tension]] · [[The Focus-Motivation Model]]

Two images for how ordinary acceptance actually is. Jesse's **missed flight**: the tension is all in the sprint to the gate, and the moment it's missed, *"my mind is clear, I'm going into the next steps. I don't bring the past into the present moment."* Dave's **roadblock**: nobody sits at one asking *why me, what's wrong with me* — *"they're just like, okay, I guess I gotta find another way."*

> If you could live life that way, especially as a founder — **'cause there's tons of roadblocks and setbacks.**

> **That's how you achieve wild success without suffering:** you get really practiced at accepting the now as it is and imagining some desired future.
