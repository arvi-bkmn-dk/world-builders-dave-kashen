# Willing means literally right now, not someday in the future

Dave's unpacking of the word that punctuates his coaching. He hyphenates it to make the root visible — **will-ing** — and the meaning follows from that.

> A lot of people use the word willing like, "oh yeah, I'd be willing to lose 10 pounds someday in the future." That's not how I mean it. I mean, literally right now: are you willing to use your human will to have this experience?

The distinction is between an intention about the future and an act in the present. Future willingness costs nothing and commits nothing, which is exactly why it's so readily given. Present willingness is a decision being made while you answer.

Jesse adds the coaching reason the question is asked at all: without willingness, the work doesn't land. "You could say, *Andrew, could you learn from this?* and he'd say yes, I could — and that's not the same thing as *are you willing to learn from it*." Capability is never the constraint.

Andrew's "no" later in the episode is the proof the question is real. A question that can only be answered yes isn't a question. → [[The Willingness Question]]
