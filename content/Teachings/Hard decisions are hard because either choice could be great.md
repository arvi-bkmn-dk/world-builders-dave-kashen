# Hard decisions are hard because either choice could be great

> Hard decisions often don't matter as much as you think. **If one option was obviously better, it would be obvious.** Hard decisions are hard because either choice could be great.

> The real risk is **staying stuck.**

The difficulty is evidence of near-equivalence, not of high stakes. The agonising is spent on the margin between two good outcomes, while the cost of the delay itself goes uncounted.

This is the practical companion to [[Indecision is a feeling problem, not a thinking problem]] — once you notice the options are close, the thing you are actually protecting yourself from is the feeling of having been wrong, not a materially worse result.
