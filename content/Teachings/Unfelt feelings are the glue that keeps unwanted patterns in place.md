# Unfelt feelings are the glue that keeps unwanted patterns in place

Dave credits the line to Tara at Art of Accomplishment, and says he is leaning on it more and more with clients.

> Unfelt feelings are the glue that keep unwanted patterns in place.

It explains a failure everyone has experienced: seeing clearly that a belief is false and being unable to drop it anyway. Dave's examples are the lifelong ones — "I'm conflict avoidant," "I'm a nice guy," and above all the founder's "I'm an imposter, I'm not good enough."

> They're like, could you let go of that belief? They're like, well, I can't. And it's interesting — why can't you let go of that belief?

Insight alone does not dissolve it, and neither does the next layer of insight. A founder may correctly identify that the belief has been driving them and that they are scared to release it — "okay, that's a useful insight" — and still be stuck:

> But until they actually feel the fear, it doesn't go.

Dave's revision of his own method is the notable part. For years he worked the belief directly — "what evidence do you have that you are worthwhile even without being busy?" — which he still considers useful. What he now sees is that a feeling underneath is holding the belief in position, and that the feeling has to be felt or the belief regenerates.

He pairs this with the operating instruction it implies: **feel the sensation, drop the story.**

> The common pattern is we don't feel the feeling, but we believe the story. You wanna swap it, so you see through the belief — and at the same time fully feel literally the physical sensations of it.

His own worked example is shame, described from the inside: hunched over, small, wanting to hide, throat closing, chest and stomach constricting. "If I can allow all that, the shame tends to move through, and that pattern can shift."

> [!quote] [[Unfelt feelings are the glue that keep unwanted patterns in place|Unfelt feelings are the glue that keep unwanted patterns in place]]

## Also in [[WS 01 - The Inner Keys to Becoming a World-Class Leader]]

Dave calls it non-intuitive, something he only saw after practicing it:

> All of our stuck patterns are actually like, the glue that keeps them in place is some emotion we haven't yet been willing to feel.

His common example is overthinking: *"I don't wanna feel the fear, so I go into my head and I try to figure it out."*
