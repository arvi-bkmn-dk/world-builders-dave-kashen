# Founder is not a role, it is a vestige of time

The conversation Dave says co-founders almost never have, and the bluntest line in the newsletter:

> **Founder is not a role; it's a vestige of time.** Just because you founded the company doesn't mean you're **entitled** to that seat forever.

> As companies grow, the non-CEO co-founder often gets **leveled over time and ends up with a boss.**

The questions to ask before it's live:

> **Is the non-CEO truly okay ceding authority?** Does the CEO have the authority to decide about everyone's role, **including letting go of the co-founder if they're not performing?** Make clear agreements about how governance will work.

> Subject both of you to **vesting over four years or more.** I've seen co-founders leave with a huge percentage of the company even though they didn't contribute that much.

And the reason this belongs in a newsletter about choosing well rather than one about protecting yourself:

> At the beginning, you're bright-eyed and optimistic. But it's important to be grounded in reality. These things don't always work out, and **good governance protects both of you.**

Dave's related warning about how equity gets set — where the fix is somatic, not analytical:

> I've seen co-founders where the equity split was a **hero move**: accepting 30-70 because the other person is more experienced, **even though something inside felt constricted.**

> If you sense something feels off but convince yourself to just do it because you don't want to seem disagreeable, **that can fester for years.**
