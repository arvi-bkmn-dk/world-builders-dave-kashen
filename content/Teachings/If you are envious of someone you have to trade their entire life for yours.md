# If you are envious of someone you have to trade their entire life for yours

The test Gagan uses on his own comparison, from Naval:

> If you're envious of someone, think of it like you have to trade their entire life for your life.

Envy works by extraction — you take the one slice you want and imagine it added to everything you already have. The trade makes that impossible. It is all of it or none.

Gagan runs it live: *would I give up my wife to be CEO of Airbnb?*

> Absolutely not. Like, like there's, there's not a question in my life.

And the qualifier, which is the honest part: "now, if I can bring my wife with me — yes." Dave finishes it: "you can't just take the one slice you're envious of and leave all the rest."

The story underneath is the one that changed his comparison. Gagan flew back to the US during a years-long sabbatical for one meeting — Brian Chesky, recruiting him to Airbnb. Gagan described what he was doing, travelling and staying a month in each place, and Chesky said: *if it weren't for Airbnb, I'd be doing exactly that.*

> That is a guy I was comparing myself to.

Dave's general form: "the human mind tends to compare, and notice — it doesn't tend to compare to all the billions of people who are quote worse off than us. It just compares to the few people we can find who are quote better off."
