# You make it mean so much more about you than it actually means

Dave names this as the core takeaway he wants entrepreneurs to leave with.

> You make it mean so much more about you than it actually means. Like, you make it so personal. And it's not that personal.

> Whether you end up Larry, Sergey, Jeff Bezos, Elon, Sam — or something else — is not truly a reflection of how valuable you are as a human. I mean, not at all. I believe all humans are equally valuable.

The argument runs on outcome variance. Gagan makes the case at length: he cannot find a difference in intelligence or capability between founders of iconic companies and founders of ones that didn't work. *"Jeff Bezos could have been 20% dumber and still had the same outcome, and you wouldn't be able to predict it."* → [[Talent is not the defining factor of your success in a high variance business]]

Dave's own evidence is timing. His last startup sold to Cisco in 2016 and nearly went to Zoom; started two or three years later, "I think it would've been a really different outcome. And certainly didn't know COVID was coming."

If the outcome is substantially timing and luck, then reading it as a verdict on your worth is a category error — and an expensive one:

> That trap, I think, is one of the things that creates attachment, creates fear, creates this feeling of pain, this heaviness, this stress.

Which is why Dave positions it as what the whole podcast is for: "part of the idea of Heart of Entrepreneurship is to shift away from that."
