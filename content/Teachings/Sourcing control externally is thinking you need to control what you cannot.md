# Sourcing control externally is thinking you need to control what you cannot

Dave admits this one took him a while — security made sense, control didn't. The explanation came from Diana Chapman.

> It's when I think I need to control things that I can't control. If all I need to control is the things that are actually within my control — well then there's no problem. There's no unmet need.

That is the whole resolution. The need for control is not excessive in size; it is **misaimed**. Pointed only at what is genuinely yours, it is satisfiable, and satisfied.

Jesse names the Serenity Prayer, and Dave takes it: *the strength to change what I can, accept what I can't, and the wisdom to know the difference.* Jesse's rider: "that last part is always the doozy."

The tool is [[The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder|sorting the files]], and Dave's list of what belongs in *can't* is uncomfortably long: what people think of you, the outcome of your startup, "and sometimes even your own thoughts and your own feelings."

> If you really look, most people spend so much of their lives trying to control things they can't actually control. And that's what takes us away from presence, and below the line.

Jesse adds the inverse, which is easy to miss: people also **under-control what is theirs.** "You're so focused on what's not in your control that you forget all these things that actually are." Dave: "which obviously make a lot more sense to focus on."
