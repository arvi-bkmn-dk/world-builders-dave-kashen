# The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are

The claim that puts enjoyment and performance on the same side, against everything hustle culture assumes.

> In our culture, especially entrepreneurial hustle culture, there is this idea of — I need to gut it out and be disciplined and manage and sacrifice. And he presents this provocative notion that really resonates with me: actually the more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are.

The mechanism is friction. "Think about how little effort it requires to get you to do something you enjoy." Anything driven by discipline spends energy on the forcing before any goes into the work — and generates the counter-will that makes it worse. Dave's example is the gym: gutting out a routine versus "how could I just enjoy exercise? Oh, I'll go play tennis."

The conclusion he draws for founders is unusually blunt for him:

> Most entrepreneurs I know don't really enjoy the journey. And if it's true — and I think it is true — that enjoyment leads to more effectiveness and efficiency, then that's a lose-lose. Why would you possibly be trying to manage and hustle and force and discipline your way to success, when it's both more joyful by definition *and* more effective to just enjoy the journey?

Not a trade-off being resolved in favour of joy. A claim that there was never a trade-off — the grind is worse on both axes. Jesse's response: "this feels like something we should unpack in a full episode."
