# Self-ing is the mental effort to create and protect an image of who you are

Dave names the process by which identity distorts decisions — and treats it as an **activity** rather than a thing, which is what makes it droppable.

> The **"Self-ing" Process** — the unconscious effort to create, maintain, and protect a mental image of who you are.

The word matters. A self is something you have; self-ing is something you are continuously doing, at a cost.

Its effect on decisions is specific and expensive:

> Most people are filtering this data through the lens of **identity preservation.** They're choosing based on who they think they need to be, rather than what's actually right for the company.

> * "I need to prove I'm a bold leader." → So they make reckless decisions instead of waiting for clarity.
> * "I can't be seen as weak." → So they hold on to too many responsibilities, afraid of looking ignorant.
> * "I have to be the visionary." → So they resist feedback and fail to trust their team's ideas.

Each is a defensible-sounding leadership stance that is actually an image being maintained. None of the three founders would describe themselves as protecting a self-image.

> The more you believe in and protect the image of yourself, the harder it is to make decisions from true intuition. Because real intuition isn't about proving anything.

Dave takes it further in [[NL 11 - Unlearning, The Overlooked Key to Scaling as a Founder]], where unlearning ends here: "it takes mental effort to create an image of who you are, and you've been spending most of your life trying to aggrandize and protect that image of self you made up." → [[What you are defending tends to be your ego]]
