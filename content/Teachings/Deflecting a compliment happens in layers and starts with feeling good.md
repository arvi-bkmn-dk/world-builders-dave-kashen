# Deflecting a compliment happens in layers and starts with feeling good

Jesse finally gets Andrew to ask for acknowledgement, and gives him one: getting everyone focused on an objective, a good eye for levelling up content, how much he cares about winning.

Asked how it feels, Andrew says **embarrassed**. Dave pushes — what else? — and gets *good*. Then Jesse slows the tape, and the sequence is the teaching:

> I suspect part of you felt something good for a second. And then another part jumped in and said: no no no, why didn't he say better things? And then the other part said: I'm embarrassed now that that was the best he's got.

Three movements in perhaps a second. What reached Andrew's awareness — and what he reported as the whole experience — was the third.

Andrew's own description of layer two is worth keeping because it's so recognisable:

> Is this what I'm being acknowledged for, at this level? Wouldn't it be better if I could be acknowledged for doing even more? Oh yeah, I'm not really doing enough.

An acknowledgement converted into evidence of insufficiency, instantly.

**The good feeling came first.** Which matters, because it means the capacity to receive isn't missing — it's overwritten before it registers. Andrew confirms where it landed most: *you said that I want to win, because that's such an important part of my personality.* → [[Appreciate who someone is, not only what they did]]

Jesse's broader note:

> Every time I think I'm self-aware, I find another layer where I'm like — oh, there's still something happening there, automatic, that I didn't realise.

→ [[Self-awareness is moment-to-moment, not a list of your strengths and weaknesses]]
