# Visualize the being, not just the having

A correction to how founders picture their desired future.

> People have a tendency, if you think about the realms of **be, do, have** — people have a tendency to **visualize the have.** *Have this much revenue, have this.*

> And you're pointing to: there's the **doing** — okay, but **what's it like to be in the office every day?** What does winning look like? You're high-fiving your team, people are smiling, people are excited.

> And **who are you being, and how are you being?** *I feel energized and alive and clear and confident and excited.* Those are **as if not more important.** There's some school of thought that the **being** is the most important — just imagine **who you're being** a year from now.

→ [[Be-Do-Have]] · [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]

Jesse's practical version, which Dave says got him unstuck before starting Gateway X:

> **What do you want your week to look like?** What's your Monday? What's your Tuesday? What do those look like and **how do they feel?**

> Because when you actually say a feeling — *I wanna feel energized, I wanna feel calm on Monday mornings* — and then I don't feel that way, **it's a natural alert system I create for myself in the future.**

A revenue target tells you nothing until the quarter closes. A felt description of an ordinary Tuesday can be checked against this Tuesday.

**And the precondition matters**, or the whole thing inverts:

> A lot of people try to **create from lack.** *I feel the sense of lack, so I visualize having the thing that I lack.* **That's not the energy that creates flow — that creates more lack.**

> So it's really important to **first** be like: there's nothing wrong, there's nothing missing. And **from this place of presence, what do I really want?**
