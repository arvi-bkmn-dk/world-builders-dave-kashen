# Beliefs that were adaptive in childhood become maladaptive in adulthood

Why the beliefs holding a founder back are also the hardest to reach.

> The beliefs that we develop to survive or adapt to threat are seen as **critical by our nervous system** and often get locked deep in our unconscious for safety.

> Beliefs that were adaptive in childhood so often become maladaptive in adulthood. But because they are locked deep in our unconscious, we don't generally see them for what they are.

Two claims worth separating. The belief **worked** — it was a functional adaptation to a real situation, not an error. And it was filed as *survival-critical*, which is exactly the category the mind protects from inspection.

So the difficulty isn't that these beliefs are deeply held. It is that they are deliberately guarded, by a system doing its job.

Dave's list of the seven he sees most in founders:

> 1. "I'm not enough / I'm an impostor" — worth must be earned through achievements
> 2. "I need to control everything to succeed"
> 3. "I have to work hard to deserve success" — struggle is a prerequisite
> 4. "Failure means I am a failure"
> 5. "There isn't enough to go around"
> 6. "Wanting financial success is shallow or selfish"
> 7. "I need everyone to like and approve of me"

Every one reads as a plausible operating principle rather than a wound, which is what makes them durable.
