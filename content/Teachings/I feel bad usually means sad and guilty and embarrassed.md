# I feel bad usually means sad and guilty and embarrassed

A small correction with large consequences, made when Gagan says he feels bad every time someone leaves.

> We use that language a lot — like, oh, I feel bad. What you really mean is: I feel sad, most likely. And guilty.

Gagan immediately does the work himself and adds a third: **"sad and guilty and embarrassed."**

The distinction matters because *bad* is not a feeling and cannot be worked with. It is a verdict on your state — undifferentiated, therefore unactionable, therefore something you can only try to escape. Named precisely, each component has its own shape and its own question. → [[Each emotion carries its own question, and that is where its wisdom is]]

Gagan's account of the embarrassment is unusually specific, and shows what precision buys:

> I'm usually a little bit embarrassed about the fact that I was wrong. And usually by the time someone's being let go, I've already done things that are embarrassing — because generally I let them go a little late, and I'm already telling them they aren't doing a good job in ways that are not the nicest way to do that.

That is a diagnosis. *I feel bad* would have hidden all of it.
