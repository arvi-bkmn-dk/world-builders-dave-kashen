# The voice in your head is mostly playing those three roles

The move that turns the drama triangle from an interpersonal model into an internal one. Jesse notes that everyone's first instinct is to map victim, villain and hero onto three different people — and that the more useful reading is that all three are running inside one head.

> If you really start paying attention, the voice in your head is mostly playing those three roles most of the time.

Dave narrates the rotation:

> What's wrong with you, blah blah blah. And then — oh God, poor me, this is so stressful, this is so hard. And then, I know, we'll... and we just cycle through it.

Villain, victim, hero, in about ten seconds. He has an **inner bully** — "you screwed that up, what's the matter with you, you need to do better" — and notes that an active inner bully tends to get played outwardly too.

Hero moves internally are the ones hardest to spot, because they don't look heroic:

> Another version of hero is like — I'll just go have a glass of wine, or I'll just go watch Netflix or veg out. A lot of the distractions that people think of as addictive behaviour.

Jesse's founder version: "I just won't look at the numbers." Dave: "I don't wanna feel the disappointment or fear, so I'll just not look at it, or I'll pretend things are better than they are." → [[Busyness is the most respectable way to avoid a feeling]]

Which lands on the practical claim: this is how a leader stops seeing straight. "The team that sees reality the clearest wins — and this is one way we tend to avoid reality, by seeing it through this distorted lens."
