# Imagine there was nothing anyone could say about you that would bother you

The payoff of shadow work, and Dave states it as freedom rather than virtue.

> That's one of the most useful things about recognising the shadow — it gives you freedom. Imagine there was nothing anyone could say about you that would bother you, to your face, behind your back. Think about how empowering that is.

The logic is that an unowned trait is a lever other people hold. Every accusation you cannot tolerate is a place you can be moved. Own it — *yes, I lose all the time* — and the lever stops working, because there is no longer anything to defend.

Dave links it straight to the previous episode's definition: empowerment as "being your authentic self no matter what, regardless of the consequences." → [[Empower is being fully authentic regardless of the consequences]]

And explains why the two are the same problem:

> The parts of ourself that are real, that are authentic, but that we deny and repress, get in the way of us being fully authentic. 'Cause we feel shame, or, oh I can't be that way.

So the shadow is not merely uncomfortable — it is the specific obstacle to authenticity. You cannot be fully yourself while excluding parts of yourself.
