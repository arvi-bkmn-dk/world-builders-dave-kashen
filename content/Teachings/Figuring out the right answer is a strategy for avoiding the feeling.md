# Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling

Having established that hard decisions are frightened ones, Dave names what the mind does with the fear.

> Most of the time we don't want to feel the fear. So we escape into our minds, and our minds try to figure out the right answer.

The escape has a premise buried in it, and stating the premise is what exposes the move:

> It's like they somehow believe they can accurately predict the future. And if I could just figure out the right answer, then I can control the future. All of it is just a strategy to avoid feeling the fear.

Analysis promises control over an unknowable outcome. It cannot deliver that, but it does deliver immediate relief from the feeling — which is what it's actually for, and why it is so compelling despite never concluding.

The tell is that the figuring doesn't terminate. A genuine analysis reaches an answer; avoidance loops, because resolving it was never the function.

Dave's practical instruction is to invert the sequence — feel first, then look:

> Feel the fear that arises when you consider not focusing on the business for an hour. Literally welcome the fear in your body. And then as you do that, just see what occurs to you to do.

Note "see what occurs to you," not decide. Once the fear isn't driving, the want is usually already visible. → [[Following your wants usually leads to the same actions without the baggage]]

## Also in [[WS 01 - The Inner Keys to Becoming a World-Class Leader]]

In the workshop Dave frames rightness as a school habit, and preference as the key:

> And what I'm finding way more effective is letting go of even the idea that there's a right answer and tuning into what's your actual preference.

> So you go from what should I do to what do I want to do.
