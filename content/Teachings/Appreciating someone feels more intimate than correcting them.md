# Appreciating someone feels more intimate than correcting them

The explanation for why leaders who are perfectly willing to deliver hard feedback go quiet when something goes well:

> It can feel **more intimate (therefore scarier) to genuinely appreciate someone than to give them constructive feedback.**

Correction is protected by its own form. It has a stated business purpose, a professional register, and a defined distance between the two people. Genuine appreciation has none of that — it exposes that you noticed, that you care, and that this person matters to you.

Which reframes an appreciation deficit as an **avoidance** pattern rather than an oversight or a time problem, and puts it in the same family as everything else in his work that a founder does to not feel something. → [[The behavior is often just a way to avoid feeling some feeling]] · [[Deflecting a compliment happens in layers and starts with feeling good]]

The other two reasons Dave gives are more commonly admitted: that appreciation will cost motivation or raise expectations, and that closeness makes a future termination harder — *"we keep people at arm's length because we might have to let them go, and that's easier if we haven't gotten too close."*

That last one is worth sitting with. It trades certain, daily connection for insurance against a possible future conversation. → [[Keeping someone who is not a fit is a lose lose]]
