# Forcing from lack gets in the way of the growth that wants to happen

The efficiency argument for clean fuel, which is the one that actually moves founders who don't care about wellbeing.

Dave credits Joe Hudson's distinction — **enjoy versus manage**:

> Most people, from lack, try to **manage** and almost **force** things to happen. That management — grinding, eating glass, **forcing yourself to do things you don't really want to do**, forcing other people, listening to the bully voice in your head — **actually gets in the way of the natural growth that wants to happen.**

> It's a **less efficient way to grow** yourself and your company.

> When you and your company **follow enjoyment** — which is another way of saying flow — follow what you authentically want, what feels right, what feels alive, **follow your energy** — that's a **much more efficient path to growth.**

The image:

> The **oak tree** is not like *what's the matter with me, I should be taller, I should reach out my branches more.* **It just grows. Nature grows, and we are part of nature.** Humans grow — good luck, **try not to grow.**

Joe Hudson's test, which Dave repeats: he challenged a client to make **no improvement** for two weeks, *"and the client came back and reported a ton of ways that they grew."*

And the line that makes it a business argument rather than a philosophical one:

> **Your growth as a founder determines the company's growth.**

So the hypothesis on offer isn't *stop driving yourself because it's unpleasant.* It's: **the driving is costing you growth**, and it's testable.
