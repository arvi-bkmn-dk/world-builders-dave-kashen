# If you are not your thoughts, the question of who you are opens

The question Dave says follows logically once the first distinction lands.

> If you have thought that's you, and suddenly you're learning — oh, that's not me — the logical next question is: well, okay then, who is me? Who's the I?

He walks the eliminations, crediting Michael Singer's *The Untethered Soul* for doing it well (Jesse: "written for entrepreneurs and business people, it's hard to argue with a lot of the logic"):

- **Not the body.** Were you *you* at two years old with a completely different body? The body regenerates continuously. Singer's thought experiment: remove the body under anaesthesia, keep the brain alive — you'd still think you're you.
- **Not the feelings.** They change constantly.
- **Not the mind.** Already established.

Dave's answer, offered as one framing rather than a conclusion:

> One way to think about it is awareness. I'm the awareness in which the thoughts and feelings are arising.

Then the observation that gives it traction:

> If you think of what's essential, what has not changed since you were born or at least since you were little — there's a sense of self, like *I'm in here*. That's probably the one thing that's been consistent.

And the extension he's visibly moved by:

> That sense of self is actually the same in all of us. I sometimes think of God or awareness or consciousness looking out at the world through 8 billion pairs of eyes.

He's careful to hold it loosely — people reach for words like love, God and spirit "to encapsulate something that can't really be put into words." → [[The divine is the unknown]]
