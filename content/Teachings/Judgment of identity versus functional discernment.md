# Judgment of identity versus functional discernment

The distinction that separates doubt worth listening to from doubt that paralyses — and Dave says founders who can't tell them apart end up either fighting all doubt or obeying all of it.

**Destructive self-doubt attacks identity:**

> * "Maybe I'm not really a good entrepreneur"
> * "Maybe I can't do this"
> * "Why did you think you could do this?"
> * "Maybe I'm not good enough"

> It's **binary thinking**: either you're good enough or you're not.

**Healthy self-questioning is functional:**

> * "How could I be better?"
> * "How am I awesome at this? And how am I not so awesome at this?"
> * "Where do I have opportunities to improve?"

> Destructive self-doubt makes your **identity dependent on your performance.** Healthy self-questioning treats challenges as **information you can work with.**

Dave's test for which one you're in is the badminton comparison:

> If someone told you that you weren't great at badminton, you probably wouldn't take it personally. You don't have "badminton player" as a core part of your identity. But when someone questions your ability as an entrepreneur — something you've tied your sense of worth to — you either get defensive or spiral into self-criticism.

The same critique, two different responses, and the variable is how much identity is loaded onto the domain. Which makes the sting diagnostic: **the sharper it lands, the more of your worth is resting there.**

> The most successful founders I work with have learned to **separate who they are from how they're currently performing.**
