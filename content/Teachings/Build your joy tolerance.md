# Build your joy tolerance

Treating joy as a capacity to be trained rather than a reward to be earned — which follows from Dave's claim that people resist it more than they resist pain.

> If you quickly dismiss feeling good, just increase your capacity to hold it.
> * Take 30 seconds to sit with a moment of joy — let yourself fully feel it.
> * Breathe through the discomfort. If joy makes you squirm, take deep breaths and allow it to stay.
> * Say out loud: "It's safe for me to feel joy."

The middle instruction is the one that marks this as capacity work. Joy is expected to be **uncomfortable**, and the discomfort is what you breathe through — the same relationship his work prescribes for fear or grief, pointed at the pleasant end. → Treat the startup as an emotional intensity gym

Before the practice comes noticing, and Dave gives the tells:

> The next time something great happens — a big win, a compliment, a moment of deep peace — watch what your mind does. Do you immediately find something else to worry about? Minimize the achievement? Distract yourself with work? Feel restless or undeserving?

> Just notice it. **That resistance isn't a sign something is wrong. It's a sign you're expanding beyond your old limits.**

That reframe is what stops the noticing from becoming another round of self-criticism — the discomfort is evidence of edge, not of defect. → [[The upper limit problem is a thermostat for how good life is allowed to get]]

And the reason it is not indulgence: "joy fuels creativity, energy, and resilience. The more joy you allow, the better you lead."
