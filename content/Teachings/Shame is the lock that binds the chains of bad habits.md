# Shame is the lock that binds the chains of bad habits

Joe Hudson's line, and the inversion that makes habit change possible: the shame you feel *about* a habit is what holds it in place.

Dave walks his own cycle with ice cream, and the sequence is exact:

> I'd have a little bit of ice cream, and then almost immediately I'm like — ah, what's the matter with you? You said you weren't gonna do it, why'd you do it?

> And then I'd feel the shame around it, and then I'd want to distract myself or alleviate that feeling. So then I might have a little more ice cream, 'cause I wanna feel better.

> I feel shame, and then I want the shame to go away. So then I do something to distract myself, and then I feel shame around the behavior I just did.

The shame is not the consequence of the habit — it is the fuel supply. Each round of self-reproach generates a feeling that the habit then gets used to relieve.

Which is why more discipline makes it worse. The correction *is* the mechanism.

Dave also notes where the cycle started: "I set the intention from a place of shame. It wasn't like I really want to do this thing — it was an agreement from below the line, like okay, I should do this thing." A commitment made from self-judgment carries the judgment into every failure to keep it. → [[Welcome the shame but do not believe the thoughts that come with it]]
