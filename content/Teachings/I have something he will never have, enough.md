# I have something he will never have, enough

The story Dave tells, hedged as probably apocryphal and kept because the point survives either way.

Joseph Heller is in a pub with another author when some hedge fund managers walk in. The other author says: *you wrote one of the most extraordinary books of our time, and that man makes more money in a day than you've made from all the sales of Catch-22. How do you feel about that?*

> He's like, oh, I feel fine. And the guy says, really? Why? He says — well, I have something he'll never have.

> **Enough.**

The word does the work. Enough is not a quantity; it is a **relationship** to a quantity, which is why more money cannot produce it and why the hedge fund manager's position is genuinely worse despite the larger number.

It lands directly on the rigged game — [[The whole game is rigged because when never arrives]] — because *when* is precisely the promise of a future enough, and the promise is what prevents the present one.

Jesse's own version is the sharpest case in his work that the pile does not deliver. After the Ampush exit — "a life-changing amount of money" — the relief lasted hours:

> And then I felt this insane sense of scarcity. The pile is here. I can't lose the pile. I can't let the pile dwindle.

His childhood dream car was an Aston Martin. He went to look at one and stopped: *that's gonna bring the number down.* Dave: "because my safety depends on having now this pile."

> For most people, the amount of money that they need is **more.** More than they have now.
