# You cannot decouple strategy from what you actually want

Jesse brings this one in from Red Ventures founder Rick Elias, and reports it as the first time the idea fully landed for him:

> You can't decouple strategy from your own wants. If you think you can, you're wrong. Because if you don't wanna make the strategy that you're coming up with, it ain't gonna happen, Jesse.

The claim is stronger than "do what you love." It says a strategy the founder does not want is not merely unpleasant to execute — it is *unlikely to execute*, because the person responsible for driving it will not. Correctness of the plan does not compensate: "it doesn't matter how right the answer is."

Dave supplies the mechanism, and it is the same self-cancellation that produces the yo-yo elsewhere in this episode:

> You're gonna be like fighting yourself the whole way and then wonder why it didn't work. You're choosing to do it and resisting it at the same time. That's two different vectors moving in opposite directions. That's not a lot of force.

Jesse's own comparison between two companies is the evidence: at one he "always felt like I was running for the door," at the other, "I'm energised, I'm excited by this — because this is what I want." He also reports the reverse signal arriving unbidden when he drifts off it: "I get a real clear message that came back."

The practical consequence is that a founder's authentic wants are a strategic input, not a personal indulgence to be handled separately from the plan.
