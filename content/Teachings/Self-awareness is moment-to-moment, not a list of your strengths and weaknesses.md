# Self-awareness is moment-to-moment, not a list of your strengths and weaknesses

Jesse's correction of what he thought self-awareness was, and it's the most common version of the mistake.

> I always tell people: I thought I was self-aware, and the way I defined self-awareness was — if you ask me my strengths and weaknesses, I could tell you, and I'd be honest. It wouldn't be bullshit. But that's actually not a great definition.

Being able to give an accurate account of yourself is a *retrospective summary*. It is compatible with having no idea what is happening in you right now, which is the only place anything can be done about it.

> Self-awareness is moment to moment — what's coming up for you, what's happening in that second, in that moment.

The distinction explains an otherwise puzzling pattern: people who describe their patterns with real insight and keep running them anyway. The description and the noticing are different faculties, and only the second interrupts anything.

Jesse's route in was the body, and his starting point was zero:

> I had never ever noticed my body. I had never noticed sensations in my body. So I didn't know what emotions were as a result of that.

The dependency runs in that order — sensations first, then emotions become legible. → [[Thoughts, Feelings, Sensations]]
