# Scarcity is a mindset, not a reality

Dave's opening on the myth entrepreneurs are trained into — that time, money, talent and opportunity are fundamentally short, so you hustle, fight for your share, and protect what you have.

> Scarcity is not an objective truth; it's a mindset — a perspective rooted in fear and resistance.

He catalogues how it speaks, and the phrases are unremarkable enough to pass as observation rather than belief:

> "There's never enough time in the day." · "We don't have the budget for that." · "The market is too competitive." · "I can't find the right people."

> These stories create a cycle of stress, burnout, and reactive decision-making. They lock us into small thinking, where every challenge feels like a zero-sum game.

On **money** specifically, the argument is from the founder's own history:

> Before you started your company, it had zero money. Your resourcefulness brought in the capital. What makes you think you can't do it again?

> The real issue isn't a lack of money; it's often a lack of creativity, conviction, or willingness to ask for what you need.

Dave cites Tony Robbins — *"it's not a matter of resources, it's a matter of resourcefulness"* — and is careful to frame the fix as perspective rather than denial: "reframe *I don't have enough time* into *I have the time I need to focus on what matters most right now.*"
