# What you are defending tends to be your ego

The seventh and final distinction, which the other six build toward.

> Most of us learn, especially in the first eight years of our life, to defend ourselves and close our hearts to feel safe. And the journey is to learn to live undefended, and open your heart back.

The move that makes it possible is identifying what is actually under guard:

> The thing that you're defending tends to be your ego, your self-image, avoiding certain feelings. And the more you recognise that that's not who you are — and at least start tuning into the question, who am I really? — you start to shed this identification with a thought basically, a mental construct that you're defending. Which makes it easier and easier to open your heart and live undefended.

The argument is not *stop defending*, which would be another discipline. It is that the defending stops on its own once the thing being defended is seen clearly as a construct rather than a self. You cannot be threatened through something you are not.

That puts this distinction downstream of the talker/listener work — [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]], The narrating voice in your head is a survival mechanism, not your self — and makes the same first-eight-years claim as [[Personality is a defence structure built from the feelings you would not feel]], arriving from Joe Hudson's direction rather than Conscious Leadership's.
