# Projection runs in two directions

The refinement that makes projection actually usable, because the two mechanisms feel nothing alike from the inside.

**You do it, and judge yourself for it.**

> We know we have a tendency to be self-centred, and then someone else is being self-centred and we're like: you're so selfish. Just because we don't love the selfish part of ourselves.

**You forbid yourself from doing it.**

> We don't let ourselves be selfish, because we have a story that good people are generous and not selfish. And then someone else lets themselves be selfish and we're like: what? Why do they get to and we don't?

The second is the one people miss, and missing it makes the standard formulation ("what you spot you got") feel wrong and get dismissed. The charge in that case isn't guilt — it's the resentment of someone escaping a rule you're still paying for. Which is why it presents as principle rather than projection.

The practical difference is what each calls for. The first asks you to accept a part you already have. The second asks whether the rule is one you actually endorse — and often reveals a permission you've been withholding from yourself.

That reading connects it directly to [[People sandbag their authentic wants to avoid seeming self-indulgent]], which is the same rule operating before anyone else is involved.
