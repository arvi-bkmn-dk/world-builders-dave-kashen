# Do-have-be is backwards and it never arrives

> The water we swim in, in our culture, is: do, have, be. If I do all this stuff, then I'll have these results, and then I'll be happy. And it literally doesn't work.

The sequence is so ambient that Dave has to name it as the water rather than as a belief anyone holds deliberately.

Its failure is demonstrated by following the *why* chain. Ask why someone needs $25 million and you arrive, reliably, at two words: **freedom** and **peace**. Both are states. Both are available now. And the mind refuses:

> If you ask people "could you just be free and peaceful?" their brains short-circuit. No, no, no — I need the money first.

The refusal is the teaching. The evidence against do-have-be is not scarce; it's simply not admitted. We have all met billionaires who are visibly unhappy and unfree, and people with very little money who are neither.

The Mexican fisherman parable makes it concrete: the banker's entire recommended arc — fish all day, buy five boats, build international distribution, sell, retire — terminates in exactly what the fisherman already does each evening. The plan was a detour back to the starting point, and the only thing it would reliably add is the years spent on it. → [[Be-Do-Have]]
