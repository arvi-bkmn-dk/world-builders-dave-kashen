# An impulse feels lighter and a compulsion has constriction attached to it

The test for whether a want is yours, and it is somatic rather than analytical.

> An impulse, for me at least, feels much **lighter.** And a compulsion has **constriction** attached to it. Am I open, or am I constricted?

Jesse describes the practice Dave gave him: quiet down, then notice — *oh, I'm being pulled that way* versus *something in my head is saying you should go do that.* His honest note on difficulty: "when I first heard it I was like, dude, what are you talking about, this is too hard." It takes practice.

**The hardest case is the good one.** Jesse's observation, which Dave endorses:

> Often the most challenging is the thing that I think I'm meant to do or want — and I turn that into a should. Nobody has the conflict of *I should start a nonprofit.* It's: I want to start a nonprofit, I'm called to feed America. And then — why isn't my nonprofit bigger?

Dave takes it further than expected:

> Funny enough, non-profit people and activists can be the most below the line. Because it starts with the premise: **the world is not the way it should be.** Which by definition means you're not present.

And then draws the line carefully, because the distinction is subtle and matters:

> We can appreciate so deeply the passion of someone who wants to stand for a world with no hunger. And how subtly distinct — but importantly distinct — that is from *there shouldn't be hunger in the world and I have to stop it.* This compulsion, versus this impulse to *I feel called to create a world where no child starves.* It literally feels different.
