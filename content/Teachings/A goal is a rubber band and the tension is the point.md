# A goal is a rubber band and the tension is the point

> Imagine a rubber band stretched between where you are now and where you want to be.

> Too much **slack** — goals seem too easy — and there's no tension. You coast, nothing interesting happens.

> Stretched too **tight** and the rubber band **snaps.** And once it snaps, there's no tension either. Just resignation, going through the motions, everyone quietly waiting for the quarter to end.

Both failure modes end in the same place, which is the useful part of the image: a goal that is too hard produces exactly as little as one that is too easy.

> The sweet spot is that middle zone where the stretch feels significant but possible. Where the gap makes you ask **"how might we?"** instead of **"why bother?"**

Dave calls this **healthy creative tension**, and tests it with basketball: down 20 going into the fourth quarter, you don't adjust the goal to losing by 15 — the gap is the thing that galvanises you.

His three questions before adjusting anything:

1. **Has the rubber band actually broken?** — "can't imagine how it's possible" is not the same as "unlikely." If there are levers you haven't pulled, the tension is doing its job.
2. **Is there any excitement left?** — does any part of you get curious about *how might we*, or is it dread?
3. **What happens if you don't hit it?** — teams "set goals from above the line, with inspiration and creativity, but then relate to missing them from below the line, with blame and shame." → [[Separate the accounting from the response]]

He didn't answer the founder who asked. He asked back: *"What do you want to do? What would best serve you and your team?"*
