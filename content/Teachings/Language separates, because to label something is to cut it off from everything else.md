# Language separates, because to label something is to cut it off from everything else

The flip side of Dave's love of distinctions. Labelling is what gives you power (the mechanic seeing parts where you see a blob of engine), and it is also what divides:

> language by its nature is divisive.

A table is no less a table because you call part of it a tabletop and part of it legs, but naming the parts separates them. Dave credits Alan Watts, his favourite philosopher, with the other view: there's just the happening.

> Because the moment we label something, we. By doing that, we're separating it from something else.

Because thoughts happen in language, identifying with thoughts means living in separateness. That's why he leaves himself a reminder after peak experiences: don't try to understand oneness with the mind, since it's the instrument doing the separating. "The less I think the more I know." → [[Creating distinctions gives you power]] · [[Alan Watts]]
