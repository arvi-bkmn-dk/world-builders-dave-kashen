# When you know, you are relating to the current situation from the past

Dave's argument for *"I don't know"* as a leadership asset, and it runs on an unusual claim about knowledge.

> When you **know**, you are relating to the current situation from the **past.** When you are in the past, you are not in reality. You are in the mind experiencing memory, which no longer exists here in reality.

Knowing is pattern-matching — applying something learned earlier to what's in front of you now. Efficient, and it means you are looking at a memory rather than the situation.

> So the bridge outside of the mind into the present moment, where everything is real, alive, and true, is **curiosity.** Or in other words, saying the words "I don't know" and seeing everything for the first time with all its novelty.

Dave then names what founders have trained out of themselves:

> Most of us have atrophied our childlike wonder. We're generally much more oriented toward **convergence than divergence** — we want to know the right answer and get to it as quickly as possible.

> I see this constantly with the founders I coach. They've trained themselves to:
> * Always have a plan (even when the situation calls for exploration)
> * Appear confident (even when uncertainty would serve them better)
> * Solve problems quickly (even when the problem isn't fully understood yet)
> * Know their numbers (but ignore their intuition and emotional intelligence)

Each is a competence that became compulsory. And his diagnosis of the client spiralling over a Series B is the summary:

> He wasn't stuck because he lacked information. He was stuck because he was **living in an imagined future based on past patterns.**

→ [[Most adults have atrophied their dreamer]] is the same atrophy claim, pointed at wanting instead of wondering.
