# I was so busy trying to become worthy of my family that I almost lost them

Dave's own version of the Chesky story, and the most compressed statement of the trap in his work.

> I think I almost lost my wife because of this. The way I often tell the story is: **I was so busy trying to become worthy of my family that I almost lost them.**

What makes it precise is that no single decision was wrong:

> Any given marginal decision — *should I go home and spend this hour with my wife and young children, or should I do one more hour of work?* — **it always felt like the extra hour of work was the more marginally beneficial thing to do, because there's a potential tangible outcome.**

> It wasn't a super conscious choice. Especially at a time where we felt like we weren't winning, we're like: **obviously we just have to work harder. 'Cause we can't fail.**

The hour with the family has no measurable output. The hour of work might. Run that comparison a few thousand times and you have traded the thing for the pursuit of deserving the thing — without ever making a decision you could point to as the mistake.

Jesse's parallel: he and his co-founder used to talk about **how selfish entrepreneurship felt** — *"you become single-mindedly focused on success… all my energy has to be organised to being successful."* Dave's addition: *"ironically, because it doesn't actually give you the experience you want, it's like a bad version of selfishness where you end up unhappy."*
