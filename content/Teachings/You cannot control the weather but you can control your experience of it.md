# You cannot control the weather but you can control your experience of it

Dave's pre-emption of the objection that 100% responsibility is magical thinking — and he concedes the factual point immediately.

> There are things we can't control. As far as I understand, we don't control the weather. But you can control your experience of the weather.

> You look outside and it's raining. You can think, oh God darn it, I wish it was sunny — we can resist what is. If you resist reality, you create suffering for yourself. Or you can say: oh, it's raining, I'll go splash in the puddles.

The scope of the commitment is therefore narrower and stronger than it sounds. It doesn't claim authorship of events. It claims authorship of your experience, which is the one domain where the claim is straightforwardly true.

His second image makes the same point with the conditions held perfectly fixed:

> Two people on a rollercoaster. One's gripping for dear life, scared, cortisol coursing through their veins. The other one's like, woohoo. Same exact experience, same life conditions and circumstances — totally different internal experience.

Identical inputs, opposite outputs, which localises the variable precisely. Whatever is producing the difference is not in the rollercoaster.

This is the same claim as [[The state you operate from is what determines your results]], approached from the other side: that one says state determines outcomes, this one says state isn't determined by circumstances. → [[One Hundred Percent Responsibility]]
