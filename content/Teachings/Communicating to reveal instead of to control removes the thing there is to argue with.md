# Communicating to reveal instead of to control removes the thing there is to argue with

Dave's abstraction over unarguable language is a distinction in intent: most people communicate **to control** — the other person, the outcome, what feelings get felt — and the alternative is to communicate **to reveal**: here is my experience, in real time.

The mechanism is simple and mechanical. Arguable speech states the thoughts your mind produces as though they were the truth — *you shouldn't speak to me that way* — which makes a claim about how reality should be, and claims can be contested. The predictable reply is "I'll speak to you how I damn well please."

> Speaking unarguably is simply revealing the thoughts and feelings and wants as they're arising. It's unarguable that Jesse doesn't want that, or that he doesn't appreciate it. There's nothing to argue with. He doesn't have to honour it — but it's a very different experience.

Jesse's landlord call is the worked case: real anger, a breath, then *I don't appreciate the way you're speaking to me* rather than *who do you think you are*. He got the boundary and the outcome without the escalation.

Note the honest limit Dave builds in: the other party is not obligated to honour what you reveal. Revealing isn't a technique for getting your way; it removes the argument, not the disagreement. → [[Where your heart is matters more than whether the language is technically unarguable]]
