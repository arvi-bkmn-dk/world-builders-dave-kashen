# Loneliness is the distance between what you experience and what you let anyone see

Brian Chesky built Airbnb into a hundred-billion-dollar company. In an interview:

> "No one ever told me how lonely you would get, and I wasn't prepared for that."

The founder of a company whose mission is belonging, surrounded by thousands of employees, felt alone. Dave cites a Harvard Business Review study: half of CEOs report feeling lonely, and 61% believe it hurts their performance.

His picture of it is small: the minute in the car in the driveway, done being the confident leader, about to go be steady for your family.

> That minute is the only part of the day that belongs to the person underneath both roles.

It builds gradually: carrying the full burden, taking more than 100% responsibility, everyone else posting their glory days, old friends squeezed out, a family that resents your absence.

> Nowhere to set it down.

> Deep connection comes from feeling like you can totally be yourself and be seen and loved for who you are.

> Loneliness is the distance between what you're actually experiencing and what you let anyone see.

> It's only lonely at the top if you have to be someone else the whole way up.
