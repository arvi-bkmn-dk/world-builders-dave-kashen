# Ask someone to teach you how they produce their own suffering

Dave's technique for making someone the author of an experience without accusing them of authoring it.

> If I wanted to experience overwhelm the way you do, will you teach me how to do it? Maybe you're teaching a class at the business school — Overwhelm 101. Give me the lesson.

> Every time, if they're willing — and most of my clients are willing — they'll come up with this perfect recipe.

The recipe a client produced:

> Step 1: think of all the things you possibly could do. Step 2: believe you should do all of those things. Step 3: make a list of 30 hours of tasks. Step 4: believe you should get them done in 10 hours.

**The recipe is itself the evidence.** Nobody can specify a procedure that cleanly unless they're running it. And because the client produced it, there's nothing to dispute — the coach never made a claim.

Dave notes it's "a little playful," which is structural rather than stylistic. *You're doing this to yourself* is true and triggers defence; *teach me your method* invites expertise, and people enjoy demonstrating expertise. The authorship is admitted sideways.

Note the precondition: he asks *are you willing* first. → [[The Willingness Question]]

Then the arithmetic does the rest — you're never going to do more than ten hours of work in ten hours, so all that's actually available is deliberate prioritisation, and the overwhelm layered on top contributes nothing to getting any of it done.
