# People sandbag their authentic wants to avoid seeming self-indulgent

Prompted by Jesse redesigning his week around what he actually wanted, Andrew admits the thing he does instead.

> When we were doing annual planning, Adam said: I want to hear you say something like, I want to go see my kids' soccer games. And I thought — if anyone hears that, they're going to think I'm being so self-indulgent that they're not going to want to support me in doing the things that are just for me.

So he hides them, or launders them:

> I always make excuses for it, or I try to cover it up somehow so that they'll forgive it — instead of realising that they're going to be as excited as I am to help Jesse get to his Wednesday.

The prediction is exactly backwards, and he has just watched the disproof. Jesse asked plainly for his Wednesdays and Andrew's reaction was *more* generous: "I got more excited about giving him his Wednesdays."

Dave's response is one of his most direct lines in his work:

> I want you to want what you want, and to believe that you can have what you want just because you want it.

No justification required, no business case. Which is the same claim as [[Everyone is inherently worthy of what they authentically want]] at the scale of a Wednesday.

Andrew names where the rule came from — growing up in New York "where you really are trained to be like a killer business person and suppress your feelings for it" — and what makes it negotiable: Jesse repeatedly saying business is a way to become a better version of yourself. "The fact that that's in the atmosphere gives me permission."

The sandbagging is also [[Projection runs in two directions|projection's second mechanism]] before anyone else is involved: a rule kept so strictly it has to be hidden.
