# The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder

An exercise Dave takes from Conscious Leadership, called **sorting the files**. Two manila folders: *Things I Can Control* and *Things I Can't Control.*

> The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder.

The problem is never that founders want control. It is that enormous effort goes into the second folder while it is mislabelled as the first.

Dave's list of what actually belongs in *can't control* is longer and more uncomfortable than most people expect:

> Your thoughts. What people think of you. What people say about you. What other people feel. Even to some degree your own feelings.

His example from that morning: a client working out what to say to a departing employee *to get them to feel a particular way.* That is the second folder. "It's just speaking your truth, and they'll have the experience they have." Let someone go and they may be sad, angry or scared — none of which is yours to arrange.

Gagan's version is the macro one. Below a certain scale you control nearly everything — "you can build a multimillion dollar business in freaking Q-Tips" and cannot blame the environment. Above it, timing and luck dominate: "there's not a startup out there with the potential to get to hundreds of millions that doesn't have macro risk."

> A lot of energy is wasted trying to control things that you actually can't control.

## Also in [[NL 64 - The Only Things You Can Control]]

The newsletter gives the exercise its name and adds the standard for the first folder:

> And to accurately place something in the "can control" folder, you must be able to absolutely guarantee its outcome.

Why people missort: when you source security, approval and control externally, putting something in *can't control* can feel like a survival threat.

| Can't control | Can control |
|---|---|
| what other people think and feel | how you respond to your thoughts and feelings |
| their choices, reactions, agreements | whether you believe your stories |
| the macro environment | what you say and do |
| the outcome of your startup | where you place your attention |
| your next thought, the arising of your feelings | your boundaries, your agreements, your state |

> The key to sorting the files is taking 100% responsibility. Not more, not less.
