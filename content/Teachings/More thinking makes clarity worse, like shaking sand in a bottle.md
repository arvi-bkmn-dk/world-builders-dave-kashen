# More thinking makes clarity worse, like shaking sand in a bottle

Dave's metaphor for why founders cannot think their way to clarity:

> You have a bottle of water with sand in it and it's murky. And going into your head to try to get clear is like shaking the bottle of water — which just gets more and more murky.

Clarity is what settling produces, and rumination is agitation. So the harder you work the problem cognitively, the less available the answer becomes.

> The real unlock here is to realize more thinking is not gonna get you clearer. Most of the time it's the feeling — allowing the feelings that will create that clarity.

Dave argues that clarity, not capability, is usually the actual bottleneck: "most founders are pretty badass — if they're clear, they can run after something. So it's the lack of clarity that's really the issue."

His worked example is a founder weighing an opportunity that would strand months of his team's work. Rather than analysing it, Dave had him feel the specific avoided feelings — the fear of his team's disappointment, the sadness of letting the old thing go — and the answer surfaced immediately: "what do you wanna do? He's like, yeah, I want to go do this thing." Dave's read is that the man already knew and "wouldn't let himself really know it," because knowing required feeling those things first.

He connects indecision to one absent emotion in particular, from his own experience: "I would classify myself in many ways as indecisive, and I think it comes especially from not feeling my anger — 'cause when you really feel your anger, you get this determination."

Jesse's parallel is that psychedelics and similar experiences work the same way: "they just give you a feeling, and the second you have that feeling, you're like, I know this is true now, and I don't need to think about it."
