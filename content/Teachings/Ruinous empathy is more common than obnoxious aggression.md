# Ruinous empathy is more common than obnoxious aggression

Jesse opens with the asshole-CEO archetype, but Dave redirects to the quadrant nobody worries about.

The framework: the y-axis is whether you **care personally** — *"especially whether the person **feels** like you care about them as a person"* — and the x-axis is whether you **challenge directly.**

**Obnoxious aggression** (challenge, no care) is the one everyone fears: *"if you feel like a widget in the company's machine and they're trying to squeeze more out of you, that's the feeling."*

But:

> The piece — and why I think this conversation is particularly important — is the upper left quadrant, where you **do** really care about people. And because we tend to think anger is something bad or negative, and because we tend to want to **hero** or protect people from feeling sad or down, we end up in this quadrant where we care about them but **we don't actually challenge them.** That quadrant is called **ruinous empathy.**

> I think that's maybe **even more common these days** than the obnoxious aggression.

Why it earns the word *ruinous*:

> They don't have a chance to improve. And then down the road you may end up needing to **let them go.** Or they're just floundering in a role they're not thriving in — which is a **lose-lose**, because they didn't know what they needed to do to improve.

And Dave's diagnosis of the cause, which is what ties it to the episode:

> Most people are **less candid, less authentic** than they could be, than I would say is optimal — for their own wellbeing and that of their team. And I think it is because **we are not very comfortable with anger.**
