# He did not find more resources, he found more resourcefulness

A founder with **two weeks of runway**, spiralling — *"refreshing his bank balance, rehearsing the conversation he'd have with his team when the money runs out. He was languishing in victimhood."*

Dave's intervention is a reallocation of attention, not reassurance:

> I helped him see that with two weeks left, **nothing could be more important than using his precious creative energy to create the future he wanted instead of wasting it imagining the future he didn't.**

> Something unlocked. He started getting creative. He brainstormed people he hadn't thought to ask for money. He made a list of angel investors, got in action, reached out, and set up meetings.

> **Weeks later, he had raised over a million dollars.**

> **He didn't find more resources. He found more resourcefulness.**

And the summary that keeps it honest about what actually changed:

> His situation didn't change. **What changed was his state.** He let go of the story he was telling himself about scarcity, came back to the present moment, and his creative energy came back online.

> **The scarcity was never in reality. It was in his interpretation of reality.**

This is [[Creative Tension]] under maximum pressure — the two-week deadline is current reality, accepted; the raise is the desired future, imagined. What he had been doing instead was the second trap: spending imagination on the outcome he didn't want. → [[Flow is being present to current reality and imagining your desired future]]

> The issue often isn't a lack of resources or money but **a lack of creativity, conviction, or willingness to ask for what you need.**
