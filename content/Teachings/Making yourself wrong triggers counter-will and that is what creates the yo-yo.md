# Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and that is what creates the yo-yo

Dave is precise about where lack-based motivation fails, and it is narrower than "negative motivation doesn't work." Lack **does** motivate — "we're not crazy, the reason people use a sense of lack for motivation is because it works." The failure is specific: it shows up when you cross from *wanting a different result* into *making yourself wrong*.

> So there's kind of two different cases. If it's like, oh, we're at 10, I wish we were at 30 — that might or might not cause it. But if it's like, I should be better, I should be smarter, I should be more disciplined — there's this part of us that when it feels judged, it just sort of digs in.

The mechanism he names is **counter-will**. A part of you that is judged pushes back against the judgment rather than the goal:

> I should weigh less, I shouldn't be eating the chocolate cake. The one who wants the chocolate cake is like, screw you, I'm gonna have the chocolate.

So the yo-yo is not weak discipline. It is two parts of a person in conflict, each cancelling the other — you improve while the judged part is suppressed, then it reasserts and you return to the starting point. This is why the fix is not more pressure. Pressure is the input that produces the counter-will.

The practical line sits between "we are at 10 and I want 30" and "we are at 10 and that is wrong of us." The first can coexist with [[Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong|creative tension]]. The second reliably produces the oscillation.
