# Anger is how you attune to what you want

Dave connects two things usually treated separately — the emotion he was taught not to have, and the wanting he could not locate.

> I learned growing up that **anger was not good.** My parents didn't get angry as far as I could see, didn't express anger. I had a **stepdad** who did, and it was a very **brutish** way. And so I was like — oh, that's bad.

> Anger is the **energy of boundaries**, and anger shows up when **what we want is not happening.**

It runs at every intensity. Light: *"oh, you guys all want Chinese, I don't really want that, I want Italian."* Heavy: *"this is not okay with me, to be spoken to that way."*

And the consequence of refusing it:

> **Not accessing my anger, and not allowing it, was one of the primary ways I cut off my ability to attune to what I want** — or where my wants are not being met.

> That's part of how we end up feeling stuck. **I disconnect from my wants because I don't wanna feel my anger, and then I'm just sort of not clear.**

So the "I don't know what I want" that shows up as stuckness is often not an absence of wanting. It's an intact signal with the receiver switched off — and the receiver was switched off in childhood for good reason. → [[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]] · [[The Five Core Emotions]] · [[Most people have atrophied their dreamer]]

## Also in [[NL 72 - The Most Misunderstood Emotion in Leadership]]

Dave's spectrum:

- **Light anger** — a want or preference isn't being met: *I'd really like this handled differently.*
- **Strong anger** — something is genuinely not okay: *this needs to stop.*

> It's a real superpower.
