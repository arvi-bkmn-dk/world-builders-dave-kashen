# Are you willing to see how your startup failing could be as good as it succeeding

The question Dave puts to clients, and he reports the reaction plainly: **most of them resist, at first.**

> I often ask my clients: are you willing to see how your startup failing could be as good as your startup succeeding?

> They fear that without the attachment to success, they'd no longer be motivated.

The fear is the point of the question. It surfaces the belief that **attachment is the engine** — that caring less about the outcome means doing less about it. → [[Founders choose fear because they believe their drive would disappear without it]]

Dave takes the worry seriously rather than dismissing it, and names the failure mode on both sides:

> If you confuse letting go of attachment with simply not caring, your business and life might descend into chaos. But if you don't resolve the internal drive to finally feel enough, you'll always be searching — running on the same hamster wheel of striving and dissatisfaction, no matter how much you achieve.

Which is why the whole newsletter is a list of eight replacement fuels — desired future state, zone of genius, anonymous integrity, visible progress, get-to language, service, curiosity, and growth-framed accountability. The question opens the gap; the practices fill it.

Note the phrasing is *willing to see how it could be* — not a claim that failing is as good, but a check on whether you can hold the possibility without the system rejecting it. → [[The Willingness Question]]
