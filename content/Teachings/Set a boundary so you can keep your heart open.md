# Set a boundary so you can keep your heart open

The reframe that makes boundaries an act of love rather than a withdrawal of it.

> I really like this notion that **boundaries actually enable you to keep your heart open.** Another way that anger is coming from love.

> *I'm having difficulty keeping my heart open in this condition. So I'm gonna set a boundary so that **I don't be with you with my heart closed.*** 'Cause I don't wanna have to protect myself.

> **I set the boundary so that I can keep my heart open.**

And crucially, it isn't an accusation:

> I'm **not blaming you** for yelling at me or being below the line. But I don't want to be with it. So I'm gonna **remove myself to take care of myself**, so I can keep my heart open.

> Versus — usually it's like we start blaming each other, when we don't set good boundaries.

The alternative to a boundary is not openness. It's staying in the room with your heart closed, which looks like tolerance and is actually a slow withdrawal. That is the same lose-lose as [[Ruinous empathy is more common than obnoxious aggression|ruinous empathy]], run inside a relationship instead of a performance review.

Dave's warning about what happens when you don't:

> We'll **hero** other people — put their wants and needs ahead of ours, or try to protect them from feeling something. You're gonna create this **lose-win** where you lose and they win. And at some point you're gonna get angry. **It'll build and build, and then you'll have resentment.**
