# The fear that you will drown in a feeling is the lie that keeps it stuck

When Dave asks clients whether they're willing to feel the guilt, or the shame, most say a version of the same thing — Andrew's phrasing in the episode:

> If I let it in, I'm going to drown in it. And I don't want to live like that.

Dave's assessment is unusually blunt:

> That's the lie that our minds tell us. And it's a total paradox, because it's actually the resistance that's keeping it there. What you resist persists.

The paradox is exact. The feeling appears permanent *because* it's being resisted, and the apparent permanence is then read as evidence for continuing to resist it. The fear manufactures its own proof.

The counter-evidence is the duration. Authentic feelings move in a wave of roughly 90 seconds; anything still going is the brain re-running the scenario — what Jesse names as the **cognitive emotive loop**, thinking about it again and getting angry again. → [[An unresisted emotion moves through the body in 60 to 90 seconds]]

> For my actual experience of allowing — sometimes there's a few waves of it — but that allowing it, welcoming it, then allows it to pass through and moves through you.

And the general form, which Dave attributes — hedging, and to a name the transcript renders as "Matt Mashari" — to [[Matt Mochary]]:

> Fear makes bad predictions.

The specific prediction here — *I will be engulfed, if I allow anger I'll be enraged and cause carnage* — is a story about a future that doesn't happen. "It's not my experience. When you allow it, it just moves through you, and you come back to presence."
