# The acknowledgement party flips from asking to giving

The mechanism inside [[The Acknowledgement Party]], and it's the part Dave clearly engineered.

The exercise begins with the *harder* act. Asking for acknowledgement is more exposing than giving it, and worse the more senior you are — Jesse, as CEO, felt he wasn't supposed to need or want it from people he was paying.

> It was so awkward, I'm talking like, you rather would hear a chalkboard screeching.

First round: thirty minutes. Second round: ten. Then, partway through, nobody is asking any more:

> Rather than asking for acknowledgement, people started saying — actually, John, I really want to acknowledge you for running the New York office.

Two hours, everyone in tears, colleagues of many years saying things they had never said.

The flip works because the awkward part is front-loaded. Having survived asking, giving is easy, and the room has established that this is the kind of conversation now happening. A session that began with *let's all appreciate each other* would have stayed polite and thin.

Jesse's verdict, and the reason it's a party rather than an exercise: "everybody walked away feeling seen and felt in a way that had never happened."

Dave's reaction when Jesse realised it had been designed: *yeah, that's called an acknowledgement party.*
