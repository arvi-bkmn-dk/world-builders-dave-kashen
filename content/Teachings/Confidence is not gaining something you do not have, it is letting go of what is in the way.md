# Confidence is not gaining something you do not have, it is letting go of what is in the way

Dave's reframe for the most common thing clients ask him for.

> Many of my clients tell me that they'd like to be more confident. Consider that being confident is not about gaining something you don't have, but rather letting go of what's in the way of it.

Which makes confidence **subtractive**. Not a quality to accumulate through achievement or preparation — something already present, obscured.

What is in the way is specific: belief in the inner voice.

> When you recognize that the voice inside your head has no more authority than a stranger muttering to himself, you'll unlock a wellspring of confidence **that has been within you all along.**

This matters because the additive approach has an obvious failure mode — his work is full of people who accumulated the achievements and did not get the confidence. → [[The burden of proof is an illusion]] · [[The whole game is rigged because when never arrives]]

It also explains why welcoming self-doubt increases confidence rather than reducing it, which otherwise sounds backwards: the fight against doubt is itself the evidence that something is wrong. → [[Welcome the self-doubt and you actually feel more confident]]

Same structure as [[Via negativa says there is nothing to attain, only barriers to remove]] — the work is removal, and what's left was there the whole time.
