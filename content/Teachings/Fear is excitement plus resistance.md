# Fear is excitement plus resistance

Dave pairs two emotions most people treat as opposites and shows they share a source.

> Fear is the energy of the unknown. And this is an interesting one to couple with excitement, 'cause excitement is also the energy of the unknown. The simplistic way to think about it is: fear is excitement plus resistance. Excitement is the unknown without resistance.

Same input, different relationship to it. Which means the goal is not to eliminate uncertainty — Dave's thought experiment shows founders do not actually want that:

> Let's imagine you could watch the whole movie all the way through to your IPO right now, and you'll know exactly what's gonna happen at every step. They're like — it sounds boring. I don't even know if I'd do it anymore.

The uncertainty is the reason they started. Jesse names the bind: "anyone who starts a company fundamentally wants the adventure, but then this other part of you is like, ah, this is scary." Dave's correction is that the trouble is not the amount of fear but the resistance to it — "I wanted it to be *this* scary, but oh, it's *this* scary."

**The garden hose.** Dave's image for why approaching fear works:

> Imagine there's a garden hose way off in the distance and it really looks like a snake. As you get closer, you get more and more scared and it gets more and more intense, because you think you're getting closer and closer to a snake. And then finally you get close enough to see it's a garden hose.

Two things follow. Intensifying fear is not evidence you are heading the wrong way — it is what approach feels like. And the wisdom in fear is a question: **what wants to become known?** Which is also why, as Dave puts it, people who are "arrogant or righteous or think they know it all don't learn" — refusing the fear costs them the knowledge on the other side of it.
