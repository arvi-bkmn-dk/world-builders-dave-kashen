# The mind is a storyteller and good stories need conflict

Dave's account of **why** the mind manufactures problems, rather than just the observation that it does.

> The mind is like **the storyteller.**

He asks what makes a good story; Jesse answers **drama**, and then **conflict**. Dave:

> Conflict problems. Yeah.

Then the image he had that morning:

> I was envisioning a short story of this storyteller throughout time. A thousand years ago, there was so much to tell — there was lions and tigers and dragons, all these real dangers. And now all that stuff's gone, **but the storyteller is bored.** So they keep **weaving these stories of problems and conflicts.**

The consequence is not merely unpleasant, it is **inaccurate**:

> The mind lives in **the past and the future**, and in stories that are not true. It's not what's actually occurring now.

> **Most people's realities are, I think, like ten times better than their emotional experience.**

He adds the two observable signatures of the storyteller's output: a **massive negativity bias** — *"80, 90% of the things are negative, which is not most people's experience; we don't actually have many more negative things happening than positive"* — and being **self-referential**: *"we're in a world of 8 billion other humans, but most of our thoughts are just about us."*

And why it is built that way: survival patterning calibrated for the wrong century. *"We're wired for a world of constant physical survival threats, and we don't live in that world… so what are we so scared about all the time?"*
