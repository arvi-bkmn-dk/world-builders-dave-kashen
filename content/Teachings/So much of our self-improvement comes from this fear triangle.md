# So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle

An aside that indicts most personal development, including the kind that works for a while.

> So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle. Oh, what's the matter with you? You should be better at this, you should be more confident. Oh, but poor me, but it's hard. Okay, I'm gonna have a 30-day plan.

Three moves in sequence: **villain** (the self-criticism), **victim** (the complaint), **hero** (the program that will fix it). The 30-day plan feels like the escape from the first two, but it's the third corner of the same shape.

Dave extends it to what gets sold:

> Think of how many products and services are sold to us as hero moves. This will be the thing that will — you know, you'll finally lose the weight. And how many times does it actually work?

The answer is in the structure rather than the product:

> When we're in that cycle, we just tend to recycle.

A hero move produces temporary relief and leaves the thing that generated the cycle untouched — the same failure Dave describes when a founder rescues their team, applied to rescuing yourself. This is also why the yo-yo shows up here as it does in [[Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and that is what creates the yo-yo]]: a plan launched from self-criticism carries the criticism with it.
