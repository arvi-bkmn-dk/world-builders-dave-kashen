# I am a loser and I am also a winner, and both are partially true

Why the shadow is unbearable in the first place, and it is not the content of the accusation.

> Part of the issue is because our minds, especially when we're scared, tend to get so polarized. If I think I'm a loser, and I think that's the whole truth — that's pretty hard to be with. But I'm also a winner. It's like getting to the place where you can see both are partially true.

The intolerable thing is the **totality**, not the trait. *I lose sometimes* is unremarkable; *I am a loser, entirely* is a verdict. Polarisation converts the first into the second, and then it has to be fought off.

Dave's own accounting holds both at once: "there's so many ways that I am a loser" and "I've won in so many ways — I have a wonderful life and a successful career and family." Neither cancels the other.

And the cost is in insisting on only one:

> When I need just one to be true and not the other, I'll twist myself in knots trying to make sure that I'm not seen as a loser, that I don't lose. And that really limited me.

This is his work's general claim about stories applied to identity — [[Every story your mind tells is partially true]] — which is also why [[Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong]] works on shadows as well as beliefs.
