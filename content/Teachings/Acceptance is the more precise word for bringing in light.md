# Acceptance is the more precise word for bringing in light

The last exchange of the episode is Dave making a small correction that changes the whole instruction. Jesse has just advised bringing more light in rather than fighting the dark parts. Dave agrees with the direction and then names the trap in the metaphor.

> I might translate the word light to acceptance — radical self-acceptance. The risk of light is some people start to think: oh, so joy is good and sadness is bad, excitement good, fear bad, anger bad, as if those are dark. Those are not dark. It's actually the acceptance of all the parts and all the feelings.

The distinction is exact. "More light" can be heard as *have more of the pleasant feelings and fewer of the unpleasant ones*, which is another sorting of emotions into acceptable and unacceptable — the same machinery that made anger unfeelable and shame unspeakable in the first place. Acceptance doesn't sort. It's what makes anger available as boundary information and fear available as data rather than as a verdict on you.

Dave lands it on love: love is unconditional by nature, and anything conditional isn't love but manipulation or dependence. Which makes the path "becoming increasingly unconditional" — including toward your own feelings.
