# Start from entitled to nothing and appreciation wells up on its own

Dave's practice for producing real appreciation rather than performing gratitude.

> If we're really honest, most of us feel entitled to lots of things — entitled to being good at your job, entitled to having the results you want. And it really gets in the way of appreciation.

The reset:

> I am entitled to nothing. Nothing. I shouldn't even be here — the statistical probability of me being alive rounds to zero. So I'm not entitled to having any experience at all.

> And if I start with that ground of zero, suddenly what naturally wells up is a ton of appreciation. For being alive, for breathing air, for having running water, for friendship, for getting to do work that I love, for a loving family.

**Wells up** is the operative phrase. Nothing is manufactured; the appreciation was being suppressed by a baseline, and lowering the baseline releases it. This is why it works where gratitude journalling often doesn't — it changes what you're measuring against instead of instructing you to feel something.

The default it displaces:

> Most of us start the other way. We imagine the absolute ideal version of our life, feel entitled to it, and then get frustrated and disappointed the moment our life doesn't match that.

Applied to people, it's worse, because they can tell: *this is exactly how I expect my wife to be* — so the moment she doesn't meet the mind's ideal, disappointment, "instead of just appreciating that she's even spending one moment with you."

This also explains why appreciation is scarce in ambitious environments without anyone intending cruelty. If the reference point is the ideal, nothing actual clears it, and a shortfall doesn't prompt thanks. → [[The Entitlement Practice]]
