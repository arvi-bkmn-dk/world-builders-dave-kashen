# Your mind is a wonderful servant but a terrible master

The line Dave uses to place presence, and it settles what the practice is *for*.

> It's been said that "your mind is a wonderful servant but a terrible master." Being present allows you to choose which thoughts to listen to and engage with, and which to ignore.

The mind is not the problem and silencing it is not the goal:

> Being present isn't about eliminating thoughts or emotions, but about observing them without attachment, allowing you to engage fully with life as it unfolds.

The variable is **who is in charge**. Serving, the mind is the most useful thing a founder has. Directing, it selects what you notice, decides what things mean, and generates the futures you then react to.

Dave pairs it with a second half people skip: "being present is also about **feeling all of your feelings**, instead of repressing or denying feelings, or escaping into your mind." Presence is not only a cognitive stance — escaping into thought is one of the main ways feeling gets avoided. → [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]

And the payoff he claims is operational: "lead with clarity, make better decisions, and stay grounded under pressure."
