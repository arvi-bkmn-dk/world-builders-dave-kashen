# Meditation is not quieting the voice, it is noticing

The misunderstanding that makes most people conclude they're bad at meditating, corrected directly.

> A lot of people think the goal is to quiet the voice in the head. It is nice when the voice quiets, and it does sometimes. And it often doesn't. That's not a problem — if you can see through it.

The goal is relocated from the voice's behaviour to your relationship with it:

> It's just like: oh, this is just occurring, this voice is chattering on in my mind. And then you go about your business.

Which is why success doesn't depend on silence. Dave returns to the café metaphor — a muttering stranger sits next to you saying *you're terrible* — and adds a refinement he hadn't made in earlier episodes:

> Just because the voice appears to be inside us and sounds like us, it's not necessarily even really talking *to* us. It's useful to think that it's almost talking to itself.

That's a further step than ignoring it. Not *I choose not to take this personally*, but *this was never addressed to me.*

The practical consequence is the one that keeps people practising:

> If you have nine and a half minutes of just focusing on different thoughts, but one time you notice that's occurring and bring your attention back — that's useful. That's the practice.

A wandering session isn't a failed session; the wandering supplies the reps. → [[The Meditation Practice]]
