# A disowned quality becomes a third rail your system will not let you go near

Why most people never accept a disowned quality: it feels dangerous. Accept your selfish side and surely you'll become more selfish.

> But the opposite is true.

The denied part leaks out in ways you didn't choose, or you overcompensate until you lose the healthy version too. Deny your arrogance completely and you may not be able to assert yourself or own what you're great at.

When the quality is core to your identity, Dave says it goes further:

> It becomes what I think of as a third rail, a line your system won’t let you go near, even when going near it would actually serve you.

His retreat example: a woman for whom *"I am not cruel"* was central couldn't be direct with people, because directness felt too close to cruelty. And the energy didn't vanish:

> The quality she refused to express outward was being expressed inward as self-attack.

> The disowned part doesn’t disappear. It relocates.
