# You cannot change what you do not accept

> What you resist persists. You can't change what you don't accept.

The line that makes acceptance a practical instrument rather than a spiritual attitude. Fighting the facts — "this shouldn't be happening," "this isn't fair," "I shouldn't have to deal with this" — keeps you stuck, because each of those statements is an argument with a condition that already obtains, and arguments with conditions don't alter them.

What happens when you accept the facts as they are, *without collapsing into resignation or blame*, is described physiologically rather than philosophically: the nervous system relaxes, creativity returns, solutions become visible again, and you can respond from alignment rather than fear.

That sequence is the actual claim. Acceptance isn't valued for being wise; it is valued because a threatened nervous system cannot see options, and an unthreatened one can. The same shift is what moves you out of a state of threat and back into presence. → [[Presence or threat is the simple version of above and below the line]]

The qualifier matters as much as the claim — acceptance that tips into resignation is a different thing and produces none of this. → [[Acceptance is the doorway to effective action, not apathy]]
