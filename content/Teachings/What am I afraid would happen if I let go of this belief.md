# What am I afraid would happen if I let go of this belief

The question Dave substitutes when the obvious one fails — and his explanation of *why* it fails is the valuable part.

> The mind operates with **"meta-beliefs"** — beliefs about beliefs — that create fear around letting go of familiar patterns. This fear, that it's not safe to let go of a particular belief, makes us avoid looking directly at the truth or questioning our assumptions.

> This is why simply asking **"Is this belief true?"** often doesn't work.

That is the diagnosis most people never reach. The limiting belief is not held because it survived examination; it is held because a second belief makes examining it feel dangerous. Interrogating the first one directly runs into a guard you cannot see.

So:

> Instead, we need to ask: **"What am I afraid would happen if I let go of this belief?"** This question reveals the psychological safety mechanism that's keeping the belief in place.

Dave's worked example: if I believe I'm unworthy, I may also believe *I need to believe I'm unworthy in order to be motivated to improve.*

> When you start to see and question the meta-belief, it then clears the path to let go of the initial core belief.

Note the order — you cannot drop the core belief while its guard is intact, and the guard always presents as something practical rather than something false. → [[Founders choose fear because they believe their drive would disappear without it]]

It is the same structure Dave finds in [[Unfelt feelings are the glue that keeps unwanted patterns in place]]: insight alone doesn't shift a belief, because something underneath is holding it.
