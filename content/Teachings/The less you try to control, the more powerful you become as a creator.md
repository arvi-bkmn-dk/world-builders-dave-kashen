# The less you try to control, the more powerful you become as a creator

The obvious objection to accepting what you can't control is that it sounds passive. Dave answers with the gravity image again:

> We don't fight gravity. And that doesn't make us passive.

> We build planes. We climb mountains. We launch rockets.

> The less you try to control, the more powerful you become as a creator.

The ambition doesn't shrink. What changes is where it comes from:

> You still play to win. You still have a vision. You still take massive action.

> But it comes from presence rather than threat.

And there is a second cost to control beyond wasted energy. Trying to manage someone's reaction or your own discomfort is what stops you doing the authentic thing: the hard conversation, letting someone go, the honest feedback.

> So the question is: where is your wanting to control or needing to control things getting in the way of you doing what feels authentic?
