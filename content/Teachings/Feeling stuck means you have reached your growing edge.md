# Feeling stuck means you have reached your growing edge

Jesse describes the call founders make in private and almost never in public — *"Hey Dave, I'm stuck. I don't know where to take the company."* Dave's reaction is not sympathy:

> Those conversations usually **excite** me, because I'm like — ah, this client has reached their **edge.** Their **growing** edge.

The logic is airtight once stated:

> If they were within their comfort zone, they wouldn't feel stuck. 'Cause they'd be like, *I know how to do this.* So they've hit their edge of what they currently know how to do — or which feelings they're comfortable feeling.

Jesse catches it immediately: *"that's a powerful reframe, even right there."* And Dave extends it — *"if I feel stuck, it's like, oh, maybe I've hit some edge of my development, and that means I'm gonna expand beyond my previous limitations."*

The cost of the ordinary reading:

> Seeing stuck as a problem is **self-fulfilling.** It's like, *oh, it's bad that I'm stuck* — now you're **stuck around being stuck.**

→ [[Fear lives at the edge between the familiar and unfamiliar parts of yourself]] · Your personal edges create your company's ceiling · The Edge Expansion Process

Dave also warns against assuming every slow patch is an edge at all:

> I think about a river. A river will flow mightily, and then there'll be a part where the banks widen and it'll just go real slow, and then it speeds up again. Sometimes we think we should always be moving quickly towards something, and we don't honor the **ebbs and flows.**
