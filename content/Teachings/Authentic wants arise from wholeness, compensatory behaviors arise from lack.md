# Authentic wants arise from wholeness, compensatory behaviors arise from lack

The foundation beneath all four levels of [[The Four Levels of Behavior Change]], and the reason Dave says no framework rescues a behavior you don't actually want.

> You have to be following something **your heart genuinely wants.** Not what you think you *should* want. Not what will **prove your worth.**

The distinction that does the work:

> When you're operating from self-judgment, you develop behaviors to **soothe that pain.** Scrolling social media to numb the anxiety. Overworking to prove you're enough. Drinking to escape the pressure.

> **Those aren't wants. Those are compensatory behaviors.**

> Authentic wants are different. **They arise from wholeness, not from lack.** They don't try to complete you **because you're already complete.** They lead you deeper into who you actually are.

> They're **expressions of love, not attempts to earn it.**

And the cost that makes them hard to follow:

> Following an authentic want often means **disappointing someone.** Leaving the "safe" path. Choosing your truth over their expectations. Your conditioning says: *stay safe, follow the rules, don't rock the boat.*

Dave's diagnostic question before attempting any change:

> **Is this something I authentically want? Or am I trying to fix something I think is wrong with me?** Because if it's the latter, **no framework will make it sustainable.** You're trying to fill a void instead of authentically expressing yourself.
