# Summarising what someone said and guessing their feelings and wants makes them feel heard

The conscious-listening half of Dave's communication work, demonstrated live rather than described. Jesse reflects Andrew's position back to him — the story Andrew is making up, and what he actually wants when he builds a business — then checks: *is that right? Is there more?*

Andrew's reaction is the teaching, and it includes his prior scepticism:

> When you did that in our sales accelerator you said: when you've got a question or frustration from a customer, sum it up. And at the time I go, this would sound so phony. And then I talked to you a few days later about an issue we had and you summarized it just like this, and I go — I do feel very heard. I feel like he actually cares about me.

The "phony" objection is the same one raised against Speaking Unarguably, and it dissolves the same way: on the receiving end, the mechanics are invisible and only the attention registers. → [[Conscious leadership language is scaffolding, not the destination]]

Dave then pushes Jesse a step further — *guess what he might be feeling and wanting* — producing: "I'm guessing you're feeling a little scared and a little frustrated, and what you really want is to know and feel like you're having success." A guess, offered as a guess, checkable.

The check is what keeps it honest. Summarising without *is that right?* is assertion wearing the costume of listening.
