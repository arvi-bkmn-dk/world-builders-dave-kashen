# The whole game is rigged because when never arrives

The structural argument for why external sourcing fails — not that it is unwise, that it is impossible.

> It's like a rigged game, because **when** never arrives. Or the whatever's after the *when* always changes.

Dave walks the loop:

> I'll have a sense of safety when I have  million. You arrive at the  million and you're like — oh, I don't feel a sense of safety. Then you say, okay, I guess I was wrong about the X. Maybe it's 20 million. And you arrive at 20 million and — nope, still don't feel it. Maybe it's 50, maybe it's a hundred, maybe it's a billion.

The revision is always to the number, never to the premise. Each arrival produces evidence that the model is wrong and gets read as evidence that the target was wrong.

> The joke of it is the whole game is rigged. If you can't experience it now, you'll never experience it. But you can experience it now.

And when the number stops being believable, the *game* changes rather than the logic. Jesse's observation about people far wealthier than him asking how to build an audience: "now they want influence." Dave: "you just change the game to something you don't already have, because it's inside that same mindset — there must be something out there that's gonna give it to me."

His image for it: the **hungry ghost.** "You can never fill the hungry ghost. Everything you try to fill it with goes right through."

The reason it cannot land, in Dave's reading: "who we really are is already whole and complete and enough. So the belief that I need this thing to feel safe inherently obfuscates us from recognising our inherent wholeness." → [[You are already whole, perfect and complete]]
