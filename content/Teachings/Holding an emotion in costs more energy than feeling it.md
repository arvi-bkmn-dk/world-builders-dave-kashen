# Holding an emotion in costs more energy than feeling it

Asked what a person who says they are not emotional is actually doing, Dave's answer is plain — "repressing and denying their feelings" — and then he adds the part that reframes it as an economic question rather than a moral one:

> If you think about it, it takes a lot of energy to hold in the emotions. A lot more than just feeling them. So a lot of us, our whole lives have just been holding it in, and it becomes an unconscious thing.

Suppression is not the absence of effort, it is continuous effort — and because it became automatic in childhood, the expenditure never shows up on the ledger. The founder running it experiences the drain without a line item to attribute it to.

Two things follow.

The energy is finite and spoken for. Dave calls creative energy "the most precious resource you have" for building something; a standing withdrawal against it is expensive in a way that has nothing to do with emotional life and everything to do with capacity.

And it is stored in the body rather than dissipated — the constriction "lives in the fascia," which is Dave's explanation for why "so many people have chronic health issues and stress issues."

The comparison he draws is to physical training, where the same person accepts discomfort without argument: "we go to the gym and we push ourselves — no pain, no gain. We run straight up a hill and burn out our leg because we want to get stronger. But then, oh, a little bit of constriction in my belly — no, no, no, I don't want to be with that. So I'll just stay in this marriage."

The avoided sensation is smaller than the one willingly chosen. Only the category differs.
