# You are already whole, perfect and complete

Dave plants this near the end as a seed for a future conversation, flagging it as provocative.

> You actually are already whole, perfect and complete. All the voices that say I need to be better, I need to be more visionary — it's just a voice yabbering on in your mind. At your essence you literally are already everything you need to be. There's no *there*. You don't need to improve yourself.

He names the objection before anyone raises it:

> And then the fear comes up: ah, but if I believe that, then I won't improve myself, I won't get better, I won't achieve.

Which is the same fear as [[Founders choose fear because they believe their drive would disappear without it]], now aimed at the deepest layer. Not *if I stop whipping myself I'll stop working*, but *if I stop needing to become someone, I'll stop becoming anyone.*

Dave's reported experience is the counter-evidence:

> This like deep knowing of the truth, truth of that, which sort of alleviates a lot of the like hustle…

The drive doesn't vanish; the frantic quality does. Which makes this the endpoint of [[Self-discovery matters more than self-improvement]] — if the true self can't be improved, then discovering it is the whole of the work, and the improvement project was always aimed at the persona.

It's also why the [[The Talker and the Listener|listener]] matters. "It's just a voice yabbering on in your mind" is only a usable claim for someone who has separated from the talker; to anyone still identified with it, the voice isn't commentary, it's an accurate assessment. → [[There is a way of creating results that feels like flow rather than grind]]
