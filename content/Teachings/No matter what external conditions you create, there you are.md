# No matter what external conditions you create, there you are

Dave's report from the other side of the exits, and he is specific about the sample: several clients a year who take $10M or more off the table.

> There **is** a celebration, there's a joy. But like **within a week** they're like — *okay, now **this** is the problem.*

> *Now I've never really worked for a big company. What if I'm not gonna be happy here? Now I'm trapped and I gotta stick around. Now I'm worried about **losing** this money — how do I invest it?*

The case that lands hardest:

> I had a client yesterday — hundreds of millions of dollars sale, made substantially more than $10 million. And it was: **"I'm more stressed about money today than I was before I started the company."**

Jesse admits the ordinary reaction: *"I used to not understand why people were still working. I'd be like, oh, they must not have made enough."*

Dave's conclusion:

> The nature of the ego seems to be **to create problems and suffering.** And so **no matter where you go, there you are. No matter what external conditions you create, there you are.**

Which turns into the question he says he feels passionate about:

> Examine whether **the game you're playing is the one you actually want to win.** Or said another way — **whether winning the game you're playing will actually give you the thing you think it will.**

The same trap runs in the spiritual version, and Jesse raises it: people for whom *"spiritual enlightenment is the exact parallel to a founder wanting to sell their company for a billion dollars."* → [[The limited perspective cannot have the expanded perspective]]
