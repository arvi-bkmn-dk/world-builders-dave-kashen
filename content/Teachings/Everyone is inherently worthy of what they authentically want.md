# Everyone is inherently worthy of what they authentically want

Dave's resolution of the worthiness question, arrived at by squaring Charlie Munger's *you have to deserve what you get* with the woo version, *raise your vibration*. Both say you must bring yourself up to the thing. The question is how.

> Most people hear that as: I have to become worthy of it by achieving all these things. Another way to become worthy of it is by shifting the belief that you're not worthy of it. My view is that everyone is inherently worthy, period — and worthy of what they authentically want.

Note the qualifier he flags himself: *authentically*. Not whatever the [[The Seven Storyteller Archetypes|comparison storyteller]] has nominated this week — what you actually want.

The reason the achievement route fails is structural, and it's the same shape as [[Bigger is better is a treadmill that never arrives]]:

> It's just around the corner, I'll become worthy, but I'm not yet. And that little trick can perpetuate forever. You never get around the corner.

Because the unworthiness was never a factual deficit that an achievement could close, arriving doesn't resolve it — the threshold simply advances. Dave's point is that the belief is the thing to address directly, as a [[Core Beliefs|core belief]], not to out-achieve.
