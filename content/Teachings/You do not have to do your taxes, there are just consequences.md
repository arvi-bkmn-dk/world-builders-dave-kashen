# You do not have to do your taxes, there are just consequences

Diana Chapman's line, and the one that broke Dave's years-long resistance to a task he was doing anyway.

> Diana Chapman at one point said, you don't have to do your taxes. Like — you don't have to, it just, there's just consequences. I was like, oh, that's true.

What it dissolves is the category of **obligation**. Nothing is compulsory; everything is a choice with outcomes attached. Dave still files — "I'd rather stay in America and not pay fines" — but now as something chosen rather than imposed.

Why that matters is in his description of the before state: "for years I resisted doing my taxes. When I say I resisted, I did it — but I resisted it *while doing it.*" That is the expensive configuration: full cost of the work, plus the cost of fighting yourself through it. → [[Most people choose to do a thing and resist doing it at the same time]]

The reframe removes the second cost without changing the activity. "I stopped seeing it as an obligation, and then I let go of resisting it, because I'm like, okay, I'm gonna do this thing, I might as well just do it."

It is the same mechanism as [[Willing means literally right now, not someday in the future]] — restoring the fact that you are choosing, which is what makes resistance unnecessary.

## Also in [[GA 02 - A CEO Coach Reflects on His Performance as a Dad, Dad Pod]]

The parenting version comes from Dave's own coach, after he told his kids he had to work:

> you don't have to work you're choosing to work and what message are you saying them when you tell them I have to work

Dave now says *I'm choosing to focus on work right now, I'll play with you later*. He adds that the old breadwinner script has flipped into a counter-narrative, where pursuing purpose is wrong, and both are too binary.
