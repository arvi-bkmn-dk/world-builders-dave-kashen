# Withhold, withdraw, project is how relationships calcify

The mechanism by which a small unsaid thing becomes a permanent verdict about a person.

> There's a phenomenon known as **"Withhold-Withdraw-Project."** Once we withhold our truth from someone, we tend to disconnect from them. We then see them through the lens of our projected stories about them — gathering evidence that confirms our pre-existing story and ignoring evidence to the contrary.

> Over time, we calcify our view ("that guy in accounting is a jerk") leaving no possibility for repair or reconnection.

Three stages, each making the next more likely. **Withholding** is usually protective — avoiding conflict, sparing feelings, not losing control of the situation. **Withdrawal** follows because you cannot stay close to someone you are managing. **Projection** then fills the gap with a story that gets confirmed, because you are no longer gathering disconfirming evidence.

The verdict at the end feels like something you learned about them. It is something you built.

Dave's correction of the premise underneath:

> We think the person can't handle the truth, or we're afraid of what sharing our true thoughts might do to our relationship. But in fact, **not sharing is what kills relationships.**

His opening story is the case in reverse: a CEO whose board member kept cancelling their 1:1s had built the story that he thought little of her. She checked it, and got *"you're my strongest CEO — I figured you didn't need me."* All the tension "vanished in minutes once they communicated."

## Also in [[NL 65 - Co-Founder Relationships That Can Navigate Anything]]

The newsletter credits the pattern to Gay and Kathlyn Hendricks and spells out the antidote, **Reveal, Connect, Own**:

- **Reveal** what's true for you, including the story you made up.
- **Connect**: scary as the conversation is, revealing is itself what creates connection.
- **Own** the projection. *How is it actually true about me?*

> The act of revealing creates real connection between two people.

> Usually what we're seeing in the other person is something we're projecting from ourselves.

> Without a shared commitment to candor, it's incredibly easy to fall into this cycle. Alex did for eight years.

→ [[One reveal can dissolve years of projection]]
