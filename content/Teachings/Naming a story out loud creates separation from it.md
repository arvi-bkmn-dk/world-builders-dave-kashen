# Naming a story out loud creates separation from it

Step 1 of [[The Story Release Technique]], and the smallest high-leverage move in Dave's toolkit. When you hear the mind generating thoughts, pause long enough to name the narrative:

> "The story my mind is making up is…" This instantly creates separation between you and the narrative.

The mechanism is structural rather than persuasive. Saying the sentence requires occupying a position outside the story in order to describe it, which is itself the separation. You do not have to disbelieve the story for this to work — only to put it in quotes.

Two optional follow-ons, for when the story is sticky: look for evidence to disprove it, or ask *how might the opposite of that story be true?* Dave's claim is modest and honest — you can typically see how the story is "partially, if not entirely, untrue." Not always entirely. Partially is enough to loosen the grip.

The same sentence appears in Speaking Unarguably as a communication move, which is worth noticing: naming a story does the same work whether the audience is another person or only yourself.
