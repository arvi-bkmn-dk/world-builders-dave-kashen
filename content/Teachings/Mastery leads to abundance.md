# Mastery leads to abundance

The mechanism Dave offers for why following purpose didn't end in poverty — and it is a chain, not a promise.

The first year after quitting was genuinely lean: a laundry room converted into an office, *"my wife's friends would come over and I'd make them cry,"* charging very little and eating into savings. *"God bless my wife. She just believed in me."*

Then Nir Eyal, then two friends, then two more, through the Stanford network. And the reason it compounded:

> I kept following that, being **in flow and my zone of genius.** And I just kept getting better at the work **because I genuinely loved it.** Not because I was fiercely competitive or trying to be the best coach. I just love learning about how to help human beings thrive.

> **When you love something that much, you naturally keep getting better at it. That leads to mastery, and mastery leads to abundance.**

The order is the claim: love → improvement → mastery → abundance. Abundance is the fourth term, never the first, which is why aiming at it directly breaks the chain. → [[Spend more of your time in your zone of genius]] · [[There is a way of creating results that feels like flow rather than grind]]

Dave notes the cliché and endorses it anyway:

> I truly thought I was making the choice of purpose and passion over money. And I was. But it's also this total cliche: **do what you love and the money will follow.**

And the coda: Chris Schroeder, the CEO he resigned to, *"was super gracious. He appreciated what I was doing. And then he ended up hiring me as his coach. He was one of my first clients."*

> When you have the courage to follow what you're truly here to do, **life has a way of supporting you in unexpected ways. But none of that happens if you're not willing to make the first terrifying call.**
