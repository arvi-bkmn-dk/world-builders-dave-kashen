# Blame and vengeance recreate the conflict, investing in a shared future ends it

A historical contrast Dave calls instructive, raised while asking how to break a cycle of violence rather than win it.

> The Treaty of Versailles focused on blaming the Germans (and their allies) and making them pay for what they had done.

> The focus was on creating a peaceful, prosperous future.

The first settlement looked backward at who was to blame and what they owed. Dave notes that most historians see the humiliation and economic toll it created as central to what led to the Second World War. The second, the Marshall Plan, invested in the economies of the defeated countries and ushered in a sustained peace.

It is the drama-triangle argument at the scale of nations. Blame keeps the victim and villain roles alive, while a declared shared future pulls every party toward the same outcome.
