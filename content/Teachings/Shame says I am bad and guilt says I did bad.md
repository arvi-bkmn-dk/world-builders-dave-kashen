# Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad

Dave adds shame and guilt to the conscious leadership set of primary emotions — joy, excitement, sadness, anger, fear — on pragmatic grounds: "a lot of people experience those."

Strictly they are composites rather than primaries:

> Conscious leadership would say shame tends to be kind of fear and sadness and a story that there's something wrong with *me*. And guilt is the same, but a story that there's that *I did* something wrong.

Which gives the compression:

> Shame is like fundamental — I'm bad. Guilt is — I did bad.

Both are the same emotional ingredients differing only in where the story attaches. Guilt indicts an action, which leaves a person intact and something to repair. Shame indicts the person, which leaves nothing to act on — and this is why shame tends to immobilise where guilt can motivate.

The distinction earns its place elsewhere in the episode. When Dave walks founders down through what they are actually afraid of, shame is repeatedly what they land on: "I think I'm most scared of feeling disappointed and ashamed." The startup failing is not the feared object — the shame underneath it is.

Note the structural rhyme with the wider model. Both are feelings **plus a story**, which is what makes them workable: the sadness and fear are just energy, and the story attached to them is the part available for examination.
