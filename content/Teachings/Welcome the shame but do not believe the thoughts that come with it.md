# Welcome the shame but do not believe the thoughts that come with it

The precise instruction, and the precision is the whole thing — welcoming shame does *not* mean agreeing with it.

> I welcome the shame. Now, I don't welcome or believe the thoughts that come with the shame. So it's not like — yes, I am bad and undisciplined. I don't believe those thoughts, I see through those thoughts. But I welcome the physical feeling of shame.

The split is between the **sensation** and the **story**. Shame arrives as both: a physical state, and a verdict about you. The sensation is safe to feel and moves through. The verdict is a story and stays false whether or not you feel the sensation.

Dave describes doing the physical half deliberately: "I might let myself hunch all the way down and just — ugh. I feel that tightness in my stomach, and I just stop resisting it."

What he reports is modest and worth the modesty: "pretty consistently what I see is the pattern at least lessens, lightens." Not elimination — he still eats sweets, and is not sure he wants to stop. What changed is the charge: "it just doesn't feel charged for me anymore."

The general rule underneath it is his work's standing one, applied to the hardest case: [[No emotion is painful, the resistance to it is]]. The resistance to shame is what keeps the habit cycle turning, so shame is the one feeling most worth welcoming — and the one people are least willing to.
