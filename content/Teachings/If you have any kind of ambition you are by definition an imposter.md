# If you have any kind of ambition you are by definition an imposter

Dave removes the premise rather than treating the symptom.

> Most founders think the problem is that they're in over their head. But **being in over your head is literally the job description.** You're doing things you've never done before, facing challenges that have no playbook, making decisions without complete information.

> If you have any kind of ambition, you are, by definition, an imposter. You're attempting things today you haven't mastered yet.

> The real trap is thinking you **shouldn't** be in over your head.

And the cycle that belief runs:

> You don't know what to do → You think you should know → You pretend you know → **Your team senses the pretense** → Trust erodes → You feel even more pressure to perform.

The cost is not only trust: "the intelligence and perspective of your entire team sits unused because you've signaled there's no space for collaborative problem-solving."

The exhaustion founders attribute to workload is often this instead — "not the hours, but the exhaustion of trying to be something you're not all the time." → [[The hours are not the point, the quality of those hours is]]

And the paradox at the end:

> People are far more willing to follow a leader who admits when they don't know than one who pretends to know everything.
