# Being in your head is a strategy to avoid a feeling

Dave's simplest diagnostic, and he expects the objection:

> One of the **easiest tells** that you're not feeling your feelings, and likely blocking aliveness and flow, is that **you're in your head.** So I suspect every human being listening to this is like — *wait, what? That's like every minute of every day.*

> Especially you're in your head **spinning, ruminating** — *oh, thinking about what might go wrong. Oh, I just screwed up that meeting. Oh God, now my people are gonna be upset and morale's gonna be low.*

> And what I think is really interesting and **not intuitive** is that **all of that spinning is actually just a strategy to avoid a feeling.**

That reverses the usual account. The rumination isn't a symptom of distress you can't resolve; it's the machinery doing its job — generating enough cognitive noise that the feeling underneath never has to be felt.

Jesse adds the cultural layer, from their shared background: *"most of my friends are still living in the world which is like, thoughts are truth, logic is truth."* And the quote he offers as the best one for the topic — **the mind is a wonderful servant but a terrible master.**

Dave's consequence:

> If you're walking through the world thinking that your body is just a **tool to carry your head around**, and thinking that **you are your thoughts** — you're not going to be attuned to the intelligence of your body, of your energy, of your feelings. And you're likely not going to know **what has you feel alive.**

Which costs you at both scales: *"whether that's at a macro level like this life path or this life partner, or at a micro level like this little decision."*

## Also in [[NL 63 - Why Presence Is the Foundation of Everything]]

Dave's fullest statement of the idea, and the sequence he says shows up everywhere once you see it:

> One of the things I've been realizing more and more over the past couple years is how thoughts are really just reactions to not welcoming feelings.

> Every time your attention is hooked by your thoughts, your mind is running from something it doesn't want you to feel.

1. A feeling arises you don't want to face (fear, self-doubt, shame, even too much excitement)
2. The mind generates thoughts to escape it
3. You disconnect from yourself and from what's actually happening
4. The mind fills in its own version: stories, predictions, interpretations
5. You see everything through those stories and gather evidence for them
6. The stories calcify

> The thinking IS the escape.

> You're no longer reacting to reality. You're reacting to a world your mind constructed.
