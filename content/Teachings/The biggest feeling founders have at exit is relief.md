# The biggest feeling founders have at exit is relief

Dave reports from a vantage point almost nobody has — beside founders through the striving, the exit, and the months after.

> What I've seen as a pattern is: there is a definite relief. **That's probably the biggest feeling founders have — relief.** *Oh, finally, I'm done striving to get to this thing and I got it.*

> Which is funny — in a way it's **more relief than even joy.** And it's **not fulfilment**, generally. Or maybe it is for a day or two. **Give it a week at most.**

Then:

> You sort of start feeling **the same feelings.** Your mental patterns just start applying to **something different.** *Oh God, what if I don't perform well at the parent company that just acquired us?*

Jesse: *"I'm a one-hit wonder."* Dave: *"I see that again and again. They go to start the next one and they're **even more stressed** — what if I get found out, that the last one where I made a hundred million was just a fluke?"*

The relief/fulfilment distinction is the useful part. Relief is the **ending of a pressure**, so it is bounded by how long the pressure took to build and gone the moment a new one starts. It was never evidence that the thing delivered; it was evidence of how much it had cost.

Jesse's accounting of the price:

> Non-acceptance is gonna **mortgage** anywhere from five to fifteen years — with your family, with your life. There are parts of the Ampush journey, especially early on, that are **complete blurs** to me. **I genuinely don't remember them.**

> Only to find out you're gonna land in a place where, even with $20 million in the bank, you're **still not gonna feel fulfilled.**
