# Repressed anger goes inward and shows up as depression

Dave flags this as recent learning rather than settled doctrine, which is worth preserving:

> When it gets repressed, it can then be **inward.** I actually think — I've learned the last like year or two — **a lot of depression is actually anger repressed, and therefore at self.** *What's wrong with me? I'm bad. I'm broken.*

> That's another form where it can leak out in unhealthy ways, when it's anger **at self.** This really loud **inner bully, inner critic** — *you're terrible.*

The energy doesn't disappear when it isn't expressed; it changes target. Anger that can't go outward as a boundary goes inward as a verdict, and the verdict sounds so much like ordinary self-assessment that it is almost never recognised as anger at all.

This connects two things the graph otherwise keeps separate: the inner critic material — [[Your inner voice has no more authority than a stranger muttering in a diner]], [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]] — and the boundary material. On this account the inner bully is not a thinking problem to be argued with. It is misdirected fight energy with nowhere legitimate to go.

It also gives the practice in [[Move anger with sound and movement in a safe way]] a second purpose: moving anger safely outward is how you stop it being aimed at yourself.

Dave discloses his own periodic depression elsewhere in his work — [[Rejecting humanity is just ego in disguise]] — and there concedes that when it becomes biochemical it needs biochemical support alongside the inner work. The two claims are compatible: some of it is trapped anger, and not all of it is.
