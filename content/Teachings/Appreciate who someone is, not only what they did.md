# Appreciate who someone is, not only what they did

Jesse offers a working distinction between appreciation and acknowledgement — appreciation for something done *for you* (thanks for the dishes), acknowledgement for something they're doing in their life (your book seems really cool). Dave doesn't have a firm line and suggests playing with both.

But he does draw a distinction he thinks matters more:

> It's worth looking at the distinction between appreciating you for some way that you *are*, versus some thing that you *did*. Both are valuable.

And why the first goes deeper:

> When I appreciate you for some way that you are, it feels deeper, and it allows you to see yourself more clearly and fully. Most of us have this really negative bias, this inner voice that sees us as less than we are. So it's really powerful when someone else is like: wow, I appreciate you for how loving and confident and kind and passionate you are. Those are the ways I *wish* I was — you see that in me.

> The way we see each other can allow us to rise, our own sense of self to meet that appreciation.

The mechanism is a counterweight to the [[The Storyteller Mind|storyteller]]. A negatively biased narrator runs continuously and internally; being-level appreciation is external evidence against it, which is why it lands differently from praise for a task. A completed task can be filed away. A named quality has to be reckoned with.

Dave's own example of how long it lasts: a business school professor in 2005 told him *I really appreciate your mix of head and heart.* "To this day I remember that moment."

> Because those moments of just raw appreciation are so rare.
