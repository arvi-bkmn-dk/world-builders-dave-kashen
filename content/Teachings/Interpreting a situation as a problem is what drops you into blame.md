# Interpreting a situation as a problem is what drops you into blame

The counterintuitive hinge of [[The Responsibility Roadmap]]. The obstacle to taking responsibility is not laziness or ego — it is a single interpretive act performed before anyone has decided anything.

> The moment you interpret the facts of your situation as a "problem," you drop into blame, righteousness, and disempowerment — and your team follows you there.

The mechanism is that "problem" is a category that demands resolution, and the fastest available resolution is not a fix but an attribution. The mind asks *who's to blame?* — and the question sounds like analysis while functioning as an exit.

The forms it takes are worth recognising because several of them feel responsible: "why can't my team take ownership?", "investors are making this harder than it needs to be", "everyone's dropping the ball — why am I the only adult in the room?", and, crucially, "I screwed this up, I should have known better." Self-blame lands on the victim path too. It is not a more rigorous form of responsibility; it is the same move aimed inward.

> Blame creates a temporary sense of righteousness… but it robs you of your power.
