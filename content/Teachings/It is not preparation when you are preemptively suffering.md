# It is not preparation when you are preemptively suffering

The line that separates useful foresight from the thing that impersonates it.

> Your mind constantly projects into the future, imagining scenarios that might happen, then reacts **as if they're already happening.** You haven't even pitched the investor yet, but you're already feeling the rejection. You haven't launched the product, but you're already experiencing the criticism.

> **It's not preparation when you're preemptively suffering.**

The distinction is whether you are *planning for* an outcome or *experiencing* it. Planning is cognitive and finishes. Preemptive suffering is emotional, doesn't finish, and produces nothing — you pay the full cost of the rejection without the information that an actual rejection would give you.

It is the third of three ways Dave says the mind blocks clear perception:

> 1. **Collapsing stories into facts.** When an investor doesn't respond, the fact is: they haven't replied. The story is "they hate our pitch." → [[A fact is something a video camera could record]]
> 2. **Creating problems that don't exist.** Growth slowed — that's a fact. "This shouldn't be happening" is a story. "When you resist what is, you create **unnecessary suffering** — the gap between reality and your expectation of reality." → [[Interpreting a situation as a problem is what drops you into blame]]
> 3. **Living in imagined negative futures.**

Dave's intervention with a hedge fund manager coming off his worst quarter, spiralling through scenarios of client withdrawals:

> "Q1 is done. It is what it is. Are you going to waste your precious creative energy ruminating over what's already happened, or channel it toward creating your desired future?"

> The tension in his shoulders visibly released.
