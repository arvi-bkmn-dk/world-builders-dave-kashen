# What do you love about yourself that has nothing to do with your money or success

The question Dave's wife asked him after he lost a significant portion of his net worth — and his own account of what it exposed.

He had competing parts around money: one focused on accumulation, treating financial success as validation and safety; another questioning whether that focus matched his values. When the loss came, "my achievement-oriented part was devastated."

Then:

> **"What do you love about yourself that has nothing to do with your money or success?"**

His answer:

> "I'm just a kind, caring, fun-loving guy."

And what the answer revealed:

> The parts of me that were fighting actually wanted the same thing: **a sense of peace and worthiness.** The achievement-focused part was just using financial success as its strategy for getting there.

That is the resolution — not choosing between the parts but finding that their goal was identical and only the strategy differed. → [[Both parts want the same outcome and have different strategies]]

The question works by forcing the answer onto ground the loss couldn't touch. Kind, caring and fun-loving were unaffected by the number moving, which is what makes them a different *kind* of worth than the one under threat.

Worth noting Dave answers this about himself, having lost real money, in a published newsletter — the same self-exposure as [[Give up the fight]] and [[What is the thing you most do not want people to say about you]].
