# Preference and righteousness produce different energy from the same goal

Dave's distinction for holding an ambition without it running you: *preference versus righteousness.*

A righteous idea — bigger is better — "creates rigidity." A preference holds the same content with nothing riding on it. His deliberately provocative test case: being a couch potato eating Doritos watching Netflix is "just as valid an experience to have" as being a wildly successful business person, *and then you might choose*. The point isn't to recommend the couch; it's that a real choice requires both options being permitted.

> What do you want to do with this wild and precious life? Oh, I want to go create this kind of impact, this kind of business.

Same ambition, no righteousness. The founder wants the big business without needing it to be correct that bigger is better.

The clean test is the one Dave gives Andrew at the end — the two sentences that name the identical outcome:

> "It should be bigger" — or "how amazing would it be if Bootstrap Giants were bigger?"

Andrew's reaction is the evidence: the *should* clicks instantly ("I know what to do with this"), and the *how amazing would it be* takes real effort to feel. That asymmetry is the measure of how established the dirty fuel is. → [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]]
