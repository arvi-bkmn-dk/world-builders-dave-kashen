# Suffering is the gap between life and your mind's idea of how life should be

> A really simple way to think about the experience of life is: there's **life** on the one hand, and then there's **your mind's idea of how life should be** on the other hand. Those things **rarely match.**

Which reframes the moments people describe as the best of their lives:

> Beautiful sunset. The day your kids are born. Maybe you just sold your company for a billion dollars. And by the way, those are the experiences where people say *oh my God, I felt tremendous peace and joy* — **for a minute.** 'Cause in that one minute, **life and your mind's idea of how life should be finally matched.**

So the peak experience was never produced by the event. It was produced by the gap closing, briefly, from the wrong side.

> If you want to have **more than three minutes of joy in your life** — instead of constantly trying to get life to match your ego's idea of how life should be, **and your ego's idea of who you are and how you should be** — that's a **fool's errand.** That's what causes suffering.

> **Choose life.** Accept life as it is, and ignore your mind's idea of how life should be. Because that's what creates the problem.

Dave's standing question for clients:

> **In this now moment, could you just allow everything to be as it is?** Or — **could you let go of wishing anything was different than it is?**

Most are willing, and report *"total peace"* — and then the mind immediately produces its next problem: *"yeah, but how do I experience this in every other moment?"*

> Which again is just **the mind creating a problem in the future that doesn't exist right now.** The ego lives outside of the now. In this now moment, there's nothing wrong.

And the important caveat, so this isn't read as passivity:

> I might have **visions and goals and things I want to create.** I don't need to declare that there's **anything wrong.**

## Also in [[HoE 02 - Facts, Stories and the Willingness Question]]

*The earlier rendering, which frames the gap as a choice made moment to moment.*

Dave's most compressed statement of where suffering comes from, offered as the "really meta" version.

> There's life, and then there's our mind's idea of how life should be. And at any given moment, we're sort of choosing between the two. If we choose our mind's idea of how life should be, we create suffering for ourselves. And if we choose life as it is, then we don't suffer.

The framing as a *choice made continuously* is what makes it usable — not a state you achieve but a selection available in every moment.

He then names the game most people play, entrepreneurs especially: *let me get life to match my mind's idea of how it should be.* His objection isn't that it fails. It's that it works briefly and doesn't hold — the fulfilment and peace after the big payoff "doesn't last very long." The alternative game is learning to be with life as it is.

The mediating variable between the two is belief in your stories about how life should be, which is what connects this to [[The Facts and Stories Exercise]] — the exercise is how you stop believing them.

This is the same structure as [[Resistance and attachment are what make the founder journey feel heavy, not the circumstances]], stated as one choice rather than two patterns.
