# Your judgments of others mark the places you have not grown

Jesse's compression of projection into a diagnostic:

> If you notice your judgments on others, those are areas where you haven't grown.

Which makes judgment useful rather than shameful. It's a map of unfinished work, available for free, continuously, without introspection.

Family is the ideal test rig because of reversion:

> Family in some ways can be the most valuable, because you become a little kid again. You realise, oh wait, I actually haven't grown past this issue with my brother. It still shows up only around him, but I've been hiding it.

The phrase *only shows up around him* is the important one. Most of the time a pattern isn't resolved, merely unexercised — you've arranged a life that doesn't trigger it. Growth and successful avoidance are indistinguishable until something tests them.

Jesse notes the mirror reads in both directions: things that would have bothered him five years ago don't register now. Same instrument, evidence of actual change.

Dave adds Michael Singer's *samskaras* — when this material surfaces, it's surfacing to be healed or released. "It's not like the stuff wasn't there and suddenly appeared. It was there festering." Which turns a hard visit into an opportunity rather than an ordeal. → [[Projection as a Mirror]]
