# Empower is being fully authentic regardless of the consequences

The sixth distinction, and it starts by explaining where the appetite for power comes from.

> When we don't want to feel our fear, we crave power over people, over life — because we don't want to feel scared. So we think, oh, if I have enough power then I won't have to feel scared, because I can control everything. Which of course is a fool's errand.

Power is a strategy for not feeling fear, and it fails on its own terms because control is never complete enough to remove the feeling.

The alternative is defined without reference to control at all:

> They define empower as being fully authentic regardless of the consequences.

The image from the AoA podcast:

> Imagine you're Superman or Superwoman and you fly to the top of this mountain and you're in a world with no kryptonite. That's the feeling of being fully in empowerment.

No kryptonite means nothing to protect, which means no reason to manage how you come across. "You don't need to pretend to be someone else. You just speak your truth."

Jesse supplies the consequence: someone genuinely in that state is *more* open-hearted, not less, because "you don't feel the need to make someone small." Dave agrees completely — which is what carries the model into [[Empowerment and love meet at the top of the mountain]].
