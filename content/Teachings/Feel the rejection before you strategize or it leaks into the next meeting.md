# Feel the rejection before you strategize or it leaks into the next meeting

After a no, most founders go straight to iteration: what did I do wrong, what should I change. Dave doesn't dispute the instinct, only the timing:

> The instinct to learn from it is right. The timing usually isn't.

> When you skip to strategy before you've processed what just happened, the emotion doesn't disappear. It leaks into the next meeting, the next pitch, the next conversation.

His client's eight meetings with no reset were the case in point: the weight accumulated unnoticed until he crashed at the end of the week.

> A feeling is worth a thousand thoughts. I mean that literally.

And the reframe for founders who think this means the inner work has failed:

> That's not evidence the inner work isn't working. That's what the inner work actually looks like in practice, by showing you exactly where your edge still is.

> The most resilient founders I know don't necessarily feel rejection any less sharply.
