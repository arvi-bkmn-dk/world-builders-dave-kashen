# A story is the meaning your mind adds to neutral facts

The foundational distinction of [[The Storyteller Mind]], and deliberately mundane in its examples:

| Fact | Story |
|---|---|
| A team member missed a deadline. | "They don't care." |
| An investor hasn't replied. | "They're not interested." |
| You wake up feeling tired. | "I'm losing my edge." |

The reason this needs teaching at all is speed. The interpretation arrives so fast that the join is invisible:

> You just feel the emotional consequence (pressure, resentment, worry, shame) and lead from that state without realizing you're reacting to an interpretation rather than reality.

Dave's proof is comparative rather than introspective, which makes it hard to argue with: two founders with identical challenges can have completely different experiences. If the facts are the same and the experience differs, the difference is the story. It is the cleanest available demonstration that interpretation, not circumstance, is doing the work.

The distinction also does double duty as the first category of Speaking Unarguably — facts are shared and agreeable; stories are yours and should be named as such.

## Also in [[MB 02 - The Course in Inner Peace]]

Stated as the second principle of *The Course*, and extended to money, health, relationships and death.

> All events are neutral. All circumstances are neutral. It is only your thoughts about the events and circumstances that are positive or negative.

## Also in [[GA 01 - The Inner Game of Life, Danny Miranda Podcast 394]]

Taken to the hardest cases: two people with the same cancer diagnosis, one living it as *poor me* and the other as *life is sacred*.

> consider that facts don't create suffering.
