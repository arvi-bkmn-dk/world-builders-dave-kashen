# It takes energy to keep these parts at bay

Dave picks up Jesse's phrase and turns it into a diagnosis founders will recognise from the inside.

> I love what Jesse said — it takes energy to keep these parts at bay. That's a really important point, 'cause a lot of founders out there are feeling drained. And they think they're drained because of the hustle and all the activities they're doing. But you could actually do all those activities from a place of flow — and when you're in flow, you do lots of stuff, but you don't feel drained.

The misattribution is the teaching. Exhaustion gets blamed on **volume**, so the remedy people reach for is doing less. Dave's claim is that the drain is coming from somewhere else entirely: the continuous effort of holding parts of yourself down, which runs underneath the work and never appears on the schedule.

Which is why doing less doesn't fix it, and why someone in flow can do more without the same cost.

> I like the notion of flow, 'cause it feels like the intersection between that feeling of fulfillment and wholeness and peace, and being highly effective.

Jesse's report from his own shadow work is the evidence: "my whole insides have changed, because I'm not constantly resisting this part of me that feels bad for itself." → [[Holding an emotion in costs more energy than feeling it]]
