# Founders choose fear because they believe their drive would disappear without it

The reason founders stay on dirty fuel is not that they enjoy it. It is that they believe it is load-bearing. Dave tests this by asking clients whether they'd be willing to accept themselves and their startup exactly as they are — and the answer comes back as something close to panic. "Absolutely not. That's crazy. Then what would motivate me to do anything?"

Jesse names the belief in its own words, from 2019: *if I don't create bigger goals, if I don't tell myself something's broken in this moment, how do I get to the next level?* Underneath it sits a specific catastrophic prediction about what a person without self-criticism becomes — in Jesse's case, someone who watches Netflix all day and gambles in Vegas.

Jesse's own framing of why this persists is worth keeping: it isn't that founders want fear.

> What they would say is, I think I need the fear to be successful. I want peace and I also want to be successful — but I just don't believe that's possible, and therefore I shall choose fear.

That last move is the whole trap. It is not a preference for fear, it's a forced choice made under a false premise. Which means the intervention is not motivational; it's evidential. → [[Letting go of self-criticism does not make creative drive disappear]]
