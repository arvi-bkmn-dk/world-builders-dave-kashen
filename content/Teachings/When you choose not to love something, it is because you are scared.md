# When you choose not to love something, it is because you are scared

In *The Course*, love is defined as a way of experiencing rather than a feeling: meeting a person, event or circumstance directly, without judgment, resistance or attachment.

> To love is to experience directly with no judgment, resistance or attachment.

> Love is a choice.

The love-versus-fear framing echoes **[[A Course in Miracles]]**. Dave quotes its line *"the opposite of Love is Fear, but what is all encompassing can have no opposite"* in this post without naming it, and credits the book by name in the Danny Miranda interview. The application to founders' hardest cases is Dave's.

That makes peace a choice too, which raises the obvious question of why anyone would refuse it. Dave's answer is a single word:

> Fear. When you choose not to love someone or something, it must be because you are scared. Why else would you choose to disturb your own peace?

He tests it on the hardest cases (losing money, heartbreak, illness, a loved one dying) and argues that the refusal is a prediction about suffering, and the prediction is usually wrong. Most feared futures never arrive, and the ones that do rarely feel the way they were imagined. → [[Fear makes bad predictions]] · [[No emotion is painful, the resistance to it is]]
