# It feels safer for a child to believe something is wrong with them than with their parent

Dave's account of where his own sense of unworthiness came from. His parents divorced when he was five, and his father was not emotionally expressive. A child in that situation needs an explanation, and Dave thinks the one children reach for is the one that preserves hope:

> it's a lot safer to believe there's something wrong with you because creates the hope of fixing it versus oh you know there's something Limited in him

If the problem is in you, you can work on it. If the problem is in the parent, there is nothing a child can do. So the story "I am unworthy" is not a random wound; it is the most workable story available at that age.

The same passage carries a mentor's line that reframes the whole relationship: parents mostly do love unconditionally, but

> parents love their children unconditionally but children put conditions on the love

His father loved him in a form Dave didn't recognise as love, so the child concluded he wasn't loved. → [[Love that does not arrive in the expected form does not get received]] · [[You can never have enough success to prove you were worthy]]
