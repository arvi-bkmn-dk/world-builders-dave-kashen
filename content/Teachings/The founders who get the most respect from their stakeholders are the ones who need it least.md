# The founders who get the most respect from their stakeholders are the ones who need it least

Dave calls it the **Stakeholder Meeting Paradox**, drawn from sitting in on hundreds of board and investor meetings.

> The founders who get the most respect from their stakeholders are often the ones who need it least. Why? Because they're not seeking approval.

The two patterns, and the split is measurable:

| Seeking approval | Seeking partnership |
|---|---|
| 80% of the meeting reporting **past performance** | 80% on **future strategy and challenges** |
| Carefully script and rehearse | Prepare thoroughly, speak authentically |
| Avoid raising problems until solved | Bring half-formed ideas for input |
| Rarely ask for help on the biggest challenges | Openly share the biggest unknowns |

The cost is not only in perception:

> The paradox is that the more you try to impress your stakeholders, the **less valuable insight you extract from them** — and the less impressive your actual results become.

Two losses compounding. You waste the room — a set of experienced people reduced to an audience for a report they could have read. And the concealment degrades the business, because problems surface late.

The tell is the **80% split**. A meeting dominated by what already happened is a meeting being used for approval; the past is safe because it is settled. Strategy and unknowns require exposure.

Dave's alternative framing: "lead with candor, not impression management. True leadership isn't about looking good — it's about seeing reality clearly."
