# Startups do not fail because they run out of money, they fail because founders run out of energy

Dave's reframe of the most common startup post-mortem, and he means it causally rather than poetically.

> Startups don't fail because they run out of money. They fail because their founders run out of energy. When a founder runs out of emotional, mental, and creative energy, the company no longer has the fuel needed to sustain itself.

The argument from origin:

> The company didn't exist and had no money before it was started. It was only the founder's creativity, energy and vision that attracted the capital in the first place. If the founder still has the energy and drive, they can attract the capital.

He traces the actual sequence, and the point is how early it starts:

> 1. Founder's energy starts depleting (often unnoticed) → 2. Creative problem-solving and clarity diminish → 3. Team morale and performance decline → 4. Opportunities get missed → 5. Decision quality deteriorates → 6. Financial problems emerge → 7. Fundraising becomes harder → 8. "We ran out of money"

> By the time they hit step 8, most founders have been running on empty for so long they can't see how their depleted state contributed to the outcome.

His case is a founder with two weeks of runway. Dave gave him a basketball frame — *fourth quarter, two minutes, down by eight, how do you want to show up?* — got back *"give me the ball, coach,"* and within a week he had over $1m in checks. "While his bank account was nearly empty, his energy reserves were full."

Dave is careful not to turn this into an obligation to persevere: "it can be just as courageous and virtuous to choose to stop as it is to persevere."
