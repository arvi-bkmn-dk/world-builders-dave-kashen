# You do not want to get stuck in any mode

Dave opens by disowning the whole debate: *"I've coached founders who've created over $100 billion in combined value. And I don't believe in Founder Mode."*

Not because Brian Chesky was wrong — the permission to care about details again was genuinely valuable — but because of what happened next:

> We just traded one dogma for another.

The old gospel was delegation: hire great people and get out of their way, stay strategic not tactical. Founders obeyed, and felt guilty every time they wanted to dive into a product detail. Then Founder Mode swung the pendulum, and now founders "use Founder Mode to justify micromanagement. To avoid delegation and the genuinely scary work of letting go."

> The real insight? **You don't want to get stuck in any mode.**

His image is a gearbox:

> Imagine your car stuck in fifth gear. Great on the highway. Disaster in traffic. You need different gears for different situations.

> The best leadership isn't choosing a mode and sticking with it. It's developing the **discernment** to know what serves best in each moment.

Three questions replace the mode:

- **What lights you up?** — "The question isn't 'Should I be in Founder Mode or CEO Mode?' It's 'What energizes me and what am I uniquely great at?'"
- **What serves the business?** — sometimes you have context nobody else has; sometimes your head of marketing would genuinely do better than you.
- **What am I avoiding?** — the hard one. → [[Founder Mode gives the fear of letting go a strategic name]]
