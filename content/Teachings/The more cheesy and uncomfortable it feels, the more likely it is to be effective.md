# The more cheesy and uncomfortable it feels, the more likely it is to be effective

Dave's answer to the eye-roll, and he turns the objection into the indicator.

> I often say to clients — does this feel really cheesy to you? Great. In some way, the more cheesy, the more woo, the more uncomfortable, the more likely it's gonna be effective. Because it's different from what you're already doing. That's why it feels so uncomfortable.

The reasoning is structural rather than defensive. Discomfort marks distance from your existing pattern, and the existing pattern is what produced the situation you want changed. An exercise that feels natural is one you are already running.

It sits alongside Jesse's insistence that exercises are where the change happens, not concepts: you can read about bench presses or how to ride a bike, and "your chest will not get any bigger." He reports the arc honestly — "at the beginning of most of mine I feel that way, and then at the end I'm like, oh my God, I can't believe that was so effective."

Dave grounds it in why the Masterclass is built the way it is: "very little content and a lot of practice exercises." Concepts do not shift patterns — "if it's just an intellectual concept but you haven't had the shift somatically, you just don't shift the patterns." → [[Unfelt feelings are the glue that keep unwanted patterns in place]]
