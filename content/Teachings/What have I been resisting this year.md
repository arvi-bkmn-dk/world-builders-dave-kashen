# What have I been resisting this year

Dave's one pragmatic practice for the year, offered as a reflection question with a checklist attached — because resistance is hard to see in the abstract and obvious once itemised.

> If you do some honest reflection, you'll see that you're resisting quite many things. I know I have been. You are likely resisting:
> 1. Tasks that you don't want to do
> 2. Feelings that you don't want to feel
> 3. Thoughts you don't want to be having
> 4. Habits that you wish you didn't have
> 5. Potential 'bad' outcomes you envision

Then: *make a list of everything you can think of. And then ask — what would my life be like if I stopped resisting these things?*

The list is doing specific work. Most people would name only the second category as resistance; the other four are usually experienced as ordinary life. Seeing a disliked task, an unwanted thought and a feared outcome as **the same move** is the point.

Dave also names the fear that keeps the resistance in place, in the voice it actually uses:

> If I stop resisting doing this task, I'll have to do it all the time. If I stop resisting sadness, I'll be engulfed in it forever. If I stop resisting indulging in sugary snacks, I'll gorge on them.

> From my experience, the opposite is true. What you resist, persists. It's the resistance that actually keeps an experience going and has us feel trapped in it. Our tendency is to then resist it even more.
