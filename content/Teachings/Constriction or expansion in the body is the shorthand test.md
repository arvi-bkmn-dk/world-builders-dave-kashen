# Constriction or expansion in the body is the shorthand test

Dave's practical answer to a problem the frameworks themselves create.

> The shorthand for all of that, for me, is: **do I feel constriction in my body, or do I feel expansion in my body?**

> I find that a really useful distinction as a way to **cut through all the other stuff.** Because when people learn conscious leadership, or these distinctions of being present versus being in threat, or fear versus love — **our minds tend to go to: well, I would act this way if I was coming from love. I would say these things.**

Two objections to working it out from behaviour:

> First of all, that's just kind of **unknowable.** And second — **you can say the same exact words from a place of constriction and threat, or from a place of openness and love.** And they'll feel really different, to you and to the other person.

> **It's really about your being, and what's happening inside you.**

Which is why the body is the only reliable instrument available in the moment: the script is identical either way, and the state is not.

The skill refines with use: *"at first you notice the gross obvious — I'm pissed off, I'm reacting, I'm yelling at people. But over time you can notice **more and more subtle constriction** — we're bracing against life, instead of open and flowing."*

And the second, even simpler test:

> If I ask myself — **is there anything I'm wishing was different right now?** — that is **the most reliable way** to know whether I'm operating from constriction or openness.

Dave adds a caveat he arrived at that morning, about the common framing *can you trust life?*: *"it started to feel absurd. Life just **is.** Trust it to do what? Life just is the way it is. So if you're in your head thinking it should be different, you're creating all sorts of needless suffering."*
