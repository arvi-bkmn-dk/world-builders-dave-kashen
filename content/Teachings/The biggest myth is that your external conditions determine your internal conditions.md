# The biggest myth is that your external conditions determine your internal conditions

> I just think **the biggest myth is that your external conditions determine your internal conditions.** That is probably the **biggest, most pervasive myth in the human experience.**

Dave immediately refuses the overcorrection, which is what makes it credible:

> It's a little extreme to say they have **no** impact. Is it easier to feel joy and peace when you have a lot of money versus no money? **Sure.** On the beach in Hawaii versus in prison? **Of course.** There's a lot of research on this — maybe it's **20, 30%.** But it's **not nearly as much as we think.**

And then the argument that should persuade a founder on its own terms:

> It **is** possible to intentionally change your internal conditions, and it takes **way less time** and **way, way less effort.** So the **ROI on investing in your internal growth is extraordinary** — relative to the ROI of spending **seven years** trying to get success and millions of dollars and then **still not experiencing it.**

The most valuable part is that Dave does not exempt himself:

> I just want to own — **I didn't really believe it.** There's a part of me that's like, *yeah, yeah, but once I hit my number.* And honestly, **I can feel a part of my mind that still believes it** now — that does that same game: *if I had twice as much and I was generating this much passive income, then I'd really be free.*

> It's a really **ingrained pattern that lives in us.**

He also names the irony in the exit itself. The peace people feel on the day is real, and it is **acceptance** — arrived at from the wrong direction:

> Finally your **external conditions have met your mind's idea of how they should be.** Another word for that is **acceptance.** That's the **joke** of it.
