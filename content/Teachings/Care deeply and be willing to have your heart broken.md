# Care deeply and be willing to have your heart broken

Dave narrows non-attachment to exactly two options, which is what makes the choice visible:

> **Option 1: Don't care at all.** No heartbreak when it doesn't work out. But also no aliveness. No joy in building something that matters. You protect yourself from pain by staying numb.

> **Option 2: Care deeply AND be willing to have your heart broken.**

And he is concrete about what option 2 costs:

> If you care deeply about building category-defining technology, and you give it everything, and it still fails? That's heartbreak. If you pour your soul into your team, and your CTO leaves for Google? Heartbreak. If you bet your reputation on a vision, and the market says no? Heartbreak.

> Most people, when they think of non-attachment, choose option one to protect themselves from heartbreak. **But that protection costs you everything.**

> The aliveness you want, the meaning, the joy of building something that authentically matters to you, lives on the other side of your **willingness to be heartbroken.**

Which is the general law in a specific costume — what you are unwilling to feel sets the ceiling on what you can create. → Your results are limited by which feelings you are unwilling to feel · [[Apathy is not caring, non-attachment is not believing your well-being depends on the outcome]]
