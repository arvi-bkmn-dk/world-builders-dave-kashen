# Emotional capacity problems get misdiagnosed as performance problems

When a company outgrows a founder's emotional capacity, Dave observes three predictable outcomes:

1. **You shrink your vision** — because you can't hold the emotional weight of a bigger one.
2. **You escape into coping behaviours** — overworking, overthinking, numbing, avoidance.
3. **You collapse under pressure** — self-doubt, overwhelm, shutdown, defensiveness.

> Most founders misdiagnose these as performance issues. They're emotional capacity issues.

The misdiagnosis is costly because it routes the response to entirely the wrong place. A shrunken vision gets treated as prudent strategy. Overwork gets treated as commitment, and is often rewarded. Defensiveness gets treated as a communication problem and sent to a workshop. Each response addresses the symptom's surface and leaves the constraint untouched — and the constraint keeps generating new symptoms.

Dave's reframe of the intensity itself is the counterweight: rising fear, doubt and shame during a growth phase do not mean something has gone wrong. "It means you're evolving." That reading matters, because treating the intensity as evidence of a mistake is what triggers the shrinking response in the first place.
