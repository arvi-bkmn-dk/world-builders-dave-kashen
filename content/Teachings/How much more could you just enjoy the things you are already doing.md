# How much more could you just enjoy the things you are already doing

The question Dave calls one of the most striking exercises he did in the Masterclass, and it cuts against the obvious move.

> If people say I want to enjoy my life more, where we tend to go is — I need to rearrange my life and all the things that I'm doing so that I enjoy it more. And he really challenged us to: how much more could you just enjoy the things you're already doing?

What surprised Dave was that it worked: "when I discovered that I could, I'm like, that's fascinating — what am I doing differently that I'm doing the literal same activity and I'm enjoying it more?"

His worked example is preparing his taxes, which he resisted for years — "when I say I resisted, I did it, but I resisted it *while doing it*." Two things changed, and only one of them was external:

- **He stopped treating it as an obligation.** Via Diana Chapman: "you don't have to do your taxes — there are just consequences." → [[You do not have to do your taxes, there are just consequences]]
- **He asked his wife to sit next to him.** Not to help, just to be there.

The first changed nothing about the activity. The second changed almost nothing. Both made it enjoyable.

Dave's ongoing practice is to run the question continuously — "how could I enjoy this moment even more?" — and he notes it often resolves into connection: "I can feel my — I love you, I'm with you, we're together doing something. I wasn't paying attention to that. Sometimes just shifting what we pay attention to."
