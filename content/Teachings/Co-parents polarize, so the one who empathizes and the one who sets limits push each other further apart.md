# Co-parents polarize, so the one who empathizes and the one who sets limits push each other further apart

Something Dave learned from the parenting coach he and his wife hired (unnamed in the episode). Two core skills of a parent are validating feelings and setting limits, and couples split them:

> my wife and I were polarizing around those two so she would set limits and I would empathize and validate their feelings

The split felt good to Dave's ego ("oh Daddy you're so nice"), which is why he calls it permissive and indulgent, really for him rather than for the kids. His test: *is this for me or for them?*

The mechanism is general. The softer one parent gets, the firmer the other becomes to compensate, and each reads the other as the problem. Dave sees the same thing with money in marriages (spend versus save), where it feels personal and isn't. Once it's seen it can be played with. His correction is to set limits himself, and he knows it's working when his kids say *mommy's corrupted you*. → [[Parenting by contrast to your own childhood creates its own blind spot]]
