# You have had thoughts you never acted on, which proves you are not your thoughts

Jesse's argument, developed while trying to explain the idea to his sister-in-law, and it's the most efficient version in his work because the evidence is already in the listener.

> Have you ever had the thought that you wanted to kill somebody? Or have you ever had the thought you want to punch your kid in the face? Most people first go: no, no. And I'm like — no, be honest.

Everyone has. And then the question that does the work:

> Why didn't you do it? If you're your thoughts, why didn't you do it?

There is no comfortable answer that preserves the identification. Something had the thought, and something else declined to act on it — which is [[Hearing a voice in your head means there is a talker and a listener]] demonstrated rather than asserted.

The choice of example is deliberate. A mild thought lets you dismiss the gap as trivial. A thought you find genuinely repugnant forces the distinction, because you are certain you are not the kind of person who would do that — while also certain you had the thought.

> There's this other part of you that's talking, talking, talking all the time. And then there's the other part that's actually listening.

Jesse's definition of spirituality follows from it: "there's something bigger beyond you, there's this little voice that connects all of us in some way." And meditation's role is to create space between the two. → [[The Meditation Practice]]
