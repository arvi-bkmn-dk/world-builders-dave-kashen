# Keeping someone who is not a fit is a lose lose

Dave's answer to the founder who keeps someone out of kindness — and he argues the kindness is not kindness.

> If somebody is not a good fit for your team, it's actually a lose lose. You're really not doing them a favor by keeping them.

Two reasons. The first is that it does not stay hidden:

> In all these subtle ways, they will get the message that you don't think they're awesome. And that has a huge effect on their experience and their psychology.

The second is structural: "if they were thriving, you wouldn't be wanting to let them go. So they're clearly not in a role where they're thriving, and letting them go find a role where they can thrive is—" and he stops short of calling it a gift, deliberately: "let's be empathic and recognise that it is a really scary, sometimes sad, frustrating experience to be let go."

Then the observation with no counterexample:

> **I always wish I had done it sooner.** I have literally never met a founder who's like, oh God, I wish I'd waited a little longer to let go of that person. Literally never.

And what actually happens, against the catastrophic stories — *if I let go of my head of sales the company will burn down*: "in many cases I see the literal opposite. Team members step up in surprising ways. The culture sort of uplifts, because that person was anchoring it down."

What keeps the person in the seat is Dave's standard diagnosis: the hero move, and the unwillingness to feel the sadness. → [[Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship]] · [[Heroes require victims]]
