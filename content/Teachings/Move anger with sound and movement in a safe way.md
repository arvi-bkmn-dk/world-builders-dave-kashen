# Move anger with sound and movement in a safe way

Dave credits Joe Hudson for naming the false choice:

> For some reason we think there's **two choices**: either we can hold it all in and repress it, **or** we can scream and yell at people.

> There's this **other way** — you find a **healthy, safe way to move it through you.**

The premise is physical: anger is energy that wants to move, so *"if you don't find a healthy, safe way to move it, it'll come out"* — as a scream, a punch, a cut-down. *"This is how I think violence happens. We bottle up the anger, and at some point it just explodes out of us."*

**The practice**, in escalating order: welcome it and locate it in the body; **sigh loudly**; breathe in and through it. If it's more intense, **express it with sound and movement, ideally both.**

Dave keeps a **wiffle ball bat** in his bedroom for bashing a pillow — an idea from [[Diana Chapman]], who kept **anger stations** around her house. His other option is a qigong wood-chopping motion: *"if you combine the sound and the movement — ha — you can feel, ooh, there's something really moving."* Jesse uses his car, *"such a private space… you can yell really loud and no one can hear you,"* and lets his son punch his hands.

**If it's deeply repressed, fake it.** At the Hoffman Process Dave went in thinking *"I'm not really angry about it anymore"* —

> About a couple minutes in, it went from **I'm faking it** to **I'm accessing it.**

And the general rule he draws from it: → [[The more embarrassing the activity, the more useful it probably is]]

**If you're prone to anger**, this matters more, not less:

> If your pattern is *I move it by expressing it at people*, that has a lot of downstream damage. Make a practice of expressing anger in safe ways, and you won't be constantly at this **saturation point where you're about to explode.**

→ [[Feeling anger is different from being angry at someone]] · [[Holding an emotion in costs more energy than feeling it]]

## Also in [[MB 03 - My Experience of the Hoffman Process]]

The Hoffman Process's Expression step works on the same principle: patterns live in the body as trapped energy, and they are released with voice, body and intention rather than by thinking.

## Also in [[NL 72 - The Most Misunderstood Emotion in Leadership]]

Dave's four-step process for accessing anger:

1. **Identify it.** For many people it lives in the shoulders and neck.
2. **Welcome it.** It's the resistance to the emotion that's painful.
3. **Express it in a healthy, safe way.** Dave keeps a wiffle ball bat for his pillow; the Qigong chopping-wood move with a *ha*; or scream in your car.
4. **Communicate your want or set a boundary.**

> For this reason, I keep a wiffle ball bat in my bedroom.

> The specific method matters less than the principle: give the energy somewhere healthy to go.
