# The marshmallow test measured strategies, not willpower

Dave revisits the famous experiment — a marshmallow in front of a child, two if they can wait ten minutes — and the grit mythology built on it.

> Researchers went back later and studied the videos of the kids. And what they found is: the kids who were successful in not eating the marshmallows just had better strategies. They weren't disciplined.

One sat on his hands so he couldn't reach. Another turned his back. Another sang a song.

The reinterpretation matters because of what the original reading implies. If the variable is willpower, it's a trait, it's roughly fixed, and failing means you lack it. If the variable is strategy, it's portable, learnable, and failure means your setup was bad.

Dave's timing is deliberate — "it's good timing, because we're about at New Year's resolution season." Resolutions are willpower bets by construction, which is why they fail and why the failure feels like a verdict on the person.

It also reframes Andrew's phone problem. Not *I lack the discipline to stay off my phone* but *I haven't found the arrangement that works yet* — charge it in another room, a lockbox, screen time off. Jesse's eight-year-old ski student had the grammar right without being taught: *I can't do it yet.*
