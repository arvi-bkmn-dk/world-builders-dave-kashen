# Connection is felt, not checked off

The first distinction, and the warning that comes with it — connection can be faked into a procedure and then stops working.

> Connection is felt. It is not like a concept of — okay, I checked the box, I told them about my personal history, or I asked where they're from.

The difference is where attention sits:

> When you bring your attention to how connected do we feel, it's a different orientation than: am I doing this right? Am I doing this right? Did I get it right?

Dave includes himself: "I have times when I'm coaching where I'm like, ah, am I saying the right thing? — versus just connecting." And he notes the paradox that dropping the performance produces more of what the performance was after: "maybe paradoxically, it's like more charisma when you're in this mode."

> [!warning] Unsourced
> Dave cites this study without naming it. Recorded as his claim.

The business case Dave cites is a study of used-car salesmen — the archetype of the perfected pitch — where the ones who genuinely connected with the buyer as a human reportedly outsold the playbook-followers several times over.

Jesse's sales practice is the same claim from the operator's side. He skips discovery on a first call, against every playbook: "nobody wants to hear my questions until they know who I am, trust me, think I'm credible." And on objections, he replaces the trained rebuttal — *I can see why you'd think that, but imagine the value* — with genuine curiosity: "huh, it seems like a lot to you. How do you think about budgets?" His verdict on the trained version, tested on a student: "I feel like you didn't really listen to me."
