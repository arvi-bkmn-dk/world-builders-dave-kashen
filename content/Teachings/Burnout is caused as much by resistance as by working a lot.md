# Burnout is caused as much by resistance as by working a lot

The founder who opens the newsletter works **50 hours a week** — fewer than most — and is completely drained. *"I don't get it. Other founders work way more hours than me and seem fine. What's wrong with me?"*

> Burnout is caused as much by **resistance** as it is by just working a lot.

Which explains the comparison that makes no sense on an hours basis. Dave's own case is tax season: the work wasn't complicated, but every year he'd approach it as *ugh, I have to do this*, drag his feet, get grumpy with his family, and feel tortured.

> **That internal resistance was draining my energy more than the actual work.**

Meanwhile he knew entrepreneurs pulling all-nighters who seemed genuinely excited. Same expenditure, different cost — because one of them was paying twice.

What shifted it was his coach pointing out that taxes are a choice with consequences, not an obligation. → [[You do not have to do your taxes, there are just consequences]]

> When I stopped resisting and simply chose it, doing my taxes became no big deal.

And the generalisation to the company:

> This applies to everything in startup life. Fundraising, hiring, administrative work — **anything you're doing while simultaneously resisting will drain both you and your team fast.**

Note *and your team*. Resistance is visible, and a leader grinding against their own work sets the tone for everyone doing it alongside them. → [[Your state is contagious to your team]]

Same claim as [[Startups do not fail because they run out of money, they fail because founders run out of energy]], with the mechanism named: the drain is the fighting, not the work.
