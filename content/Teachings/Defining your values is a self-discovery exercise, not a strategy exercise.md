# Defining your values is a self-discovery exercise, not a strategy exercise

> Nine times out of ten, no one in a company knows what the company's values are.

Most founders start by studying admired companies and borrowing values that sound good. A founder Dave has coached for years did exactly that at his first company:

> He'd look at companies he admired and think, "What are Amazon's principles? Ooh, I like that one."

His team could feel it and joked about it behind his back: *he wishes we were like this, but we're not.*

> Turns out a team can sniff out borrowed values quickly, and a hodgepodge of other people's values is what creates the cynicism around culture in the first place.

> Defining your values is more a self-discovery exercise than a strategy exercise.

> What's actually true for me? What do I actually value?

That founder spent years rebuilding his culture; today people who worked there are known to recruiters as multipliers.
