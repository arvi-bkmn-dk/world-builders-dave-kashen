# What are you willing to risk for your full aliveness

The question the episode ends on:

> **Diana Chapman** used to ask this question I love: **what are you willing to risk for your full aliveness?**

It lands hard because of the caveat Dave puts immediately before it. Someone had asked him: *what if I followed aliveness and then it didn't turn out the way I expected?*

> There's still a little bit of a **trap** there — as if your **ego mind knows how things are supposed to go.**

> I don't necessarily hold that following aliveness will lead to you succeeding **in the exact way your mind thinks you should succeed.** But I do think it will lead to your **most authentic path of self-discovery.**

So the question is not rhetorical, and the answer is not free. Following aliveness may cost the outcome you were counting on — and it may reveal something you'd rather not know: *"you might realise, I actually don't want to do this anymore. And that could be terrifying."*

Dave is careful with the surrounding claim too. He thinks you are *more likely* to be successful following aliveness — *"there's no guarantee"* — and he hesitates even to say it, because of how it will be heard:

> As a listener you could be like — *okay, so the key is I have to follow aliveness so that I can be successful so that I no longer have to feel the shame of being unworthy.* **That won't work.** The shame will kind of eke in.

Aliveness pursued as a strategy for relieving shame is [[We take a path that does not lead to aliveness as a strategy to get to aliveness|the same substitution]] wearing better clothes.
