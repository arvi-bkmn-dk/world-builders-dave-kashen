# Without a number, people assume they are below the bar

Dave opens the newsletter on the person nobody is talking to:

> Right now, someone on your team is probably wondering where they stand with you.

> They're not asking. You're not telling.

> So they're left guessing, and because we're human and we're wired for threat, they're worried they're below the bar.

His fix is concrete. Once a quarter, give each person a number:

> Once a quarter, give them a number: "You're at an 8 out of 10, and here's why."

> The number matters because people have a huge negativity bias. Hand someone improvement points with no rating, and they'll assume they botched it.

The same honesty applies when someone really is below the bar:

> That conversation is the one leaders avoid most, and it's actually kind.

> Softening the message and letting them go later gives them no chance at all.

→ [[Keeping someone who is not a fit is a lose lose]] · [[Ruinous empathy is more common than obnoxious aggression]]

## Flip the number upward

Ask each direct report how you're doing as their manager, on a scale of 1 to 10. When they say 8, ask what contributes to the 8 and what would get you to a 10.

> They've already said something positive out loud, which makes the honest answer feel safer to give.
