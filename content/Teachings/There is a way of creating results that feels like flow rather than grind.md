# There is a way of creating results that feels like flow rather than grind

The claim Dave is most tentative about, and he flags it as something still percolating rather than a finished model.

> I want to name what's emerging: a state of flow where I just feel flow, I feel flow of love — and then the magical thing is, things are flowing. There's a different way of creating in the world that isn't this efforting and hustling and doing. It feels more like I'm just following flow and aliveness, and magical things are happening.

He anticipates the eye-roll and pre-empts it with the size of the outcomes:

> Real tangible important results are happening. Billion-dollar companies being built, likely going to go public.

His example is a founder from a recent Inner Game cohort:

> I'm having investors reach out to me, I'm getting speaking opportunities, out of nowhere. This used to be really hard work where I felt like I had to hustle to make these things happen, and now they're just happening. It's weird — it's almost hard to trust, scary to trust.

*Scary to trust* is the honest detail, and it connects to [[The conditions you learned to survive become the conditions your survival depends on]]. Results arriving without struggle violate the configuration the system has survival evidence for, which is why ease registers as risk.

Andrew's report is the same thing at a smaller scale, and his analysis of it is sharp:

> Sometimes a little thing will have happened — maybe I'll close a sale. Truthfully the sale doesn't impact my life financially that much. But it triggers something and then I'm in that state of flow. I know now that it's not a significant external thing that created it — it's just what *allows* me to feel it and justifies being okay with feeling it.

The sale as a permission slip, not a cause. → [[You are at this level of success because you are experiencing this level of joy]]
