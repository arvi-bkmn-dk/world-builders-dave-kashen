# Every yes you did not want was said to avoid a feeling

Somewhere on your calendar is something you didn't really want: the call you couldn't find a reason to decline, the candidate you were on the fence about, the investor you took when the ones you wanted said no, venture capital because *that's the way it's done.*

> Each of those yeses felt reasonable in the moment.

> But forcing yourself to do things you don't want to do drains a lot of energy. It shows up as frustration, and eventually as burnout.

> It's a great question to ask yourself: What has you say yes to things you don't really want to do?

Dave's four common answers:

- **People-pleasing** — not wanting to disappoint someone or have them angry with you.
- **The should voice** — an externally imposed idea of the right way to live, internalised: *"That's what good founders do."*
- **Fear of letting go** — if I don't do it, it won't get done well.
- **Guilt** — if I don't like it, it isn't fair to give it to someone else, when *"one man's heaven is another man's hell."*

That guilt usually points to the belief that success requires suffering.

> All that pressure to say yes, to please everyone, to do it all, to get every decision right, is the shaking.
