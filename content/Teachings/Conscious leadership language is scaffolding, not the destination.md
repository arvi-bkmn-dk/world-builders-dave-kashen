# Conscious leadership language is scaffolding, not the destination

The robot phase is real and both men admit it. Jesse's wife told him he sounded like a robot; Dave's wife's version was "just be my husband, stop trying to coach me." Neither treats this as a sign the practice is fake.

Jesse's analogy is tennis: in the beginning you have to manufacture the strokes, because the natural motion isn't there yet and the deliberate one feels absurd. What changes with maturity isn't that the strokes get more manufactured — it's that he uses the explicit language less, because the thing it was scaffolding is now actually present. The words were training wheels for a state, and once you can hold the state the words become optional.

Dave adds the practical fix for the awkward middle, which is to make the practice explicit rather than to hide it:

> When I said to her, yeah, I'm practising this new thing, I'm going to sound weird and clumsy — is that OK? And she gives me permission, or we have an agreement around it, then it's not so weird when I do it.

Creating a container for the practice is the move. Practising a new form of speech on someone without telling them reads as manipulation; naming it as practice and asking for permission turns the same clumsiness into something collaborative.
