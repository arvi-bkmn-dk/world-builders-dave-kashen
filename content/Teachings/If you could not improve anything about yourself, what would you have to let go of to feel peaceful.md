# If you could not improve anything about yourself, what would you have to let go of to feel peaceful

Tara's question from the Masterclass, and the only exercise Dave felt he could share — most of the course he keeps confidential so participants get the whole arc.

The question works by removing the escape hatch. Self-improvement lets you defer peace indefinitely: you are not at peace *yet*, but you are working on it, so the gap is temporary. Close off improvement and the deferral collapses — whatever you were waiting to fix has to be grieved instead.

Dave answers it on himself, and the answer is not small:

> For me it was basically enlightenment. The thing I've been playing with is — I need to be enlightened, I need to be fully enlightened. I've had these glimpse experiences, but I need to fully be awake. And I realise it's been like a manic sort of over-.

> I actually let my heart break. I was like, oh yeah — what if I never get there? And I just let my heart break. And paradoxically, more peaceful, more awake.

Two things worth noting. The most spiritually advanced-sounding goal was functioning as the same deferred life as *once I sell the company*. And what resolved it was not an insight but a grief — he let himself feel the loss of never arriving. → [[Grieving the future you expected is what frees you to create one]]
