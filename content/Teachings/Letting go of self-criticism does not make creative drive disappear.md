# Letting go of self-criticism does not make creative drive disappear

Rather than argue Jesse out of the belief that his drive was made of self-criticism, Dave ran the experiment. He asked what Jesse was afraid would happen without the whipping — Netflix, Vegas, nothing — and then said, in effect, fine: let's try it. He even secured Jesse's wife's permission so the test could be run honestly.

What happened is the teaching. For the first few days, nothing. Then around day five or six, Jesse calmed down, enjoyed himself, and the ideas came back on their own.

> Oh, I have a cool idea. This would be a cool thing in the world, I want to go do this, this would be fun.

Two things make this more than a nice anecdote. First, the creative energy turned out to be *a different thing* from the fear, not a product of it — "there's this other part of me, this creative guy inside of me who wants to build things and enjoys that." Second, it took a lived experiment to shift, not an insight. Jesse notes he was already two or three years into coaching. The belief was too structural to be argued with; it had to be disproven in the body.

This is a template for the general case: when a founder's fear is that removing dirty fuel removes the engine, the move is to find a bounded, permissioned way to actually remove it and see.
