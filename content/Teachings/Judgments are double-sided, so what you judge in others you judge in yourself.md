# Judgments are double-sided, so what you judge in others you judge in yourself

The mind filters everything through one set of beliefs about what's good and bad, and because it's the same filter pointed both ways, every judgment has two sides.

> What you judge in others is what you judge in yourself. What you won’t allow in yourself, you won’t allow in others.

| | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| **You** | qualities you value in yourself | qualities you feel shame around |
| **Others** | qualities you admire but don't see in yourself | qualities you judge but deny in yourself |

Dave credits the short version to [[Diana Chapman]]:

> “If you spot it, you got it.”

His example: a founder who couldn't stand his co-founder's indecisiveness. Underneath his quick decisions was a fear of getting it wrong, and he couldn't tolerate hesitation in himself.

> Because the reality you perceive is really just your own beliefs looking back at you.

Both sides are invitations:

> Every person who triggers you and every person you admire is pointing you toward where you can expand.
