# The presenting issue is almost never the real issue

Jesse describes the leadership capability that arrived once he could read emotions in himself — and Dave's gloss is categorical:

> 99% of the time the presenting issue is not the real issue. And we waste so much time trying to solve intellectually the presenting issue.

Jesse's before-and-after is the useful part. His old method was to win:

> I would do master debate — here's number one, number two, number three, here's why this is right, here's why this is wrong. And then the person would walk away from it. I'd probably be right intellectually, they'd feel like shit, and the problem — some new problem — would show up the next week. Because they weren't actually mad about the spreadsheet formatting.

Being right resolved the stated problem and left the actual one untouched, so it regenerated in a new form.

The replacement is to name the emotion rather than argue the content: "you seem angry. What are you mad about?" What follows, in his account, is two-stage — "first of all, someone sees me, and then second of all, they wanna let some anger out" — and the real grievance surfaces, which is usually nothing to do with the spreadsheet.

> Just letting them air the anger usually solves 80% of the problem. And then you can actually deal with the real problem, not the fake problem that comes in.

Dave adds the prerequisite, which is why this cannot be adopted as a technique alone: reading it in someone else depends on having felt it in yourself. "That's what allows us to feel empathy — it's the mirror neurons, where we can feel in our body something and then recognise, oh, that's what they're feeling." A leader who has blunted their own instrument has no equipment for the diagnosis.
