# Planning happens in the present moment, so presence never requires doing nothing

> When you're building a house, which moments of planning, architecting, and construction happen outside the present moment?

> None of them.

> Planning is something you do in a given present moment, the same as swinging a hammer.

Mistake thinking about the future for leaving the present, and the things you want to build start to feel like something you must leave presence to do. People get very good at responding to whatever shows up, and slowly stop creating.

A client had delegated well, freed his calendar, and could sit on a park bench for hours in presence; then he'd open his to-do list and be back at the grindstone. Nearly everything he worked on had come to him rather than from him.

> It sounded like he thought that to be in a state of presence, he had to be doing nothing.

> Your work is where you train presence, rather than what you leave to find it.

> Presence and activity were never in conflict. Planning is an activity too.
