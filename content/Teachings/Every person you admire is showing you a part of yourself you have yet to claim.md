# Every person you admire is showing you a part of yourself you have yet to claim

The half of projection his work usually skips. Everywhere else the mirror shows what you have rejected; here it shows what you have not yet allowed yourself to be.

> Just as our aversions expose the parts of ourselves we reject, our admiration exposes parts of ourselves we aren't willing to be acknowledged for yet.

> * The leader you admire for their charisma? Maybe you haven't fully owned your own ability to inspire.
> * The founder you respect for their boldness? Maybe you have boldness inside you waiting to be expressed.
> * The mentor whose presence feels magnetic? Maybe you have a powerful presence you haven't fully stepped into.

Dave's pairing:

> **Every person who triggers you is showing you a part of yourself you've rejected. Every person you admire is showing you a part of yourself you've yet to claim.** Both are invitations to expand.

The move it enables is different from the one for triggers. With an aversion you ask *where does this exist in me, and could I love it?* With admiration you ask:

> Instead of just idolizing that trait, ask: how can I recognize and express this in myself? If you deeply admire someone's specific trait, where can you start embodying it today?

Which converts admiration from a comparison — usually unflattering — into a directive. Idolising someone keeps the trait theirs; claiming it makes it a next move. → [[You could behave any way any other human behaves]]
