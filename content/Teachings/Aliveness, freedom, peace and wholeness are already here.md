# Aliveness, freedom, peace and wholeness are already here

Jesse runs the *why* ladder on Andrew's desire for money — why money, why a goal, why a reason to keep going — and lands where it always lands: aliveness. He notes he's done this with everybody, and most people arrive at freedom, then peace.

Dave takes it one step further, and the step is the teaching:

> Aliveness, freedom, peace, fulfilment, sense of wholeness — which is often what people are chasing, trying to fill some gap or hole — they're **always already here**.

> To me that's one of the beautiful promises of quote-unquote spirituality: recognising, oh, I'm already whole, I'm already fulfilled. I don't actually need anything else.

This is the strong form of [[Do-have-be is backwards and it never arrives]]. That teaching says the sequence fails; this one says the destination was never elsewhere.

The mechanism for recognising it is subtractive rather than additive:

> When you get still and start noticing all the thoughts and beliefs that are in the way of recognising that, then you have the opportunity to see through those thoughts and beliefs — which are just made up.

And Dave insists the result isn't conceptual: "not as a concept, but literally start to feel the most alive you've ever felt."

**The beggar's chest.** A man begs his whole life sitting on a chest. At the end of it he finally looks inside — bars of gold, the entire time. → [[Via Negativa]]
