# Being a victim of something is different from living in victim consciousness

[[Danny Miranda]] pushes responsibility to its extreme: did someone in the Holocaust create their experience? Dave doesn't dodge the question. He separates two things that "responsibility" usually blurs together:

> and there's a difference between being a victim, like a victim of a crime, a victim of a genocide, and victim consciousness.

The first is a fact about what happened, and there are real victims. The second is a present-tense stance: living *now* as if you are at the effect of something that happened long ago. Responsibility only ever applies to the second, and it doesn't deny the first.

What keeps someone there, in his account, isn't the event:

> The stories you made up about yourself, when that experience happened, are what you're carrying around with you.

So taking responsibility means seeing how you create present suffering out of old stories (*I'm bad, I'm unworthy*). He calls that liberating, and says it is explicitly not blaming the victim. → [[Victim Consciousness]] · [[Responsibility is not self-blame]]
