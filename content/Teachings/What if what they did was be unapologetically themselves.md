# What if what they did was be unapologetically themselves

Dave's answer to founders reverse-engineering the people they admire.

> I've said to a bunch of founders — okay, you're trying to figure out what did Sam Altman do, what did Brian Chesky do, what would Larry and Sergey do. It's like: **what if what they did was be unapologetically themselves?**

The move exposes a contradiction in the copying. If what produced those outcomes was each person following their own read rather than someone else's playbook, then studying the playbook is precisely the wrong inference from the evidence.

> I see this pattern a lot of trying to emulate these other people who have the results that you want. And I think we get overly attached to the results because we make them mean something about who we are, and trying to prove something.

Which links the copying to the comparison — you emulate because the result carries your worth. → [[Equating your value to the value of your company is a major trap]]

The alternative Dave offers is not a different playbook:

> Versus really just following your authentic truth, your authentic desires and wants. And I believe that leads you to whatever you're here to learn and experience — rather than some idea you have of what you're supposed to be learning, which probably came from someone else, or some need to prove something by some external standard.

He closes on what the podcast's title actually means:

> I just love to ski, so I ski. You love to start companies, so you start companies. That's coming from love. What if you could just do it because you'd love to do it?
