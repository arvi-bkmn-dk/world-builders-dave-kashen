# Expand your presence surface area

Dave's phrase for what "seeing reality clearly" actually requires, and it reframes presence as a **range** rather than a state.

> Being in a state of threat distorts your perception of reality. When you don't feel safe, you interpret what's happening in the most negative way possible.

> Seeing reality clearly is about dramatically expanding the areas in life which you remain feeling safe. It's expanding your **presence "surface area"** — the set of conditions under which you can remain in a state of presence.

The reframe matters because presence is usually treated as binary: you are present or you are not. Surface area makes it a **coverage question**. Everyone is present somewhere. The work is widening the set of situations where it survives — a hostile board meeting, a bad quarter, a co-founder conflict.

His mechanism for the distortion:

> Our feelings are created by our **interpretations** of reality. Not from reality itself. Our brains perceive 'what is' and then delete, distort, and generalize based on our interpretations, which creates an inner experience that is separate from reality itself.

And the target state:

> Seeing reality clearly is similar to being like a newborn child — free from conditioned interpretations, defenses and projections. Free to observe reality from curiosity instead of from the layers of resistance and emotional baggage that we create within ourselves.

> Which means the process of seeing clearly is largely a process of **'undoing' or 'unlearning.'**

Subtractive, not additive — the same claim as [[Via negativa says there is nothing to attain, only barriers to remove]] and [[What got you here will not get you there]].
