# A choice only feels like a hard decision when you are scared of it

Dave, crediting Joe Hudson, reframes what a difficult decision actually indicates.

> We make tons and tons of choices throughout the day that we don't even think about. The times when these choices occur to us as hard decisions is when we're scared. We just notice the ones we're scared around, and they feel like decisions.

The observation is comparative and hard to argue with. Hundreds of choices a day pass without deliberation. The handful that present as agonising aren't distinguished by complexity or stakes — Andrew's is whether to go for a twenty-minute run — but by fear.

This reclassifies the felt sense of difficulty as a **signal about your state**, not information about the options. The heaviness isn't telling you the decision is finely balanced; it's telling you something here is frightening.

Which changes what to do with it. Deliberating harder addresses a problem that isn't there, and can run indefinitely because there is no amount of analysis that resolves a fear. → [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]
