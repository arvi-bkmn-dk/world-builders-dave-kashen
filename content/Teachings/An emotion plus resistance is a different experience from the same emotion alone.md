# An emotion plus resistance is a different experience from the same emotion alone

Dave credits Joe Hudson, and says it blew his mind:

> The experience of an emotion plus resistance is actually a fundamentally different experience than the emotion without resistance.

Not milder. **Different in kind.** Which means most people have never encountered their own sadness or fear — only the resisted composite, and they have been drawing conclusions about the emotion from that.

The self-sealing loop follows:

> It's just that we tend to resist the same emotions all the time. Because we resist them, they feel painful. Because they feel painful, we resist them. So we're stuck in a loop, not realising that we're the one creating it.

The evidence that the emotion was never the problem is unobtainable while the loop runs, because the resistance is always present in the sample.

Dave breaks it live rather than arguing it. He has Jesse locate sensation — tightness in the chest, shoulders pulled — then deliberately exaggerate the contrast:

> Just go ahead and resist. Like, go away, I don't wanna feel you. And just notice what actually occurs.

Jesse: "my body gets tight." Dave: "we have to get tight to stop feeling. And ironically, they'll likely intensify when you do that." Then the reverse — welcome the sensation "like a guest in your home that you're truly excited to welcome in" — and the experience changes character.

Doing it in both directions within a minute makes the variable visible: same emotion, two experiences, and the only difference is the stance toward it.
