# Anytime you are experiencing something as serious you are making it mean something it does not mean

One of Dave's favourite Jim Dethmer lines, and it works as an instant diagnostic.

> Anytime you're experiencing something as serious, you're making it mean something that it doesn't mean.

Seriousness is treated as **data about you**, not about the situation. And Dave names what the meaning usually is:

> Usually what you're making it mean is you're making it a proxy for your sense of security, control or approval.

Which makes seriousness a detector for the thing this whole episode is about. Wherever something feels grave, an external source has been attached to it.

The consequence he cares about is **play**:

> A lot of founders I work with are like — it doesn't feel like play, it doesn't even feel like it *could* feel like play, this is hard, this is serious. It's because they're still sourcing their sense of security and approval from whether the startup succeeds. Which leads to attachment, which makes the whole thing feel—

Jesse supplies the version anyone can test, and it works on families as well as companies:

> The most extreme example most people can relate to is you may have something you can't talk about in your family, because it's so serious that it can't even be mentioned. Versus something you can play and joke around about. **If you can't joke around with someone about it, it shows you—**

Dave: "you're definitely making it personal, or making it mean something."

So the ability to joke about a thing is a live readout of how much of your identity is invested in it.
