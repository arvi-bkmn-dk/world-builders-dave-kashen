# Presence or threat is the simple version of above and below the line

Dave gives the compressed definition of the conscious-leadership distinction he returns to constantly:

> The really simple version is you're either in a state of presence or in a state of threat. And when we're in a state of threat, we just tend to want to be right, and get defensive, and get reactive.

Two states, one binary, no gradient to argue about. What makes it usable is the tell: **wanting to be right**. You often can't detect your own threat state directly, because from inside it, it doesn't feel like threat — it feels like being correct about something important. But the urge to win the point is observable, and it reliably indicates below the line.

This is also why Dave says the mechanics of unarguable language can serve as a pattern interrupt. Hearing yourself say *my story is…* out loud briefly separates you from the certainty — "oh, you're triggering yourself, this might just be a story, I might not be right about it." The language doesn't move you above the line, but it can make visible that you're below it. → Speaking Unarguably

## Also in [[WS 01 - The Inner Keys to Becoming a World-Class Leader]]

Dave grounds the threat state in evolution: our ancestors lived in an environment far more dangerous than ours, and the nervous system hasn't caught up.

> our nervous system doesn't realize we're not still living in that world, so it's constantly scanning the environment for threats and perceiving threats to our status, as if they're threats to our survival

In threat, vision narrows, curiosity drops and reactivity rises. That's the first of the workshop's five default obstacles. → [[Five default patterns drain a leader's energy, and each has an inner key]]
