# Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong

The question Dave attaches to every story in [[The Facts and Stories Exercise]]: *how could the opposite of that be true?*

What makes it work is what it does **not** ask. It doesn't ask you to concede, to adopt the opposite, or to decide you were wrong.

> It's not like, oh, I'm wrong and the opposite is more true. It's just: if you see both sides, your righteous grip on the story loosens.

The exercise also demonstrates the fact/story boundary from the other direction. Try it on a fact — *how could the opposite of "started the company in October 2009" be true?* — and it fails immediately; that's the incorporation date. Try it on "picked the wrong business model" and five answers appear. The question is a detector as much as a solvent: if the opposite is arguable, the item was never a fact.

Jesse's worked example: *I should have sold the business in 2015* → had he sold it, he'd never have worked with Dave. Not a proof that staying was correct, just enough to demote a certainty to a perspective.

It is also step 2 of loosening a [[Core Beliefs|core belief]] — shown to be equally true in reverse, a belief becomes neutral rather than reversed.

## Also in [[GA 01 - The Inner Game of Life, Danny Miranda Podcast 394]]

Dave's case is losing about 70% of his net worth between late 2021 and mid-2022. Asked by coaches whether losing millions could be as good as making them, he'd said no way. After living through it:

> are you willing to see how the opposite of your story might be just as true?

> me losing that money I can genuinely in this moment see it was an absolute gift
