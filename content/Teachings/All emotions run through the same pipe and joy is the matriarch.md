# All emotions run through the same pipe and joy is the matriarch

The argument against selective feeling, and the reason you can't keep the pleasant emotions and decline the rest.

> All of your emotions flow through the same pipe. So if you're going to block one, you're going to block all, in some way.

Then the image Dave prefers:

> Joy is the matriarch of all the feelings — and if you're not letting her children in, she ain't coming.

Which reframes what most people are attempting. Blocking anger, sadness and fear in order to protect joy is the move that makes joy unavailable, because the mechanism doesn't discriminate; a system closed to intensity is closed to all of it.

Dave's definition of joy follows, and it is not an emotion you pursue:

> I actually think joy is sort of what's left over when you just allow everything else to move through.

A residue rather than a target. Which explains why chasing joy directly tends not to work, and why people who have done serious work with grief and anger report more of it without having aimed at it.

Dave's homework for Andrew is *Inside Out* — where Joy is literally the matriarch, runs the show, tries to block Sadness, and the film is built on what that costs. "Such an amazing way to understand how these energies inside of us move."
