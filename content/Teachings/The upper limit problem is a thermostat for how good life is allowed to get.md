# The upper limit problem is a thermostat for how good life is allowed to get

Dave's framing of the Hendricks concept, and the thermostat metaphor is exact:

> It's this homeostatic setting for how good life can be, how good we can feel. And when we start to raise our temperature higher than that — we start to feel too good, or life starts to go too well — it's scary in a way for our system, because it's unfamiliar. And so a thermostat, like a good thermostat does, will find a way to bring us down to our familiar level.

The key move is that the correction is **automatic and intelligent**. A thermostat doesn't debate; it acts to restore the setting, and it will use whatever mechanism is available.

That is why the symptoms look so unrelated to each other. A stray worry about a child who is fine. The thought *I should be doing more* arriving on a good day. A lottery winner deciding his wife isn't pretty enough. All the same function, running at different scales.

Jesse's initial rejection is the most instructive part: *I don't have an upper limit problem, I'm the most ambitious person, I have no ceiling.* He heard it as a claim about his aspirations. It is a claim about his tolerance — which is why it is possible to have unlimited ambition and a very low setting at the same time. → [[The Upper Limit Problem]]
