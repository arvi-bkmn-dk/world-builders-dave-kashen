# Beliefs determine what you notice and filter out

Dave's mechanical account of "your beliefs create your reality" — offered specifically to strip the mysticism out of it.

> Not in some mystical way, but through a concrete sequence:
> 1. **Beliefs** determine what you notice and filter out
> 2. **Perception** shapes your thoughts and emotions
> 3. **Thoughts and emotions** drive your decisions and actions
> 4. **Actions** create your results
> 5. **Results** reinforce your original beliefs

It closes into a loop, which is why beliefs feel like observations. You are being handed evidence continuously — evidence your belief selected.

> This cycle explains why two founders can face identical circumstances yet experience completely different outcomes. One sees opportunity in market volatility. Another sees only threat. One interprets feedback as valuable data. Another hears only criticism.

The dangerous ones are invisible by design:

> The most dangerous beliefs are the ones you don't even know you have — the invisible operating system running in the background of your mind.

And their origin is protective. Most were formed young, "as a way to make us feel safe when our environment or parents didn't." Dave's example is exact: a child who doesn't get the attention they want concludes they are *unworthy of it* — because "this creates the possibility of becoming worthy, rather than the untenable belief that there might be something deficient about their parent."

The belief is a bargain a child made to keep the world survivable. Which is why [[Beliefs that were adaptive in childhood become maladaptive in adulthood]], and why they resist being examined rather than simply being wrong.
