# Fact, feeling, story, want are the four buckets of speaking unarguably

The most concrete version of Speaking Unarguably in his work — four labelled slots that convert an accusation into something nobody can dispute.

> * **Fact:** "You arrived 15 minutes late."
> * **Feeling:** "I feel frustrated."
> * **Story:** "I'm telling myself you aren't valuing our meetings."
> * **Want:** "I'd like us to start on time."

The failure it prevents:

> We often confuse facts — what a video camera would record — with stories, our interpretations and assumptions. Phrases like "you're disrespecting me!" or "you don't care about this project!" might be stories, not observable facts.

> Stating our stories as if they're facts creates confusion that leads to defensiveness and endless debates about "who's right?"

The load-bearing slot is **Story**, and specifically the act of *naming it as one*:

> By labeling your interpretation as a story, you avoid arguing over "who's right" and keep conversations grounded in reality.

*You don't value our meetings* is a claim about them, and they will contest it. *I'm telling myself you don't value our meetings* is a report about you, and there is nothing there to argue with — which is what makes it unarguable. → [[Communicating to reveal instead of to control removes the thing there is to argue with]]

Dave's own test for the fact slot: crossed arms is a fact; "upset" or "bored" is a story.
