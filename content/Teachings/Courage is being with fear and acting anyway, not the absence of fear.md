# Courage is being with fear and acting anyway, not the absence of fear

Founders arrive assuming the task is to eliminate the fear. Dave redirects:

> The work is simply learning to feel the fear — without collapsing into it or pushing it away — and then act anyway.

Both failure modes are named, and they're opposites: collapsing into the fear (letting it decide) and pushing it away (suppressing it, which leaves it running underneath). The narrow path between them is *feeling it while acting*.

> Courage is not the absence of fear. It's being with fear and doing it anyway.

The framing that makes this trainable is "one rep at a time." Each choice of courage at the edge expands the edge slightly, and the compounding is described in terms of defaults rather than achievements: authenticity becomes default, bigger risks get taken, honesty becomes easier, delegation becomes natural, decisions get simpler. None of those is a one-off breakthrough; each is a shifted baseline.

Dave's coach supplied the image he uses for the size of the gap: our most fulfilling lives and relationships are one five-minute sweaty-palmed conversation away — and the same is true of a highest-performing team.
