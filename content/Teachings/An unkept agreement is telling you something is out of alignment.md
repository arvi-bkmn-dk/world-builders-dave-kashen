# An unkept agreement is telling you something is out of alignment

Dave heads off the obvious misreading of the *impeccable agreements* pillar in The Four Pillars of Integrity:

> People sometimes hear that as: **oh, that means I have to keep all my agreements.** Which is not true. Like that would make you a **slave to your former self.**

Because the agreement itself may have been out of integrity when you made it:

> Sometimes we say yes because we want to **manage the other person's feelings**, and we **hero** them, or we feel bad, or we think we **should.**

The test he applies instead is somatic — *"do you have a **whole body yes**? Do you really want to do that?"*

So the reading flips:

> When you have an unkept agreement, it doesn't mean you're bad and you should keep it. It means **it's telling you something might be out of alignment.**

> That's often as useful as *oh, I gotta go keep all my agreements* — **oh, I gotta go renegotiate or cancel a bunch of agreements that I made that I actually don't want to keep.** Which often is as powerful.

The cost of not doing it is Jesse's example: *"you're dead inside 'cause you work at a corporate job you don't want… you've kept an agreement so long that you didn't actually want."*

This completes the picture with [[The 90-9-1 Integrity Agreement]] — the 9% renegotiation slot isn't a concession to human weakness, it's the mechanism for keeping your agreements aligned with what you actually want. → [[Integrity is not just keeping your word, it is honoring your word]]
