# Judging yourself for doing it is what makes the action ok

The mechanism that keeps [[Most people choose to do a thing and resist doing it at the same time|doing-and-resisting]] running, and it's the least obvious thing in the episode because the guilt appears to be a *restraint* on the behaviour.

> If they do that, it makes the action okay — because at least they're judging themselves for doing it. As long as I call out to myself that I shouldn't be doing this, even though I'm doing it, then at least I'm aware that I shouldn't be.

The guilt purchases the permission. It is a toll paid for doing the thing, and once paid, the thing proceeds. Which means the guilt is not opposing the behaviour at all — it is what makes the behaviour tolerable to the self.

This explains why it never resolves anything. If guilt actually restrained the action, it would stop it; if it licenses the action, it recurs indefinitely, extracting its fee each time. You get the run and the misery, permanently.

It is the same structure as [[We slap ourselves on the wrist and let ourselves off the hook instead of taking the learning]] — self-punishment standing in for a decision. In both cases the honest alternative is harder: either choose it cleanly, or don't do it.
