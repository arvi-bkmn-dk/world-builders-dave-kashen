# High performance comes from clarity, presence and focus, not pressure

The third thing Dave says founders have to unlearn, and the one they defend hardest.

> Most founders are running on dirty fuel — fear, stress, and pressure. They think that if they let go of the stress, they'll lose their edge. I hear this all the time: **"If I stop stressing, won't I get complacent?"**

His answer, from coaching top founders:

> High performance doesn't come from pressure. It comes from **clarity, presence, and focus.**

Three replacements, and none of them is *relaxation* — which is the substitution the worried founder assumes is on offer. Clarity, presence and focus are demanding states. They are simply not *pressurised* ones.

What follows in practice:

> * Stop treating stress as a sign of productivity. It's not.
> * Shift from reactive problem-solving to proactive leadership.
> * Redefine what "high standards" mean — holding your team accountable doesn't mean overworking or operating from scarcity.

That third line separates two things founders routinely fuse. High standards are about what you accept; pressure is about the state you generate to get there. You can hold the first without the second, and the fear of "going soft" usually comes from assuming you can't.

Dave's summary, aimed squarely at the belief that calm is weakness:

> **Calm, focused leadership isn't weak — it's a competitive advantage.**

Which makes it a claim about *performance*, not about wellbeing. → [[The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are]]
