# The tragedy is placing a lower order value above a higher one

The episode's closing move, and it's notable for how much it gives away.

Jesse refuses to make any of this obligatory:

> This is not a should. Being fear-driven, or the game you play with yourself where you set goals — that's great, it can be highly effective. It will make you money.

And he concedes the trade-off in his own case, which most people in his position wouldn't:

> Dave was like: will you be willing to see that if you were old Jesse, you would be more successful? And I'm like, yeah. And then still choose — do you want to choose this other path? Just because I want to, because I want to live a certain way.

> I don't think you have to choose this. You can be very effective from below the line, from using fear, from dirty fuel. Lots of people are.

That admission is what makes the rest credible. The claim was never that dirty fuel doesn't work.

Dave's frame for how to actually decide:

> The tragedy is when we place a lower order value above a higher order value. I had a mentor once who said there's nothing wrong with marrying for money — but if you're marrying for money *over* marrying for love, it's just sort of tragic. Because love is actually more valuable than money.

> It's not wrong or bad. But I invite people to reflect honestly: is that actually more valuable to me than this other thing I'm trading off?

No ranking is imposed. The question is only whether your ordering is one you'd endorse if you looked at it — which is the difference between a choice and a default. → [[Preference and righteousness produce different energy from the same goal]]
