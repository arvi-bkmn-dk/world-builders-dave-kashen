# You can train the inner voice but it is still not you

Andrew asks whether the inner voice is the same for everyone. Jesse's answer distinguishes two things that get conflated.

> The ego is trained. There are people whose inner voices are much more critical because maybe their parents were critical. There are some who are more medium. There are probably some who are positive. But you can shift that inner voice with practice.

Which is what affirmations are for — Dave sent Jesse his morning affirmation once, and Jesse does his own. "I've shifted mine a lot over time."

Then the qualifier that keeps it from becoming a different project:

> It's still the voice. It's still not you.

The distinction matters because the two moves are easily confused. Improving the voice's content is worth doing; it is not the same as disidentifying from it. A supportive narrator you're still fused with still runs you — the verdicts are just more pleasant, and you remain dependent on them.

This is [[Self-discovery matters more than self-improvement]] applied to the inner monologue: training the voice is self-improvement, recognising you aren't it is self-discovery. Both are available; only the second changes what you are.

Dave's default case is why the training is worth doing anyway: most people's voice is "massively distorted toward negativity," so shifting it is a straightforward accuracy gain. → [[Your inner voice reports a dystopian version of a reality you are not in]]
