# Your inner voice has no more authority than a stranger muttering in a diner

The best image in his work for non-identification, and it works by making the familiar absurd.

You're in a roadside diner. A dishevelled man shuffles in, sits in the booth behind yours despite empty tables, and mutters:

> "You're worthless." · "You don't deserve to be where you are." · "You'll never be enough no matter how hard you try." · "Nobody actually likes you for you." · "You're a failure and deep down you know it."

> You sit there thinking — *poor guy* — and go on eating your breakfast.

Then the turn:

> Now imagine instead, you decide to listen intently to what he's saying, tell yourself he's talking to you, and believe he's right. **Wouldn't that be insane?**

> Yet every day, many of us do exactly this with our inner voice that speaks these exact same words — and we call it **"thinking."**

The sentences are identical. Only the assumed source differs, and that assumption is doing all the work. Coming from a stranger, they're noise; coming from inside, they're self-knowledge.

Dave uses it to redefine confidence:

> Being confident is not about gaining something you don't have, but rather **letting go of what's in the way of it.** When you recognize that the voice inside your head has no more authority than a stranger muttering to himself, you'll unlock a wellspring of confidence that has been within you all along.

→ [[Confidence is not gaining something you do not have, it is letting go of what is in the way]] · [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]]

And his account of where the voice came from: "oftentimes it comes from the voices of your parental figures growing up, which eventually become your own inner voice… **like outdated software running in the background.**"
