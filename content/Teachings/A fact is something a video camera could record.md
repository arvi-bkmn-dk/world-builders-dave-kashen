# A fact is something a video camera could record

Dave's test for separating fact from story, and its value is that it is mechanical rather than a matter of judgment.

> A fact is something that a video camera could record. It's what actually literally occurred. And the story is just the narrative, the interpretation that our mind does around the facts.

His demonstration in groups is better than the definition. He crosses his arms and looks down, then asks what people notice about him. *You're closed off. You're uninterested. You're hardened.* Then: which of those are facts and which are stories? The nervous laugh in the room is the teaching — everything offered was interpretation, produced instantly, by people who believed they were observing.

> Humans are like storytelling machines — we just do it so automatically that we don't realize it.

The camera test works because it removes you as the arbiter. You don't have to decide whether an interpretation is fair; you ask whether a lens could have captured it. "A team member missed the deadline" passes. "They don't care" does not. → [[A story is the meaning your mind adds to neutral facts]]
