# Life is a series of extenuating circumstances

Dave quotes Clayton Christensen, and uses the line in two different newsletters for two different decisions — which is what makes it structural rather than decorative.

> As Clayton Christensen said in *How Will You Measure Your Life?*: **Life is a series of extenuating circumstances.** And especially a startup is a series of extenuating circumstances.

> What that means is **it never feels like the right time.**

The excuses are interchangeable and always available:

> *"I can't right now because we're launching next month." "I can't right now because we're raising a round." "I can't right now because the team is understaffed."*

In [[A vacation is a forcing function|NL 53]] it explains why rest has to be scheduled against your own judgment. In NL 55 it explains why Dave couldn't wait the six months for his retention bonus:

> What happens after "just six more months"? **You make that same choice again. And again.** Six more months of choosing status and financial safety over purpose.

The insight is about the structure of the reasoning, not the content of any given excuse. Each individual postponement is defensible; the sequence of them is a life.
