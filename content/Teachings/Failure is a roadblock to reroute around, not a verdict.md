# Failure is a roadblock to reroute around, not a verdict

Andrew's challenge is about reputational cost: if you declare a desired future state publicly and miss it, you leave a trail of visible failures, and the people around you stop believing. Shouldn't you take fewer shots and stay quiet until you're close?

Dave's first move is to name what's actually happening in the question — the mind generating stories about a future that hasn't occurred. "They're just bullshit." He points out the asymmetry in the evidence, too: you know about the successful thing, not the T-shirt business or the high-school Facebook that went nowhere.

His metaphor for the relationship itself is a roadblock on a road trip. You hit it and conclude *I'm a loser, I'm never going to get there, forget it* — where what actually happens, as his kids demonstrate, is you find another way. Reroute.

> There was a sportscaster who said, I can't lose. I either win or I learn.

Jesse's structural version is the defence that makes declared goals survivable: stubborn on vision, flexible on the details. A podcast is one way of accomplishing the DFS, not the DFS itself, so the podcast failing is not the DFS failing. → [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]
