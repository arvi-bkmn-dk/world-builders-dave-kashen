# Heart open does not mean soft, you can be fierce with your heart open

Dave stops to correct a misreading before it spreads:

> I really liked your phrase **heart open.** And one thing I want to clarify is that **doesn't mean soft and nurturing and nice and kind.**

> People sort of think of it as: either I'm **hard-charging and harsh** and hold my team accountable, **or** I'm **soft and gentle with no accountability.** And I think that's **totally a false trade-off.**

> You can bring **ferocity.** You can be **fierce** — with your heart open. You can even say *"that is not okay with me, I expect more."* And certainly **you can create accountability with your heart open.**

He flags the same false binary one level up, in the fear-versus-love shorthand:

> The challenge of oversimplifying it that way is it might give you the impression that **you shouldn't feel fear, and your team shouldn't feel fear** — which **ironically is a way of closing your heart.** 'Cause we're human, and sometimes we feel fear. And if you don't **want** to feel the fear, then you **close your heart.**

> To me that's the big aha, is it's **the closing your heart** — that tends to be what creates loneliness, what creates constriction, what creates drama, what creates internal angst.

So the variable was never how forceful you are, or whether fear is present. It is whether you are open while it happens — which is why identical words land completely differently depending on the state behind them.

Dave and Jesse mark this as needing its own episode: *"how do you create accountability with your heart open?"*
