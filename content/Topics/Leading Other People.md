# Leading Other People

What the inner work looks like once other humans are involved — and the reason it can't stay private. **[[Your state is contagious to your team]]**, and **[[Your stories set the emotional climate of your team]]**. The team is already reading you; the only variable is whether you know what they're reading.

## The founder is the bottleneck

[[Your personal edges create your company's ceiling]]. Wherever a founder stops — the conflict they won't have, the decision they won't make, the person they won't release — the company stops too, and it presents as a business problem rather than an avoidance.

The most common misdiagnosis: [[Emotional capacity problems get misdiagnosed as performance problems]]. The team isn't underperforming; something in the leader can't be felt, so something in the company can't be said. → [[Fix the holes below the waterline, then go back to your strengths]]

The capability version: [[Denying a part of yourself makes you less behaviorally flexible]]. A denied part is a removed option, so every situation calling for it gets handled with something worse. Which is why [[You could behave any way any other human behaves]] is the goal and [[That is not authentic is usually just not comfortable]] is the standing excuse — Dave's answer to the founder who won't be the cheerleader.

## Boundaries, and why they don't get set

The single highest-leverage diagnosis here: [[A leader who cannot feel anger cannot hold a boundary with their team]]. It doesn't present as anger — it presents as a dull frustration about why people won't do what you want.

[[Anger is boundary energy and a form of love, not its opposite]], and [[Love in leadership can be fierce]].

The distinction most leaders have never made: [[Boundaries do not require the other person to do anything]]. *Get off your screens by 9pm* is a request, and when it's declined you resent an outcome you never controlled. *I'm coming upstairs to take the screen at 9pm* is a boundary.

And the third option nobody uses: [[Healthy expression is the third option between reacting and holding it in]].

## Conflict is not the problem

[[Resolving conflict without allowing the feelings is an insane act]] — the feelings are what generated it. Suppressing conflict doesn't remove it; it relocates it, and the drama leaders fear is produced by the containing.

The most portable move in the corpus: [[The presenting issue is almost never the real issue]]. Nobody is angry about the spreadsheet formatting. Name the emotion rather than arguing the content — [[Saying I feel fear was more effective than yelling at people]] — and [[Summarising what someone said and guessing their feelings and wants makes them feel heard]].

The obstacle is usually wanting to win: [[The compulsion to be right is what gets in the way]]. Being correct resolves the stated problem and leaves the real one to regenerate next week.

**Speaking Unarguably** is the discipline — [[Communicating to reveal instead of to control removes the thing there is to argue with]] — with two cautions: [[Where your heart is matters more than whether the language is technically unarguable]], and [[Conscious leadership language is scaffolding, not the destination]].

## Motivating without dirty fuel

The question underneath: what are you running the team on?

[[Motivating a team from vision unlocks ideas hustle cannot reach]], and [[Planning from fear is control, planning from desire is creation]]. → [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]] · [[The Focus-Motivation Model]]

Vision has to be renderable. [[A desired future state has to be a picture you can see, not a statement on a wall]], and the tension it creates needs calibrating — [[Creative tension is a rubber band that can be too slack or snapped]]. A snapped band is worse than none: the team can double the business and still feel it loses daily.

On declaring things you can't yet deliver: [[Declaring a future you do not know how to reach is part of how you reach it]], and [[It is not a reality distortion field, it is a possibility distortion field]] — you bend the future, not the present. [[Language is a tool to create reality, not only to describe it]].

The simplest lever, and the one leaders skip: [[Did you tell them you want them to do it]]. Most are busy figuring out how to manipulate their way there.

And a hiring consequence: [[If the person wants to do the thing you need them to do, they do not require a lot of management]] — management load is an output of hiring, not a skill. → [[You cannot decouple strategy from what you actually want]]

## Rescuing, and why it backfires

The role founders defend hardest: [[Heroes require victims]]. Casting yourself as rescuer casts everyone else as someone needing rescue, and they feel it. [[The hero creates temporary relief but undermines learning and growth]] — nobody gets to learn, so the cycle repeats, and the hero ends up resenting people who never asked.

The replacement is a question rather than a solution: [[Asking someone what they want shifts them above the line faster than solving the problem]]. Solving it resolves one instance; asking it teaches the move.

Related traps: [[Saying it is my fault is usually a way to end the conversation]] — the leader taking the blame as a way to stop thinking about it — and [[Imagining someone's ideal self and criticising them for not being there yet is not support]].

## Appreciation

Systematically underused, and Dave treats it as a business tool rather than a nicety. [[What you appreciate appreciates]], and [[Appreciate who someone is, not only what they did]].

[[The Acknowledgement Party]] is the practice, with the counterintuitive turn that [[The acknowledgement party flips from asking to giving]].

Its dark twin, which more leaders run than admit: [[A lot of leaders look at it as a strategy to keep you jumping through hoops]] — withholding appreciation deliberately, as motivation.

## What the best of it feels like

[[Empower is being fully authentic regardless of the consequences]] — Superman on a mountain with no kryptonite, nothing to protect, so no need to manage how you land. [[Empowerment and love meet at the top of the mountain]]: the two arrive at the same state from opposite directions.

Which makes vulnerability a leadership capability rather than a risk. [[Welcome the self-doubt and you actually feel more confident]], and [[Imagine there was nothing anyone could say about you that would bother you]].

And the standing correction to hustle: [[The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are]].

One rule against all management doctrine: [[Anytime there is a should involved it is situational awareness distortion]]. Micromanagement isn't bad and founder mode isn't good — both are shoulds, and a should overwrites the situation in front of you.
