## Vision, and the tension that does the work

The engine is **[[Desired Future State (DFS)]]**, borrowed from Peter Senge's creative tension. Tension motivates because tension seeks resolution; the only question is which kind you manufacture.

Most people build it from lack or fear — effective, and expensive. → [[Clean Fuel and Dirty Fuel]] · [[Need is dirty fuel and want is clean]] · [[Planning from fear is control, planning from desire is creation]]

The alternative holds two things at once: full acceptance of where you are, and a vivid future. [[Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong]]. The whole distinction is one of **time** — [[Frustration is a desired future collapsed into the present]], and the suffering is in the collapse, not the ambition. Hence [[You cannot get there until you are here]].

Execution details that decide whether it works:

- [[A desired future state has to be a picture you can see, not a statement on a wall]] — a sentence generates no tension; an image you can inhabit does
- [[Creative tension is a rubber band that can be too slack or snapped]] — and a snapped band is worse than none
- [[Declaring a future you do not know how to reach is part of how you reach it]] — the declaration generates the creativity that finds the route
- [[It is not a reality distortion field, it is a possibility distortion field]] — bend the future, never the present
- [[What you focus on is where you steer]] · [[Language is a tool to create reality, not only to describe it]]

Before any of it: [[Most adults have atrophied their dreamer]]. Plenty of founders can't name a desired future because the faculty went unused for twenty years.

## Decisions

[[We make decisions emotionally and rationalise them afterwards]] — the finding, not a provocation. Which makes the standard approach backwards.

[[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]], and [[A choice only feels like a hard decision when you are scared of it]]. The hardness is the fear, not the complexity.

So the route to clarity is downward, not harder: **[[Feel Over Figure]]**. [[More thinking makes clarity worse, like shaking sand in a bottle]] — agitation, where clarity comes from settling.

When genuinely torn: [[Both parts want the same outcome and have different strategies]] — treat it as a negotiation between parts rather than a defect, and [[You can have it all, but it requires more creativity]], because most trade-offs are invented.

And the rule against inherited doctrine: [[Anytime there is a should involved it is situational awareness distortion]]. A should is a rule imported from outside the situation, so holding it stops you seeing what's in front of you. [[Preference and righteousness produce different energy from the same goal]].

## Motivation that doesn't cost you

The claim that inverts hustle culture: [[The more you enjoy it, the more efficient and effective you are]]. Not a trade-off resolved in favour of joy — an argument that there was never a trade-off, because grinding is worse on both axes.

[[There is a way of creating results that feels like flow rather than grind]], and the starting move is smaller than restructuring your life: [[How much more could you just enjoy the things you are already doing]].

Why discipline keeps failing: [[Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and that is what creates the yo-yo]] · [[Shame is the lock that binds the chains of bad habits]] · [[So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle]]. And [[The marshmallow test measured strategies, not willpower]] — even the canonical willpower study wasn't measuring willpower.

Two reframes that dissolve obligation: [[You do not have to do your taxes, there are just consequences]] and [[The same activity feels different from have-to than from get-to]]. Also [[Most people choose to do a thing and resist doing it at the same time]] — the most expensive configuration there is, paying full cost for the work plus the cost of fighting yourself through it.

## Wants, and why they're strategic

[[You cannot decouple strategy from what you actually want]]. A strategy the founder doesn't want doesn't execute, however correct — the person responsible won't drive it. Rick Elias: *if you don't wanna make the strategy you're coming up with, it ain't gonna happen.*

Which makes wants an input to planning rather than a personal indulgence. [[Following your wants usually leads to the same actions without the baggage]], and [[Did you tell them you want them to do it]] is the same principle pointed at the team.

The obstacle is embarrassment: [[People sandbag their authentic wants to avoid seeming self-indulgent]], and [[Are you willing to have what you want]].

## Risk, failure and the ceiling

[[Run experiments so that the experiment fails instead of you]] — **[[Experiments Instead of Failure]]** — which puts the outcome outside your identity, where a failed test costs information rather than self-worth.

The ceiling is usually internal: [[The upper limit problem is a thermostat for how good life is allowed to get]], and [[It takes energy to keep these parts at bay]] is where a lot of founder exhaustion actually originates.

On time, the most common scarcity story: [[Time is finite but not enough is always a story]].

And the one that quietly costs the most: [[Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship]] — Jesse declined a sale over a number in his head, never grieved it, and the enterprise value didn't move for five years.

## The order that actually works

[[Do-have-be is backwards and it never arrives]] — most people run *do this, then have that, then I'll be confident.* **[[Be-Do-Have]]** reverses it: choose who you're being, and the doing follows.

Which is also why [[Bigger is better is a treadmill that never arrives]] and [[If you cannot be happy now, you will not be happy then]].
