# Decisions and Clarity

Founders spend their days deciding. Dave's claim is that **most hard decisions are hard because of a feeling, not a lack of information**, and that clarity comes from settling, not from thinking harder.

## Subtopics

- **Decision-Making and Clarity** (166 notes)

## Decisions are emotional

[[We make decisions emotionally and rationalise them afterwards]]. So [[A choice only feels like a hard decision when you are scared of it]], and [[Figuring out the right answer is a strategy for avoiding the feeling]]. [[Unfelt sadness keeps you in the wrong business, job or relationship]].

## Feel, then figure

[[Feel Over Figure]] is the model. [[Fear feels urgent and constricting, intuition feels clear and spacious]], which is how to tell a gut signal from a scared one. [[Your authentic wants are the right answer for you]], and [[Following your wants usually leads to the same actions without the baggage]].

## Seeing the situation clearly

[[A fact is something a video camera could record]]. [[Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong]]. [[The real issue with control is confusing which things go in which folder]]. [[Scenario planning is chosen once, rumination replays the undesired future all day]].

## Creating, not only choosing

[[Creative Tension]] and [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]: hold a clear picture of what you want against an honest picture of now. [[Creative tension requires accepting current reality, not making it wrong]], and [[The less attached you are to an outcome, the easier it is to create]]. Being is the seed of doing.

## Lowering the stakes

[[Experiments Instead of Failure]]: [[Run experiments so that the experiment fails instead of you]]. [[Preference and righteousness produce different energy from the same goal]].

## Deciding about people

[[The Keeper Test and Talent Density]] and [[There are very few things only you can do]]: the decisions founders put off longest are usually about who is on the team.
