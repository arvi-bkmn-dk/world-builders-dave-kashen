## Start here: you are not the voice

The entry point to all of it is a distinction most people have never made. There is a voice narrating your experience, and there is something noticing the voice. [[Hearing a voice in your head means there is a talker and a listener]] — and the fact that you can *hear* it is the proof that you are not it.

The voice is not malicious. [[The narrating voice in your head is a survival mechanism, not your self]]: it evolved to keep you alive in a world of physical threat, and it still treats a hard conversation like a predator. [[Your inner voice reports a dystopian version of a reality you are not in]], which is why its reporting feels urgent and is usually wrong.

Two moves follow. [[Identify as the listener rather than the talker]] — a relocation, not a silencing; [[You can train the inner voice but it is still not you]]. And [[Meditation is not quieting the voice, it is noticing]], which reframes the practice entirely: [[We act on a thought, feel it, or judge it, and meditation is doing none of those]].

Once the gap opens, a harder question arrives: [[If you are not your thoughts, the question of who you are opens]].

## Facts, stories, and why problems feel real

The machinery running underneath is the **[[The Storyteller Mind|Storyteller Mind]]**. [[A story is the meaning your mind adds to neutral facts]], and [[A fact is something a video camera could record]] — a test sharp enough to use in a meeting.

The mind does not generate neutral stories. [[Your mind tells stories with problems in them because problems hold attention]]. And the stories are not simply false, which is what makes them sticky: [[Every story your mind tells is partially true]].

So the lever is not disproving them. [[Naming a story out loud creates separation from it]], and [[Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong]]. Beneath the stories sit beliefs — [[A belief is just a deeply held story]], and [[One to three core beliefs shape everything]].

There is also a named cast. [[The Seven Storyteller Archetypes]] — Achiever Alex, Bossy Brett, Comparison Charlie and the rest — with [[The Story Release Technique]] as the procedure.

## Above and below the line

The simplest frame in the corpus: [[Presence or threat is the simple version of above and below the line]]. Below the line, [[Interpreting a situation as a problem is what drops you into blame]], and blame forecloses learning.

The alternative is not optimism. [[Acceptance is the doorway to effective action, not apathy]]; [[You cannot change what you do not accept]]; and [[Accepting reality means facing the numbers, not spiritual bypass]] — acceptance *requires* looking harder at the number, not softer.

What converts it into power is a question: [[How am I creating this is the question that converts acceptance into power]] → [[One Hundred Percent Responsibility]] and [[The Responsibility Roadmap]].

Which is not the same as self-blame. [[Saying it is my fault is usually a way to end the conversation]], and [[When you blame yourself you are much less curious]] — the wrist-slap closes the inquiry exactly where it should begin. → [[We slap ourselves on the wrist and let ourselves off the hook instead of taking the learning]]

## Parts, personas and the shadow

[[All humans have all the same parts]] — the foundational claim of this whole region, and the one Dave returns to most.

What we call character is largely selection. [[Personality is a defence structure built from the feelings you would not feel]], assembled in the first eight years, and [[The conditions you learned to survive become the conditions your survival depends on]].

Personas are the visible version. [[Either you play your persona or it plays you]], and they are self-confirming because [[We do not see the world as it is, we see the world as we are]]. Under threat we rotate three of them — [[Fight is villain, flight is hero, freeze is victim]] — and [[The voice in your head is mostly playing those three roles]]. The hero is the one founders defend: [[Heroes require victims]], and [[The hero creates temporary relief but undermines learning and growth]].

The **shadow** is whatever got excluded. [[Anything you judge in yourself or in other people is a shadow]], found via [[What is the thing you most do not want people to say about you]] and [[The one you most do not think you are is probably the blind spot]]. Projection is the standing instrument: [[Projection as a Mirror]], [[Turn the whole world into a mirror]], [[Being irritated by someone shows you a part of yourself you have not loved]], [[Your judgments of others mark the places you have not grown]].

What makes a shadow bearable is refusing the totality: [[I am a loser and I am also a winner, and both are partially true]].

And the reason to bother is capability, not virtue. [[Denying a part of yourself makes you less behaviorally flexible]] — a denied part is a removed option — which is why [[You could behave any way any other human behaves]] is the payoff and [[That is not authentic is usually just not comfortable]] is the obstacle. Also: [[It takes energy to keep these parts at bay]], which is where a good deal of founder exhaustion actually comes from.

Every part is working for you, including the ugly ones: [[Every part of you has an intended positive outcome]], and when two conflict, [[Both parts want the same outcome and have different strategies]].

## Shame, and why self-improvement stalls

Shame gets its own treatment because it is the mechanism that stops the others. [[Shame says I am bad and guilt says I did bad]]; [[Shame is the electric fence]]; [[Shame puts the brakes on all the other emotions]]; [[Shame is the lock that binds the chains of bad habits]].

Which explains the failure of effort. [[Making yourself wrong triggers counter-will and that is what creates the yo-yo]], and [[So much of our self-improvement comes from this fear triangle]] — villain criticises, victim complains, hero buys the 30-day plan.

The replacement is [[Self-discovery matters more than self-improvement]], with [[Nature grows and we are part of nature]] as the argument and [[What are the benefits of the behavior you want to change]] as the first move. The instruction for the feeling itself: [[Welcome the shame but do not believe the thoughts that come with it]].

Underneath all of it: [[You are already whole, perfect and complete]], and [[Via negativa says there is nothing to attain, only barriers to remove]].

## The practices

- **Notice, moment to moment** — [[Self-awareness is moment-to-moment, not a list of your strengths and weaknesses]] · [[The Meditation Practice]]
- **Check in** — [[Thoughts, Feelings, Sensations]] · [[People can access the physical sensation more easily than the feeling]]
- **Separate fact from story** — [[The Facts and Stories Exercise]]
- **Ask willingness** — [[The Willingness Question]] · [[Willing means literally right now, not someday in the future]]
- **Investigate the pattern** — [[Ask someone to teach you how they produce their own suffering]] · [[What you are getting is what you are committed to]] · [[Your life reflects your unconscious commitments]]
- **Use the work itself** — [[The startup is a growth dojo but most founders run it as a whipping room]]

## Where it shows up in the company

[[Your state is contagious to your team]] and [[Your stories set the emotional climate of your team]] — which is why none of this stays private. [[Asking someone what they want shifts them above the line faster than solving the problem]] is the smallest usable version of it.

And the ceiling claim, again, because it is the reason the inner game is a business topic: [[The upper limit problem is a thermostat for how good life is allowed to get]].
