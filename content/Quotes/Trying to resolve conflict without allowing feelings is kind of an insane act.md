> Trying to resolve conflict without allowing feelings is kind of an insane act, 'cause it's the feelings that are really creating it.

## What He Means

Dave's case for emotions at work, argued from inevitability rather than preference. Conflict will happen — "a group of human beings trying to accomplish things together" with different values and styles — and suppressing it doesn't remove it, it relocates it. "It's gonna leak out in different ways."

His diagnostic example is disagree-and-commit, the thing leaders complain fails: it fails because "people haven't fully been able to express their feelings around the idea." The commit collapses because the disagree never completed.

Jesse's reframe is the one that lands with skeptics: "the feelings are always there, whether you say them or not." → [[Resolving conflict without allowing the feelings is an insane act]]
