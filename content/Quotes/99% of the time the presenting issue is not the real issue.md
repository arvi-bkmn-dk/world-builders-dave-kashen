> 99% of the time the presenting issue is not the real issue. And we waste so much time trying to solve intellectually the presenting issue.

## What He Means

Why winning the argument doesn't end the problem. Jesse's old method was to out-debate the complaint — "here's number one, number two, here's why this is right" — and be correct, and watch a new version of the same issue appear the following week. "They weren't actually mad about the spreadsheet formatting."

The replacement is to name the emotion rather than argue the content: *you seem angry, what are you mad about?* "Just letting them air the anger usually solves 80% of the problem."

Dave's prerequisite is the part that can't be skipped: reading it in someone else depends on having felt it in yourself. → [[The presenting issue is almost never the real issue]]
