> All these walls that we put up metaphorically to protect us become our prison when we become adults.

## What He Means

The cost of a defence that outlives its usefulness. Each wall was built for a reason — a child learns which feelings earn negative attention and constricts accordingly — and each one was adaptive at the time.

What changes is the context, not the wall. The protection stays in place long after the threat is gone, and what remains is a narrowed range of feeling that gets experienced as character. → [[Personality is a defence structure built from the feelings you would not feel]]

Dave is explicit that this is physical rather than only psychological: the constriction "lives in the fascia," which he offers as an explanation for a good deal of chronic stress and health trouble.
