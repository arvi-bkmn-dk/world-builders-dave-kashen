> That's actually not a boundary, that's a request.

## What He Means

Dave catching his own mistake as a parent, and the reason so many "boundaries" produce resentment. *Get off your screens by 9pm* depends on someone else complying — so when they don't, it reads as a violation of something you never actually controlled.

The corrected version moves the action to the only person who can perform it: *I'm going to come upstairs and take your screen away at 9pm.* "Just like an actual boundary — you hit a wall, the wall's there."

The test: if keeping it requires their cooperation, it's a request. → [[Boundaries do not require the other person to do anything]]
