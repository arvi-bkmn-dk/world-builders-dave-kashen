# Quote Attributions

**Dave attributes deliberately.** It is one of the most consistent habits in his work and it shapes how this graph has to record him.

> I'm parroting Joe Hudson here just to give him credit. He's a great coach.

> This was something I learned from the Conscious Leadership Group, to give credit where it's due — which I think they learned from Katie and Gay Hendricks.

## Why it matters here

The graph's job is to record what Dave teaches. A large share of what he teaches, he learned — and says so. If the graph flattens that, three things break:

1. **People get misattributed.** A compiled quotes sheet that prints *emotion as energy in motion* or *unfelt feelings are the glue* as Dave's own would credit him with real people's work.
2. **The lineage disappears.** Dave is a synthesist. Chains like Hendricks → Conscious Leadership → Dave are visible in the transcripts and are part of the content.
3. **It weakens the provenance work.** Teaching titles carry `phrasing:` for Dave's words vs our compression. Attribution is the same question one level out: whose *idea* is this, not just whose wording.

## The convention

- Quote notes carry an **`attribution:`** field whenever the line is not Dave's own, naming the person and their organisation.
- The attributed party gets a node in `Orgs and Collaborators/` with `relationship: teacher` once they recur.
- **Report the chain as Dave reports it**, hedges included. He says "I *think* they learned from Katie and Gay Hendricks" — the graph says the same.
- A teaching can be Dave's own phrasing (`phrasing: dave`) and still carry an inherited idea. The two fields answer different questions.
