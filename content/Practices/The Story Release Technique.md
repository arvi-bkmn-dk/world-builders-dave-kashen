# The Story Release Technique

**Practice for:** [[The Storyteller Mind]] — this is the exercise that model is meant to be done with.

## What It Is

The three-part practice for disengaging attention from a story and returning to presence. It is the actionable end of [[The Storyteller Mind]] — the part a founder actually *does* in the moment.

## The Model

**1. Realise it's a story.** When you hear the mind generating thoughts, ask whether what it's saying is constructive and true, or a negative story about you or your reality. Then name it out loud:

> "The story my mind is making up is…"

This instantly creates separation between you and the narrative. Optionally, look for evidence against it, or ask *how might the opposite of that story be true?* → [[Naming a story out loud creates separation from it]]

**2. Recognise the storyteller.** Map it onto one of [[The Seven Storyteller Archetypes]]. Identifying the *character* makes disengagement easier than contesting the content.

**3. Return to presence.** Having disengaged, attend to what is actually occurring here and now. "This process puts you back in the driver's seat" — responding from awareness rather than reacting to a fear-based narrative.

## How Dave Uses It

He frames the payoff in team terms, not personal calm: a founder who leads from presence rather than story produces higher trust, better collaboration, more psychological safety, less drama, more innovation, clearer communication. "One of the most underrated levers for unlocking your team's full potential."

Note the shared machinery with Speaking Unarguably — naming *the story I make up is…* is simultaneously a communication move and a pattern interrupt on yourself.
