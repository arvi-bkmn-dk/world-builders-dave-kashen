# The Entitlement Practice

## What It Is

Dave's method for generating genuine appreciation rather than performing gratitude — by resetting the baseline it's measured against.

> If we're really honest, most of us feel entitled to lots of things. Entitled to being good at your job, entitled to having the results you want. And it really gets in the way of appreciation.

## The Model

**1. Drop the baseline to zero.**

> Every now and then I'll do this practice: I am entitled to nothing. Nothing. I shouldn't even be here — the statistical probability of me being alive rounds to zero. So I'm not entitled to having any experience at all.

**2. Notice what wells up on its own.**

> And if I start with that ground of zero, suddenly what naturally wells up is a ton of appreciation. For being alive, for breathing air, for having running water, for friendship, for getting to do work that I love, for a loving family.

The appreciation is not manufactured — it's what's left when the entitlement is removed. Which is why this works where gratitude lists often don't: it changes the denominator instead of asking you to feel something on command. → [[Start from entitled to nothing and appreciation wells up on its own]]

**3. See the default it replaces.**

> Most of us start the other way. We imagine the absolute ideal version of our life, feel entitled to it, and then get frustrated and disappointed the moment our life doesn't match that.

Same with people: *this is exactly how I expect my wife to be* — so the moment she doesn't meet the mind's ideal, disappointment, "instead of just appreciating that she's even spending one moment with you."

## How Dave Uses It

To distinguish two things Andrew conflates — seeing the good in people and holding high expectations of them. → [[Imagining someone's ideal self and criticising them for not being there yet is not support]]

The practice also explains why appreciation is scarce in high-performing environments. It isn't callousness; it's that the reference point has been set at the ideal, so nothing actual can ever clear it. Everything real is a shortfall, and a shortfall doesn't prompt thanks.

Note how it relates to [[Bigger is better is a treadmill that never arrives]] — that's the same mechanism aimed at outcomes; this is it aimed at your whole life.
