# The Meditation Practice

**Practice for:** [[The Talker and the Listener]] — this is the exercise that model is meant to be done with.

## What It Is

Dave's actual instruction when a founder says *fine, you've convinced me — what do I do?* Deliberately unmystical, and built around correcting the one misunderstanding that makes people quit.

## The Model

**The instruction:**
1. Set a timer for five or ten minutes.
2. Focus on the breath — the belly expanding and contracting, or the air moving in and out of your nostrils.
3. Your attention will get hooked by a thought. This is not a malfunction.
4. When you *notice* that has happened, gently bring attention back to the breath.
5. Repeat.

**The correction that matters:**

> It's not what people get confused by. So many people are like, I'm bad at meditating. It's not that you're supposed to stay focused on your breath for 10 straight minutes. **The practice is just the noticing.**

Having the intention to focus on the breath is what lets you notice the drift. The drift isn't failure; it's the event the practice is built around.

> If you have nine and a half minutes of just focusing on different thoughts, but one time you notice that's occurring and bring your attention back — that's useful. That's the practice.

**The metaphor:** training a puppy. *Oh, it went off. Okay, come on back.* No making yourself wrong.

**What is actually being built:**

> If there was a muscle to build, it's the muscle of exerting conscious choice around where you put your attention.

**What it is not:** quieting the voice. It's nice when that happens and it sometimes does, but → [[Meditation is not quieting the voice, it is noticing]]

**The theory underneath:** with a thought we normally act on it, feel something, or judge it. Meditation is having the thought and doing none of the three. → [[We act on a thought, feel it, or judge it, and meditation is doing none of those]]

## How Dave Uses It

He names the benefits in pragmatic order — living in the reality you're actually in, then resilience — and points to apps for people who want structure: **Waking Up** (Sam Harris, a guided 35-day on-ramp starting at four minutes) and **Insight Timer** ("almost like the Netflix of meditation," free, thousands of guided sessions plus a plain timer). His own approach is to treat different modalities as experiments. → [[Experiments Instead of Failure]]

His personal account of the payoff is a touchstone rather than a state: having touched a depth of peace, aliveness and love in meditation, "I can kind of bring it with me into my every day more and more."
