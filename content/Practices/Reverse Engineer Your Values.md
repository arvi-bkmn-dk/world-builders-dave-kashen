# Reverse Engineer Your Values

**Practice for:** [[Defining your values is a self-discovery exercise, not a strategy exercise]]

> Sitting down with a blank page and asking "what do I value" rarely gets anywhere.

## Find the values

1. **The people who light you up.** Your favourite people you've ever worked with: what qualities make you think *I get to spend time with this person today?* Those people are a mirror.
2. **The peaks.** Graph your life against your energy level. What values were you honouring at the peaks?
3. **The bathroom mirror.** What would be useful to write there: things you truly value and might forget on your worst days?

> Values are a bridge from your best self to your everyday self.

## Build them in, working backwards

4. **Your interview questions.** If you could ask only five to seven questions to learn who someone is, what would they be?
5. **A culture-based review.** For each value, write a behaviour, and have everyone rate each other one to five on it.

> Writing those lines forces a clarity that a list of words never will, because for every value you have to answer: How will you know?
