# Turn On the Lights

**Practice for:** [[Fear of failure gets its power from staying vague]] — imagining your startup dying, deliberately and once.

Dave's exercise for getting what a founder learns on the other side of failure, without losing the company.

> Facing the fear is different. It's something you choose.

Set aside uninterrupted time, and welcome the raw feelings and sensations as they arise:

> For the fear to fully mature, it must be completely allowed in the body.

## Steps

1. **Imagine your startup completely failing.** Bankruptcy, the investor email, telling the team, the media coverage. Let it be vivid.
2. **Notice the stories your mind generates.** *I'll never get funded again. Everyone will think I'm a loser.*
3. **Pull each fear out of the vague.** What specifically would happen, and then what? Separate what's close to fact from what your mind is filling in.
4. **Ask how the opposite of your biggest stories might be true.** *My investors will think I'm a loser* becomes *my investors want to back what I do next.* → [[Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong]]

> Getting specific is what lets you see the difference, and once you see it, there's surprisingly little substance to the fear.

## What It Opens Up

Underneath the stories, the fear was a ball of sensation. Welcomed completely, it matures into how much you care, and often into gratitude:

> "Right now, this company is alive. I get to do this. I get to build something from nothing."

> For as long as you're playing the game, play to win. It's more fun that way.
