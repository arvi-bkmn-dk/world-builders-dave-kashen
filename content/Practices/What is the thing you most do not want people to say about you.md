# What is the thing you most do not want people to say about you

The most direct route to your own shadow, and the one that finally got Andrew interested.

> One way to know what your shadow is — what's the thing that you most don't want people to say about you? If you imagine people in a room talking about you, maybe you're not there, and they said: oh, that guy's a total narcissist. Or he's a liar, a cheater, a fraud, a hack.

Dave answers it on himself without softening it. His was **loser**:

> It was like a third rail, this idea of being a loser. I feel disgusted.

And then does the work in public — asking how it's *true*:

> I lose all the time. I lose money, I make bad bets. I lose clients that I maybe wanna work with. There might be a CEO of some cool company I'd love to work with, and I don't go for it 'cause I don't want the rejection. So there's so many ways that I am a loser.

The cost was concrete: less risk, holding back in client sessions, programs not attempted. "I'll twist myself in knots trying to make sure that I'm not seen as a loser — and that really limited me."

Andrew's reaction is the honest arc of the episode: he starts out calling it "wussy stuff that is going to make me weaker" and ends up asking for the question. "I'm definitely too embarrassed to even think about this with you two looking at me, but you are right, that is a good one."
