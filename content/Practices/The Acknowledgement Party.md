# The Acknowledgement Party

## What It Is

A facilitated exercise Dave runs with teams and at retreats. Its design is counterintuitive: it starts by making people **ask** for acknowledgement, which is the hardest possible entry point — and that difficulty is what makes the flip happen.

## The Model

**The rules:**
1. Go round the table. Each person **asks** a specific person for acknowledgement.
2. It must be **specific** and **directed**. Not *acknowledge me for having a nice turban* — but *Dave, I want you to acknowledge me for learning how to ski this year.*
3. The named person gives it. *Jesse, I acknowledge you for your courage and persistence in learning to ski this year.*
4. Next person.

**What happens.** Jesse's account of Half Moon Bay, 2018, with his exec team:

> It was so awkward. You'd rather hear a chalkboard screeching, that's how awkward it was. And the higher the leader, the more uncomfortable — for me to ask people on my team to acknowledge me, I'm their boss, I'm paying them, I'm not supposed to need or want that.

First round: thirty minutes. Second round: ten. And then, partway through the second round, it inverts:

> Rather than asking, people started saying: actually, John, I really want to acknowledge you for running the New York office. Karina said to Rosy — you're a mom, you just had a baby, and you're also the CFO.

> It's two hours, everybody's crying. Everyone stopped asking and instead started giving each other acknowledgements they'd never said after many years of working together.

**Why asking first works.** Asking is more exposing than giving, so it breaks the ice at the hardest point. Once people have survived that, giving feels easy by comparison — and the room has already established that this is what's happening here.

## How Dave Uses It

At the first Sun Valley Founders Retreat it ran for **five hours**, and nobody wanted to stop — after days of deep work and skiing. Dave's read: "a testament to how powerful it is and how hungry we are for it."

Jesse took it home the same night and started acknowledging his wife, who cried: *we've known each other forever and you've never said any of those things.*

**The one-to-one version** Andrew negotiates toward, and Dave's prescription for the opener: *I appreciate you for…* — direct, no throat-clearing, no explanation of why you're saying it. → [[People hide behind authenticity when they mean comfort]]
