# The Sedona Method

**Practice for:** [[Security, Control and Approval]] — this is the exercise that model is meant to be done with.

## What It Is

A releasing practice Dave runs live on air, applied to the three needs in [[Security, Control and Approval]]. He describes it as "a practice of deep welcoming and deep noticing," working through contrast.

He was introduced to it by Jim Dethmer and [[Diana Chapman]] of the [[Conscious Leadership Group]]; the method itself is Hale Dwoskin's, and Dave credits "Kin, who's the head of it."

## The Model

Three steps, run separately for **security**, then **control**, then **approval**:

**1 — Find the one who wants it.**
> Can you find in yourself the one who wants security — that seeks safety outside yourself?

**2 — Welcome that part.**
> Don't go making that wrong or bad. We're all human. We all have that part.

**3 — Relax as the one beyond it.**
> Just for now, could you relax as the one that's beyond wanting security?

For control, the prompt widens: the part that "wants to control how life goes, wants to control your startup, wants to control other people." For approval: the part that "really wants people to think certain things about you."

→ [[Welcome the one who wants security, then relax as the one beyond wanting it]]

## The Triple Welcoming

Each layer catches what the previous one leaves. Welcoming a feeling still leaves the resistance to it; welcoming the resistance still leaves the identification with it. The third step is the one that separates *fear is here* from *I am afraid.* → [[Saying I am angry turns a passing feeling into an identity]] · [[If you cannot welcome the feeling, welcome the resistance]]

## Why It Is Built This Way

**The wanting part is welcomed, never eliminated.** Dave: the method points toward the true self "without making it wrong or bad that we're human and have these ego patterns." An attempt to *stop* wanting security would be more striving. → [[Welcome the shame but do not believe the thoughts that come with it]]

**"Just for now."** Every step is temporary and therefore available. Not *become someone who needs no approval* — only relax as that one, for a moment.

## How Dave Uses It

Jesse's addition for aiming it at something live: rather than the abstract need, take a specific issue — *I really want this fundraising round to go well*, *I want my wife off her phone* — and find which of the three it sits under. "They all come back to one of those three, or multiple."
