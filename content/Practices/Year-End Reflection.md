# Year-End Reflection

## What It Is

Six questions Dave produces on the spot when asked to close out the year. Five look back, one looks forward, and the ordering is deliberate — completion before vision.

## The Model

1. **What do you need to do or say to get complete with the year?**
   > I think most people don't create completion. There's something really powerful about completing the year.
2. **What do I want to let go of or leave behind?**
   > Most people are way too focused on attaining and gaining. There's a lot of value in reflecting on what do I want to shed.
3. **What did I learn?** The most important things learned, or the ways you grew.
4. **What do I want to celebrate?** Biggest wins.
5. **What were my biggest challenges or setbacks?** Both 4 and 5 yield learning.
6. **What's my most beautiful, exquisite vision for next year?**

**The optional practice:** write next year's letter from its end. *Dear Dave, here I am on 31 December — what an amazing year it's been…* → [[Language is a tool to create reality, not only to describe it]]

## How Dave Uses It

Questions 1 and 2 are the distinctive ones and carry the model's real argument. A year that isn't completed is carried, and what's carried can't be built from — which is [[You cannot get there until you are here]] applied at annual scale. Letting go gets equal billing with attaining, for the same reason Dave prescribes [[Grieving the future you expected is what frees you to create one|grieving the version of the future that didn't arrive]].

Question 6's adjectives — *beautiful, exquisite* — are doing work too. A vision that has to meet that description can't be a target list, and it has to be felt, which is what makes it clean fuel rather than another set of numbers to fall short of. → [[Desired Future State (DFS)]]

Jesse notes a broader pattern here: good questions are Dave's actual superpower. "Sometimes ahead of a thing I'll be like, I need 3 questions to get my thinking clear, and he'll just send me questions."
