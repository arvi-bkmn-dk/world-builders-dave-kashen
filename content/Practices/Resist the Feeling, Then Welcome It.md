# Resist the Feeling, Then Welcome It

## What It Is

A short guided exercise Dave runs to make the difference between an emotion and the resistance to it felt rather than argued. You deliberately resist a feeling, then deliberately welcome it, and compare the two.

## The Steps

1. **Pick the feeling you most resist**: fear, anger, sadness, shame or joy.

> So first, see if you can identify fear, anger, sadness, shame, joy.

2. **Conjure it**, using a past or imagined scenario where you'd expect to feel it.
3. **Locate it in the body.**

> Where in your body do you feel the feeling?

4. **Resist it on purpose.**

> As best you can, just just see if you could just just resist it.

5. **Then welcome it**, like a guest.

> Could you even welcome it like a welcome guest in your home?

> And you're not welcoming it to make something happen.

## How Dave Uses It

In the workshop, a participant reported that resisting took them into deeper fear and an internal quivering, while fully allowing it let things settle: *"the air is clearer now."* Dave calls that the usual result, and the paradox that makes it work: you can't welcome a feeling to make it leave. → [[You cannot welcome a feeling in order to get rid of it]]

The insight behind it, that an emotion plus resistance is a different experience from the emotion alone, Dave credits to [[Joe Hudson]] of [[Art of Accomplishment]].
