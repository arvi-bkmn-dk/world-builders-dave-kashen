# The Reset After a Rejection

**Practice for:** [[A fundraising rejection lands at three layers at once]] — what to do in the moment after a no.

## Steps

1. **Create a reset.** Don't go straight to the next thing. Even 10–15 minutes between meetings makes a real difference.
2. **Drop from your head to your body.** Ask where this shows up right now: chest, stomach, throat. Just locate it.
3. **Welcome what's there.** The goal isn't to make the feeling go away, only to let it move through you.
4. **Look at the story your mind is making.** Temporary and specific, or permanent and pervasive? → [[Label a setback temporary and specific, not permanent and pervasive]]
5. **If you're short on time, just breathe.** Four in, four out, four times. → [[Four by Four Breathing]]

> Give yourself a moment to land before you're back on.

> The goal isn't to make the feeling go away. It's just to let it move through you.

> Four seconds in, four seconds out, four times.

## Also in [[NL 76 - What to Do in the Moments After a Rejection]]

The second version adds two steps between feeling it and acting:

- **Accept yourself for being below the line.** Judging yourself for it puts you below the line about being below the line.
- **Take 100% responsibility for how you created the experience.** Not what you did wrong, which is *"self-blame wearing responsibility's clothing,"* but *how did I create this experience for myself?* Usually: an attachment to an expectation you didn't know you held.

> The lie your mind tells is that if you let yourself feel it, you'll turn into a puddle and be useless for your next meeting.

> Only then, from presence, get curious about what you might do differently.
