# The Facts and Stories Exercise

**Practice for:** [[The Storyteller Mind]] — this is the exercise that model is meant to be done with.

## What It Is

The first exercise Dave gave Jesse, and the one Jesse now hands to other entrepreneurs. A worksheet with two columns — **facts** on one side, **stories** on the other — applied to a period of your history that still carries a charge. It is [[The Storyteller Mind]] made concrete on your own material rather than on hypotheticals.

## The Model

**1. Write the facts.** The test is mechanical: *a fact is something a video camera could record.* → [[A fact is something a video camera could record]]

Jesse's column looked like: started the company in October 2009 · ran the first Facebook ad in June 2010 · Facebook called in January 2011 · made a decision at a meeting to switch business model · ran a process to sell the company · chose not to sell.

**2. Write the stories alongside them.** The interpretation the mind wrapped around each fact. Jesse's: shouldn't have sold the first division · really should have sold in 2015 · this business model was holding us back from our true greatness.

The point of the parallel layout is the comparison. Both columns had felt identical from the inside — "it was a mistake not to sell the business in 2015" felt like a recording of what happened.

**3. For each story, ask how the opposite could be true.** Not to install the opposite as the new truth, but to demonstrate that the item is contestable and therefore not a fact. → [[Asking how the opposite could be true loosens the grip without requiring you to be wrong]]

Jesse's worked example: *I should have sold the business in 2015* → if I had, I'd never have ended up working with Dave. "That was not a mistake. It was actually exactly what was meant."

## How Dave Uses It

The outcome he's after is discharge, not accuracy. In Jesse's words it "reduces the charge around your perspective on something that took place," which then frees attention for what's next — "you can't play the next point if you're still beating yourself up over the shot you just missed."

It also surfaces what has merely been suppressed. Jesse thought he was over things and wasn't, until the exercise dredged them up and let them actually process. → [[Entrepreneurs suppress a lot of stuff and think they are over it when they are not]]

## The Book Chapter

Chapter 3 gives the fullest written treatment, and grounds it in Kahneman.

**Kahneman's System 1 and System 2** — fast, automatic, instinctive and emotional versus slow, logical and analytical. *"Most of us identify with System 2 and consider ourselves rational. According to Kahneman, we actually make most of our decisions based on System 1."* Which Dave calls mostly good news, before the sting:

**The coffee shop.** You spot a friend, arms crossed, looking down and frowning, and decide to wait until she's in a better mood. *"In a single instant, you made up a story about your friend, believed it, and acted accordingly."* Cost here: a delayed conversation. But ask her later *what she was so upset about*, and —

**The highway.** Someone cuts you off and the voice says *that jerk made me so angry.*

*(For all you know, he adds, the other driver had a pregnant wife and was racing to the hospital.)*

**And how stories serve beliefs.** Cynthia from Chapter 2, carrying *I'm not worthy*, meets an employee who ignores a request: *"Cynthia might tell herself a story about him **disrespecting her and not seeing her as worth responding to.** This satisfies her mind's need to make sense of what happened **and allows her to reinforce her belief.** Unfortunately, it's likely untrue."* → [[Core Beliefs]]
